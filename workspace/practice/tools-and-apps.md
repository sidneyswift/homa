# tools-and-apps

> ✅ FRESH — Last researched: 2026-07-02

Survey of useful homeschool tools and apps across all ages (PreK–12), organized by **purpose** rather than by product. For each tool: age-fit, free vs. paid, and what it actually does well. Kid-facing screen use should be intentional and adult-mediated — the AAP recommends ≤1 hr/day of high-quality programming for ages 2–5, and hands-on, adult-led learning generally beats app-based learning in the early years. Treat apps as supplements and "bridges" to offline activity, not core curriculum.

## Sources / frameworks

- **AAP (American Academy of Pediatrics) media guidelines** — Ages 2–5: ≤1 hr/day high-quality programming, co-viewing, quality > quantity, interactive > passive. Foundational filter for any kid-facing app.
- **Common Sense Media / Common Sense Education** — Independent, ad-free reviews with age ratings and a "learning rating" separate from the entertainment rating. Best single vetting source before installing anything.
- **The "bridge concept" (minimal-screen philosophy)** — The best tools inspire offline activity (finite loops, clear off-ramps, real-world transferable skills, no manipulative/engagement-maximizing design). By this metric a printable pipeline (worksheets, flash cards, decodable readers) can out-perform most apps because the child's output is physical.
- **ESSA evidence tiers / independent efficacy ratings** — Prefer tools with real evidence (e.g., Zearn's ESSA Tier 1 rating, verified by Johns Hopkins) over self-reported vendor metrics.
- **COPPA compliance** — For any account a child uses directly, confirm COPPA compliance and check whether the developer has filed App Store privacy details (a missing filing is a yellow flag).

## Math practice

### Khan Academy / Khan Academy Kids — free, PreK–12+
- **Khan Academy Kids** (ages ~2–8, free, no ads/IAP): adaptive reading, math, SEL, creativity; built with Stanford GSE; COPPA compliant. The consensus best free app for **PreK–Lower Elementary**.
- **Khan Academy** (Upper Elementary through **High** and beyond, free): mastery-based K–12 math paths plus official SAT/AP prep. The default free spine for structured math at every older band. No subscription.

### XtraMath — fact fluency, Lower Elementary–Middle
- Free (paid ~$2/yr adds printables). No-frills ~10 min/day drill for addition, subtraction, multiplication, division facts. Best fit **K–5** once facts are being memorized; efficient, not "fun." Pairs well with a conceptual program.

### Prodigy Math — game-based practice, grades 1–8 (Upper Elementary–Middle)
- Free to play (RPG wrapper, 1,500+ skills, grades 1–8); premium ~$59–180/yr adds cosmetics. The free tier teaches fine; note aggressive upselling of members-only cosmetics — a distraction for some kids. Strong for reluctant/game-motivated learners.

### Zearn — free K–8 curriculum-aligned (Lower–Upper Elementary, early Middle)
- Free, curriculum-aligned, **ESSA Tier 1** evidence (independently verified). One of the strongest free complete K–8 supplements; good conceptual instruction, not just drill.

### For older / advanced students
- **DeltaMath** (grades 6–12, free core; ~$95/yr adds videos): teacher-style problem sets for **Middle–High** algebra through calculus. Excellent free option.
- **Desmos** (free): graphing, scientific, geometry, 3D calculators — essential utility for **Middle–High** algebra/precalc.
- **GeoGebra** (free): dynamic geometry, graphing, and collaborative math tools; **Upper Elementary–High**.
- **IXL** (paid, ~$10–20/mo; K–12): diagnostic + comprehensive practice, school-standards aligned. No meaningful free tier (~10 questions/day cap). Useful mainly if you want its diagnostics/reporting.

## Reading & phonics

### Duolingo ABC — free, PreK–Lower Elementary (ages 3–6)
- Completely free, no ads/IAP, COPPA compliant. Letter recognition, phonics, sight words, early comprehension with spaced repetition and speech recognition. Best free early-literacy app for **PreK–K**. (Distinct from the main Duolingo app — see World languages.)

### Teach Your Monster to Read — free web / low-cost app, PreK–Lower Elementary (ages 3–7)
- Free on web; ~$4.99 mobile app. Game-based systematic phonics for children in the earliest reading stages. Strong, research-informed phonics progression for **PreK–K**.

### Epic — reading library, Lower–Upper Elementary
- ~$13.99/mo or ~$84.99/yr (free tiers for educators historically). 40,000+ leveled ebooks, audiobooks, read-alongs, and educational videos. Best as a **volume-of-reading** engine for **K–5** once a child is decoding; not a phonics teacher.

### Other phonics / early-reading options
- **Starfall** — free core "Learn to Read" (phonics mini-books), COPPA-conscious; paid Starfall Home (~$35/yr) unlocks more. Good **PreK–K** self-serve free tier; much good content is now paywalled, so treat the paid tier as optional.
- **Endless Alphabet** (~$8.99 one-time, no subscription/ads): animated vocabulary/word-puzzle builder; strong for **PreK–Lower Elementary** vocabulary. Finite content (eventually exhausted) but excellent value.
- **Hooked on Phonics / ABCmouse** — structured paid programs (ABCmouse ~$12.99/mo). Broad but pricey; ABCmouse is widely flagged for reward-heavy design and deliberately difficult cancellation. Vet against Common Sense Media first.

*For fluency, comprehension, and writing at older bands, the highest-leverage "tools" are a **library card + a decodable/leveled reader supply** and a writing feedback loop — not an app. See Free-resource hubs.*

## World languages

### Duolingo — free, ~ages 7+ (Upper Elementary–High)
- Free (Super/Max paid tiers add features; Max ~ $30/mo adds AI tutoring). 40+ languages, gamified bite-sized lessons. Main app requires age 13+ to self-register; under-13 needs a parent-managed account. In 2026 Duolingo added a **Kids mode** (roughly ages 7+) within the main app with parental controls and restricted social features. Fills a real gap for families where the parent doesn't speak the target language — best as structured input, weaker on communicative/conversational fluency.
- **Duolingo Kids** (ages 4–7) and **Duolingo ABC** (3+, literacy not foreign language) cover the younger bands.
- Note: for genuine early-childhood *second-language acquisition* (PreK), character-driven immersion (kids' shows in the target language, a tutor, family communicative phrases) generally beats vocabulary apps, which teach labels rather than connected communicative speech.

## Typing

### TypingClub — free, Lower Elementary–High (ages ~5–12+)
- Free for home users with no meaningful compromise; 600+ lessons from home-row to advanced fluency, plus **Jungle Junior** for early learners. Scales across the whole age band without feeling babyish. Best all-around free pick.

### Typing.com — free, Upper Elementary–High (ages ~8+)
- Free K–12 curriculum with games, plus digital-citizenship and coding modules; progress tracking for parents. Strong structured practice for **grades 3+** hitting grade-level WPM.

### Nitro Type — free, ~ages 10+ (Middle–High)
- Competitive real-time typing races (same team as Typing.com). Best as a **motivator** for kids already past the basics who respond to leaderboards.

### Typesy Homeschool — paid, all ages
- Teacher dashboard, multi-child tracking, large lesson bank plus a literacy layer. Worth it only if typing is a formal, tracked part of the school day; otherwise TypingClub is enough.

## Science & simulations

### PhET Interactive Simulations — free, Upper Elementary–High
- Free interactive math/science simulations from Univ. of Colorado Boulder (founded by Nobel laureate Carl Wieman, 2002). 150+ physics, chemistry, biology, earth-science, and math sims; research-based, game-like inquiry; searchable by grade, subject, and accessibility. Best for **grades 4–12** exploration and virtual labs.

### Mystery Science — free tier, PreK–Upper Elementary (strongest K–5)
- Video-based "mysteries" drive inquiry, followed by low-prep hands-on activities using household items (30–45 min). The strongest **elementary** secular science option; minimal parent prep. Free access has historically been generous; confirm current tier.

### Supplemental
- **CrashCourse** (YouTube, free): dense video series across science, history, and more — best for **Middle–High** as lecture-style enrichment/review.
- **CK-12** (see hubs): free simulations and adaptive practice bundled with its FlexBooks — good **Middle–High** science practice.

## Record-keeping & planning (parent-facing)

*These are for the educator, not the child. Match the tool to your state's requirements — some states need attendance, portfolios, and/or transcripts; others require almost nothing.*

### Homeschool Planet — paid, all ages
- Purpose-built, drag-and-drop weekly scheduler with attendance tracking, grade books, progress reports, and **transcript** generation; can cover portfolio-style documentation in one place. The most full-featured commercial option; best when your state requires formal reporting or you're managing high-school transcripts.

### Planbook — paid, all ages
- Clean, professional lesson planning and polished records; lighter on multi-student tracking and attendance than purpose-built homeschool tools.

### Trello — free tier, all ages
- Flexible Kanban boards; can be customized into a digital **portfolio** by attaching student work, photos, and descriptions. No grading, attendance, or report generation built in — pair it with something else if you need formal records.

### Lightweight / free stack
- A general note/knowledge app (e.g., **Obsidian**, **Notion**, **Google Docs/Drive**) plus a paper planner and a shared calendar (co-op, classes, field trips) is often a better, cheaper system than a dedicated homeschool planner — especially at younger ages with minimal reporting requirements. Portfolio/sharing apps (**Seesaw**, **Bloomz**) matter mainly where a state requires a documented portfolio.
- **Trend to watch:** AI-assisted planners that auto-generate portfolios/transcripts from lesson logs, reducing double entry. Useful, but verify what data leaves your control.

## Free-resource hubs

The highest-value "tools" are often free public resources. Build the stack around these before paying for anything.

- **Khan Academy** (free, PreK–12): math/science/humanities instruction spine; distance-learning resources. Khan Academy Kids for the youngest.
- **CK-12 Foundation** (free): customizable digital textbooks (**FlexBooks**), simulations, and adaptive practice, aligned to Common Core; strong **Middle–High** math/science.
- **K12 Learning Hub** (free): 17,000+ lessons/videos/activities, K–12, no enrollment. K12 also runs **free virtual field trips** (aquariums, art museums, expeditions).
- **OER Commons** (free): open educational resources across subjects, searchable K–12.
- **Your local public library** (free, all ages): physical + digital collections via **Libby/OverDrive** and **Hoopla** (ebooks, audiobooks, learning videos); many libraries run homeschool programs and reference help. The single best free reading and research resource.
- **Museums, national/state parks, and cultural institutions** (mostly free): **Google Arts & Culture** virtual tours and exhibits (all ages); Smithsonian, Library of Congress, and NASA education portals; museum **free days**; national-park **Junior Ranger** programs and virtual field trips. Excellent for social studies, art, and science across every band.

## Key takeaways

- **Start free, add paid only for a proven gap.** Khan Academy (math/all-subject spine), Duolingo ABC / Teach Your Monster (early reading), TypingClub (typing), PhET + Mystery Science (science), and the public library cover PreK–12 at zero cost. ([Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org/), [PhET](https://phet.colorado.edu/))
- **In the early years (PreK–Lower Elementary), adult-mediated hands-on learning beats apps.** Keep kid-facing screens ≤1 hr/day, co-view, and prefer apps that "bridge" to offline activity. ([AAP media guidelines](https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/media-and-children/))
- **Match math tools to their job:** conceptual instruction (Zearn/Khan), fact fluency (XtraMath), motivation (Prodigy), older/advanced work (DeltaMath, Desmos, GeoGebra). Don't stack redundant math apps. ([Zearn](https://www.zearn.org/), [DeltaMath](https://www.deltamath.com/))
- **Phonics apps teach decoding; libraries build fluency.** Use Duolingo ABC / Teach Your Monster / Starfall for phonics (PreK–K), then shift to volume reading (Epic + library card) once decoding is solid. ([Teach Your Monster](https://www.teachyourmonster.org/), [Epic](https://www.getepic.com/))
- **Vet before installing:** check Common Sense Media's learning rating, confirm COPPA compliance, and watch for missing App Store privacy filings and hard-to-cancel subscriptions (ABCmouse, some Starfall tiers). ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/))
- **Right-size record-keeping to your state.** A free note app + calendar + Trello portfolio suffices at younger ages; move to Homeschool Planet when you need formal transcripts/attendance (typically **High**). ([Homeschool Planet](https://homeschoolplanet.com/))
- **Language apps give structure, not fluency.** Duolingo (~7+) is great progressive input; genuine communicative fluency still needs human interaction or immersion, especially in the youngest band. ([Duolingo](https://www.duolingo.com/))
