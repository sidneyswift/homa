# curriculum-reviews

> ✅ FRESH — Last researched: 2026-07-02

Tier B practice reference: an all-ages (PreK–12), subject-organized survey of widely-used homeschool curricula with honest notes on pedagogical approach, rigor, age range, and secular vs. faith-based orientation. **This is a neutral starting reference for comparison shopping, not an endorsement.** No curriculum is "best" in the abstract — fit depends on the child's learning style, the parent's prep budget, the family's worldview, and where the child actually is (use each publisher's free placement test). Cross-check anything here against a live review site and, ideally, a sample lesson before buying.

## Sources / frameworks

- **Cathy Duffy Reviews** (cathyduffyreviews.com) — long-running independent homeschool curriculum reviews, organized by subject and grade; notes worldview/religious content explicitly.
- **Kate's Homeschool Math** (kateshomeschoolmath.com) — deep, practitioner-level math curriculum reviews and buying guides (Singapore/Dimensions, Math-U-See, etc.).
- **Homeschool Picks** (homeschoolpicks.com) — comparison-style reviews across math, science, and language arts; secular-vs-faith breakdowns.
- **Homeschool Start Guide** (homeschoolstartguide.com) — side-by-side curriculum comparisons (writing, science) with fit guidance.
- **Homeschooling with Dyslexia** (homeschoolingwithdyslexia.com) — Orton-Gillingham reading program comparisons; useful even for typical readers.
- **UF Literacy Institute** (ufli.education.ufl.edu) — publisher/research home of UFLI Foundations; research-backed phonics.
- **The HomeSchoolMom** (thehomeschoolmom.com) — user reviews and curriculum category guides.

**Two orientation questions to ask before evaluating anything:**
1. **Mastery vs. spiral?** Mastery programs (Singapore, Math-U-See, Beast Academy) teach one topic to depth, then move on. Spiral programs (Saxon, Horizons) cycle through many topics with continuous review. Kids who like depth and get bored by review lean mastery; kids who forget without repetition lean spiral.
2. **Secular, neutral, or faith-based?** "Secular" = no religious content and teaches mainstream science (evolution, deep time). "Neutral" = no religious content but leaves worldview to the parent (e.g., BookShark, largely Story of the World). "Faith-based" = religious content woven in; some (e.g., Apologia) also teach young-earth creation and omit/critique evolution. This is the single biggest source of buyer's remorse — check it first.

## Math

### Singapore Math / Dimensions Math — mastery, conceptual, secular
- **Approach:** Concrete → Pictorial → Abstract (CPA); bar-model problem solving; strong mental math. Dimensions is the U.S. edition (PK–8) that refreshed the classic Primary Mathematics.
- **Rigor / age:** High conceptual rigor. Widely regarded as a top choice for a complete PreK–8 foundation. Excellent word-problem training; the mental-math emphasis pays off in middle school when rote procedures break down. Ends at grade 8, so needs a high-school follow-on.
- **Watch-outs:** Requires meaningful parent involvement, especially early; lessons run long if you do every activity; consumable workbooks. Not the pick if you want fully independent work.

### Beast Academy — mastery, puzzle/depth, secular
- **Approach:** Comic-book style (from Art of Problem Solving); problem-solving-first, forces genuine reasoning over pattern-matching. Grades ~1–5 (with a Pre-Level).
- **Rigor / age:** Very high; a child who finishes Beast Academy 5 has done pre-algebra-quality thinking in elementary. Best for a mathematically inclined / puzzle-loving learner who needs challenge and depth.
- **Watch-outs:** Can frustrate kids who aren't math-confident; not a gentle on-ramp; elementary only (feeds into AoPS for older). Pair-able with or as an enrichment layer over a core program.

### Math-U-See — mastery, multisensory, secular
- **Approach:** Steve Demme's mastery program built around distinctive manipulative blocks and video instruction; 13 levels named with Greek letters (Primer → Calculus), not grade-locked. K–12 in one system.
- **Rigor / age:** Deliberate, repetitive, conservative sequence — a strength for kids who need to *see* and *build* every concept (including struggling learners or those with math anxiety), a weakness for accelerated kids. Reviewers explicitly say it's *not* the fit for a gifted math kid (steer those to Singapore or Beast Academy). Its one-system-through-high-school coverage is a real draw for families committing long-term.
- **Watch-outs:** Manipulatives slow things down by design; word-problem coverage is lighter than Singapore's.

### Saxon Math — spiral, incremental, secular
- **Approach:** Traditional, incremental, drill-heavy. Each lesson adds a small concept then mixes it into a cumulative problem set for months. K–12, including Algebra 1/2, Geometry, Calculus.
- **Rigor / age:** Builds durable computational fluency — kids finish knowing facts, procedures, and problem types cold. The reps genuinely work.
- **Watch-outs:** Low conceptual "why," high problem volume. Mathematically inclined kids often find it tedious by upper elementary; less-inclined kids can find the volume crushing. Reputation as a solid but joyless workhorse; strong for high-school upper math where procedural mastery matters.

**Quick math map:** Singapore/Dimensions = best all-round K–8 conceptual foundation • Beast Academy = depth/challenge for the puzzle-lover • Math-U-See = multisensory mastery, great for strugglers, K–12 continuity • Saxon = procedural fluency and drill, strong in high school.

## Reading / Phonics

### All About Reading (AAR) — Orton-Gillingham, multisensory, secular
- **Approach:** Scripted, open-and-go, multisensory O-G-based phonics with decodable readers, tiles, and review cards. Levels Pre-1 through 4 (roughly PreK–grade 4 decoding). Companion All About Spelling.
- **Rigor / age:** Explicit, systematic, Science-of-Reading-aligned; very gentle and hand-holding — low prep. Popular with dyslexia families for its incremental pacing.
- **Watch-outs:** Some parents report slower progress for kids who need more kinesthetic engagement; consumable components add cost; teacher-intensive (not independent).

### Logic of English (LOE) Foundations — Orton-Gillingham, kinesthetic, secular
- **Approach:** Phonics + spelling + handwriting + grammar integrated; heavy on kinesthetic games and multisensory activity (handwriting practice comes in game form). Levels A–D for early readers; Essentials for older/remedial.
- **Rigor / age:** Systematic and thorough; praised for wiggly, active early learners. Teaches the "why" behind English spelling (phonogram/rule-based).
- **Watch-outs:** Prep-heavier and busier than AAR; the density can overwhelm; strong fit for a hands-on child but a lot to run.

### UFLI Foundations — research-backed phonics, secular
- **Approach:** Created by the University of Florida Literacy Institute; explicit systematic phonics with a clear scope & sequence, roughly K–2. Inexpensive single manual (plus free/low-cost digital resources).
- **Rigor / age:** Strongly research-backed — a 2025 study found large gains (equivalent to ~8 extra months of instruction for kindergartners and ~1.5 years for 1st graders vs. traditional instruction). Growing fast in both classrooms and homeschools.
- **Watch-outs:** Covers phonemic awareness, phonics, and word study but **not writing** — add a writing/composition component around grades 1–2. Not fully open-and-go unless you use the digital tools (otherwise you print fluency sheets and decodable passages yourself).

**Also common:** decodable-reader sets (e.g., Bob Books) as *applied practice* — controlled vocabulary that only uses learned patterns, so no picture-guessing. These are supplements, not standalone programs — always pair with systematic phonics. Heggerty is a widely used scripted 10-min/day *oral phonemic-awareness* supplement (PreK–2), rooted in Science of Reading; low prep, but a supplement, not a full reading program.

**Quick reading map:** AAR = gentlest open-and-go O-G • LOE = integrated + kinesthetic for active kids • UFLI = research-backed, cheap, add writing.

## Writing / Composition

### IEW (Institute for Excellence in Writing) — structured, process-first, worldview-neutral
- **Approach:** Teaches one replicable method — Key Word Outline (read source → extract keywords → rebuild in own words) plus a "dress-ups" stylistic toolbox. Structured units, grades ~3–12.
- **Rigor / age:** The program most recommended for the child who *dreads* writing — gives reluctant writers a concrete, repeatable process and gives strong students an academic-writing framework. Families often report notable jumps on standardized writing and college essays. Works well for dyslexia/ADHD learners who need explicit structure. Content is largely worldview-neutral (theme-based packs exist across genres).
- **Watch-outs:** Can produce formulaic, samey writing; kids who already love writing and want their own voice may find it constraining.

### Brave Writer — exploratory, creative-first, secular
- **Approach:** Julie Bogart's "writing lifestyle" — copywork, dictation, poetry teatimes, narration, literature-paired guides; builds a love of language toward independent writing over time. All ages (Jot It Down → Partnership Writing → Faltering Ownership → the Boomerang/Arrow for lit).
- **Rigor / age:** Develops genuine voice and enjoyment of language; excellent for literature-lovers and Charlotte Mason / classical families.
- **Watch-outs:** Low structure and few hard benchmarks frustrate parents/kids who need a measurable path. Progress is harder to see week to week.

**Note:** Some families run **both** — IEW for academic/structured writing, Brave Writer for creative expression — accepting the extra time cost. Rule of thumb: a child who dreads writing → IEW; a child who loves writing but is messy/unstructured → Brave Writer.

## Science

### BFSU (Building Foundations of Scientific Understanding) — secular, rigorous, discussion-based
- **Approach:** Teaches science as an interconnected web (not isolated units); Socratic, discussion-driven, K–8 in three volumes. Teacher-guide only (~$30) — you supply readings/materials.
- **Rigor / age:** Arguably the strongest at building genuine scientific *thinking* at the lowest cost. Best for a parent comfortable leading discussion.
- **Watch-outs:** **Not** open-and-go — high parent prep and planning; no scripted lessons or ready kits.

### Elemental Science — secular, classical, living-books + experiments
- **Approach:** Classical four-year rotation (Earth/Astronomy → Biology → Chemistry → Physics) using living books, narration, and experiments; grades PreK–high school. Integrates cleanly with a classical history rotation.
- **Rigor / age:** Solid, affordable, moderate hands-on. Pairs with library books + inexpensive kits for a complete program often under ~$100/year.
- **Watch-outs:** Lighter on labs than lab-intensive options (e.g., Real Science Odyssey); more scheduling/assembly than a boxed textbook.

### Apologia — faith-based (young-earth), textbook-heavy
- **Approach:** Single-subject, one-topic-per-year textbooks written for homeschoolers (Botany, Zoology, Astronomy, Anatomy, Chemistry, Physics, etc.); Christian worldview woven throughout. Elementary through high school (high-school courses are college-prep, lab-based).
- **Rigor / age:** Rigorous and thorough at the high-school level, well-regarded for lab science and transcripts within faith communities.
- **Watch-outs:** **Young-earth creationist**; does not present evolution as a valid theory. A poor fit for secular families or anyone wanting mainstream science on origins/deep time. Check this before buying.

**Quick science map:** BFSU = deepest thinking, cheapest, most prep • Elemental = classical rotation, balanced, affordable • Apologia = faith-based, strong high-school labs, young-earth (verify worldview fit).

## History / Social Studies

### Story of the World (SOTW) — narrative, chronological, mostly-neutral (secular-friendly)
- **Approach:** Susan Wise Bauer's four-volume story-based world history (Ancient → Middle Ages → Early Modern → Modern), read chronologically; activity guide with maps, projects, and book lists. Grades ~1–8 (classical "first pass" through history).
- **Rigor / age:** Engaging, integrated narrative kids remember; the activity guide makes it flexible for multiple ages together. Broadly usable by secular families (covers world religions as history from a fairly even-handed stance — spot-check content to your comfort).
- **Watch-outs:** Not a full stand-alone "social studies" scope (light on civics/geography-as-subject); pair with map work and read-alouds. A few sections reflect the author's framing — preview if sensitive.

### BookShark — literature-based, fully-planned, secular/neutral
- **Approach:** Secular spin-off of Sonlight (same literature-rich, boxed, scheduled model, with the Bible content removed). History + literature + language arts packages, PreK–12, laid out in a day-by-day Instructor's Guide.
- **Rigor / age:** Rich reading lists and open-and-go planning; "religiously neutral" — it prompts parents to discuss beliefs rather than teaching a doctrine. Great for families who want everything scheduled and reading-heavy.
- **Watch-outs:** Premium price; reading-load-heavy (a strength or a burden depending on the child); literature-based history means less textbook-style structure.

**Quick history map:** SOTW = narrative chronological spine, cheap, mix-ages • BookShark = boxed, scheduled, literature-rich, secular. (Faith-based analog: Sonlight; young-earth analog: Mystery of History / Notgrass.)

## All-in-One / Boxed Programs

### The Good and the Beautiful (TGTB) — faith-based (LDS-founded), open-and-go
- **Approach:** Integrated open-and-go program; Language Arts combines reading, phonics, spelling, grammar, vocabulary, art, and geography in one; spiral math with an included manipulatives box. Very low parent prep.
- **Rigor / age:** LA is PreK–11; Math is K–8; handwriting PreK–7. **Math and Language Arts K–8 PDFs are free to download** (a major draw), with inexpensive printed options. Beautiful design, literature-forward.
- **Watch-outs:** Christian theology (LDS-founded, broadly Christian in content) is woven throughout — hard for a secular family to strip out. Math is sometimes called light/slow for accelerated kids; verify rigor against your child's level.

### Oak Meadow — secular, Waldorf-influenced, gentle
- **Approach:** K–12 secular program with strong Waldorf influence in the early grades (arts, nature, storytelling, slower pace); becomes more standard/academic and independent in the upper grades. Accredited distance-learning option available.
- **Rigor / age:** Gentle and holistic early; solid and flexible through high school (one of the few secular boxed options that truly spans K–12). Good for families wanting less screen time and a nature/arts emphasis.
- **Watch-outs:** Early grades are intentionally unhurried — may feel "behind" to families wanting early academics; premium price, especially with enrollment/teacher support.

### Blossom & Root — secular, Charlotte-Mason-inspired, nature/arts
- **Approach:** Secular, CM-inspired early-elementary program emphasizing nature study, living books, art, poetry, and gentle exploration; typically PreK–grade 6.
- **Rigor / age:** Warm, literature- and nature-rich, low-pressure — well-suited to young children and families wanting a gentle, creativity-forward start. Often paired with a separate math program.
- **Watch-outs:** Early-elementary ceiling (doesn't run through high school); not a heavy-academics or standards-checklist program; usually needs a math (and later, science) supplement.

**Quick all-in-one map:** TGTB = free-tier, beautiful, faith-based, low-prep • Oak Meadow = secular Waldorf, gentle→academic, true K–12 • Blossom & Root = secular CM nature/arts, gentle early elementary.

## Key takeaways

- **Fit beats reputation.** Match mastery-vs-spiral and secular-vs-faith to your child and family *first*; a "top" curriculum used against the grain of your child's style underperforms a "lesser" one that fits. [Homeschool Picks](https://homeschoolpicks.com/curriculum-reviews/math-curriculum/singapore-math-vs-beast-academy/)
- **For math, the classic split:** Singapore/Dimensions for conceptual K–8, Beast Academy for depth/challenge, Math-U-See for multisensory mastery (and struggling learners), Saxon for procedural drill and high-school fluency. [weblogg-ed](https://weblogg-ed.com/2026/math-curriculum-saxon-singapore-beast-academy/) · [Kate's Homeschool Math](https://kateshomeschoolmath.com/math-u-see-review-buying-guide/)
- **For phonics, prioritize systematic + Science-of-Reading-aligned.** UFLI is research-backed and cheap (add writing); AAR is the gentlest open-and-go; LOE suits active, kinesthetic kids. [EdWeek on UFLI](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/researchers-created-a-phonics-program-with-dramatic-results-how-it-works/2025/01) · [Homeschooling with Dyslexia](https://homeschoolingwithdyslexia.com/comparison-orton-gillingham-reading-programs/)
- **For writing, pick the ends of the spectrum deliberately:** IEW for reluctant/structured writers, Brave Writer for creative/literature-loving ones; some families run both. [Homeschool Start Guide](https://homeschoolstartguide.com/blog/homeschool-writing-curriculum)
- **Verify science worldview before buying.** BFSU and Elemental Science are secular; Apologia is young-earth creationist and omits evolution — a dealbreaker for many families either way. [Homeschool Picks – Science](https://homeschoolpicks.com/curriculum-reviews/science-curriculum/best-secular-science-curriculum/)
- **All-in-one saves planning but locks in worldview and pace.** TGTB is faith-based (LDS-founded) with a free tier; Oak Meadow and Blossom & Root are the go-to secular boxed options (Oak Meadow spans K–12, Blossom & Root is gentle early elementary). [Nature Homeschool](https://naturehomeschool.com/best-homeschool-curriculum/)
- **Use free placement tests and samples.** Nearly every publisher (Singapore, Math-U-See, Saxon, TGTB) offers a free placement test and sample lessons — a child's actual level, not their age/grade, should choose the level. [Math-U-See](https://mathusee.com/)
- **This list is a starting reference, not an endorsement.** Homeschool needs shift year to year and child to child; re-evaluate at each transition rather than assuming last year's choice still fits.
