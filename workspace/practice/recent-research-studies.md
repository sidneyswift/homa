# recent-research-studies

> ✅ FRESH — Last researched: 2026-07-02

A living, all-ages (PreK–12) digest of notable recent and authoritative research relevant to homeschooling and child learning: homeschool outcomes (academic, social, long-term), the science of reading, math cognition, executive function / self-regulation, screen time & learning, SEL efficacy, and the effect sizes worth knowing. Each entry gives the finding, the source + year, and a practical implication. Meant to be refreshed by the nightly research cron — newest, most decision-relevant findings float to the top of each section.

## Homeschool outcomes (academic, social, long-term)

### Academic achievement
- **~62% of studies show a positive academic effect for homeschoolers vs. institutionally schooled peers; a minority show mixed/negative results.** The honest read is a *selection-adjusted* one: outcomes track parent involvement and structure, not the label alone. Source: NHERI research facts + the 2025 *Peabody Journal of Education* systematic review update (Ray/Valiente), 2017–2026 window. https://nheri.org/research-facts-on-homeschooling/ · https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0161956X.2025.2605900
- **Practical implication:** Don't over-index on the "homeschoolers score higher" headline. The lever is *what you do* — structured scope-and-sequence, regular assessment, and content breadth — not homeschooling per se. Assess periodically against grade-band standards (see the benchmarks/ files) so gaps surface early.

### Socialization
- **~64% of studies show clearly positive social/emotional outcomes for homeschoolers vs. conventionally schooled peers.** The mechanism is *deliberate outside engagement* — co-ops, sports, scouting/4-H, volunteering, faith communities, museums — not isolation. Source: NHERI socialization fact sheet + the 2025 systematic review. https://nheri.org/homeschooling-socialization-fact-sheet/
- **Practical implication:** Socialization is an *active* line item, not a default. Budget weekly for mixed-age and peer group time; for an only child especially, engineer recurring peer co-regulation reps (co-op, team, class) rather than leaving it to chance.

### Long-term / adult outcomes
- **The 2025 Cardus Education Survey found long-term homeschoolers (8+ years) had the lowest depression/anxiety and highest life-satisfaction scores of the schooling groups studied; a large (12,000+) analysis found home-educated adults more civically engaged (volunteering, community participation).** Roughly half of representative-sample studies of adult success (including college) find home-educated adults perform statistically better; the rest find no significant difference. Source: Cardus 2025; NHERI adult-outcomes summary. https://www.cardus.ca/research/education/ · https://nheri.org/research-facts-on-homeschooling/
- **Practical implication:** Duration and stability matter — the strongest adult results attach to sustained, coherent home education, not a single transitional year. Keep college/career doors open through High band: transcript-worthy coursework, standardized-test readiness, and documented extracurriculars.

## Science of reading

- **Structured literacy beats balanced literacy: a 2025 quantitative synthesis of 40 structured-literacy vs. 28 balanced-literacy studies found a mean effect of ~d = 0.43–0.46 favoring structured literacy.** Systematic, explicit phonics (defined scope-and-sequence + decodable text) remains foundational. Source: *Discover Education* (Springer), 2025. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44217-025-00922-8
- **The field has moved "beyond phonics" — decoding is necessary but not sufficient.** The 2025 National Reading Panel–update discourse and IES Bayesian meta-analysis emphasize that fluency, vocabulary, syntax, and especially *background/content knowledge* drive comprehension once decoding is in place (Scarborough's Reading Rope / Simple View made concrete). Source: Phonics.org 2025 update; IES NCEE Bayesian meta-analysis, Jan 2025. https://www.phonics.org/the-2025-national-reading-panel-update-whats-changed-in-phonics-research/ · https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/2025/01/using-bayesian-meta-analysis-explore-components-early-literacy-interventions
- **Practical implication (by band):** *Lower Elementary (K–2)* — teach phonics explicitly and systematically with decodable readers; don't cue-guess from pictures. *Upper Elementary onward* — once decoding is fluent, the biggest comprehension gains come from building broad content knowledge (science, history, geography) and vocabulary, not more phonics drills. Read widely across domains and talk about the ideas.

## Math cognition

- **Spatial reasoning is reliably linked to math achievement, and it is trainable.** Meta-analysis shows a robust spatial–math association (stronger for logical/relational reasoning than rote arithmetic). Guided spatial *talk* during block/puzzle play ("rotate it in your head first; which piece fills that gap?") transfers to math — the SPACE program (ages 6–7) improved mental rotation and math, with the largest gains for disadvantaged children. Source: *Frontiers* spatial–math editorial (PMC); Farran & Gilmore, *Mind, Brain & Education*, 2025 (DOI 10.1111/mbe.70006). https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7000369/ · https://www.lboro.ac.uk/media-centre/press-releases/2025/june/lego-imrpove-math-and-spatial-ability/
- **Number sense is the foundation and is trainable; fact fluency is flexible/accurate retrieval, not just speed.** Number-sense tutoring improves symbolic-quantity discrimination across ability levels (including learning difficulties) and remediates atypical neural number representations. Source: Stanford integrated number-sense tutoring work (bioRxiv/PMC); Numeracy review, *Encyclopedia on Early Childhood Development*. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9097592/ · https://www.child-encyclopedia.com/pdf/complet/numeracy
- **Math anxiety is a real drag on achievement by adolescence, interacting with working memory.** A 2025 study of 7th graders found cognitive ability *and* math anxiety jointly predict math achievement. Source: PMC, 2025. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12028032/
- **Practical implication (by band):** *PreK–Lower Elementary* — build intuitive number sense (subitizing, counting, comparing quantities) and layer guided spatial talk onto block/puzzle play. *Upper Elementary–Middle* — develop true fact fluency (flexible strategies, not timed-test panic) before piling on procedures; watch for and defuse math anxiety (low-stakes practice, growth framing). *Middle–High* — spatial visualization underpins geometry, functions, and STEM; keep it explicit.

## Executive function & self-regulation

- **EF training works but effects are modest and age-dependent — younger children gain more, and near-transfer beats far-transfer.** A 2025 meta-analysis of 57 cognitive-training studies found an overall g ≈ 0.23, larger for younger children; working memory showed the biggest near-transfer, with some spillover to numeracy/literacy. Source: Birtwistle et al., *SAGE Open*, 2025. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/21582440241311060
- **In typically developing preschoolers, direct EF interventions produce only small effects (verbal WM d ≈ 0.13, inhibitory control d ≈ 0.10, near-null for nonverbal WM).** EF is best understood as a *downstream integrator* of language, motor, and social-relational development rather than a standalone module you drill. Source: *Child Neuropsychology* systematic review/meta-analysis, 2025. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09297049.2025.2595075
- **What moves EF in practice:** curricula with an explicit, repeated *plan → do → review* loop (implemented with fidelity, not "good teaching in general"); cognitively engaging physical activity; play-based mindfulness (noticing emotions without suppressing them builds the impulse–action pause); and unstructured nature play, which especially supports "hot" EF (regulation under emotional arousal). Sources: cognitively-engaging-PA RCT (PMC 2025); play-based-mindfulness and nature-play studies (MDPI *Children* / *Behavioral Sciences*). https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12426270/
- **Practical implication:** Don't buy the "brain-training app will fix focus" premise — transfer is weak. Instead, (1) run a tiny daily plan-do-review ritual ("What's your plan? → do it → how'd it go?"), (2) protect unstructured outdoor time, (3) use movement and play-framed breathing games for hot-EF/frustration-recovery, and (4) feed EF through rich language, motor, and peer interaction rather than isolated worksheets. Scale complexity by band (a PreK plan is one step; a High-schooler's is a multi-week project plan with self-monitoring).

## Screen time & learning

- **The weight of 2025 evidence links excessive screen time to weaker language/vocabulary development in early childhood — but context (co-viewing, interactivity, total dose, family environment) moderates the effect, and some studies find no significant association.** It is not a clean dose-response for all content. Sources: systematic review of screens & language (Cureus/PMC, Nov 2025); *Children* (MDPI) developmental review, 2025. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12718534/ · https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9067/12/10/1297
- **In school-age children, higher screen time is associated with lower academic performance and more anxiety/behavioral problems; school-based reduction programs modestly cut screen time and raise physical activity.** Source: PMC screen-time & academic performance study, 2025; Lai et al., *Child & Adolescent Mental Health* meta-analysis (39 studies, ~95k participants), 2025. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12066102/ · https://acamh.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/camh.70022
- **Practical implication (by band):** *PreK* — minimize passive/solo screens; when used, co-view and talk, since interaction is what preserves language benefit. *Elementary–Middle* — treat screens as displacement risk (they crowd out reading, movement, sleep, play); protect those first. *High* — shift from blanket limits to teaching self-regulated, purposeful use (research, creation, coursework) and defending sleep. Content type and co-use matter as much as raw minutes.

## SEL efficacy

- **Universal school-based SEL programs produce small-but-real academic gains — and the effect grows with age.** A 2025 meta-analysis (40 studies, 33,737 students, grades 1–12) found g ≈ 0.10 for academic achievement. A parallel 2025 meta-analysis (22 studies, 24,510 students) found g ≈ 0.08 overall, larger for middle school (g ≈ 0.12) than elementary (g ≈ 0.075), and a notable g ≈ 0.33 for GPA. Sources: Ha et al., *Review of Educational Research*, 2025; MDPI *Behavioral Sciences* meta-analysis, 2025. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543251367769 · https://www.mdpi.com/2076-328X/15/11/1527
- **Practical implication:** SEL is not a trade-off against academics — it modestly *supports* them, with the clearest payoff by middle school. In a homeschool, "SEL" is largely embedded (emotion coaching, conflict practice, responsibility) rather than a separate curriculum; be intentional about naming and practicing skills (emotion vocabulary, second-strategies for frustration, perspective-taking) especially as social demands rise in the Middle band.

## Effect sizes worth knowing

- **Hattie's hinge point is d ≈ 0.40** — the average effect of "a year of schooling." Use it to triage interventions: below ~0.40 is likely not worth reorganizing your week around; well above it is. *Visible Learning: The Sequel* (2023) synthesizes 2,100+ meta-analyses / 350+ influences and cautions that context and implementation fidelity swing these numbers a lot. https://visible-learning.org/2023/01/visible-learning-the-sequel-2023/
- **Retrieval practice (testing yourself) ≈ g 0.50–0.61 vs. re-reading; spacing ≈ g 0.28+; combined ("distributed practice testing") is among the most durable study strategies of all.** For long-term retention, low-stakes quizzing spread over time beats re-studying and cramming. Sources: retrieval-vs-restudy and spacing meta-analyses; large synthesis (242 studies, 169k participants) naming distributed practice + practice testing as top techniques. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10035-1 · https://evidencebased.education/resource/retrieval-and-spaced-practice-study-strategies-that-must-be-combined/
- **Structured literacy ≈ d 0.43–0.46; spatial-reasoning/math training and number-sense tutoring show meaningful gains; direct EF training is small (g ≈ 0.10–0.23); SEL academic spillover is small (g ≈ 0.08–0.10, GPA ≈ 0.33).** (See sections above for each.)
- **Practical implication:** Spend your finite hours on the high-yield, low-cost levers — explicit phonics early, wide content-knowledge reading, retrieval + spacing for anything meant to stick, guided spatial talk, and protected play/movement/sleep — before chasing expensive programs with sub-hinge-point effects. Treat any single effect size skeptically: it's an average across contexts, not a guarantee.

## Second-language acquisition (bonus)

- **Comprehensible-input syntheses (2026) reaffirm that vocabulary sticks better inside understood connected passages (i+1) than in isolated word lists, and flag "output-only / premature production before enough listening" as a classic pitfall.** Source: Krashen-tradition review summaries. https://melolingua.com/blog/comprehensible-input-language-learning
- **Practical implication:** For any age learning a second language, prioritize large amounts of *understood* input (TPR commands, narrated routines, story-based listening at the right level) over flashcard labeling or forced early output.

## Key takeaways

- **The homeschool advantage is a structure-and-engagement advantage, not magic** — assess against standards, and treat socialization as a deliberate weekly line item. (NHERI 2025; Cardus 2025.) https://nheri.org/research-facts-on-homeschooling/
- **Teach reading with explicit systematic phonics early, then pivot hard to content-knowledge + vocabulary for comprehension** (structured literacy d ≈ 0.43–0.46). https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44217-025-00922-8
- **Build number sense and guided spatial talk from PreK; guard against math anxiety and drill fact *fluency*, not just speed, before piling on procedures.** https://www.lboro.ac.uk/media-centre/press-releases/2025/june/lego-imrpove-math-and-spatial-ability/
- **Skip generic "brain-training"; grow EF through a plan-do-review loop, movement, play-based breathing, and protected nature/free play** (direct EF training g ≈ 0.10–0.23). https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/21582440241311060
- **Manage screens as displacement risk and prioritize co-use/interactivity over raw minutes; defend sleep, reading, movement, and play first.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12066102/
- **SEL supports (doesn't compete with) academics, with the biggest payoff by middle school** (g ≈ 0.08–0.10; GPA ≈ 0.33). https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543251367769
- **For anything that must stick, use retrieval practice + spacing** (g ≈ 0.50–0.61 / 0.28+) — the highest-leverage, lowest-cost study habit at every age. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10035-1
- **Use d ≈ 0.40 (Hattie's hinge) to triage where your hours go, and stay skeptical of any single averaged effect size.** https://visible-learning.org/2023/01/visible-learning-the-sequel-2023/
