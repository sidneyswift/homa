# math

> ✅ FRESH — researched 2026-07-02

Age bands used throughout:
- **PreK (ages 3–5)**
- **Lower Elementary (K–2, ages 5–8)**
- **Upper Elementary (grades 3–5, ages 8–11)**
- **Middle (grades 6–8, ages 11–14)**
- **High (grades 9–12, ages 14–18)**

## Frameworks used

1. **Common Core State Standards for Mathematics (CCSSM), K–12** — the backbone K–12 benchmark. Grade-by-grade domains: K–2 (Counting & Cardinality, Operations & Algebraic Thinking, Number & Operations in Base Ten, Measurement & Data, Geometry); 3–5 add Number & Operations—Fractions; 6–8 add Ratios & Proportional Relationships, The Number System, Expressions & Equations, Functions (gr 8), Statistics & Probability; High School organizes by six conceptual categories (Number & Quantity, Algebra, Functions, Modeling, Geometry, Statistics & Probability). Standards are cited as CC-K.CC.1, 3.OA.1, 6.RP.3, 8.F.1, HSF-IF.4, etc.
2. **NCTM Principles and Standards for School Mathematics (PSSM)** — national vision PreK–12. Five content standards (Number & Operations, Algebra, Geometry, Measurement, Data Analysis & Probability) and five process standards (Problem Solving, Reasoning & Proof, Communication, Connections, Representation), organized across four grade bands (PreK–2, 3–5, 6–8, 9–12). Used to frame coherence and the process/practice dimension across bands.
3. **Head Start ELOF (Early Learning Outcomes Framework)** — gold-standard US preschool math milestones, 36–60 months, across counting & cardinality, operations, patterns, measurement, geometry. Anchors the PreK band.
4. **CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early"** — developmental milestone checklists (75th percentile). Floor reference for what most children can do by a given age (used at PreK / early K).
5. **Clements & Sarama Learning Trajectories** — the definitive research-based developmental progressions for early math (birth–age 8). Granular levels within each strand (e.g., counting, subitizing, addition, spatial). Anchors PreK–Lower Elementary sequencing.
6. **NAEYC/NCTM Joint Position Statement — Early Childhood Mathematics** — affirms high-quality, play-based, hands-on math for ages 3–6; bridges PreK to K.

**Not used as benchmarks (and why):**
- Any specific published curriculum (e.g., Singapore Math, Eureka/EngageNY, Illustrative Math) — these are *curricula*, not *benchmarks*. Score the child against external standards, not the textbook being followed.
- Montessori scope — useful for pedagogy (how to teach), less useful for age-normed milestones. Referenced in `practice/` files instead.

## Age-band milestones

Early strands (Rote Counting through Comparison) are the PreK–Lower-Elementary foundation. Older strands (Multiplication & Division through Statistics & Probability) carry the domain forward through grade 12. Every band is grounded in a specific standard.

### Rote Counting

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3y0m–3y6m) | Says some number words in sequence to 10; may skip numbers | Counts to 10 reliably without skips | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 1 |
| PreK (3y6m–4y6m) | Counts to 10–20 in sequence | Counts to 20–30; begins counting backward from 10 | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 1; Clements & Sarama |
| PreK (4y6m–5y0m) | Counts to 20+ reliably | Counts to 50; begins skip-counting by 10s | Head Start ELOF; CC-K.CC.1 |
| Lower Elem (K) | Counts to 100 by ones and by tens; counts forward from any number | Counts to 100+ by 1s, 2s, 5s, 10s | CC-K.CC.1–2 |
| Lower Elem (gr 1–2) | Counts to 120 (gr 1); counts within 1000, skip-counts by 5s/10s/100s (gr 2) | Counts past 1000; fluent skip-counting patterns | 1.NBT.1; 2.NBT.2 |

**Clements & Sarama counting trajectory:** *Counter (small numbers)* → *Counter (10)* → *Counter and Producer (10+)* → *Counter from N (start from any number)* → *Skip Counter by 10s* → *Counter to 100* → *Counter forward and back*.

### Cardinality (counting objects / "how many?")

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3y0m–3y6m) | Begins 1-to-1 correspondence with small groups; may not yet grasp last-number = total | Reliably counts 5 objects with 1:1 correspondence | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 3 |
| PreK (3y6m–4y6m) | Points to objects while counting to 5–10; understands cardinality | Counts 10–15 objects; each successive number is one more | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 3 |
| PreK (4y6m–5y0m) | Counts ~10 objects incl. scattered arrangements | Counts 20 objects in any arrangement | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 3 |
| Lower Elem (K) | Counts to answer "how many?" up to 20; number → quantity to 20 | Counts 30+; conservation of number emerging | CC-K.CC.4–5 |
| Lower Elem (gr 1–2) | Understands two-digit numbers as tens + ones; counts collections into groups | Counts and structures collections into hundreds/tens/ones | 1.NBT.2; 2.NBT.1 |

**Number conservation (Piagetian — later milestone, gated by inhibitory control):** typically emerges ~5–6y; before then children are fooled by a spread-out row looking "more." Do not expect a pass before K, and don't drill it (Watanabe 2022; Simply Psychology 2025).

### Number Recognition (written numerals)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3y0m–3y6m) | Begins to grasp numerals represent quantity; recognizes 1–2 numerals | Recognizes numerals 1–5 | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 5 |
| PreK (3y6m–4y6m) | Recognizes/names some numerals (1–10) | Recognizes 0–10; begins writing them | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 5 |
| PreK (4y6m–5y0m) | Recognizes numerals to 10; writes some with support | Recognizes to 20; writes 0–10 independently | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 5; CDC 5y |
| Lower Elem (K) | Writes numbers 0–20; represents a quantity with a written numeral | Reads/writes numerals to 100+ | CC-K.CC.3 |
| Lower Elem (gr 1–2) | Reads/writes numerals to 120 (gr 1), to 1000 (gr 2); place-value notation | Fluent expanded form and comparison of multi-digit numbers | 1.NBT; 2.NBT.1,3,4 |

### Subitizing (instant quantity recognition without counting)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3y0m–3y6m) | Recognizes 1–2 objects instantly | Subitizes up to 3 reliably | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 2 |
| PreK (3y6m–4y6m) | Subitizes 1–4 objects | Subitizes to 5; begins conceptual subitizing (3+2) | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 2; Funexpected |
| PreK (4y6m–5y0m) | Instantly recognizes up to 5 (perceptual subitizing) | Sees 6 as 3+3 (conceptual subitizing) | Clements & Sarama |
| Lower Elem (K–1) | Conceptual subitizing on ten-frames/dice; sees 7 as 5+2 | Fluent subitizing structures underpinning "make a ten" | Clements & Sarama; CC-K.OA precursors |

**Key distinction (Clements & Sarama):** *perceptual subitizing* (instant recognition of 1–4) vs *conceptual subitizing* (seeing larger groups as composed of smaller ones, e.g., 7 as 4+3). Conceptual subitizing is a direct bridge to addition fluency, place value, and later multiplication.

### Patterns & Early Algebraic Thinking

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3y0m–4y0m) | Recognizes/duplicates simple AB repeating patterns | Extends AB; begins ABB/ABC | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 7 |
| PreK (4y0m–5y0m) | Creates/extends repeating patterns; identifies the core unit | Creates growing patterns; translates patterns across media | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 7 |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Recognizes number patterns (skip counts, odds/evens); intro to the equal sign as "same as" | Determines the unknown in an addition/subtraction equation | CC-K.OA; 1.OA.7–8; 2.OA |
| Upper Elem (gr 3–5) | Generates and analyzes numeric/shape patterns from a rule; identifies apparent features | Explains relationships between two related patterns (e.g., "each term is 2× the other") | 3.OA.9; 4.OA.5; 5.OA.3 |
| Middle (gr 6–7) | Writes and evaluates variable expressions; recognizes equivalent expressions | Generalizes patterns into symbolic rules | 6.EE; 7.EE |

Patterns are the on-ramp to algebra: NCTM places Algebra as a content standard from PreK onward, beginning with pattern and structure and maturing into symbolic reasoning.

### Shapes & Geometry (2D/3D, spatial reasoning)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3y0m–4y0m) | Names circle, square, triangle; recognizes shapes in the world | Names rectangle/oval; describes attributes (sides, corners) | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 9 |
| PreK (4y0m–5y0m) | Describes shapes by attributes; composes shapes (two triangles → square); names basic 3D shapes | Decomposes shapes; uses positional language; explores symmetry | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 9–10 |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Identifies/analyzes 2D & 3D shapes by attributes; composes composite shapes; partitions into equal shares (halves, fourths) | Recognizes shapes from different perspectives; reasons about defining vs non-defining attributes | CC-K.G; 1.G; 2.G |
| Upper Elem (gr 3–5) | Classifies 2D figures by properties (angles, parallel/perpendicular); area & perimeter; coordinate plane; volume of prisms (gr 5) | Classifies figures in a hierarchy (all squares are rectangles); reasons about attributes | 3.G; 4.G; 5.G; 5.MD.3–5 |
| Middle (gr 6–8) | Area/surface area/volume; scale drawings; angle relationships; congruence & similarity via transformations; Pythagorean theorem (gr 8) | Applies the Pythagorean theorem in 3D and on the coordinate plane; volume of cones/cylinders/spheres | 6.G; 7.G; 8.G |
| High (gr 9–12) | Congruence & similarity proofs via transformations; right-triangle trigonometry; circles; coordinate & analytic geometry; 3D measurement | Trig identities, laws of sines/cosines; modeling with geometry | HSG-CO, HSG-SRT, HSG-C, HSG-GPE, HSG-GMD, HSG-MG |

**Spatial reasoning (Clements & Sarama + Springer 2023 review):** *Shape Recognizer → Identifier → Describer → Composer → Decomposer.* Movement-based spatial learning (building, rotating, navigating real objects) transfers to abstract math more effectively than screen-based work, and spatial skill predicts later math achievement strongly — yet it is under-assessed in early grades.

### Measurement

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3y0m–4y0m) | Compares attributes (tall/short, big/small); lines things up to compare | Orders 3 objects by size; non-standard units (blocks, hand spans) | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 8 |
| PreK (4y0m–5y0m) | Measures with a repeated unit; seriates several objects | Understands the need for standard units; weight/capacity ideas | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 8 |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Measures/estimates length in standard units; tells time; works with money; relates addition/subtraction to length | Measures with two different units and interprets the difference; line plots | CC-K.MD; 1.MD; 2.MD |
| Upper Elem (gr 3–5) | Area & perimeter; mass/liquid volume; elapsed time; unit conversions within a system; volume as cubic units | Solves multistep measurement word problems; converts across a system fluently | 3.MD; 4.MD; 5.MD |
| Middle (gr 6–8) | Surface area & volume; unit rates as measurement; scale factor | Derives and applies volume formulas (cylinder, cone, sphere) | 6.G; 7.G; 8.G |

### Compose / Decompose (number bonds, part–whole)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3y–4y) | Combines/separates small groups with objects; no symbolic part–whole yet | Sees 4 split into 2-and-2 with objects | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 6 |
| PreK (4y–5y) | Composes/decomposes numbers to 5 with objects/fingers; intro to number bonds | Composes/decomposes to 10; records as a number bond | CC-K.OA.3 |
| Lower Elem (K–1) | Decomposes numbers ≤10 into pairs, records (5 = 4+1, 3+2); makes a ten | Fluent part–whole to 20; "make a ten" addition/subtraction strategies | CC-K.OA.3–4; 1.OA.6 |
| Lower Elem (gr 2) | Decomposes to add/subtract within 100–1000 using place value | Fluent flexible decomposition strategies for multi-digit work | 2.NBT.7,9 |

The **number bond** (whole on top, two parts below) is the canonical tool: objects → fingers → number-bond drawings → equations (K.OA.3). Decomposition is also the conceptual-subitizing bridge and the precursor to place value.

### Addition & Subtraction

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3y0m–4y0m) | Adds/subtracts very small collections (1+1, 2+1) with objects | Solves "1 more / 1 less" to 5 | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 6 |
| PreK (4y0m–5y0m) | Joins/separates objects to add/subtract within 5; begins "counting on" | Adds/subtracts within 10; uses counting-on from the larger number | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 6 |
| Lower Elem (K) | Adds/subtracts within 5 fluently; represents with equations; solves word problems within 10 | Fluent within 10; decomposes numbers | CC-K.OA.1–5 |
| Lower Elem (gr 1) | Adds/subtracts within 20 (fluent within 10); word problems with unknown in any position; understands the equal sign | Fluent within 20 using make-a-ten and known-fact strategies | 1.OA.1–8 |
| Lower Elem (gr 2) | Fluent within 20 (from memory); adds/subtracts within 100–1000 using place value | Fluent multi-digit strategies; two-step word problems | 2.OA.1–2; 2.NBT.5–9 |

**Clements & Sarama operations trajectory:** *Small Number +/−* → *Counting-All* → *Counting-On* → *Part-Whole* → *Deriver* (uses known facts, e.g., 6+7 = 6+6+1). The counting-all → counting-on shift is a major cognitive leap (understanding the first addend as a completed count).

### Comparison & Number Sense

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3y0m–4y0m) | Compares two small groups (more/same) with help | Compares groups of 3–5 and names which has more | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 4 |
| PreK (4y0m–5y0m) | Compares groups to 5–10 regardless of object size; uses ordinals (first–fifth) | Compares written numerals ("which is bigger, 7 or 4?") | Head Start ELOF P-MATH 4 |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Compares two numbers using greater/less/equal; uses >, =, < symbols | Compares three-digit numbers using place value and symbols | CC-K.CC.6–7; 1.NBT.3; 2.NBT.4 |
| Upper Elem (gr 3–5) | Compares fractions and decimals with reasoning and models | Orders fractions/decimals across representations; benchmarks | 3.NF.3; 4.NF.2; 4.NF.7; 5.NBT.3 |

### Multiplication & Division

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Works with equal groups, arrays, and repeated addition as a foundation | Writes an equation for equal-group/array situations (gr 2) | 2.OA.4 |
| Upper Elem (gr 3) | Understands multiplication/division as equal groups & arrays; fluent within 100; knows single-digit products from memory by end of year | Applies distributive/associative properties strategically; solves two-step problems | 3.OA.1–7 |
| Upper Elem (gr 4) | Multi-digit multiplication (4-digit × 1-digit, 2-digit × 2-digit); division with remainders; factors, multiples, prime/composite | Fluent standard-algorithm precursors via place value; multiplicative comparison word problems | 4.OA.1–4; 4.NBT.5–6 |
| Upper Elem (gr 5) | Fluent multi-digit multiplication (standard algorithm); divides with 2-digit divisors; multiplies/divides decimals to hundredths | Fluent decimal operations with reasoning about place value | 5.NBT.5–7 |
| Middle (gr 6) | Fluent multi-digit division (standard algorithm); operations with multi-digit decimals; GCF/LCM | Applies to ratio/rate contexts | 6.NS.2–4 |

### Fractions & Decimals

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lower Elem (gr 1–2) | Partitions shapes into halves/thirds/fourths; names equal shares | Recognizes equal shares of identical wholes can look different | 1.G.3; 2.G.3 |
| Upper Elem (gr 3) | Understands fractions as built from unit fractions; fractions on a number line; equivalence & comparison (denominators 2,3,4,6,8) | Reasons about equivalence with visual models beyond the required set | 3.NF.1–3 |
| Upper Elem (gr 4) | Fraction equivalence; add/subtract like-denominator fractions; multiply a fraction by a whole number; decimal notation for fractions (tenths/hundredths) | Adds tenths + hundredths; compares decimals with reasoning | 4.NF.1–7 |
| Upper Elem (gr 5) | Adds/subtracts fractions with unlike denominators; multiplies fractions; divides unit fractions by whole numbers (and vice versa); operations with decimals to hundredths | Interprets multiplication as scaling; fluent decimal operations | 5.NF.1–7; 5.NBT.7 |
| Middle (gr 6–7) | Divides fractions by fractions (gr 6); operations with all rational numbers incl. negatives (gr 7) | Fluent rational-number arithmetic in multi-step problems | 6.NS.1; 7.NS.1–3 |

### Ratios & Proportional Reasoning

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upper Elem (gr 4–5) | Multiplicative comparison ("3 times as many"); scaling with tables | Reasons about equivalent scaling relationships | 4.OA.1–2; 5.OA.3 |
| Middle (gr 6) | Understands ratio and unit rate; solves problems with tables, tape/double-number-line diagrams; percent as rate per 100 | Fluent conversion among ratio representations; measurement-unit conversion via ratio reasoning | 6.RP.1–3 |
| Middle (gr 7) | Recognizes/represents proportional relationships; constant of proportionality; graphs through origin; percent problems (tax, tip, markup, % change, simple interest) | Distinguishes proportional from non-proportional; solves multistep percent problems fluently | 7.RP.1–3 |
| Middle (gr 8) → High | Connects proportional relationships to slope and linear functions (y = mx) | Interprets unit rate as slope; foundation for linear functions | 8.EE.5–6; HSF-LE |

### Expressions, Equations & Algebra

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upper Elem (gr 5) | Writes and interprets numerical expressions; order of operations with grouping | Interprets expressions without evaluating ("3× as large as 18+2") | 5.OA.1–2 |
| Middle (gr 6) | Writes/evaluates expressions with variables & exponents; equivalent expressions; solves one-step equations & inequalities | Reasons about which values make an inequality true; dependent/independent variables | 6.EE.1–9 |
| Middle (gr 7) | Generates equivalent expressions with rational coefficients; solves two-step equations & inequalities in context | Fluent with px + q = r and p(x+q) = r; interprets solutions | 7.EE.1–4 |
| Middle (gr 8) | Radicals & integer exponents; solves linear equations (incl. infinitely many / no solution); systems of linear equations | Analyzes systems graphically and algebraically; scientific notation | 8.EE.1–8 |
| High (gr 9–12) | Structure of expressions; polynomial & rational arithmetic; creates & solves equations/inequalities; quadratics via factoring, completing the square, formula; systems | Reasons about equivalent forms to reveal properties; models with equations | HSA-SSE, HSA-APR, HSA-CED, HSA-REI |

### Functions

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Middle (gr 8) | Defines a function (one output per input); compares functions across representations; models linear relationships; recognizes non-linear examples | Interprets rate of change & initial value from a graph/table/equation; qualitative graph sketching | 8.F.1–5 |
| High (gr 9–12) | Function notation, domain/range; interprets key features (intercepts, max/min, end behavior); builds & transforms functions; families: linear, quadratic, exponential, polynomial, rational, radical, logarithmic, trigonometric | Compares growth (exponential eventually exceeds polynomial); models with functions; inverse functions | HSF-IF, HSF-BF, HSF-LE, HSF-TF |

### Statistics & Probability

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lower–Upper Elem (K–5) | Represents & interprets categorical/measurement data (picture graphs, bar graphs, line plots) | Solves multi-step "how many more/less" data problems | 1.MD.4; 2.MD.9–10; 3.MD.3–4; 5.MD.2 |

## Recent research

- **Common Core organizes math around a small number of coherent domains that build across grades**, with a deliberate progression: whole-number operations (K–2) → multiplication/division and fractions (3–5) → ratios, rational numbers, expressions/equations and functions (6–8) → six conceptual categories (9–12). Fractions (gr 3–5) are treated as the pivotal on-ramp to ratios, proportional reasoning, and algebra. ([CCSSM K–2 domain progressions](https://portal.ct.gov/-/media/sde/ct-core-standards/2014/08/ccss_math_k_2_domain_progressions.pdf); [3–5 domain progressions](https://portal.ct.gov/-/media/sde/ct-core-standards/2014/08/ccss_math_3_5_domain_progressions.pdf))
- **Grade-level "critical areas" concentrate focus**: gr 3 = multiplication/division within 100 + unit fractions + area; gr 4 = multi-digit multiplication, division, fraction equivalence & decimals; gr 5 = fraction operations, decimals to hundredths, and volume. Fluency expectations (e.g., single-digit products from memory by end of gr 3; standard multiplication algorithm by gr 5) are explicit benchmarks. ([Grade 4/5 critical areas](https://www.education.com/common-core/fourth-grade/math/))
- **Ratios & proportional reasoning (gr 6–7) is the hinge between arithmetic and algebra.** Grade 7's constant of proportionality and straight-line-through-the-origin graph connect directly to grade 8's slope and linear functions (y = mx). ([CC RP domain](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/RP/))
- **Grade 8 is the "function" turning point**: students define functions, compare them across representations, solve linear systems, and meet irrational numbers and the Pythagorean theorem — the bridge into high-school algebra and geometry. ([Grade 8 critical areas, WV DOE](https://wvde.us/sites/default/files/2021/09/Math-Grade8-Standards-WEB-v2.pdf))
- **High school math is organized by six conceptual categories** (Number & Quantity, Algebra, Functions, Modeling, Geometry, Statistics & Probability). Modeling has no standalone standards — it is a starred (★) lens applied across the others. States may sequence content as traditional (Algebra I / Geometry / Algebra II) or integrated pathways. ([CCSSM Appendix A pathways](http://static.pdesas.org/content/documents/ccssi_mathematics_appendix_a-1.pdf))
- **NCTM PSSM supplies the vertical spine and the process dimension.** Its five content standards (Number & Operations, Algebra, Geometry, Measurement, Data Analysis & Probability) run continuously PreK–12, and its five process standards (Problem Solving, Reasoning & Proof, Communication, Connections, Representation) are the "how" that should be assessed alongside content at every band. ([NCTM Principles & Standards](https://www.nctm.org/Standards-and-Positions/Principles-and-Standards/))
- **Spatial reasoning is an under-measured, high-leverage early strand.** A 2023 scoping review found movement-based spatial learning builds spatial reasoning — a strong predictor of later math success — more effectively than screen-based learning, reinforcing hands-on geometry/measurement work in the early bands. ([Springer/MERJ 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13394-023-00446-0))
