## Frameworks used

1. **Krashen & Terrell SLA Stages (1983)** — The foundational model for L2 acquisition: Pre-production (Silent Period) → Early Production → Speech Emergence → Intermediate Fluency → Advanced Fluency. Applied here to map your child's trajectory. Key insight: these stages are driven by *exposure hours*, not age. Krashen's Input Hypothesis (comprehensible input at i+1) is central to recommendations.
2. **NCSSFL-ACTFL Can-Do Statements (2026 rev.)** — Proficiency descriptors for Novice Low → Novice Mid → Novice High. Designed for learner self-assessment but excellent for mapping observable behaviors. Used as the primary benchmarking scale since it's behavior-based, not age-based.
3. **Immersion school timelines (Mi Casita Preschool, Tierra Encantada)** — Practitioner-reported timelines for when children hit milestones in Spanish immersion programs (20-30+ hrs/week). Useful as a ceiling/comparison, not a direct benchmark for your child's limited exposure.
4. **Bee Bilingual (practitioner synthesis)** — Parent-facing breakdown of SLA stages in children. Confirms the five-stage model and provides practical descriptors.
5. **Ensemble Learning SLA stages guide** — Educator resource mapping Krashen stages to classroom strategies. Confirms typical timelines: Pre-production 0-6 months, Early Production 6mo-1yr, Speech Emergence 1-3 years (at full immersion; much longer for limited exposure).

## Important framing note

**Spanish cannot be benchmarked by age bands.** Unlike English (L1), where developmental milestones are age-linked, L2 proficiency depends on *cumulative exposure hours*. A 3-year-old with 20hr/week immersion for 2 years will be vastly ahead of a 5-year-old with 1hr/week for 1 year.

your child's exposure: ~1-2 hours/week formal (Tuesday class) + casual at-home reinforcement (flash cards, songs, books, Dex machine). Estimated total lifetime exposure: **~75-100 hours** (class started roughly fall 2025, ~35 weeks × 1-2hr + home reinforcement).

Benchmarking approach: **SLA stage + ACTFL proficiency level** rather than age bands. The table below maps exposure-level expectations for a child with your child's exposure profile (limited, non-immersion).

## Proficiency milestones by SLA stage

### Vocabulary (Lexical Knowledge)

| SLA Stage / ACTFL Level | Typical for limited exposure (1-2hr/week) | Strong for this exposure level | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-production → Novice Low (0-50 cumulative hrs) | Recognizes 10-20 common words (greetings, colors, numbers). Cannot produce spontaneously but responds to familiar words. | Recognizes 30+ words; can produce a few on demand with visual support. | Krashen Stage 1; ACTFL Novice Low "identify memorized or familiar words" |
| Early Production → Novice Low/Mid (50-150 cumulative hrs) | Active vocabulary 30-60 words across categories (colors, animals, food, family, numbers, body parts). Can produce single words and very short phrases. | 60-100+ active words; can categorize (e.g., all farm animals, all weather words). Starts using words in short functional phrases. | Krashen Stage 2; ACTFL Novice Mid "practiced or memorized words, phrases" |
| Speech Emergence → Novice Mid/High (150-400 cumulative hrs) | 100-200+ words. Vocabulary spans multiple domains (classroom, home, community). Can combine known words into novel short sentences. | 200-400+ words including verbs and adjectives, not just nouns. Starting to express needs/wants in L2 without prompting. | Krashen Stage 3; ACTFL Novice High "simple sentences most of the time" |
| Intermediate Fluency → Intermediate Low (400+ cumulative hrs) | 400+ words. Vocabulary includes abstract concepts. Can describe events, give opinions, narrate simply. | Broad enough to sustain multi-turn conversation on familiar topics. | Krashen Stage 4; ACTFL Intermediate Low |

### Comprehension (Interpretive Listening)

| SLA Stage / ACTFL Level | Typical | Strong | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-production → Novice Low | Understands greetings and single high-frequency words with visual/gestural support. Cannot follow multi-word instructions in L2. | Follows simple 1-step classroom commands in L2 ("siéntate," "ven aquí") when paired with gesture. | ACTFL Novice Low: "can recognize familiar names" + "follow instructions for simple routines" |
| Early Production → Novice Low/Mid | Follows simple 1-2 step instructions with visual support. Recognizes topic of conversation when supported by context. Can't follow fast natural speech. | Follows 2-step instructions without gesture. Understands simple questions ("¿Qué color es?") and responds in L2 or L1. | ACTFL Novice Mid: "understand directions," "recognize weather expressions" |
| Speech Emergence → Novice Mid/High | Understands short statements on familiar topics. Follows classroom instruction with some L2-only segments. Can grasp main idea of a simple story read aloud. | Follows sustained L2 instruction for 5-10 minutes. Understands simple questions without visual support. Gets main idea of a children's song/video in L2. | ACTFL Novice High: "follow a video on how to play a simple game," "understand a radio ad" |
| Intermediate Fluency → Intermediate Low | Understands connected speech on familiar topics. Can follow a conversation between two native speakers on everyday subjects with some repetition. | Understands main idea of age-appropriate media without translation. | ACTFL Intermediate Low |

### Production (Presentational & Interpersonal Speaking)

| SLA Stage / ACTFL Level | Typical | Strong | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-production → Novice Low | Mostly silent in L2. May repeat single words when prompted. Uses gestures and L1 to communicate. | Produces a handful of high-frequency words spontaneously (hola, adiós, gracias, sí/no). | Krashen Stage 1; ACTFL Novice Low: "name very familiar people, places, objects" |
| Early Production → Novice Low/Mid | Single words and short memorized phrases ("quiero agua," "es rojo"). Mixes L1 and L2 (code-switching is normal and healthy). Answers simple questions with 1-2 word responses. | Produces 2-3 word phrases. Beginning to use verbs ("quiero," "tengo," "es"). Can label items in categories when prompted. | Krashen Stage 2; ACTFL Novice Mid: "share information using practiced words, phrases" |
| Speech Emergence → Novice Mid/High | Short sentences with grammatical errors (normal). Can describe pictures in L2 ("El perro es grande"). Asks simple questions in L2 ("¿Dónde está?"). Sustained code-switching. | Constructs novel sentences (not just memorized chunks). Initiates conversation in L2. Can narrate simple events ("Fui al parque"). | Krashen Stage 3; ACTFL Novice High: "simple sentences most of the time" |
| Intermediate Fluency → Intermediate Low | Full sentences with occasional errors. Can sustain a short conversation on familiar topics. Narrates past events in L2. | Communicates needs, preferences, and simple opinions in L2 without reverting to L1. | Krashen Stage 4 |

## Recent research

- [Tierra Encantada — From First Words to Fluency](https://www.tierraencantada.com/blog/from-first-words-to-fluency-a-parents-guide-to-language-development-from-infant-to-pre-k/) — Immersion preschool milestones by age. 3-4y immersion: "forming full sentences, requesting things, engaging in play in both languages." Useful ceiling comparison. your child is below this because he's not in immersion — expected.
- [Spanish Academy — Milestone Moments: Tracking Kids' Spanish Progress](https://www.spanish.academy/blog/spanish-learning-milestones-for-kids/) — Overview of SLA stages for kids with practical tracking advice. Emphasizes unique timelines for bilingual learners.
- [Mi Casita Preschool — When Will My Child Learn Spanish?](https://micasitapreschool.com/when-will-my-child-learn-spanish-at-a-spanish-immersion-preschool/) — Immersion school timeline: Month 1-3 silent period, 4-6 first words, 7-12 short sentences, Year 2+ intermediate fluency. At full immersion. your child's 1-2 hr/week means stretching these by ~15x.
- [Bee Bilingual — Stages of SLA in Children](https://beebilingual.org/stages-of-second-language-acquisition-in-children/) — Parent-facing 5-stage model matching Krashen. Good for the parent to read directly if she wants the overview.
- [Ensemble Learning — 6 SLA Stages](https://ensemblelearning.org/second-language-acquisition-stages/) — Educator resource. Key: "Going through all the SLA stages typically takes 5-7 years" (at school-level exposure). Pre-production can last 0-6 months; Early Production 6mo-1yr — but these assume daily classroom exposure, not 1hr/week.
- [NCSSFL-ACTFL Can-Do Statements (Novice level)](https://www.actfl.org/uploads/files/general/Resources-Publications/Can-Do-Novice.pdf) — Proficiency benchmarks for Novice Low/Mid/High. your child maps to Novice Low-Mid across listening, speaking, and interpersonal communication. The Can-Do descriptors give the parent specific observable behaviors to watch for as your child progresses.
- [Wikipedia — Second-language acquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-language_acquisition) — Background on SLA research, Krashen's Input Hypothesis, critical period hypothesis. Confirms that individual factors (motivation, personality, exposure) drive outcomes more than age at this stage.
