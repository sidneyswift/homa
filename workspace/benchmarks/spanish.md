# spanish

> ✅ FRESH — researched 2026-07-02

_World / second-language learning, PreK–12. Spanish is used throughout as the concrete example, but the frameworks and milestones apply to any modern world language._

## Framing note (read first)

**World languages cannot be benchmarked by age alone.** Unlike a first language (L1), where developmental milestones are age-linked, L2 proficiency is driven by *cumulative comprehensible-input hours* and instructional intensity — not chronological age. A 3-year-old with 20 hr/week immersion for two years will be far ahead of a 5-year-old with 1 hr/week for one year. So this file benchmarks on **two axes at once**:

- **ACTFL proficiency level** (Novice Low → Intermediate/Advanced) — the behavior-based "what can the learner actually do" scale. This is the primary scale.
- **Age band + typical program intensity** — a realistic *placement* of where most learners land at each stage, given common exposure (FLES ~1–2 hr/wk, immersion 20–30 hr/wk, or heritage/home use). Bands are **expected ranges, not ceilings**; a high-exposure PreK child can outrun a low-exposure high-schooler.

Age bands used consistently below:
- **PreK (ages 3–5)**
- **Lower Elementary (K–2, ages 5–8)**
- **Upper Elementary (grades 3–5, ages 8–11)**
- **Middle (grades 6–8, ages 11–14)**
- **High (grades 9–12, ages 14–18)**

## Frameworks used

1. **ACTFL World-Readiness Standards for Learning Languages (the "5 C's")** — the national roadmap for K–12 language learning: **Communication, Cultures, Connections, Comparisons, Communities**. Communication has three modes/standards: **Interpersonal** (two-way, negotiating meaning), **Interpretive** (understand/interpret what is heard, read, viewed), **Presentational** (present to inform, explain, persuade, narrate). 11 content standards total across the 5 goal areas. This is the organizing spine of the file.
2. **ACTFL Proficiency Guidelines & Performance Descriptors** — the Novice → Intermediate → Advanced → Superior → Distinguished scale (Novice/Intermediate/Advanced each split Low/Mid/High). Performance Descriptors describe classroom learners across domains (Functions, Contexts/Content, Text Type, Language Control, Vocabulary, Communication Strategies, Cultural Awareness) in the three modes.
3. **NCSSFL-ACTFL Can-Do Statements (incl. Intercultural)** — behavior-based, self-assessable "I can…" descriptors for each benchmark level and each mode, plus **Intercultural Communication** Can-Dos (relating cultural *products* and *practices* to *perspectives*). Primary benchmarking instrument since it is observable and age-agnostic.
4. **Krashen & Terrell SLA / Natural Approach stages** — Pre-production (silent period) → Early Production → Speech Emergence → Intermediate Fluency → Advanced Fluency. Central insight: acquisition is driven by **comprehensible input at i+1**, not explicit grammar. Stages are exposure-driven, not age-driven; useful for reading where a learner is regardless of grade.
5. **Seal of Biliteracy (ACTFL / state programs)** — the K–12 capstone credential. Most states require **Intermediate Mid–High** (some up to Advanced Low) across all four skills (listening, reading, speaking, writing) at graduation. Anchors the High band's target.
6. **NAEYC / Head Start guidance on Dual Language Learners (DLLs)** — evidence base for the PreK/early-elementary bands: bilingualism is an asset; support the home language (L1) while building L2; use culturally affirming, play-based, thematic input. Code-switching is normal and healthy.
7. **K–16 proficiency research (ACTFL research brief)** — realistic outcome data: FLES (subject-only) learners ≈ Novice Mid by grade 8; elementary **immersion** ≈ Intermediate by grade 5; secondary learners after 4 years ≈ Novice High–Intermediate Mid speaking. Grounds the "where learners actually land" placements.

## Age-band milestones

Each subdomain below is grounded in the 5 C's and the ACTFL scale. Bands show the *typical proficiency target* and observable behaviors. Where relevant, the SLA stage and Can-Do anchors are named. **Placement assumes ordinary exposure; adjust up for immersion/heritage learners, down for very light exposure.**

### Interpretive communication (listening / reading / viewing)

- **PreK (3–5):** Pre-production → Novice Low. Understands greetings and single high-frequency words with visual/gestural support. Follows a 1-step command paired with gesture ("siéntate" + sitting motion). Grasps the topic of a familiar song/story from pictures. _ACTFL Novice Low interpretive: recognizes memorized words with strong visual support._
- **Lower Elementary (K–2):** Novice Low → Novice Mid. Follows simple 1–2 step directions; recognizes familiar words in print (labels, environmental print); gets the gist of a picture book read aloud or a short video on a known topic. Recognizes weather/color/number expressions.
- **Upper Elementary (3–5):** Novice Mid → Novice High. Understands short, connected statements on familiar topics; follows sustained L2 classroom routines; gets the main idea of a children's video, simple song, or short leveled reader without translation. _(Immersion learners reach Intermediate here — main idea + some detail on everyday topics.)_
- **Middle (6–8):** Novice High → Intermediate Low. Understands the main idea and some detail of short authentic texts and audio on familiar topics (menus, ads, announcements, short narratives). Follows a conversation between speakers on everyday subjects with some repetition.
- **High (9–12):** Intermediate Low → Intermediate High/Advanced Low (Seal target). Understands main ideas and supporting details across familiar and some new topics from more complex authentic texts, video, and connected speech; infers meaning from context. AP/heritage learners approach Advanced (main idea + detail on unfamiliar topics).

### Interpersonal communication (two-way conversation)

- **PreK (3–5):** Pre-production/Early Production → Novice Low. Mostly responds nonverbally or with single words (sí/no, gracias); exchanges memorized greetings. Heavy, healthy code-switching. _NCSSFL-ACTFL: "I can greet and respond to greetings" with practiced words._
- **Lower Elementary (K–2):** Novice Low → Novice Mid. Answers simple questions with 1–2 words or memorized phrases ("es rojo," "quiero agua"); asks a few practiced questions (¿Cómo estás? ¿Qué es?). Begins using verbs (quiero, tengo, es).
- **Upper Elementary (3–5):** Novice Mid → Novice High. Handles short exchanges on familiar topics with practiced language; expresses basic needs, likes/dislikes; asks and answers simple questions; begins creating with language rather than only reciting chunks.
- **Middle (6–8):** Novice High → Intermediate Low. Sustains simple conversations on familiar topics, creating with language in the present time; asks follow-up questions; recombines learned material to say new things; recovers from breakdowns with rephrasing.
- **High (9–12):** Intermediate Low → Intermediate High. Converses on a range of familiar topics across present/past/future; handles uncomplicated situations (ordering, directions, appointments); expresses and supports opinions; negotiates meaning when misunderstood. Seal candidates demonstrate Intermediate Mid–High spontaneous exchange.

### Presentational communication (one-way: speaking / writing to an audience)

- **PreK (3–5):** Novice Low. Names very familiar people/objects; sings memorized songs; produces a handful of high-frequency words on demand with support.
- **Lower Elementary (K–2):** Novice Low → Novice Mid. Presents a few memorized phrases with visuals (labels a drawing, recites colors/numbers, gives name and age); copies/writes familiar words.
- **Upper Elementary (3–5):** Novice Mid → Novice High. Gives a short rehearsed presentation on a familiar topic (self, family, pets) using lists and simple sentences; writes short sets of sentences with strong scaffolding.
- **Middle (6–8):** Novice High → Intermediate Low. Produces short connected presentations/paragraphs on familiar topics; narrates and describes in present time, with some past; organizes ideas in simple sentences and cohesive strings.
- **High (9–12):** Intermediate Low → Intermediate High (Seal target for writing/speaking). Delivers organized presentations and multi-paragraph writing across time frames; narrates, describes, compares, and states/supports a viewpoint on familiar and some researched topics. AP learners approach Advanced (paragraph-length, well-structured discourse).

### Vocabulary & language control (cross-mode enabler)

- **PreK (3–5):** ~10–60 receptive/active words across concrete categories (colors, numbers, animals, family, food, body). Single words + memorized chunks. No explicit grammar. _(Krashen: input before output; expect a silent period.)_
- **Lower Elementary (K–2):** Hundreds of high-frequency words in thematic sets; formulaic phrases; emerging use of common verbs; heavy reliance on memorized language.
- **Upper Elementary (3–5):** Broad concrete vocabulary plus verbs/adjectives; begins recombining known words into novel short sentences; frequent, developmentally-normal errors.
- **Middle (6–8):** Vocabulary for everyday topics and personal interests; controls present tense reliably, past emerging; sentence-level accuracy on practiced structures; strategies for circumlocution.
- **High (9–12):** Vocabulary spanning concrete and some abstract topics; controls multiple time frames; paragraph-level cohesion; accuracy strong on familiar structures, variable on new ones. Explicit grammar now productive (contrasts with L1 — a Comparisons goal).

### Cultures (products, practices, perspectives)

- **PreK–Lower El:** Experiences culture through songs, foods, holidays, stories, and greetings; notices that people use different words/gestures. _5 C's Cultures at an exposure level._
- **Upper Elementary (3–5):** Identifies cultural products (foods, music, crafts) and practices (holidays, courtesy norms) of Spanish-speaking communities; begins connecting a practice to a perspective ("we take a siesta because…").
- **Middle (6–8):** Compares products/practices across cultures and to the learner's own; explains simple cultural perspectives; recognizes regional variety across the Spanish-speaking world. _Intercultural Can-Do: relate products & practices to perspectives._
- **High (9–12):** Investigates and explains connections between cultural products/practices and perspectives; interacts with cultural competence, adjusting register and behavior appropriately; engages authentic cultural texts.

### Connections & Comparisons (content-through-language; language/culture insight)

- **PreK–Lower El (Connections):** Learns content *through* the language — counting, colors, weather, plants/animals — reinforcing other subjects. Total Physical Response (TPR) ties language to action.
- **Upper Elementary–Middle (Connections):** Uses Spanish to access simple content in other subjects (a science or geography topic via authentic children's materials); acquires information unavailable in English.
- **Comparisons (K–2 →):** Notices differences in sound system and words vs. L1 (e.g., ñ, rolled r; cognates like *animal*, *color*). Grows into understanding grammar contrasts (gendered nouns, adjective placement) and that cultures differ — building metalinguistic and metacultural awareness across bands.

### Communities (language beyond the classroom; lifelong learning)

- **PreK–Elementary:** Uses Spanish at home, with family, or in the community for real purposes; enjoys L2 media, songs, and events. Home-language (L1) support strengthens L2 (NAEYC DLL guidance).
- **Middle:** Participates in community/cultural events, service, or virtual exchanges; sets personal goals for language use; consumes authentic media for interest.
- **High:** Uses the language for real communication (travel, work, service, online communities, heritage connections); pursues the **Seal of Biliteracy**; sets lifelong-learning goals and self-assesses with Can-Do statements.

## Recent research

- [ACTFL World-Readiness Standards for Learning Languages](https://www.actfl.org/educator-resources/world-readiness-standards-for-learning-languages) — The 5 C's (Communication, Cultures, Connections, Comparisons, Communities) and 11 standards are the national K–12 roadmap; Communication is split into Interpersonal, Interpretive, and Presentational modes. This structure now anchors the file's milestones.
- [ACTFL: What proficiency levels do K–16 world language learners reach? (research brief)](https://www.actfl.org/research/research-briefs/proficiency-levels-for-k16) — Realistic outcomes: FLES/subject-only learners ≈ **Novice Mid by grade 8**; elementary **immersion** learners reach the **Intermediate range by grade 5**; secondary learners after 4 years reach roughly Novice High–Intermediate Mid speaking, Intermediate Low writing. Confirms why the same age band spans very different proficiencies by program intensity.
- [ACTFL Performance Descriptors for Language Learners (2012)](https://www.actfl.org/uploads/files/general/Documents/AppendixN-ACTFLPerformanceDescriptorsLanguageLearners.pdf) — Describes classroom learners (not just proficiency snapshots) across Novice/Intermediate/Advanced in all three modes and seven domains; the basis for the observable behaviors in each band.
- [NCSSFL-ACTFL Can-Do Statements](https://www.actfl.org/educator-resources/ncssfl-actfl-can-do-statements) — Behavior-based "I can…" descriptors per level and mode, plus **Intercultural** Can-Dos (relating products/practices to perspectives). Best instrument for tracking a child's progress regardless of age.
- [ACTFL Seal of Biliteracy](https://www.actfl.org/assessments/k-12-assessments/seal-of-biliteracy) — The K–12 capstone: most states set **Intermediate Mid–High** across all four skills at graduation (some up to Advanced Low). Sets the concrete High-band target and gives families a why for sustained study.
- [NAEYC: Supporting the Development of Dual Language Learners in Early Childhood](https://www.naeyc.org/our-work/public-policy-advocacy/supporting-development-children-who-are-dual-language-learners-early-childhood) — Bilingualism is a cognitive/social/academic asset; support the home language while building the new one; use play-based, thematic, culturally affirming input. Grounds the PreK/early-elementary bands and validates code-switching as normal.
- [Krashen & Terrell, Natural Approach / SLA stages (overview)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Input_hypothesis) — Comprehensible input at i+1 drives acquisition; expect a silent period and error-filled output as normal; explicit grammar is low-yield before Middle. Explains the exposure-hours (not age) framing.
