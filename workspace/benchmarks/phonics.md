# phonics

> ✅ FRESH — Last researched: 2026-05-15

## Frameworks used

- **Head Start ELOF (Early Learning Outcomes Framework)** — Primary anchor for the preschool band (36–60m). Uses Literacy domain sub-domain P-LIT 1 (Phonological Awareness) for the oral manipulation skills and P-LIT 3 (Alphabet Knowledge and Early Writing) for letter-sound correspondence that feeds decoding. Chosen because it maps developmental progressions by age period.
- **CCSS Kindergarten RF (Reading: Foundational Skills)** — Defines end-of-K expectations: RF.K.2 (Phonological Awareness — rhyme, blending/segmenting onset-rime, blending/segmenting CVC phonemes, substitution) and RF.K.3 (Phonics and Word Recognition — letter-sound correspondence, CVC decoding, sight words). The 24mo-ahead target for a 3y5m child.
- **CCSS Grade 1 RF** — The stretch target (36mo ahead): RF.1.2 (phoneme distinctions, blends/digraphs, long vowels) and RF.1.3 (CVCe, common vowel teams, inflected endings). Useful for plotting your child's ceiling.
- **Heggerty Pre-K Phonemic Awareness Curriculum** — The actual curriculum the parent uses. Pre-K scope: rhyme recognition → rhyme production → syllable segmenting → onset-rime → phoneme isolation → blending → segmenting across 35 weeks. Provides the instructional sequence your child is following.
- **Orton-Gillingham / Science of Reading continuum** — Systematic phonics approach: single consonants → short vowels → CVC words → blends → digraphs → CVCe → vowel teams. Listed in your child's profile as a framework reference.
- **Anthony, Lonigan, Driscoll, Phillips & Burgess (2003)** — Key research on developmental sequence: detection before manipulation, blending before segmenting. Skills don't progress linearly; children refine earlier skills while acquiring new ones.
- **Wikipedia Phonological Awareness (well-cited review)** — PA emerges in 3rd year, accelerates through 4th and 5th years (Dodd & Gillon 2001; Lonigan et al. 1998). Progression: words → syllables → onset-rime → phonemes (Treiman & Zukowsky 1991).
- **Understood.org / Reading Rockets** — Practitioner-facing summaries of the research consensus on PA development and reading readiness.

## Distinction: Phonics vs. Literacy

The **phonics** domain focuses specifically on:
1. **Phonemic awareness** — conscious manipulation of individual sounds (phonemes): isolation, blending, segmenting, deletion, substitution
2. **Letter-sound correspondence** — mapping graphemes to phonemes for decoding
3. **Blending** — combining sounds into words (oral and with letters)
4. **Segmenting** — breaking words into constituent sounds

The **literacy** domain covers the broader reading/writing ecosystem (print concepts, story comprehension, vocabulary, writing). Overlap exists in rhyming and letter knowledge — those are scored in both domains because they serve different functions (rhyming is a phonological awareness milestone in phonics but a literary/language skill in literacy; letter knowledge is a print concept in literacy but a decoding prerequisite in phonics).

## Age-band milestones

### Phonemic Awareness

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Enjoys rhyming songs/books; rote imitation of rhyme; notices alliteration in playful contexts; syllable awareness emerging through clapping games | Distinguishes rhyming pairs; isolates first sound in own name ("S-tory starts with /s/"); beginning to blend onset-rime (/c/ + /at/ = cat) | Head Start P-LIT 1 (36–48m); Anthony et al. 2003 (detection before manipulation); Dodd & Gillon 2001 (PA emerges in 3rd year) |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Recognizes rhyming pairs; identifies first sound in familiar words; claps syllables in 2-syllable words; onset-rime blending with support | Produces rhymes; blends CVC phonemes orally (/c/-/a/-/t/ → "cat"); isolates beginning AND ending sounds; segments onset-rime independently | Head Start P-LIT 1; Lonigan et al. 1998 (accelerating growth 4th year); CCSS RF.K.2a-c (K-level skills) |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Produces rhymes; isolates beginning sounds; blends onset-rime independently; syllable segmenting for 2-3 syllable words | Blends 3-4 phoneme words; segments CVC words into individual phonemes; beginning phoneme deletion ("say 'cat' without /k/") | Head Start P-LIT 1 (48–60m); CCSS RF.K.2 (Phonological Awareness); Anthony et al. 2003 (blending before segmenting) |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Blends and segments onset-rime; beginning CVC phoneme blending; identifies all three positions (initial, medial, final) | Full CVC segmenting; phoneme deletion and substitution ("change /c/ in cat to /b/"); blends 4-5 phoneme words including blends | CCSS RF.K.2c-e; Head Start P-LIT 1 (by 60m indicators) |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Blends and segments CVC phonemes; beginning phoneme substitution; recognizes and produces rhymes fluently | Phoneme manipulation across all positions; blends words with consonant clusters (st, bl, tr); segments CCVC/CVCC words | CCSS RF.K.2e (add or substitute phonemes); RF.1.2 (distinguish long/short vowels, blend phonemes including clusters) |

### Letter-Sound Correspondence

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | May know 0–3 letter sounds (typically first letter of own name) | 5–10 letter sounds; connects some letters to sounds in environmental print | Head Start P-LIT 3 (36–48m); StayAtHomeEducator |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Knows 3–8 letter sounds; growing awareness that letters represent sounds | 10–15 letter sounds; can produce the sound for most consonants they recognize | Head Start P-LIT 3; Heggerty Pre-K scope (letter-sound intro in early weeks) |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Produces sounds for 8–12 recognized letters; beginning consonant sound isolation in words | 15–20 letter sounds including some vowels; uses letter sounds to attempt simple decoding | Head Start P-LIT 3 (48–60m); CCSS RF.K.3a |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Knows sounds for 12–18 letters; primary consonant sounds established | Most/all consonant sounds + short vowel sounds; beginning CVC decoding | Head Start P-LIT 3 (by 60m); CCSS RF.K.3a |
| 5y0m–5y6m | 1-to-1 letter-sound for all consonants; primary short vowel sounds | All consonant + vowel sounds (short and long); common digraphs (sh, ch, th); CVCe pattern awareness | CCSS RF.K.3 (grade-level phonics in decoding); RF.1.3 (1st grade: digraphs, CVCe, vowel teams) |

### Blending (Oral → Decoding)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Emerging — may blend compound words (cup + cake) or syllables (rab + bit) with support | Onset-rime blending independently (/c/ + /at/ = cat); may attempt full CVC blending with scaffolding | Anthony et al. 2003 (blending develops before segmenting); Heggerty Pre-K |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Onset-rime blending (/b/ + /ig/ = big); compound word blending; syllable blending for familiar words | CVC phoneme blending (/c/-/a/-/t/ → cat); beginning to blend while looking at letters (proto-decoding) | CCSS RF.K.2c (blend onset-rime); Anthony et al. 2003 |
| 4y0m–4y6m | CVC phoneme blending emerging; blends words modeled by teacher with 3 sounds | Fluent CVC blending; beginning CCVC/CVCC (e.g., stop, fast); blends with letter cards | CCSS RF.K.2c-d; Heggerty Pre-K (mid-year) |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Fluent CVC blending; attempts words with blends (2 consonants together) | Blends 4-5 phoneme words (e.g., /s/-/t/-/o/-/p/); reads simple CVC words from print | CCSS RF.K.3b (read common CVC words); Orton-Gillingham sequence |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Reads CVC words from print; blends with consonant blends and digraphs | Reads CVCe words; blends across vowel teams; reads simple sentences with CVC/CVCe words | CCSS RF.1.3 (1st grade phonics: CVCe, vowel teams); Orton-Gillingham |

### Segmenting

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Emerging — may clap to music/rhythm but doesn't segment words into sounds; compound word awareness (base-ball has two parts) | Segments syllables in 2-syllable words (clap: "mon-key"); may isolate first sound | Anthony et al. 2003 (segmenting lags blending); Head Start P-LIT 1 (36–48m) |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Claps syllables for familiar 2-3 syllable words; beginning to isolate first sound | Segments onset-rime (c-at); isolates initial AND final sounds; attempts CVC segmenting with scaffolding | Head Start P-LIT 1; CCSS RF.K.2b (count/pronounce/blend/segment syllables) |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Segments onset-rime; isolates first sound in words; syllable segmenting for 3+ syllable words | Full CVC phoneme segmenting (cat → /c/-/a/-/t/); counts phonemes in CVC words | CCSS RF.K.2d (isolate and pronounce initial, medial, final sounds in CVC words); Anthony et al. 2003 |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Beginning CVC phoneme segmenting; identifies initial, medial vowel, and final sounds | Segments CCVC/CVCC words; segments for spelling (invented spelling attempts) | CCSS RF.K.2d; Heggerty K-level |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Fluent CVC segmenting; beginning to segment words with blends | Segments multisyllabic words into phonemes; uses segmenting for encoding (spelling); phoneme deletion and substitution | CCSS RF.1.2 (distinguish long/short; blend phonemes; segment single-syllable words); RF.K.2e (substitution) |

## Recent research

- [Wikipedia: Phonological Awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonological_awareness) — Well-cited review article. PA emerges in 3rd year, accelerates through 4th and 5th. Progression: words → syllables → onset-rime → phonemes. Detection before manipulation; blending before segmenting (Anthony et al. 2003). Not linear — children refine earlier skills while acquiring new ones.
- [Understood.org: What is phonological awareness?](https://www.understood.org/en/articles/phonological-awareness-what-it-is-and-how-it-works) — Practitioner-facing overview. PA is the foundation for reading. Most kids pick it up naturally from rich language environments. Preschool warning signs: trouble learning nursery rhymes, counting syllables, noticing alliteration.
- [Head Start ELOF Interactive Framework](https://headstart.gov/interactive-head-start-early-learning-outcomes-framework-ages-birth-five) — Five central domains including Literacy (preschool split). Indicators at 36m and 60m. Used for P-LIT 1 (phonological awareness) and P-LIT 3 (alphabet knowledge) milestones.
- [CCSS ELA Reading: Foundational Skills K](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RF/K/) — RF.K.2: Phonological Awareness (rhyme, count/blend/segment syllables, blend onset-rime, CVC blending/segmenting, substitution). RF.K.3: Phonics (1-to-1 letter-sound, CVC decoding, sight words).
- [Wikipedia: Phonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonics) — Overview of systematic phonics approaches. National Reading Panel found systematic phonics more effective than unsystematic. Orton-Gillingham and synthetic phonics methods align with Science of Reading consensus.
- Lonigan, Burgess, Anthony & Barker (1998) — Vocabulary size and other language measures are concurrent and longitudinal predictors of PA. your child's massive book exposure (10–20 books/day) is a significant advantage.
- Dodd & Gillon (2001); Gillon & Schwarz (1999) — PA appears in 3rd year with accelerating growth through 4th and 5th. your child's trajectory aligns with the upper end of this acceleration.
