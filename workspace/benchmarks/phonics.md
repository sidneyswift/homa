# phonics

> ✅ FRESH — Last researched: 2026-07-02

Phonics/decoding is a **time-limited** domain: it opens in PreK with oral phonological
awareness, peaks in K–2 with systematic grapheme-phoneme instruction, completes the
basic-code + syllable-type + multisyllabic scope by **~grade 2–3**, and then **hands off**
to morphology, vocabulary, and fluency (the `literacy` / `vocabulary` domains) by
**grades 3–5**. Per CCSS, explicit phonics/word-analysis standards run K→grade 5, but by
grades 4–5 they are almost entirely *morphology and syllabication* applied to unfamiliar
multisyllabic words — decoding of the basic code is assumed mastered. There are **no
Foundational-Skills standards past grade 5.** This file tracks the full arc: oral PA →
basic code → syllable types → multisyllabic decoding → morphology → Greek/Latin roots &
etymology, and states explicitly where phonics ends and word study continues.

## Frameworks used

- **Science of Reading / Scarborough's Reading Rope (Scarborough 2001)** — Word-recognition
  lower strands: phonological awareness → decoding (alphabetic principle, spelling-sound
  correspondences) → sight recognition (via orthographic mapping). These grow increasingly
  automatic with practice and weave together with the language-comprehension strands into
  skilled reading. This domain owns the lower strands; comprehension lives in `literacy`.
- **Ehri's Phases of Word Reading (Ehri 1995, 2005)** — The developmental spine for this file:
  Pre-alphabetic (visual/logographic guessing) → Partial-alphabetic (some letters + context) →
  Full-alphabetic (complete grapheme-phoneme decoding; orthographic mapping begins) →
  Consolidated-alphabetic (chunks, syllable patterns, morphemes; fluent sight recognition).
- **Head Start ELOF (Early Learning Outcomes Framework)** — Primary anchor for PreK (36–60m).
  Literacy domain P-LIT 1 (Phonological Awareness) for oral sound manipulation and P-LIT 3
  (Alphabet Knowledge & Early Writing) for the letter-sound base that feeds decoding. Maps
  developmental progressions by age period.
- **CCSS ELA Reading: Foundational Skills (RF), K–5** — The K–5 spine. RF.K–1.2 (Phonological
  Awareness), RF.K–1.3 (Phonics & Word Recognition: CVC→CVCe→vowel teams→inflections),
  RF.2.3 (long vowels, common prefixes/suffixes, 2-syllable words), RF.3.3 (Latin suffixes,
  multisyllable words, prefixes/derivational suffixes), and **RF.4.3 / RF.5.3, which reduce to
  one standard: use letter-sound, syllabication, and morphology (roots + affixes) to read
  unfamiliar multisyllabic words.** RF.x.4 (fluency) runs K→5. No RF standards exist past gr 5.
- **UFLI Foundations (University of Florida Literacy Institute)** — A concrete, widely-used K–2
  explicit-systematic scope: 128 lessons, roughly ordered basic code → digraphs/blends → VCe →
  open/closed syllables (~Lesson 66) → compound & multisyllabic words → 24+ affixes (~Lesson 99).
  Used here as a real scope/sequence exemplar.
- **Orton-Gillingham / structured-literacy syllable-types framework** — The six syllable types
  (closed, open, VCe/silent-e, vowel team, r-controlled, consonant-le) as the tool for decoding
  multisyllabic words. Standard OG teaching order: closed → open → VCe → vowel team →
  r-controlled → consonant-le (consonant-le last; it only appears in multisyllabic words).
- **Heggerty Phonemic Awareness Curriculum (Pre-K / Primary)** — A common oral-PA daily routine:
  rhyme → syllable → onset-rime → phoneme isolation → blending → segmenting → manipulation.
- **Anthony, Lonigan et al. (2003)** — Developmental sequence evidence: detection before
  manipulation; blending before segmenting; larger units (words/syllables/onset-rime) before
  phonemes. Non-linear — children refine earlier skills while acquiring new ones.
- **Suggate, Reese & Lenhard (2022, ASHA meta-analysis)** — PA instruction produces significant
  blending/segmenting gains; **incorporating graphemes (letters) into PA instruction boosts
  outcomes** — the empirical basis for Elkonin boxes with letter tiles.

## Distinction: Phonics vs. Literacy vs. Vocabulary

The **phonics** domain covers the *word-recognition* lower strands only:
1. **Phonemic awareness** — oral manipulation of phonemes (isolation, blending, segmenting, deletion, substitution)
2. **Letter-sound correspondence** — mapping graphemes ↔ phonemes for decoding & encoding
3. **Blending** — combining sounds into words (oral → from print)
4. **Segmenting** — breaking words into sounds (oral → for spelling/encoding)
5. **Word study (older)** — syllable types, multisyllabic decoding, and **morphology** where it serves *decoding*

**Where phonics ends:** morphology/roots/affixes are scored here *as a decoding tool* (reading
unfamiliar long words), but their *meaning* work (vocabulary growth, etymology for comprehension)
belongs to the `vocabulary`/`literacy` domains. Fluency and comprehension strategies live in
`literacy`. This is by design: by ~grade 3–5 phonics instruction is largely complete and the same
morpheme content is repurposed from "how do I read it" to "what does it mean." Overlaps (rhyme,
letter knowledge, morpheme meaning) may be scored in more than one domain because they serve
different functions.

## Age-band milestones

Bands: **PreK (3–5)** · **Lower Elementary (K–2, 5–8)** · **Upper Elementary (grades 3–5, 8–11)** ·
**Middle (6–8, 11–14)** · **High (9–12, 14–18)**. Ehri phase noted per band.

### Phonemic Awareness

| Age band | Typical | Advanced | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PreK (3–5)** — Pre → Partial-alphabetic | Enjoys rhyme/alliteration; claps syllables; recognizes then produces rhyming pairs; isolates initial sounds; blends onset-rime; beginning oral CVC blending | Segments CVC into phonemes; beginning deletion/substitution ("cat" without /k/); blends 4-phoneme words | Head Start P-LIT 1; CCSS RF.K.2a-c; Anthony et al. 2003 |
| **Lower Elem K–2 (5–8)** — Full-alphabetic | Blends & segments CVC → CCVC/CVCC phonemes; deletion, substitution, and reversal across all positions; full phoneme manipulation by end of grade 1 | Manipulates phonemes in multisyllabic words; fluent and automatic — PA is no longer a bottleneck | CCSS RF.K.2, RF.1.2 |
| **Upper Elem 3–5 (8–11)** — Consolidated | Advanced PA assumed mastered; residual work only for struggling readers | Applies phoneme flexibility to decode/encode novel multisyllabic words | Scarborough; CCSS (no RF.2-type standard past gr 1) |
| **Middle (6–8) / High (9–12)** | Mastered; only remediated (intervention) if a decoding gap surfaces | — | Structured-literacy intervention (OG) |

### Letter-Sound Correspondence (the basic code)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PreK (3–5)** — Pre → Partial | 0→~15 letter sounds emerging (name letter first); grows to most consonants + some short vowels by 60m | Most consonants + short vowels; beginning CVC decoding attempts | Head Start P-LIT 3; CCSS RF.K.3a |
| **Lower Elem K–2 (5–8)** — Full | All consonants + short/long vowels; digraphs (sh/ch/th/wh/ck/ng); blends; VCe; common vowel teams & r-controlled; inflectional endings (-s, -ed, -ing) | Full basic code + less-common graphemes (-tch, -dge, soft c/g, oi/oy, aw/au) mastered early | CCSS RF.K.3, RF.1.3, RF.2.3; UFLI |
| **Upper Elem 3–5 (8–11)** — Consolidated | Complete grapheme inventory assumed; focus shifts to grapheme flexibility within syllables/morphemes; handles schwa | Handles rare/foreign spellings, schwa in unstressed syllables | CCSS RF.3.3; OG |
| **Middle (6–8) / High (9–12)** | Mastered; intervention only | — | — |

### Blending (oral → decoding from print)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PreK (3–5)** — Pre → Partial | Onset-rime & compound blending; oral CVC blending emerging; proto-decoding while looking at letters | Fluent oral CVC blending; reads some CVC words from print | CCSS RF.K.2c; Heggerty |
| **Lower Elem K–2 (5–8)** — Full | Reads CVC → CCVC/CVCC → digraphs → CVCe → vowel teams → r-controlled from print; blends left-to-right without tapping | Reads 2-syllable words by chunking; fluent connected-text decoding | CCSS RF.K.3b, RF.1.3, RF.2.3; UFLI; OG |
| **Upper Elem 3–5 (8–11)** — Consolidated | Decodes multisyllabic words by flexible chunking; blends syllable-by-syllable automatically | Decodes technical/content-area multisyllabic vocabulary on first encounter | CCSS RF.3.3, RF.4.3, RF.5.3 |
| **Middle (6–8) / High (9–12)** | Automatic; blending subsumed by fluent word recognition | — | Scarborough (sight recognition) |

### Segmenting (oral → encoding/spelling)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PreK (3–5)** — Pre → Partial | Segments syllables & onset-rime; isolates initial/final sounds; beginning CVC phoneme segmenting | Full CVC segmenting; segments for invented spelling | CCSS RF.K.2d; Head Start P-LIT 1 |
| **Lower Elem K–2 (5–8)** — Full | Segments CVC → CCVC/CVCC → multisyllabic for spelling; uses Elkonin/sound boxes with letters | Encodes with correct advanced graphemes; self-corrects spelling by sound analysis | CCSS RF.1.2; Suggate et al. 2022 |
| **Upper Elem 3–5 (8–11)** — Consolidated | Segments by syllable + morpheme for spelling (base + affix); applies spelling rules (doubling, drop-e, y→i) | Spells multisyllabic/derived words using morphological structure | CCSS RF.3.3; morphology |
| **Middle (6–8) / High (9–12)** | Mastered; spelling handled via morphology/etymology | — | — |

### Syllable Types & Multisyllabic Decoding *(new — Lower→Upper Elementary)*

The six syllable types are the primary tool for decoding long words. Standard OG/UFLI order:
**closed → open → VCe → vowel team → r-controlled → consonant-le** (consonant-le last because it
only appears in multisyllabic words). Division patterns: VC/CV (rab•bit), V/CV (o•pen),
VC/V (cab•in), and Cle (ta•ble).

| Age band | Typical | Advanced | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PreK (3–5)** | Not yet — syllable *counting/clapping* only (oral) | Claps 3+ syllable words | Head Start P-LIT 1 |
| **Lower Elem K–2 (5–8)** | Closed & open syllables (end of K/gr 1); VCe; begins dividing 2-syllable & compound words (UFLI ~L66) | All six syllable types; divides & decodes 2–3 syllable words reliably | CCSS RF.1.3f, RF.2.3c; UFLI; OG |
| **Upper Elem 3–5 (8–11)** | Applies syllable division (VC/CV, V/CV, VC/V, Cle) + all six types to decode multisyllabic words; handles schwa | Decodes 4+ syllable content vocabulary; flexes division when a first attempt fails | CCSS RF.3.3c, RF.4.3, RF.5.3 |
| **Middle (6–8) / High (9–12)** | Automatic; only in intervention for gaps | — | OG intervention |

### Morphology as Decoding: Prefixes, Suffixes, Roots *(new — bridge to word study)*

CCSS moves phonics *into* morphology across grades 2→5: RF.2.3 (common prefixes/suffixes) →
RF.3.3 (prefixes, derivational suffixes, Latin suffixes) → **RF.4.3 / RF.5.3 (roots + affixes +
syllabication to read unfamiliar multisyllabic words).** This is the explicit **handoff** point
where "phonics" becomes "word study."

| Age band | Typical | Advanced | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PreK (3–5)** | Not applicable (oral inflections only, e.g., plurals in speech) | — | — |
| **Lower Elem K–2 (5–8)** | Inflectional endings (-s, -ed, -ing, -es); common prefixes (un-, re-) & suffixes (-ful, -less); UFLI teaches 24+ affixes (~L99) | Reads/spells base + inflection with rule changes (drop-e, double, y→i) | CCSS RF.1.3f, RF.2.3d; UFLI |
| **Upper Elem 3–5 (8–11)** | Prefixes, derivational suffixes, Latin suffixes (gr 3); by gr 4–5 uses roots + affixes to decode unknown long words; begins Greek/Latin roots (bio, geo, tele, port, dict, spect) | Analyzes multi-morpheme words; uses roots to *decode AND infer meaning* across science/social-studies vocab | CCSS RF.3.3a, RF.4.3, RF.5.3; Keys to Literacy |
| **Middle (6–8)** | Systematic Greek & Latin roots and affixes for academic/domain vocabulary; morphological word families | Uses etymology and morpheme families to decode & analyze unfamiliar academic terms independently | CCSS L.6–8.4b (word-part analysis) |
| **High (9–12)** | Applies morphology + etymology to specialized/technical & discipline-specific vocabulary (medical, legal, scientific) | Recognizes assimilated prefixes, bound roots, and cross-linguistic cognates; uses etymology for spelling | CCSS L.9–12.4b |

### Etymology & Spelling *(new — Middle→High word study; phonics fully handed off)*

By this point phonics/decoding is complete; word study is about *why* words are spelled and
structured as they are — the Anglo-Saxon vs. Latinate vs. Greek layers of English, and how
morpheme + origin predict spelling (e.g., <ph>=/f/ signals Greek; -tion/-sion; silent letters as
etymological markers). Serves spelling and vocabulary, not decoding.

| Age band | Typical | Advanced | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Upper Elem 3–5 (8–11)** | Notices Greek/Latin roots explain spelling & meaning; content-area root connections | Uses word origin to reason about spelling of derived words | CCSS L.5.4b; Keys to Literacy |
| **Middle (6–8)** | Understands three layers of English (Anglo-Saxon/Latin/Greek); uses etymology from reference materials | Predicts spelling/meaning from etymology; recognizes cognates in world-language study | CCSS L.6–8.4c |
| **High (9–12)** | Applies etymological patterns to technical vocabulary and spelling of low-frequency words | Independent morphological/etymological analysis of any unfamiliar academic word | CCSS L.9–12.4 |

## Where phonics ends (the handoff, stated plainly)

- **Basic code (K–1):** all grapheme-phoneme correspondences, CVC→CVCe→vowel teams→r-controlled.
- **Advanced code + syllables (grade 2–3):** six syllable types, syllable division, 2–3 syllable
  decoding, inflectional + common derivational affixes. **This is where systematic phonics
  instruction typically completes for on-track readers.**
- **Grades 3–5:** explicit phonics standards (RF.4.3/RF.5.3) are *morphology + syllabication
  applied to unfamiliar multisyllabic words* — the basic code is assumed. Instruction time
  reallocates to fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension.
- **Grades 4+ / Middle / High:** **no CCSS Foundational-Skills standards exist past grade 5.**
  Continued phonics work is *intervention* for the ~5–10% of readers with unresolved decoding
  gaps (structured literacy / OG). For everyone else, "word work" = morphology, Greek/Latin roots,
  etymology, and spelling — which feed *vocabulary and comprehension*, not decoding.

## Recent research

- [Scarborough's Reading Rope (International Dyslexia Association)](https://dyslexiaida.org/scarboroughs-reading-rope/) — The word-recognition lower strands (phonological awareness, decoding, sight recognition) grow automatic with practice and interweave with language comprehension to produce skilled, fluent reading; orthographic mapping underlies sight recognition.
- [Ehri's Four Phases of Word Reading (FreeReading)](https://www.freereading.net/wiki/Ehri's_Four_Phases_of_Word_Reading.html) — Pre-alphabetic → partial → full → consolidated. Full-alphabetic readers decode completely and begin orthographic mapping; consolidated readers use syllable patterns and morphemes for automatic recognition — the developmental basis for moving from phonics to word study.
- [CCSS ELA Reading: Foundational Skills, Grade 5](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RF/5/) and [Grade 4](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RF/4/) — By grades 4–5 the single phonics/word-analysis standard is: use letter-sound correspondences, **syllabication patterns, and morphology (roots and affixes)** to read unfamiliar multisyllabic words. Confirms the phonics→morphology handoff; no RF standards exist beyond grade 5.
- [Six Syllable Types (Reading Rockets)](https://www.readingrockets.org/topics/spelling-and-word-study/articles/six-syllable-types) — Closed, open, VCe, vowel team, r-controlled, consonant-le predict vowel sounds and unlock multisyllabic decoding; taught in structured-literacy order with consonant-le last.
- [UFLI Foundations Scope & Sequence (UF Literacy Institute)](https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/foundations/) — 128-lesson K–2 systematic scope: basic code → digraphs/blends → VCe → open/closed syllables (~L66) → compound/multisyllabic words → 24+ affixes (~L99). A concrete exemplar of a complete, sequenced phonics scope.
- [Using Morphology to Teach Vocabulary (Keys to Literacy)](https://keystoliteracy.com/blog/using-morphology-to-teach-vocabulary/) — Explicit Greek/Latin root, prefix, and suffix instruction builds decoding, vocabulary, and comprehension together; roots (bio=life, geo=earth) tie naturally into science/social-studies content in grades 4–5+.
- [Suggate, Reese & Lenhard (2022), ASHA meta-analysis of PA instruction](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2022_LSHSS-21-00160) — PA instruction yields significant blending/segmenting gains; **incorporating graphemes into PA instruction boosts outcomes** — empirical support for Elkonin boxes with letter tiles.
- [Anthony et al. (2003) developmental sequence (via Wikipedia review)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonological_awareness) — Detection before manipulation; blending before segmenting; larger units before phonemes. Non-linear; children refine earlier skills while acquiring new ones.
