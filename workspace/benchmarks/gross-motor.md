## Frameworks used

1. **CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early" (2022 revision)** — Primary reference. Milestones represent what 75%+ of children can do by a given age. Used as the floor — if your child meets these, he's at least at age. Gross motor milestones at 3y are minimal (CDC focuses on fine motor at this age), but 4y includes "catches a large ball" and 5y includes "hops on one foot." Source: cdc.gov/act-early/milestones
2. **NSPT (North Shore Pediatric Therapy) Gross Motor Preschool Milestones** — Detailed PT-sourced breakdown for ages 3, 4, and 5. More granular than CDC — includes running, jumping distances, kicking distances, tricycle pedaling, galloping, skipping, climbing. Cited widely in pediatric OT/PT practice. Source: nspt4kids.com
3. **Head Start ELOF (Perceptual, Motor, and Physical Development)** — Federal framework. Goals P-PMP 1 (control, strength, coordination of large muscles) and P-PMP 2 (perceptual guidance of movement). Provides 36mo and 60mo indicators. the parent specific than NSPT for individual skills but useful for overall developmental trajectory. Source: headstart.gov
4. **ASQ-3 (Ages and Stages Questionnaire)** — Screening tool used by pediatricians. Referenced for the "should we be concerned?" threshold rather than the "is he advanced?" question. If a child misses ASQ-3 gross motor items, that's a flag for evaluation.

**Not used (and why):**
- Bayley Scales / PDMS-2: clinical assessment tools, not milestone checklists. Overkill for our purposes.
- WHO Motor Development Study: focused on 0–2y milestones (walking, standing). your child is past this range.

## General developmental notes

**Key principle from Head Start ELOF:** Gross motor development in preschool is about *control, strength, and coordination* (P-PMP 1) plus *perceptual guidance* (P-PMP 2 — using visual/spatial info to guide movement). These two systems develop in tandem. A child who can hop on one foot (excellent balance + coordination) may still struggle with overhand throwing (which requires perceptual targeting + sequential coordination).

**What the research says about your child's profile:**
- His balance and coordination are clearly ahead (hopping, single-leg balance at 12mo ahead)
- His catching is strong (perceptual + coordination together)
- His gaps (throwing accuracy, pedaling) are both about *sequential motor planning* — coordinated multi-step actions where timing and force modulation matter
- This is a coherent pattern, not scattered weaknesses. Pedaling requires leg-push timing; throwing requires arm-release timing. Both develop with practice, not maturation alone.

**Implication for target (12mo ahead):** your child is solidly at_age to 12mo_ahead in most gross motor subdomains. The two gaps (throwing, pedaling) are at_age, not below_age — they're normal but should be practiced to hit the 12mo target. Active interventions (throwing games, trike time) will close these naturally.
