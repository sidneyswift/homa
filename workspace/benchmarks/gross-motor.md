# gross-motor

> ✅ FRESH — researched 2026-07-02

Gross-motor development from PreK through grade 12: fundamental movement skills (locomotor, balance, object-control) maturing into sport-specific and lifetime physical activity, plus health-related physical fitness (aerobic, muscle, bone, flexibility) and the self-management skills that sustain lifelong activity. Milestones are grounded in early-childhood pediatric frameworks (PreK) that bridge into SHAPE America's K-12 National PE Standards and CDC youth physical-activity guidelines.

## Frameworks used

1. **SHAPE America National Physical Education Standards (2024)** — The authoritative K-12 framework. Four standards: **S1** develops a variety of motor skills; **S2** applies knowledge of movement and fitness concepts; **S3** develops social skills through movement; **S4** develops personal skills, identifies benefits of movement, and chooses to engage in physical activity. Uses grade-**span** learning indicators (K-2, 3-5, 6-8, 9-12) with learning progressions from fundamental movement skills → specialized/sport skills → lifetime activities. Source: shapeamerica.org/standards/pe.
2. **SHAPE America Grade-Level Outcomes for K-12 PE** — The prior (2013) outcomes still used by most state frameworks; gives concrete per-grade verifiable examples for locomotor, non-locomotor/balance, and manipulative (object-control) skills. Source: mirrored in state PE standards (Iowa, Oregon, South Dakota).
3. **CDC Physical Activity Guidelines for School-Aged Children & Adolescents (ages 6-17)** — 60 min/day moderate-to-vigorous activity; vigorous aerobic ≥3 days/wk; muscle-strengthening ≥3 days/wk; bone-strengthening ≥3 days/wk. Source: cdc.gov/physical-activity-education/guidelines.
4. **CDC / Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans (2nd ed.) — ages 3-5** — Preschoolers should be active throughout the day; caregivers aim for ~3 hrs/day of light-to-vigorous active play. Source: cdc.gov; odphp.health.gov.
5. **CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early" milestones (2022 revision)** — PreK floor. Milestones = what 75%+ of children do by a given age. 4y "catches a large ball"; 5y "hops on one foot." Source: cdc.gov/act-early/milestones.
6. **NSPT / NAPA Center / AAP HealthyChildren.org** — Pediatric PT/AAP sources for granular PreK gross-motor targets (jump distances, tricycle, galloping, skipping, single-leg balance) and 3-4y developmental red flags. Sources: nspt4kids.com; napacenter.org; healthychildren.org.
7. **Head Start ELOF (Perceptual, Motor & Physical Development)** — Federal early-childhood framework; goals P-PMP 1 (control/strength/coordination of large muscles) and P-PMP 2 (perceptual guidance of movement). Source: headstart.gov.

**Not used (and why):** Bayley Scales / PDMS-2 (clinical assessments, not milestone checklists); WHO Motor Study (0-2y walking/standing, below this range); ASQ-3 (a screening tool for concern, not an "advanced" benchmark).

## Age-band milestones

Band labels used throughout: **PreK (3-5)** · **Lower Elem (K-2, 5-8)** · **Upper Elem (3-5, 8-11)** · **Middle (6-8, 11-14)** · **High (9-12, 14-18)**. PreK bands ground in CDC/NSPT/AAP; K-12 bands ground in SHAPE America grade-span indicators.

### Running

| Band | Typical | Advanced | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK | Runs with coordinated arm swing; stops and changes direction; runs smoothly by 4-5y | Runs with adult-like form; sustains running for organized games; pacing emerging | NSPT 3-5y; Head Start P-PMP 1 |
| Lower Elem (K-2) | Runs and jogs using a mature pattern; runs with control in space, avoiding others | Sprints and dodges fluidly in tag/chase games | SHAPE S1 K-2 (locomotor) |
| Upper Elem (3-5) | Uses running in dynamic small-sided games; combines with dodging/fleeing | Applies pace/pursuit strategy while running in games | SHAPE S1 3-5 |
| Middle (6-8) | Applies running speed and agility within modified invasion/field games | Reads defenders and adjusts speed/angle to create space | SHAPE S1 6-8 |
| High (9-12) | Uses running as conditioning within a self-chosen lifetime/fitness activity | Trains running purposefully (intervals, distance) toward a personal fitness goal | SHAPE S1/S2 9-12; CDC aerobic |

### Galloping / Sliding / Skipping (locomotor variations)

| Band | Typical | Advanced | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK | Gallops (choppy→smooth 3-4y); slides sideways; skipping emerges ~5-6y | Skips with alternating feet and rhythm; skips rope (beginning) | NSPT 3-5y; NAPA |
| Lower Elem (K-2) | Hops, gallops, jogs, slides, and skips using a mature pattern | Chains locomotor skills smoothly into simple sequences/dances | SHAPE S1 K-2 |
| Upper Elem (3-5) | Combines locomotor skills into rhythmic/dance sequences with a partner | Choreographs and performs an original locomotor sequence to a beat | SHAPE S1 3-5 (dance/rhythm) |
| Middle (6-8) | Performs locomotor patterns within a dance or rhythmic-activity form | Creates and refines movement sequences in one or more dance forms | SHAPE S1 6-8 |
| High (9-12) | Demonstrates/creates movement sequences in one or more dance forms as a lifetime activity | Leads or composes a dance/rhythmic routine | SHAPE S1 9-12 |

### Jumping & Landing

| Band | Typical | Advanced | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK | Jumps forward 2-3 ft; jumps off a step, two-footed; jumps over small obstacles by 4-5y; jumping jacks emerging ~5y | Broad jump 3+ ft; jumps sideways reliably; beginning jump rope | NSPT 3-5y; CDC 3y |
| Lower Elem (K-2) | Jumps and lands (horizontal & vertical) demonstrating mature form; jumps a self-turned rope | Jumps rope continuously; landing absorbs force softly, balanced | SHAPE S1 K-2 |
| Upper Elem (3-5) | Combines jumping/landing with locomotor and manipulative skills in games | Jumps for height/distance with a run-up; long-rope and partner jumping | SHAPE S1 3-5 |
| Middle (6-8) | Uses jumping within sport contexts (rebounding, spiking, hurdling) | Times jumps to game situations; plyometric jumping in fitness | SHAPE S1 6-8; CDC bone-strengthening |
| High (9-12) | Employs jumping in chosen activities/fitness training | Programs plyometric/jump training within a personal fitness plan | SHAPE S2 9-12; CDC bone-strengthening |

### Balance & Weight Transfer

| Band | Typical | Advanced | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK | Stands on one foot 3-5s (3y) → ~10s (5y); tiptoes; walks a line; beam walking emerges 5y | One-foot 15+ s; walks backward on a line; confident beam work | NSPT 3-5y |
| Lower Elem (K-2) | Balances on different bases of support; transfers weight from feet to hands (momentarily) | Holds still and dynamic balances; smooth weight transfers (e.g. rocking, rolling) | SHAPE S1 K-2 (balance/weight transfer) |
| Upper Elem (3-5) | Combines balance and weight transfer into gymnastics/tumbling sequences | Performs and refines a balance sequence with changes in level/direction | SHAPE S1 3-5 |
| Middle (6-8) | Applies balance/stability in specialized skills (defensive stance, agility) | Uses dynamic balance to maintain control against a defender/opponent | SHAPE S1 6-8 |
| High (9-12) | Uses balance/core stability within lifetime activities and fitness training | Trains balance/stability deliberately (yoga, resistance, agility work) | SHAPE S1/S2 9-12 |

### Object-Control: Throwing

| Band | Typical | Advanced | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK | Throws underhand & overhand (form, not accuracy) at 3y; overhand with some aim, hits 5-ft target ~4-5y | Overhand with step and directional accuracy | NSPT 3-5y; CDC 4y; AAP |
| Lower Elem (K-2) | Throws underhand with opposite foot forward; overhand throw developing (side to target, step) | Overhand throw with opposition + step to a target with force | SHAPE S1 K-2 |
| Upper Elem (3-5) | Throws overhand with mature pattern (trunk rotation, follow-through) to a target/partner in non-dynamic then dynamic tasks | Throws accurately at moving targets/partners in small-sided games | SHAPE S1 3-5 |
| Middle (6-8) | Throws/passes with accuracy and appropriate force in modified invasion & field games | Selects throw type and leads a moving receiver under game pressure | SHAPE S1 6-8 |
| High (9-12) | Applies throwing within a chosen sport/lifetime activity competently | Executes sport-specific throws with tactical intent | SHAPE S1 9-12 |

**Throwing cue stack (PT/PhysEd):** model → hand-over-hand → verbal ("put it to your ear and throw"). Developmental sequence: (1) arm-only [~2-3y], (2) point non-throw shoulder at target, (3) **step with the OPPOSITE foot** [Young-5/1st-grade milestone], (4) follow through across body, (5) trunk rotation [4th-5th grade form]. Practice a big/far target first, then shrink it.

### Object-Control: Catching

| Band | Typical | Advanced | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK | Catches a large ball with arms extended (3y) → against chest → with hands ~5y | Catches smaller balls with hands, not trapping | NSPT 3-5y; CDC 4y |
| Lower Elem (K-2) | Drops a ball and catches it before it bounces twice; catches a tossed ball from a partner | Catches at different levels with hands only, moving to the ball | SHAPE S1 K-2 |
| Upper Elem (3-5) | Catches a variety of balls (self/partner) while moving, in non-dynamic → dynamic tasks | Catches under game pressure and transitions to next action | SHAPE S1 3-5 |
| Middle (6-8) | Receives/catches in modified net, field, and invasion games with control | Catches and immediately executes a tactical follow-up | SHAPE S1 6-8 |
| High (9-12) | Uses catching/receiving within a chosen sport/lifetime activity | Performs sport-specific receiving under competitive conditions | SHAPE S1 9-12 |

### Object-Control: Kicking, Foot-Dribbling, Punting

| Band | Typical | Advanced | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK | Kicks a stationary ball; by 5y kicks straight ~10 ft | Kicks with a run-up and some direction | NSPT 5y |
| Lower Elem (K-2) | Kicks a stationary ball using a mature pattern; taps/dribbles ball with feet in space | Dribbles with feet keeping the ball close while changing direction | SHAPE S1 K-2 |
| Upper Elem (3-5) | Dribbles with feet and passes/receives with a partner; punts | Dribbles and passes against light defense in small-sided games | SHAPE S1 3-5 |
| Middle (6-8) | Dribbles, passes, shoots with feet in modified invasion games | Combines dribble-pass-shoot to create/finish scoring chances | SHAPE S1 6-8 |
| High (9-12) | Applies foot-dribbling/kicking within a chosen invasion sport | Executes sport-specific footwork/finishing tactically | SHAPE S1 9-12 |

### Object-Control: Hand-Dribbling, Volleying & Striking (paddle/racket/bat)

| Band | Typical | Advanced | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK | Bounces and catches a large ball; strikes a stationary ball off a tee (emerging) | Dribbles a large ball with one hand a few times | NSPT; SHAPE readiness |
| Lower Elem (K-2) | Dribbles with one hand attempting a second contact; volleys a lightweight object upward; strikes a ball off a tee | Dribbles continuously; volleys to a partner; strikes with a short-handled implement | SHAPE S1 K-2 |
| Upper Elem (3-5) | Dribbles with hands while moving; volleys over a net; strikes with long-handled implement (bat, racket) to a target | Dribbles against light pressure; rallies over a net; strikes a pitched ball | SHAPE S1 3-5 |
| Middle (6-8) | Applies dribbling, volleying, striking in modified net/wall, target, and invasion games | Selects and executes strikes/volleys with tactical intent under pressure | SHAPE S1 6-8 |
| High (9-12) | Demonstrates competency in the striking/net-wall skills of a chosen lifetime activity | Performs sport-specific striking with consistency in competition | SHAPE S1 9-12 |

| Band | Typical | Advanced | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK | Stairs reciprocally without rail; climbs playground ladders; monkey bars emerge 4-5y (often with help) | Monkey bars with independence; low tree/wall climbing | NSPT 3-5y; Head Start P-PMP 1 |
| Lower Elem (K-2) | Climbs playground/wall structures with control; supports body weight briefly on hands | Traverses a low climbing wall; hangs and moves along overhead bars | SHAPE S1 K-2 (weight support); CDC muscle-strengthening |
| Upper Elem (3-5) | Climbs with strength/coordination on varied structures; rope/wall climbing attempts | Sustained climbing; controlled descent; bouldering traverses | CDC muscle-strengthening; SHAPE outdoor readiness |
| Middle (6-8) | Uses climbing within challenge-course/team-building and fitness contexts | Demonstrates technique on a climbing wall as a specialized activity | SHAPE S1 6-8 (outdoor pursuits) |
| High (9-12) | Demonstrates activity-specific skills in outdoor pursuits (e.g. rock/wall climbing) as a lifetime activity | Leads/plans an outdoor-pursuit outing; manages own safety/gear | SHAPE S1 9-12 (outdoor pursuits) |

### Pedaling / Cycling / Wheeled & Gliding Activities

| Band | Typical | Advanced | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK | Pedals a tricycle ~20 ft (3y); trike with steering; balance-bike/training-wheel bike 4-5y | Two-wheel bike (esp. after balance-bike training); confident steering/stopping | NSPT 3-5y; AAP |
| Lower Elem (K-2) | Rides a two-wheel bike; scooters/skates with control (aerobic active play) | Rides confidently on varied terrain; brakes and turns competently | CDC aerobic ("scooter riding," "flat-surface biking") |
| Upper Elem (3-5) | Uses cycling/skating for sustained moderate-vigorous activity | Rides longer/hill routes; skating tricks | CDC aerobic (hill biking = vigorous) |
| Middle (6-8) | Cycling/skating/skiing as recreation and conditioning | Trains cycling/skating for fitness or sport | CDC; SHAPE outdoor pursuits |
| High (9-12) | Cycling/skating/skiing/disc sports as chosen lifetime activities | Programs a cycling/outdoor pursuit into a personal fitness plan | SHAPE S1/S4 9-12 |

**Balance-bike note:** AAP promotes no-pedal balance bikes; balance (not pedaling) is the hard part, and training wheels can *delay* independent cycling by preventing lean/correction. Balance-bike kids reach two-wheel cycling ~3.5y vs ~4.5y with training wheels.

### Health-Related Physical Fitness (NEW — K-12): Aerobic, Muscle, Bone, Flexibility

| Band | Typical | Advanced | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK | Active throughout the day (~3 hrs light-to-vigorous play); gross-motor play builds strength/endurance implicitly | Sustains vigorous play in longer bouts; recovers and re-engages | CDC/PAG 3-5; Head Start |
| Lower Elem (K-2) | Identifies that activity makes the heart beat faster; engages in active play; recognizes warm-up/cool-down | Names moderate vs vigorous activity; sustains MVPA in games | SHAPE S2 K-2; CDC 60 min/day |
| Upper Elem (3-5) | Identifies the components of health-related fitness (aerobic, strength, endurance, flexibility) and matches activities to each | Sets a simple activity goal; monitors intensity (talk test / HR) | SHAPE S2 3-5; CDC |
| Middle (6-8) | Applies the **FITT** principle (Frequency, Intensity, Time, Type); designs part of a fitness routine; distinguishes health- vs skill-related fitness | Uses a fitness assessment to set targets and adjust FITT variables | SHAPE S2 6-8; PLT4M/FITT |
| High (9-12) | **Plans and implements a personal fitness program** addressing aerobic, muscular strength/endurance, and flexibility; demonstrates competency in **two or more lifetime activities** | Self-manages a periodized program; evaluates progress and revises | SHAPE S1/S2 9-12 |

**CDC activity-type anchors (ages 6-17): 60 min/day MVPA.** Aerobic (most of the 60 min; vigorous ≥3 d/wk) — brisk walking/biking/swimming (moderate), running/soccer/basketball/tennis/jump rope (vigorous). Muscle-strengthening (≥3 d/wk) — playground/rope climbing, push-ups, tug-of-war, resistance bands; adolescents may add weight machines/free weights. Bone-strengthening (≥3 d/wk) — hopping, skipping, jumping rope, running, sports with jumping/rapid direction changes. Younger children strengthen muscles informally (gymnastics, jungle gym, climbing); formal weightlifting is appropriate only in adolescence.

## General developmental notes

**PreK principle (Head Start ELOF):** gross-motor development is *control, strength, coordination* (P-PMP 1) + *perceptual guidance* (P-PMP 2). A child can be advanced in balance/coordination (hopping, single-leg balance) yet still developing in overhand throwing (perceptual targeting + sequential timing). Gaps in throwing accuracy and pedaling are typically *motor-planning/practice* gaps, not deficits — they respond to practice, not maturation alone.

**FMS → sport-specific → lifetime activity is the K-12 through-line (SHAPE 2024).** Fundamental movement skills mastered in K-5 become the building blocks for specialized/sport skills applied in modified games (6-8), which in turn become the competencies students carry into two-or-more chosen lifetime activities and a self-managed fitness program (9-12). The affective and cognitive domains (fitness concepts, FITT, personal/social skills) scaffold alongside the psychomotor.

**Research-backed intervention insights (still current):**
- FMS interventions boost executive function (SMD≈0.40) and social-emotional outcomes in preschoolers; higher dose (>2×/wk × 30 min) yields larger EF gains; 5-year-olds benefit more than 3-4-year-olds (Frontiers, 2025).
- Structured active play is statistically equivalent to formal PE for improving FMS in preschoolers — intentional setup, not specialized gear or a PE teacher, is the active ingredient (BMC Public Health, 2025); confirmed for 6-8yo with basic-equipment school interventions (Africa et al., 2026).
- Object-control skills (overhand throw, catch, kick, dribble, strike) are *trainable*, not merely maturational; hopping and underhand throw respond least, so target overhand throw for higher yield (Yang, 2025).
- Only about 1 in 5 youth meet the CDC 60-min guideline, and only ~24% of children ages 3-5 meet activity recommendations — a large practice gap most families can close with daily active play.

**Developmental red flags (AAP, by age 4):** cannot throw a ball overhand, jump in place, ride a tricycle, or grasp a crayon between thumb and fingers → flag for pediatric evaluation.

## Recent research

- [SHAPE America 2024 National PE Standards](https://shapeamerica.org/standards/pe/) — Four standards (motor skills; movement/fitness concepts; social skills; personal skills + lifelong choice), reorganized from grade-level outcomes to grade-**span** learning indicators (K-2, 3-5, 6-8, 9-12) with explicit progressions from fundamental → specialized → lifetime activities.
- [CDC Physical Activity Guidelines, ages 6-17](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-education/guidelines/index.html) — 60 min/day MVPA; vigorous ≥3 d/wk; muscle-strengthening ≥3 d/wk; bone-strengthening ≥3 d/wk. Only ~1 in 5 youth meet the guideline.
- [CDC "What Counts" for children & teens](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/adding-children-adolescents/what-counts.html) — Concrete age-appropriate examples: bone-strengthening = hopping/skipping/jumping rope/running; muscle-strengthening = climbing/push-ups/resistance bands (weights only in adolescence); aerobic moderate vs vigorous lists.
- [CDC / PAG 2nd ed., ages 3-5](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/children.html) — Preschoolers active throughout the day; caregivers aim for ~3 hrs/day of varied active play; only ~24% of young children meet activity recommendations.
- [SHAPE America high-school expectations](https://convention.shapeamerica.org/Convention/standards/pe/new-pe-standards.aspx) — By end of HS: plan/implement personal fitness programs, competency in two+ lifetime activities, water-safety/basic swimming where a pool exists, dance/rhythm sequences, outdoor pursuits, responsible personal/social behavior.
- [FITT principle in PE (PLT4M)](https://plt4m.com/blog/fitt-principle/) — Frequency/Intensity/Time/Type is the middle-school entry point to fitness planning that culminates in the HS personal fitness program.
- [SHAPE America GLO — manipulative/locomotor examples](https://blogs.4j.lane.edu/franz_a/files/2018/08/SHAPE-America-GLO-Reference-Guide-Elementary-Editi.pdf) — Concrete K-2/3-5 outcomes: underhand throw with opposite foot forward; drop-and-catch before two bounces; hand-dribble attempting a second contact; hop/gallop/jog/slide with mature pattern.
- [Yang 2025 — PA programs & FMS in 3-7yo](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11748295/) — Structured PA produces significant gains in overhand throwing, catching, kicking, dribbling, striking, running, jump, balance; hopping and underhand throw least responsive.
