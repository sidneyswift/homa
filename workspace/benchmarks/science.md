## Frameworks used

1. **NGSS K-2 Framework (Next Generation Science Standards)** — The national standard for what kindergartners and 1st graders should know. Four strands: Physical Science (forces, sound, light), Life Science (organisms, heredity), Earth & Space Science (weather, systems, human activity), and Engineering Design. Used as the "12mo ahead" and "24mo ahead" benchmark — if your child is doing K-1st NGSS content at age 3.5, he's well ahead. DC has adopted NGSS.
2. **Head Start Early Learning Outcomes Framework (ELOF)** — Federal framework for preschool-age science cognition (ages 3–5). Covers scientific inquiry, reasoning, and content in life/physical/earth science. Used as the "at age" and "typical" baseline.
3. **AAP/HealthyChildren.org Cognitive Milestones (3-4y)** — Developmental precursors to scientific thinking: categorization, cause-and-effect, imaginative exploration, following multi-step instructions.
4. **Scholastic Developmental Checklist (3-5y)** — End-of-preschool norms for cognitive skills including basic cause-and-effect understanding, sorting by multiple attributes, and symbolic representation.
5. **Science Fusion (Houghton Mifflin Harcourt)** — your child's actual curriculum. Provides the instructional sequence the parent is following. Used for your child-specific context, not as a normative benchmark.

## Age-band milestones

### Life Sciences

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Names common animals and plants; knows animals eat food and plants need water; beginning to sort living vs nonliving with prompting | Independently classifies living vs nonliving; names basic plant parts (roots, stem, leaves); sorts animals into simple groups (farm/zoo, land/water) | Head Start ELOF; AAP 3y cognitive |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Understands animals have babies; knows plants grow from seeds; can describe basic animal needs (food, water, shelter) | Names 3+ animal groups (mammals, birds, fish, insects); describes simple life cycles (seed→plant, caterpillar→butterfly); observes and describes animal behaviors | Head Start ELOF; NGSS K-LS1 precursors |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Describes how animals and plants are alike/different; understands parent-offspring similarity; recognizes patterns in nature (seasons → plant changes) | Classifies animals into 5+ groups with defining features; describes habitat-animal relationships; understands food chains at basic level (who eats whom) | NGSS K-LS1-1; K-ESS3-1 |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Uses observations to describe what plants and animals need to survive; understands that living things grow and change over time | Describes full life cycles for multiple organisms; explains adaptations ("thick fur keeps bears warm"); connects animal needs to habitats | NGSS K-LS1-1 full mastery; 1-LS1 precursors |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Explains that plants and animals have external parts that help them survive; understands parents and offspring are similar but not identical | Describes internal structures (bones, roots); explains behavioral adaptations; designs investigations about plant needs (light/water experiments) | NGSS 1-LS1-1; 1-LS1-2; 1-LS3-1 |

### Physical Sciences

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Explores cause-and-effect through play (push toy → it moves, drop ball → it falls); notices magnets stick to some things | Describes simple cause-and-effect relationships ("I pushed it hard so it went far"); systematically tests what magnets stick to (metal vs plastic vs wood) | AAP 3y cognitive; Head Start ELOF |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Understands pushes and pulls make things move; beginning to notice differences in materials (hard/soft, rough/smooth); explores water play (sinking/floating) | Predicts outcomes of pushes/pulls ("if I push harder, it goes faster"); sorts objects by material properties (wood, metal, plastic); conducts simple sink/float experiments | Head Start ELOF; NGSS K-PS2 precursors |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Compares effects of different-strength pushes and pulls on objects; sorts objects by observable properties (color, size, shape, texture, weight) | Plans simple investigations to answer "what happens if" questions; describes multiple properties of materials; understands reversible/irreversible changes (ice↔water, but not egg→cooked egg) | NGSS K-PS2-1; K-PS2-2; 2-PS1 precursors |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Understands that sound comes from vibrating objects; knows light is needed to see things; builds structures and tests which designs are strongest | Describes sound (loud/soft, high/low) and relates to vibration; understands shadows; uses evidence to compare designs ("this bridge held more weight because…") | NGSS 1-PS4 precursors; K-2-ETS1 |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Plans and conducts investigations about pushes/pulls; understands light travels in a straight line and can be blocked (shadows); describes sound properties | Explains that vibrating materials make sound and sound can make materials vibrate; describes how light helps us see objects; designs solutions to problems using evidence from tests | NGSS 1-PS4-1; 1-PS4-2; 1-PS4-3; 1-PS4-4 |

### Earth & Space Sciences

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Describes today's weather (sunny, rainy, cold, hot); knows day and night exist; notices seasonal changes (leaves fall, it gets cold) | Tracks weather over multiple days and notices patterns; names all four seasons and describes what happens in each; shows interest in sky objects (moon, sun, stars) | Head Start ELOF; AAP 3y cognitive |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Uses simple weather vocabulary (cloudy, windy, snowy); understands that weather affects what we wear/do; recognizes some weather patterns (rain → puddles) | Records weather observations (draws daily weather); describes water cycle basics (rain comes from clouds, puddles dry up in sun); identifies moon phases by shape (full, crescent) | Head Start ELOF; NGSS K-ESS2 precursors |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Uses and shares observations of local weather to describe patterns over time; understands that plants, animals, and humans change their environment | Constructs arguments about how organisms change environments; discusses how weather forecasting helps people prepare; identifies rocks, soil, and water as earth materials | NGSS K-ESS2-1; K-ESS2-2; K-ESS3-2 |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Understands that humans depend on the environment (food, water, air) and can change it; recognizes that severe weather requires preparation | Communicates solutions to reduce human impact on environment; explains day/night cycle; describes seasonal patterns using evidence from observations | NGSS K-ESS3-1; K-ESS3-3; 1-ESS1 precursors |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Uses observations of sun, moon, and stars to describe patterns (day/night, seasons); understands that daylight hours change across seasons | Predicts seasonal patterns from evidence; explains that some events in nature repeat; describes how the sun's position appears to change during the day | NGSS 1-ESS1-1; 1-ESS1-2 |

### Scientific Inquiry / Practices

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Asks "why" questions about the natural world; uses senses to explore objects and materials; engages in simple trial-and-error | Asks multiple "why" and "how" questions in sequence; uses tools (magnifying glass, measuring cups) to explore; describes observations using specific words | Head Start ELOF; AAP 3y cognitive ("approaches problems from a single point of view") |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Makes simple predictions ("I think it will sink"); compares two things side by side; begins to describe what happened vs what was expected | Designs simple tests ("let's try this one next"); records observations by drawing or dictating; begins to explain why something happened | Head Start ELOF; NGSS Science & Engineering Practices (K-2) |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Plans and conducts simple investigations with adult support; collects and records data (tally marks, drawings); asks testable questions | Plans investigations more independently; organizes data in simple charts; compares results across trials; uses evidence in explanations ("I know because I saw…") | NGSS K-2 SEPs (practices 1-3) |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Analyzes data from investigations to draw conclusions; constructs explanations based on evidence; shares findings with others | Designs fair tests (changes one thing, keeps others the same); uses simple measurement tools accurately; critiques others' explanations using evidence | NGSS K-2 SEPs (practices 4-6) |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Uses multiple sources of evidence to support claims; develops and uses simple models; engages in collaborative investigation | Designs multi-step investigations; creates models to explain phenomena; uses mathematics in science (measuring, counting, graphing) | NGSS 1st grade SEPs; K-2-ETS1-1 through ETS1-3 |

## Recent research

- [STEAMsational — NGSS Kindergarten Standards Breakdown](https://www.steamsational.com/ngss-lesson-plans-for-kindergarten/) — Complete list of all K performance expectations across Physical Science (pushes/pulls), Life Science (what organisms need), Earth & Space (weather patterns, environment, severe weather), and Engineering Design. your child is already meeting K-LS1 and approaching K-ESS expectations.
- [HealthyChildren.org — Developmental Milestones 3-4y (AAP)](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Developmental-Milestones-3-to-4-Year-Olds.aspx) — Cognitive milestones at 3-4y: names colors, understands counting concepts, approaches problems from single viewpoint, engages in imaginative play, follows 3-part commands, recalls story parts. These are the precursors to scientific reasoning.
- [Scholastic — Developmental Checklist 3-5y](https://www.scholastic.com/parents/family-life/developmental-checklist-3-5-year-olds.html) — By end of preschool (5y): sorts by multiple attributes, basic cause-and-effect understanding, symbolic representation, vocabulary of 5,000-8,000 words. your child is already sorting by multiple attributes (animal classification) well ahead of this.
- [Preschool Bright Start — Preschool Learning Milestones](https://preschoolbrightstart.com/preschool-learning-milestones/) — Age-by-age cognitive milestones. At age 3: engages in pretend play, sorts by shape/color, completes simple puzzles, understands same/different. At age 4: counts to 10+, recognizes patterns, asks "why" constantly, develops theory of mind. your child's science cognition is operating at 4-5y level.
- [Understood.org — Developmental Milestones 3y](https://www.understood.org/en/articles/developmental-milestones-for-typical-3-year-olds) — CDC 2022 revision milestones for age 3: copies circle, avoids hot objects when warned, uses fork, strings items. Cognitive milestones are minimal for science specifically — the CDC focuses more on safety awareness and basic cognitive skills at this age.
- [Wikipedia — Next Generation Science Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Generation_Science_Standards) — Background on NGSS framework: three dimensions (Disciplinary Core Ideas, Science & Engineering Practices, Crosscutting Concepts). DC has adopted NGSS. K-2 band covers life science, earth/space, physical science, and engineering design with equal emphasis on practices and content.
