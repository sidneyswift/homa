# science

> ✅ FRESH — researched 2026-07-02

## Frameworks used

1. **NGSS K-12 (Next Generation Science Standards)** — The primary national framework. Three-dimensional: **Disciplinary Core Ideas (DCIs)** in four domains (Physical Science, Life Science, Earth & Space Science, and Engineering/Technology), **Science & Engineering Practices (SEPs)**, and **Crosscutting Concepts (CCCs)**. DCIs are elaborated at the end of grades **2, 5, 8, and 12** — these grade-band endpoints anchor the age bands below. Adopted (in whole or adapted) by ~20 states plus D.C.; many other states use NGSS-derived standards. Source: [nextgenscience.org](https://www.nextgenscience.org/overview-topics).
2. **A Framework for K-12 Science Education (NRC 2012)** — The research base underlying NGSS. Defines the 8 SEPs, 7 CCCs, and the learning progressions for each core idea. Source: [nap.edu/read/13165](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/13165/chapter/7).
3. **Head Start Early Learning Outcomes Framework (ELOF)** — Federal framework for preschool-age science cognition (ages 3–5). Detailed developmental progressions for Scientific Inquiry (P-SCI 1–6): observing phenomena, scientific talk, comparing/categorizing, asking questions/predictions, planning investigations, and analyzing results. The "at age / typical" baseline for PreK.
4. **AAP / HealthyChildren.org Cognitive Milestones (3–4y)** — Developmental precursors to scientific thinking: categorization, cause-and-effect, imaginative exploration, following multi-step instructions.
5. **Smithsonian Science in Pre-K Inquiry Cycle** — Inquiry-based PreK framework: Getting Ready → Open Exploration → Focused Exploration → Documentation → Science Talks & Reflection. Emphasizes cyclical, iterative investigation; teacher validates children's theories (even incorrect ones) to sustain questioning.
6. **NSTA (National Science Teaching Association)** — Practitioner guidance on three-dimensional and phenomenon-based instruction across the bands.

## Age bands

- **PreK (ages 3–5)**
- **Lower Elementary (K–2, ages 5–8)** — NGSS DCI endpoint: end of grade 2
- **Upper Elementary (grades 3–5, ages 8–11)** — NGSS DCI endpoint: end of grade 5
- **Middle (grades 6–8, ages 11–14)** — NGSS DCI endpoint: end of grade 8
- **High (grades 9–12, ages 14–18)** — NGSS DCI endpoint: end of grade 12

The three NGSS dimensions run through every band. **Physical Science**, **Life Science**, and **Earth & Space Science** are the DCI domains; **Science & Engineering Practices** and **Crosscutting Concepts** are cross-domain and get their own subsections below. Engineering Design is woven through K-5 (K-2-ETS1, 3-5-ETS1) and stands alone at MS/HS (MS-ETS1, HS-ETS1).

## Age-band milestones

### Physical Science (matter, forces & motion, energy, waves)

| Band | Typical | Advanced (~1 band ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK | Explores cause-and-effect through play (push toy → it moves; drop → it falls); notices magnets stick to some things; sorts by hard/soft, rough/smooth; explores sink/float | Describes cause-effect ("I pushed hard so it went far"); systematically tests what magnets stick to (metal vs plastic vs wood); predicts sink/float before testing | Head Start ELOF P-SCI; NSTA magnetism; AAP 3y |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Compares effects of different-strength pushes/pulls (K-PS2); sound comes from vibrating material (1-PS4); light needed to see (1-PS4); matter has observable properties and can change with heating/cooling (2-PS1) | Plans investigations changing one variable; explains reversible vs irreversible changes (ice↔water vs cooking); relates pitch/volume to vibration | NGSS K-PS2, 1-PS4, 2-PS1 |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Balanced vs unbalanced forces change motion (3-PS2); energy in motion/sound/light/heat, and energy transfer (4-PS3); waves as patterns of amplitude/wavelength (4-PS4); matter made of particles too small to see, conserved in mixing (5-PS1) | Predicts motion from patterns; designs devices converting energy; determines whether mixing makes a *new* substance; measures/graphs to show mass conservation | NGSS 3-PS2, 4-PS3, 4-PS4, 5-PS1 |
| Middle (6–8) | Atomic/molecular structure of matter; chemical reactions rearrange atoms (mass conserved); Newton's forces & motion; kinetic/potential energy and thermal transfer; wave properties and electromagnetic radiation (MS-PS1–4) | Models synthetic materials from natural resources; predicts reaction products; uses graphs of kinetic energy vs mass/speed; explains digital signal transmission | NGSS MS-PS1, MS-PS2, MS-PS3, MS-PS4 |
| High (9–12) | Periodic trends & bonding; chemical reaction rates and equilibrium; Newton's laws & gravitation/electrostatics; conservation of energy & momentum; wave-particle behavior of light (HS-PS1–4) | Quantitative stoichiometry; Coulomb's/Newton's law computation; models nuclear fusion/fission energy; designs solutions using energy conservation | NGSS HS-PS1, HS-PS2, HS-PS3, HS-PS4 |

### Life Science (organisms, ecosystems, heredity, evolution)

| Band | Typical | Advanced (~1 band ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK | Names common animals/plants; knows animals eat and plants need water; sorts living vs nonliving with prompting; knows plants grow from seeds | Independently classifies living/nonliving; names plant parts (root, stem, leaf); names 3+ animal groups; describes simple life cycles (seed→plant, caterpillar→butterfly) | Head Start ELOF; NGSS K-LS1 precursors |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | What plants/animals need to survive (K-LS1); external parts help organisms survive (1-LS1); parents & offspring similar not identical (1-LS3); plants depend on animals/wind to disperse seeds & pollinate; habitat diversity (2-LS2, 2-LS4) | Explains structural & behavioral adaptations; designs a pollination/seed-dispersal mimic; compares biodiversity across habitats | NGSS K-LS1, 1-LS1, 1-LS3, 2-LS2, 2-LS4 |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Life cycles: birth-growth-reproduction-death (3-LS1); traits from inheritance + environment (3-LS3); groups help survival (3-LS2); structures for info processing/senses (4-LS1); plants get materials from air & water, matter cycles through food webs (5-LS1, 5-LS2) | Argues from fossil/trait evidence about environments (3-LS4); models energy flow producer→consumer→decomposer; explains photosynthesis as matter source | NGSS 3-LS1–4, 4-LS1, 5-LS1, 5-LS2 |
| Middle (6–8) | Cells as basic unit; body systems of interacting subsystems; photosynthesis/respiration as matter & energy flow; growth/reproduction and genetic vs environmental factors; natural selection & adaptation (MS-LS1–4) | Models mutation → protein → trait; predicts population change from resource availability; uses probability for inheritance; explains speciation from selection over time | NGSS MS-LS1, MS-LS2, MS-LS3, MS-LS4 |
| High (9–12) | Structure/function from molecules to organisms; homeostasis & feedback; matter/energy in ecosystems (photosynthesis, carbon cycling, carrying capacity); Mendelian & molecular genetics; evidence for common ancestry & evolution (HS-LS1–4) | Models feedback loops mathematically; quantifies ecosystem carrying capacity/resilience; predicts allele frequency change (Hardy-Weinberg reasoning); evaluates biodiversity/human-impact tradeoffs | NGSS HS-LS1, HS-LS2, HS-LS3, HS-LS4 |

### Earth & Space Science (weather/climate, Earth systems, space)

| Band | Typical | Advanced (~1 band ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK | Describes today's weather; knows day/night and seasons; notices seasonal change; interest in moon/sun/stars | Tracks weather patterns over days; names four seasons with details; identifies moon phases by shape; basic water cycle (rain from clouds, puddles dry) | Head Start ELOF; NGSS K-ESS precursors |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Weather patterns over time & severe-weather prep (K-ESS2/3); humans depend on & affect environment; sun/moon/star patterns; wind & water shape the land; maps represent places (2-ESS1/2) | Argues how organisms change their environment; explains why we track/forecast weather; models fast vs slow land change | NGSS K-ESS2, K-ESS3, 1-ESS1, 2-ESS1, 2-ESS2 |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Weather vs climate; typical weather across seasons/regions (3-ESS2); weathering, erosion & Earth's four spheres interacting (4-ESS2, 5-ESS2); Earth-sun patterns cause day/night, seasons, shadows, star visibility (5-ESS1); protecting Earth's resources (4-ESS3, 5-ESS3) | Reads climate-hazard maps to reduce impacts; quantifies sphere interactions; explains apparent star brightness by distance; graphs shadow/daylight over the year | NGSS 3-ESS2/3, 4-ESS2/3, 5-ESS1/2/3 |
| Middle (6–8) | Solar system & gravity-driven cyclic patterns (eclipses, tides, seasons); rock cycle & plate tectonics; Earth's history from rock strata; water cycle & climate drivers; human impacts, natural hazards & resources (MS-ESS1–3) | Models eclipse/tide geometry; uses geologic time & fossil sequence; forecasts regional climate from ocean/atmosphere circulation; evaluates resource/hazard mitigation designs | NGSS MS-ESS1, MS-ESS2, MS-ESS3 |
| High (9–12) | Star life cycles & Big Bang evidence; formation & age of the solar system/Earth; energy flow driving plate tectonics & surface processes; climate feedbacks & the carbon cycle; sustainability, biodiversity & human sustainability (HS-ESS1–3) — foundations of **environmental science** | Uses spectra/red-shift as evidence; models coupled Earth-system feedbacks; quantifies climate forcing; designs & evaluates sustainability/resource-management solutions | NGSS HS-ESS1, HS-ESS2, HS-ESS3 |

### Science & Engineering Practices (SEPs)

The 8 practices — the "doing" of science and engineering. All eight are accessible to young children and deepen across bands.

| Band | Typical | Advanced (~1 band ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK | Asks "why/how"; uses senses & simple tools (magnifier); makes simple predictions ("I think it'll sink"); compares two things; describes what happened vs expected (P-SCI 1–6) | Designs simple tests ("try this next"); records by drawing/dictating; begins evidence-based explanation ("I know because I saw…") | Head Start ELOF P-SCI; NGSS K-2 SEPs |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Asks testable questions; plans/carries out guided investigations; collects & records data (tallies, drawings); constructs claims from evidence; uses simple models/drawings | Runs fair tests (changes one variable); organizes data into simple charts; critiques a peer's explanation with evidence | NGSS SEPs, K-2 grade-band |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Fair tests with controlled variables; measures & graphs; interprets data patterns; constructs explanations & designs solutions; argues from evidence; obtains info from text/media | Designs multi-trial investigations; quantifies & averages results; builds and revises models to explain mechanism | NGSS SEPs, 3-5 grade-band |
| Middle (6–8) | Develops testable hypotheses; plans investigations identifying variables & controls; uses math/computational thinking; analyzes data for correlation; constructs evidence-based arguments; develops mechanistic models | Evaluates competing designs against criteria/constraints; uses ratios/statistics on data; identifies limitations of models & data | NGSS SEPs, 6-8 grade-band |
| High (9–12) | Refines questions/models; plans investigations with error analysis; uses algebra/statistics/simulation; evaluates claims for validity & reliability; constructs & defends evidence-based arguments; communicates via technical text | Designs to optimize under constraints; quantifies uncertainty; evaluates the credibility & assumptions of published claims | NGSS SEPs, 9-12 grade-band |

### Crosscutting Concepts (CCCs)

The 7 lenses that connect all domains: **patterns; cause & effect; scale, proportion & quantity; systems & system models; energy & matter; structure & function; stability & change.**

| Band | Typical | Advanced (~1 band ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK | Notices patterns (seasons, day/night); simple cause-effect; same/different; part-whole in objects/organisms | Predicts from a noticed pattern; describes a simple system ("the plant needs sun *and* water"); relates a part's shape to its job | Head Start ELOF; NRC Framework |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Patterns as evidence; cause & effect tested by investigation; relative size/amount; simple systems; observable structure↔function | Uses patterns to predict; distinguishes cause from correlation in simple tests; tracks how a system changes | NRC Framework; NGSS K-2 CCCs |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Standard units & measurement; systems have inputs/outputs; energy & matter flow into/out of systems; structure explains function; some things change, some stay stable | Uses scale/proportion (e.g., relative distances); models system interactions; traces matter/energy through a cycle | NRC Framework; NGSS 3-5 CCCs |
| Middle (6–8) | Proportional relationships & orders of magnitude; models to explain systems; conservation of matter/energy; feedback stabilizes or destabilizes systems | Quantifies rates & ratios; predicts system behavior from feedback; explains micro↔macro structure-function | NRC Framework; NGSS 6-8 CCCs |
| High (9–12) | Uses algebraic/statistical scale reasoning; models complex systems & their boundaries; tracks energy/matter through coupled systems; analyzes stability, feedback & rates of change | Reasons across many orders of magnitude; evaluates model limits; predicts tipping points & long-term stability/change | NRC Framework; NGSS 9-12 CCCs |

### Engineering Design

| Band | Typical | Advanced (~1 band ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK–K2 | Defines a simple problem; builds & tests a solution (ramps, towers, bridges); compares which design works better (K-2-ETS1) | States criteria for success; iterates a design after a failed test; explains *why* one design beat another | NGSS K-2-ETS1 |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Defines problem with criteria & constraints; generates/compares multiple solutions; runs fair tests & improves the design (3-5-ETS1) | Controls variables across trials; uses data to combine best features of several designs | NGSS 3-5-ETS1 |
| Middle (6–8) | Precisely defines criteria/constraints incl. societal & environmental impacts; systematically evaluates competing solutions; iterates from test data toward an optimal model (MS-ETS1) | Uses a decision matrix; quantifies trade-offs; models & iteratively optimizes | NGSS MS-ETS1 |
| High (9–12) | Frames major global problems with quantitative constraints; breaks problems into subproblems; evaluates solutions against prioritized criteria incl. cost/safety/ethics/environment (HS-ETS1) | Uses computer simulation/modeling to optimize; analyzes system-level trade-offs & life-cycle impacts | NGSS HS-ETS1 |

## Recent research

- **NGSS three-dimensional design** — Every performance expectation integrates one SEP, one DCI, and one CCC; DCIs are specified at the end of grades 2, 5, 8, and 12, which is why those grades anchor the age bands. All eight SEPs and seven CCCs are meant to recur in every grade band, growing in sophistication. Source: [NGSS Overview / Topic Arrangements](https://www.nextgenscience.org/overview-topics); [NRC Framework Ch.7](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/13165/chapter/7).
- **Phenomenon-based learning (PhBL), systematic review, Frontiers 2025** — Anchoring instruction in a real observable phenomenon and building explanations via SEPs/CCCs/DCIs improves engagement and transfer versus topic-by-topic teaching, but success depends heavily on teacher capacity and sustained support. Homeschool translation: pick a real phenomenon (why does the moon change shape? why did the container collapse?) and drive the three dimensions from it. Source: [Frontiers in Education 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1648234/full).
- **Three-dimensional PhBL in practice (Saberi & Nouri 2025)** — Worked example ("collapsed container" phenomenon) showing how a single everyday event can carry matter/energy (PS), systems (CCC), and investigation (SEP) simultaneously — a model for how one demo covers multiple standards. Source: [ScholarWorks UTRGV 2025](https://scholarworks.utrgv.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1231&context=tl_fac).
- **Inquiry teaching sequence with 3-year-olds (Springer IJSME 2025)** — Scientific practices (inquiry, argumentation, prediction, explanation) appeared across ALL stages of an inquiry sequence about rain; the practice children showed matched what the adult's questions invited. Conclusion: young children are capable of authentic science practices when scaffolded — adult questioning is the mechanism, not age readiness. Source: [Springer IJSME 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10763-025-10557-8).
- **Belief–practice alignment (Frontiers in Psychology 2025)** — In 22 teachers / 159 children, science-teaching beliefs only predicted children's inquiry gains AFTER professional development aligned beliefs with practice: quality of scaffolding beats quantity of activities. Source: [Frontiers fpsyg 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1580018/full).
- **Spatial reasoning as a STEM lever (FIU/Frost Museum 2025)** — Children as young as 3 use adult-like "holistic" mental-rotation strategies; spatial language ("under," "around," "bigger," "corner") and blocks/puzzles build the vocabulary for later STEM. High-leverage early intervention across the whole science trajectory. Source: [FIU News 2025](https://news.fiu.edu/2025/building-preschoolers-stem-skills-is-childs-play).
- **Outdoor STEAM environments (Springer ECEJ 2025)** — Systematic review: no validated outdoor-STEAM assessment tools yet at preschool, but outdoor physical environments critically shape early cognitive and STEAM concept development. Nature time IS science curriculum in the early bands. Source: [Springer ECEJ 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10643-025-01911-0).
- **Engineering-design coherence (NGSS MS-ETS1)** — By end of grade 8, students are expected to reach all four MS-ETS1 expectations on a *single* problem (define criteria/constraints → evaluate alternatives → combine best ideas → model & iterate), i.e., experience the whole design cycle rather than isolated steps. Source: [NGSS Middle School Engineering Design](https://www.nextgenscience.org/topic-arrangement/msengineering-design).
