# language-oral-english

> ✅ FRESH — researched 2026-07-02

Oral English / spoken-language benchmarks, PreK–12. Early bands (PreK–grade 2) are anchored in
speech-language developmental norms (CDC, ASHA, Brown's morphemes); upper bands (grades 3–12) shift
to the Common Core ELA **Speaking & Listening** strand (comprehension & collaboration, presentation of
knowledge & ideas) plus ASHA school-age communication milestones. Reading/writing live in separate
benchmark files; this file is spoken language only.

## Frameworks used

1. **CDC Developmental Milestones (2022 revision)** — primary anchor for PreK/early bands. The 2022
   update re-set milestones to what ~75% of children can do by a given age (screening cutoffs, not
   averages). Language items: back-and-forth exchanges, W-questions, intelligibility, event-telling.
2. **ASHA Developmental Norms for Speech & Language** — clinical norms for articulation, sentence
   length (MLU), question development, and Brown's morpheme order. The reference for early bands.
3. **ASHA "Your Child's Communication Development: Kindergarten Through Fifth Grade"** — grade-by-grade
   school-age milestones for listening, speaking, social communication, vocabulary, and discourse.
   Bridges the gap between preschool norms and the Common Core standards.
4. **Brown's 14 Grammatical Morphemes (1973)** — foundational, predictable acquisition sequence
   (present progressive → prepositions → plurals → irregular past → articles → regular past → …);
   all 14 typically mastered by ~4 years. Gold standard for tracking early grammar.
5. **Common Core ELA — Speaking & Listening (CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.SL), K–12** — the authoritative K–12
   standards for oral communication. Two anchor clusters: **Comprehension & Collaboration** (SL.1
   collaborative discussion, SL.2 evaluating information across media, SL.3 evaluating a speaker's
   argument/reasoning/evidence) and **Presentation of Knowledge & Ideas** (SL.4 presenting
   claims/findings, SL.5 digital media & visual displays, SL.6 adapting speech / command of formal
   English). Used to define the upper-band milestones for discussion, presentation, argumentation,
   and active listening.
6. **Vocabulary development research (Hoff 2006; Pinker 1994; Beck/McKeown Tier-2 framework)** — early
   vocab-size norms (~1,000 words at 3, ~2,500–5,000 at 5) and, for older ages, the shift to Tier-2
   academic vocabulary (cross-disciplinary words taught explicitly) and morphological word analysis
   (roots, prefixes, suffixes).
7. **Dialogic reading & shared-book-reading meta-analyses (Du et al., 2025; Frontiers in Language
   Sciences, 2025)** — interactive, parent-delivered book reading is the single strongest oral-language
   and vocabulary intervention in the early years.
8. **Classroom discourse research (Digital Promise, 2025; talk-moves literature)** — structured
   academic talk and "talk moves" (revoicing, elaborating, agree/disagree) measurably improve reading
   comprehension and reasoning across grades. Grounds the discussion/collaboration upper-band items.

**Not used (and why):** MacArthur-Bates CDI (normed only to 30 months); PPVT/EVT and standardized
oral-language batteries (valid but require a trained administrator — not practical for home use).

## Age-band milestones

Bands: **PreK (3–5)** · **Lower Elementary (K–2, 5–8)** · **Upper Elementary (3–5, 8–11)** ·
**Middle (6–8, 11–14)** · **High (9–12, 14–18)**. Early subdomains use finer age slices where the
research is that granular; the "Advanced" column marks roughly 12 months ahead of typical.

### Vocabulary breadth

| Band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | ~900–1,100 words productive at 3y (understands 2,000+); ~1,500–2,000 at 4y; ~2,500–5,000 at 5y. Category words, colors, body parts; learns words from context; uses time words by 5y. | 1,500+ at 3y; uses comparatives/prepositions, abstract concepts (emotions, time), synonyms, early figurative language. | Hoff 2006; Pinker 1994; CDC 4y/5y |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Speaks clearly enough for most people to understand; explains words and ideas; uses words to inform, persuade, and entertain (grade 2). Vocabulary grows via conversation and read-alouds. | Precise word choice; uses categories and antonyms/synonyms deliberately; asks about word meanings. | ASHA K & grade-2 milestones |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Word-analysis skills: roots, prefixes, suffixes (e.g. *bi-* + *cycle*); learns via word origins, synonyms, multiple meanings; understands figurative language ("this room is a zoo!") by grade 4. | Independently infers meaning from morphology and context; uses domain-specific terms; explains figurative language. | ASHA grades 3–5; CCSS SL.5 |
| Middle (6–8) | Command of Tier-2 academic vocabulary across disciplines; uses precise, topic-appropriate terms in discussion and presentation (SL.6). | Flexible register-shifting; uses connotation and nuance; defines terms for an audience. | Beck/McKeown Tier-2; CCSS SL.6 |
| High (9–12) | Wide academic and discipline-specific vocabulary; uses rhetoric and word choice deliberately for purpose and audience (SL.11-12.4/.6). | Sophisticated diction; controls tone and emphasis; adapts vocabulary to formal vs. informal tasks. | CCSS SL.9-12.4/.6 |

_Early-vocabulary probe: record ~10 min of free-play/conversation and count unique words. 200+ unique
words in a 10-min preschool sample maps to a productive vocabulary well above ~2,000 words._

### Grammar/morphology

| Band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | Brown's morphemes through Stage III–V by 3y6m (-ing, plurals, possessives, prepositions, articles, irregular then regular past); overgeneralizes ("goed," "runned"). **All 14 morphemes typically mastered by ~4y.** Future tense and correct question inversion by 5y. | All 14 morphemes early; subject-verb agreement and subordinate clauses ("because," "when") by 3y6m–4y; self-corrects by 5y. | Brown 1973; ASHA |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Correct basic grammar in speech; constructs "more complex sentences" (grade 2); residual overgeneralization fading. | Consistent complex/compound sentences; minimal errors; emerging metalinguistic awareness (notices "wrong" grammar). | ASHA grade-2 milestones |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Grammatically accurate connected speech; combines clauses to signal cause, sequence, contrast in reports and retellings. | Varied sentence structure for effect; uses conditionals and passive appropriately. | ASHA grades 3–5; CCSS SL |
| Middle (6–8) | Adapts grammar/register to context; command of standard English in formal speaking tasks (SL.6). | Deliberate syntactic variety for emphasis and clarity in presentations. | CCSS SL.6.6 |
| High (9–12) | Command of formal English when indicated; controls complex syntax across formal and informal tasks (SL.11-12.6). | Rhetorically purposeful syntax; matches structure precisely to purpose and audience. | CCSS SL.11-12.6 |

_Brown's U-shaped curve: children first say irregulars correctly by rote ("went"), then over-apply the
-ed rule ("goed"), then re-learn exceptions. Overgeneralization is a **sign of learning**, not
regression; most resolves by 4y._

### Narrative skills

| Band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | 3y: names actions in a picture, retells fragments (Stadler & Cuming "listing"/"connecting"). 4y: talks about ≥1 thing from the day, predicts "what next." 5y: tells a 2+ event story in sequence ("sequencing" stage). | Sequenced multi-event stories with temporal connectors; adds character feelings; full story grammar (setting/problem/resolution) → "narrating" stage. | CDC 3y/4y/5y; Stadler & Cuming 2005 |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Recounts events and retells narratives; keeps a story on topic across turns (K–grade 2). | Retells with clear beginning-middle-end plus descriptive detail; distinguishes real vs. imaginary. | ASHA K–grade 2 |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Reports a topic/text, tells a story, or recounts an experience with appropriate facts and relevant, descriptive details, at an understandable pace (SL.3.4); summarizes in own words (grade 4). | Well-organized recounts with logically sequenced events and a controlling idea; vivid, selected detail. | CCSS SL.3.4/.4.4; ASHA grade 4 |
| Middle (6–8) | Narrative folds into presentation: sequences ideas logically with pertinent detail to accentuate main ideas (SL.6.4). | Uses narrative technique deliberately (pacing, salient detail) within informative/persuasive talk. | CCSS SL.6.4 |
| High (9–12) | Conveys a clear, distinct perspective with coherent line of reasoning; anticipates opposing views (SL.11-12.4). | Integrates anecdote and evidence seamlessly; controls development, substance, and style for the task. | CCSS SL.11-12.4 |

### Articulation & intelligibility

| Band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | 3y: ≥50% intelligible to strangers (Hustad et al., 2021); /b n m p h w d/ correct; understood by familiar adults most of the time. 4y: understood by strangers most of the time; still acquiring s, z, sh, ch, j, th, r, l. 5y: fluent, few infantile substitutions. | Understood by strangers ≥90–95% early; only late-developing sounds (r, th) residual; self-corrects. | CDC 3y/5y; Crowe & McLeod 2020 |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Speaks clearly enough that most people understand; late sounds (r, l, s, z, th) resolving through grade 2. | Fully intelligible in all contexts; clear speech in noise. | ASHA K–grade 2 |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Adult-like articulation; speaks clearly and controls volume for context (soft vs. loud voice, grade 3). | Clear projection and pacing suited to audience; no persistent errors. | ASHA grades 3–5 |
| Middle (6–8) | Adequate volume and clear pronunciation in presentations; appropriate eye contact (SL.6.4). | Polished delivery — controls rate, volume, articulation for a room. | CCSS SL.6.4 |
| High (9–12) | Delivery appropriate to purpose and audience across formal/informal tasks (SL.11-12.4). | Command of vocal delivery as a rhetorical tool. | CCSS SL.11-12.4 |

### Question use

| Band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | 3y: constantly asks who/what/where/why. 4y: answers simple questions ("what is a coat for?"). 5y: answers story questions, asks for info to complete tasks. | Full W-set incl. when/how early; hypothetical & "what if" questions; questions showing theory of mind. | CDC 3y/4y/5y |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Answers yes/no and who/what/when/where/why questions; asks questions to keep a conversation going (grade 2). | Asks clarifying and follow-up questions; uses questions to compare and check understanding. | ASHA grade-2 milestones |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Asks and answers questions in conversations and group discussions; asks questions to check comprehension and stay on topic (grades 3–4). | Asks probing questions that extend a discussion; questions target reasoning, not just facts. | ASHA grades 3–4; CCSS SL.3.1c |
| Middle (6–8) | Poses and responds to questions that connect ideas and elicit elaboration; qualifies views in light of evidence (SL.6.1c–d). | Questions surface assumptions and test evidence sufficiency. | CCSS SL.6.1/.7.1 |
| High (9–12) | Poses and responds to questions that probe reasoning and evidence; promotes divergent and creative perspectives (SL.11-12.1c). | Uses questioning to move a group toward reasoned consensus or productive disagreement. | CCSS SL.11-12.1 |

### Sentence complexity (MLU → connected discourse)

| Band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | 3y: 3–4 word sentences, 2 back-and-forth exchanges. 4y: 4+ word sentences, tells about the day. 5y: multi-event sentences, 3+ exchanges. MLU rises ~3.5 (3y6m) → 5.0+ (5y). | Compound-complex sentences with subordinate clauses ("because/when/if") early; MLU ~1 year ahead. | CDC 3y/4y/5y; Brown's stages; ASHA |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Sustains multi-turn conversation on topic; builds "more complex sentences" by grade 2. | Extended, well-linked turns; combines several ideas per turn. | ASHA K–grade 2 |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Contributes multi-sentence, on-topic turns in discussion; sequences ideas logically in reports (SL.5.4). | Elaborated turns that connect claims with reasons and detail. | CCSS SL.3.1/.5.4 |
| Middle (6–8) | Presents claims/findings with logically sequenced ideas, evidence, and well-chosen details (SL.8.4). | Tightly organized extended discourse with coherent transitions. | CCSS SL.6.4/.8.4 |
| High (9–12) | Organizes extended remarks so development, substance, and style fit purpose and audience (SL.11-12.4). | Fully controlled long-form discourse; manages complexity for effect. | CCSS SL.11-12.4 |

### Discussion & collaborative conversation *(added for older ages)*

| Band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | Participates in collaborative conversations during play/routines; takes turns; observes group interaction norms. | Builds on a peer's idea; sustains a shared topic across several exchanges. | CCSS SL.PK/K.1; ASHA |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Follows agreed-upon rules for discussion — listening with care, gaining the floor respectfully, one speaker at a time (K→grade 2); links comments to others' remarks (grade 2). | Asks for clarification, links own idea explicitly to a peer's, recaps the group's point. | CCSS SL.K.1–SL.2.1; ASHA |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Comes to discussions prepared; follows rules and carries out assigned roles; poses/responds to questions and links comments to the discussion (grade 5 SL.5.1c). | Draws on preparation to advance the discussion; synthesizes others' ideas; reviews key points (SL.5.1d). | CCSS SL.3.1–SL.5.1 |
| Middle (6–8) | Comes prepared with researched material; follows collegial rules, sets goals/deadlines, tracks progress; acknowledges new info and qualifies own views (SL.8.1). | Facilitates turn-taking; integrates multiple viewpoints; adjusts stance with justification. | CCSS SL.6.1–SL.8.1 |
| High (9–12) | Initiates and participates effectively; promotes civil, democratic discussion, sets clear roles/deadlines, propels conversation by probing reasoning, resolves contradictions (SL.11-12.1). | Moderates a group toward reasoned consensus; deepens dialogue by drawing out under-represented views. | CCSS SL.9-10.1/.11-12.1 |

### Active listening *(added for older ages)*

| Band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | Follows 1–2 step directions; attends to stories and simple conversation (K). | Follows longer sequences; recalls story details when asked. | ASHA K milestones |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Follows 3–4 directions in a row; understands direction words (here/there/next to/before/later); answers questions about grade-level stories (grade 2). | Retains and acts on multi-step spoken instructions; summarizes what was heard. | ASHA grade-2 milestones |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Listens for a purpose (to learn, enjoy, or be convinced) and forms opinions from what is heard (grade 4); recounts/paraphrases info from a speaker or media and explains how it supports the topic (SL.4.2/SL.5.2). | Identifies main idea and supporting reasons from spoken/multimedia input; distinguishes fact from opinion. | ASHA grade 4; CCSS SL.4.2/.5.2 |
| Middle (6–8) | Analyzes purpose of information in diverse media; delineates a speaker's argument and evaluates soundness of reasoning and relevance/sufficiency of evidence, flagging irrelevant evidence (SL.8.2/.8.3). | Evaluates motives and evidence quality; distinguishes supported from unsupported claims independently. | CCSS SL.6.2–SL.8.3 |
| High (9–12) | Integrates multiple sources of info in diverse media, noting discrepancies; evaluates a speaker's point of view, reasoning, evidence and rhetoric, and identifies fallacies or distorted evidence (SL.9-10.3 / SL.11-12.3). | Assesses stance, premises, links among ideas, word choice, and tone; detects rhetorical manipulation. | CCSS SL.9-10.2/.3, SL.11-12.2/.3 |

### Presentation & public speaking *(added for older ages)*

| Band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | Recounts an event or retells a story to a listener; sings songs / says rhymes for an audience. | Holds a small audience with a short organized retelling. | ASHA/CDC 4y–5y |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Tells a story or describes something with relevant detail, in coherent sentences, expressing ideas clearly (SL.2.4); speaks audibly. | Adds descriptive detail and clear sequence; uses props/drawings to clarify (SL.2.5). | CCSS SL.K.4–SL.2.4/.5 |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Reports on a topic/present an opinion, sequencing ideas logically with facts and descriptive detail, speaking clearly at an understandable pace (SL.5.4); gives clear organized speeches (ASHA grade 4). Uses visual displays to emphasize facts (SL.5.5). | Delivers a prepared presentation with audience awareness, eye contact, gesture, and volume (ASHA grade 5). | CCSS SL.3.4–SL.5.4/.5; ASHA grades 4–5 |
| Middle (6–8) | Presents claims/findings, emphasizing salient points in a focused, coherent manner with relevant evidence, sound reasoning, well-chosen details; appropriate eye contact, adequate volume, clear pronunciation; multimedia to clarify (SL.8.4/.8.5). | Adapts a presentation to task/audience; integrates multimedia purposefully; fields questions. | CCSS SL.6.4/.5–SL.8.4/.5 |
| High (9–12) | Presents info/findings conveying a clear, distinct perspective and coherent reasoning, addressing opposing perspectives, with organization/style suited to purpose and audience; strategic use of digital media (SL.11-12.4/.5). | Polished, persuasive delivery; anticipates and rebuts objections live; command of formal register on demand. | CCSS SL.9-10.4/.5–SL.11-12.4/.5 |

### Argumentation & evidence *(added for older ages)*

| Band | Typical | Advanced (~12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | States a preference and gives a simple reason ("I want the red one because…"). | Gives two reasons; begins "if/then" justification. | Brown's stages; CDC 5y |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Uses language to persuade as well as inform and entertain (grade 2); gives a reason for an opinion. | Supports an opinion with a relevant reason and a linking word ("because," "so"). | ASHA grade-2 milestones |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Uses language for arguing (grade 4); presents an opinion with facts and details that support the main idea (SL.4.4/.5.4). | Distinguishes stronger vs. weaker reasons; orders evidence for impact. | ASHA grade 4; CCSS SL.4.4/.5.4 |
| Middle (6–8) | Presents a claim with relevant evidence and valid reasoning; in listening, delineates a speaker's argument and distinguishes claims supported by reasons/evidence from those that are not (SL.6.3), then evaluates soundness and sufficiency (SL.7.3/.8.3). | Rebuts a counter-claim; identifies when irrelevant evidence is introduced (SL.8.3). | CCSS SL.6.3–SL.8.3/.4 |
| High (9–12) | Builds and defends an evidence-based claim, addressing opposing perspectives (SL.11-12.4); evaluates rhetoric and detects fallacious reasoning or distorted evidence in others' arguments (SL.11-12.3). | Constructs a full argument with warrants and rebuttals; critiques premises and links among ideas. | CCSS SL.9-10.3/.4–SL.11-12.3/.4 |

## Recent research

- **Common Core Speaking & Listening (K–12)** codifies oral communication as two anchor clusters —
  Comprehension & Collaboration (SL.1–3) and Presentation of Knowledge & Ideas (SL.4–6) — with a clear
  progression: K–2 = follow discussion rules; 3–5 = come prepared and report with detail; 6–8 =
  delineate/evaluate arguments and present with evidence; 9–12 = evaluate rhetoric/fallacies and defend
  claims addressing counter-perspectives. [thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/SL](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/SL/)
- **The K→12 argumentation jump is explicit in the standards:** SL.6.3 (distinguish supported vs.
  unsupported claims) → SL.7.3/SL.8.3 (evaluate soundness and sufficiency of evidence; flag irrelevant
  evidence) → SL.11-12.3 (assess stance, premises, word choice, tone; detect fallacies). Argument
  *evaluation* precedes and scaffolds argument *production*. [MA DOE PreK-12 S&L standards](https://www.doe.mass.edu/frameworks/ela/standards/prek-12speakinglistening.pdf)
- **ASHA school-age milestones bridge preschool norms and the standards:** grade 2 = follow 3–4
  directions, use language to inform/persuade/entertain, stay on topic and take turns; grade 4 = listen
  for a purpose and form opinions, argue and summarize in own words, give clear organized speeches;
  grade 5 = deliver prepared presentations with audience awareness, eye contact, gesture, and volume.
  [asha.org — communication development K–5](https://www.asha.org/public/speech/development/communicationdevelopment/)
- **Vocabulary instruction shifts from breadth to Tier-2 academic depth** in the upper grades: word
  analysis via roots/prefixes/suffixes begins ~grade 3 (ASHA), and explicit cross-disciplinary
  (Tier-2) vocabulary instruction becomes the driver of comprehension and discussion for adolescents.
  [ASHA grade-3 milestones](https://www.asha.org/public/speech/development/thirdgrade/)
- **Structured classroom discourse measurably raises comprehension and reasoning.** "Talk moves" —
  revoicing, asking students to elaborate, asking students to agree/disagree — improve reading
  comprehension and mathematical reasoning versus lecture, and depend on psychological safety for
  students to share incomplete thinking. Directly supports the SL.1 collaborative-discussion strand.
  [Digital Promise Classroom Discourse Primer, 2025](https://digitalpromise.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/MC2-Classroom-Discourse-Research-Primer.pdf)
- **Early oral language is causally improvable at scale.** A 65-nursery cluster RCT of a 20-week
  vocabulary + narrative + active-listening program (20 min/day) produced educationally meaningful
  oral-language gains in 3–4 year olds — a universal, not just remedial, effect.
  [West et al., 2024, JCPP](https://acamh.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcpp.13947)
- **Dialogic / shared book reading remains the strongest early oral-language lever.** A three-level
  meta-analysis (64 studies, n=10,463, 2000–2025) found dialogic reading produces significant gains in
  vocabulary and oral language, strongest when parent-delivered; a 2025 systematic review confirms home
  reading frequency consistently correlates with language outcomes.
  [Du et al., 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0883035526000340) ·
  [Frontiers in Language Sciences, 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/language-sciences/articles/10.3389/flang.2025.1540562/full)
- **Brown's morphemes and the U-shaped overgeneralization curve** remain the anchor for early grammar:
  all 14 morphemes typically mastered by ~4 years, and errors like "goed" are evidence of rule-learning,
  not regression. [ASHA — Grammatical Morphemes in Order of Acquisition](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/late-language-emergence/)
