# language-oral-english

> Last researched: 2026-05-08

## Frameworks used

- **CDC Milestones (2022 revision)** — primary age-band anchor. The 2022 update shifted many milestones to represent what "most children" (75th percentile) achieve by each age, not 50th. Reliable for screening cutoffs.
- **ASHA (American Speech-Language-Hearing Association)** — clinical speech-language norms. Standard reference for articulation, sentence length, and question development.
- **Vocabulary development research (Hoff 2006, Pinker 1994)** — vocab size norms by age. Widely cited: ~1,000 words at age 3, ~1,500–2,000 at age 4, ~2,500–5,000 at age 5. Huge individual variation; productive vs. receptive gap is typically 2–3x.
- **Understood.org (CDC-aligned summaries)** — accessible milestone summaries reflecting the 2022 CDC revision for ages 3, 4, and 5.
- **Wikipedia: Language Development, Vocabulary Development, Child Development Stages** — synthesis of nativist/empiricist frameworks, age-stage tables, and phonological development timelines.

**Why these:** CDC + ASHA form the clinical baseline. Vocabulary research gives quantitative norms the CDC checklist doesn't provide. Understood.org provides practical parent-readable milestones that map directly to what the parent would observe. Wikipedia aggregates multiple peer-reviewed sources with citations.

## Subdomains

### Vocabulary breadth
| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | ~900–1,100 words productive; understands 2,000+. Uses category words (animals, food). Names colors, body parts, common objects. | 1,500+ productive; uses adjectives for comparison ("bigger"), prepositions correctly ("behind, between"), names abstract concepts (emotions, time). | Hoff 2006; Pinker 1994; Wikipedia Vocabulary Development |
| 3y6m–4y0m | ~1,200–1,500 words productive. Names most familiar objects. Beginning to learn words from context (not just direct teaching). | 2,000+ productive; uses descriptive language ("the really big red truck"), understands/uses spatial vocabulary, begins synonyms. | Hoff 2006; typical 10-20 new words/week rate |
| 4y0m–4y6m | ~1,500–2,000 words. Answers "what is a ___ for?" questions. Uses past/future tense markers. | 2,500+ words; uses complex descriptors, explains word meanings, uses temporal vocabulary ("yesterday, tomorrow, later"). | CDC 4y milestones; Understood.org |
| 4y6m–5y0m | ~2,000–2,500 words. Vocab spurt slowing but words become more abstract. Defines simple words by function. | 3,000+ words; uses figurative language beginnings, understands "same/different" for abstract concepts. | Pinker 1994; Hoff 2006 |
| 5y0m–5y6m | ~2,500–5,000+ words (huge range). Uses time words: "yesterday, tomorrow, morning, night." Recognizes rhymes (bat-cat, ball-tall). | 5,000+ words; uses words to explain reasoning, understands multiple meanings, emerging metalinguistic awareness. | CDC 5y milestones; Understood.org kindergarten |

### Sentence complexity
| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | 3-4 word sentences typical. "I want juice." Conversations with at least 2 back-and-forth exchanges. | Full paragraphs; complex sentences with conjunctions ("and," "because"); multi-sentence narratives. | CDC 3y: "Talks with you in conversation using at least two back-and-forth exchanges" |
| 3y6m–4y0m | 4-5 word sentences. Starting to use "and" to join clauses. Begins embedding ("the dog that barked"). | 6-8 word sentences; uses subordinate clauses ("because," "when," "if"); conversation sustains 4+ exchanges. | ASHA; Wikipedia Language Development |
| 4y0m–4y6m | 4+ word sentences standard. Tells about at least one thing that happened during the day. | Multi-sentence stories with causal language; uses "but," "so," "because" fluently; explains sequences. | CDC 4y milestones; Understood.org |
| 4y6m–5y0m | 5-6 word sentences; beginning compound-complex structures. Can retell a simple story in sequence. | Adult-like sentence structure; uses relative clauses, conditionals ("if…then"), and passive voice beginnings. | ASHA; Wikipedia |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Tells a story with at least two events ("It started to rain, and the dog ran home"). 3+ back-and-forth conversation exchanges. Fluent speech with few infantile substitutions. | Tells multi-event narratives with setting, problem, resolution; uses dialogue in storytelling; metalinguistic awareness emerging. | CDC 5y milestones; Understood.org kindergarten |

### Narrative skills
| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Remembers random details from experiences. Can say what action is happening in a picture ("running, eating"). Retells fragments, not sequential. | Tells simple 2-event sequences; describes what happened earlier today with beginning-middle structure. | CDC 3y: "Says what action is happening in a picture or book when asked" |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Beginning sequential narration (2 events); still often out of order. Describes pictures with action + agent. | 3+ event sequences; uses temporal connectors ("first, then, and then"); tells about past events with some detail. | ASHA; developmental research |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Talks about at least one thing from their day. Can predict "what comes next" in a well-known story. | Tells original stories with characters; retells a book story in correct sequence; adds descriptive details. | CDC 4y milestones; Understood.org |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Can retell familiar stories with major events. Beginning to add "feelings" of characters. | Creates stories with problem-solution structure; distinguishes real vs. imaginary events in narration. | ASHA |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Tells stories with at least 2 events in sequence. Answers simple questions about a story read aloud. | Full narrative structure (setting, characters, problem, attempts, resolution); retells unfamiliar stories after one hearing. | CDC 5y milestones; Understood.org kindergarten |

### Articulation
| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Understood by familiar adults most of the time (~75%). Talks well enough for others to understand "most of the time." Common substitutions (w/r, f/th). | Understood by strangers consistently (≥90%). Few substitution errors. Clear consonant clusters. | CDC 3y: "Talks well enough for others to understand, most of the time" |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Understood by strangers ~75-80% of time. May still have f/v/s/z/th errors. | Understood by strangers ≥95%. Most consonants correct including blends. | ASHA; Wikipedia |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Understood by strangers most of the time. Still working on s, z, sh, ch, j, th, r, l. Stuttering may appear (usually transient). | Virtually 100% intelligible. Only residual errors on late-developing sounds (r, th). | ASHA; Wikipedia: "Stuttering can develop, generally resulting in slowed-down speech" |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Most speech sounds correct. Residual errors on r, l, s, z, th common. | All consonants correct in conversation. No persistent patterns. | ASHA |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Fluent speech with few infantile substitutions. "Gives age." Can be understood by unfamiliar listeners easily. | Adult-like articulation; self-corrects errors; clear speech in noisy environments. | CDC 5y; Wikipedia Child Development Stages |

### Question use
| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | "Constantly asks questions" (who, what, where). "Asks who/what/where/why questions." Why questions emerging. | Full W-question set including when/how; asks "how" to understand process; asks hypothetical questions. | CDC 3y: "Asks 'who,' 'what,' 'where,' or 'why' questions"; Wikipedia: "Constantly asks questions" |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Why questions frequent ("questioning at its height" approaching age 4). Uses questions to sustain conversation. | Uses questions rhetorically; asks for explanations ("but why does it do that?"); asks about absent objects/people. | Wikipedia Child Development Stages: age 4 = "Questioning at its height" |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Answers simple questions ("What is a coat for?"). Many infantile substitutions in speech but question grammar improving. | Asks complex multi-part questions; uses questions to compare ("is X bigger than Y?"); asks about feelings/motivations of others. | CDC 4y: "Answers simple questions" |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Asks how/why with correct grammar most of the time. Answers "when" and "how many" questions. | Uses questions to clarify misunderstanding; asks "what if" questions; questions show theory of mind. | ASHA |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Answers questions about a story read aloud. Asks for information to complete tasks. | Uses questions to drive conversation; asks about word meanings; shows metalinguistic curiosity ("why is it called that?"). | CDC 5y milestones; Understood.org kindergarten |

### Grammar/morphology
| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Uses plurals, pronouns (I/me/you), possessives. Regular past tense (-ed) emerging but overgeneralizes ("goed, runned"). Missing function words in some sentences. | Irregular past tense mostly correct (went, ran, saw); uses articles consistently; beginning to use auxiliary verbs (is, was, can). | Wikipedia Language Development: 3-5y "missing function words and misunderstand verb tenses… over time start including functional words, pronouns, auxiliary verbs" |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Overgeneralization errors common and healthy ("goed" for "went"). Uses "is" and "are." Pronouns mostly correct but may confuse he/she. | Most irregular past tenses correct; uses "because" clauses; subject-verb agreement mostly consistent. | ASHA; developmental linguistics |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Most basic grammar correct. Uses past tense (sometimes correctly, sometimes not). Uses "and" to join sentences. "Many infantile substitutions in speech." | Subordinate clauses ("because," "when," "after"); correct use of "doesn't/can't/won't"; beginning passive voice understanding. | CDC 4y; Wikipedia Child Development Stages: age 4 |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Overgeneralization errors decreasing. Uses future tense ("will," "going to"). Questions formed with correct inversion most of the time. | Near-adult grammar; self-corrects errors; uses complex verb phrases ("has been," "might have"). | ASHA |
| 5y0m–5y6m | "Fluent speech with few infantile substitutions." Uses correct grammar most of the time. Begins metalinguistic awareness (understands jokes, riddles). | Understands and uses passive voice; recognizes grammatical errors in others' speech; humor based on language (puns). | CDC 5y; Wikipedia: 5-10y "develop metalinguistic awareness which allows them to reflect and more clearly understand the language they use" |

## Recent research

- [Wikipedia: Language Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_development) — comprehensive overview of nativist/empiricist frameworks, age-stage breakdowns for 0-10y, phonological and syntactic development timelines.
- [Wikipedia: Vocabulary Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocabulary_development) — vocabulary spurt research, 10-20 new words/week acquisition rate, mapping problem theories, prelinguistic-to-linguistic transition.
- [Wikipedia: Child Development Stages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_development_stages) — milestone table from birth to 8y, speech column with specific age markers (3y: "speaks in sentences, constantly asks questions"; 4y: "questioning at its height"; 5y: "fluent speech with few infantile substitutions").
- [Understood.org: 3-year-old milestones](https://www.understood.org/en/articles/developmental-milestones-for-typical-3-year-olds) — CDC 2022 revision milestones, language/communication section: 2+ back-and-forth exchanges, W-questions, understood by others most of the time.
- [Understood.org: 4-year-old milestones](https://www.understood.org/en/articles/developmental-milestones-for-typical-4-year-olds) — 4+ word sentences, talks about daily events, answers "what is it for" questions, songs/stories/nursery rhymes.
- [Understood.org: Kindergarten milestones (5y)](https://www.understood.org/en/articles/developmental-milestones-for-typical-kindergartners) — tells 2+ event stories, 3+ back-and-forth exchanges, uses time words, recognizes rhymes.
