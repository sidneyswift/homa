# social-emotional

> ✅ FRESH — researched 2026-07-02

Social-emotional development, PreK–12, organized around the five CASEL competencies
(self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, responsible
decision-making). Early bands are grounded in CDC/NAEYC observable milestones; later bands
are grounded in CASEL developmental indicators and state K-12 SEL standards. "Advanced" =
roughly a stage ahead (the tiered philosophy), not a norm every child should hit.

## Frameworks used

1. **CASEL Framework** — The spine of this file. Five core competencies, each with named subskills, applicable PreK–12 with developmentally different indicators at each stage. Self-awareness (identifying emotions, growth mindset, purpose, identity), self-management (emotion regulation, stress management, goal-setting, self-discipline), social awareness (perspective-taking, empathy, appreciating diversity), relationship skills (communication, teamwork, conflict resolution, resisting negative pressure, leadership), responsible decision-making (reasoned judgment, anticipating consequences, ethical/constructive choices). See casel.org "What Is the CASEL Framework."
2. **CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early."** — Primary reference for PreK and Lower Elementary floor. Observable social/emotional milestones at 3y, 4y, 5y (75th percentile — what *most* children do). Used as the early-band floor.
3. **Illinois SEL Standards (ISBE, 2004; first state K-12 SEL standards)** — Three goals mapped to CASEL, with benchmarks by grade span: early elementary (K-3), late elementary (4-5), middle (6-8), early high (9-10), late high (11-12). Concrete, gradable indicators for conflict resolution, decision-making, relationship skills. Basis for our upper bands.
4. **Washington OSPI SEL Standards, Benchmarks & Indicators** — State framework articulating CASEL-aligned benchmarks across grade bands; used to cross-check upper-band indicators.
5. **CASEL developmental example practices** (schoolguide.casel.org) — What each competency *looks like* by stage: early elementary names emotions; upper elementary recognizes physical cues and that people respond differently to the same event; middle school tracks emotions over time and identifies influences; high school recognizes personal strengths/resources for hard emotional situations.
6. **Erikson psychosocial stages / Marcia identity statuses** — Developmental scaffold for the older bands. Industry vs. Inferiority (~5–12): competence through achievement. Identity vs. Role Confusion (~12–18): exploration + commitment across values, career, relationships (Marcia's diffusion → foreclosure → moratorium → achievement). Grounds the identity/self-awareness indicators in Middle/High.
7. **Wellman (Nature Reviews Psychology, 2022) — Theory of Mind in Early Childhood** — ToM develops in a predictable sequence: desires → beliefs → false beliefs. Most children pass false-belief tasks between 4y–5y. Directly grounds the emotion-recognition-others and empathy PreK/Lower Elementary bands.
8. **Simon & Nader-Grosbois (2025) — Emotion Regulation and Empathy Profiles in Preschoolers** (Child & Youth Care Forum, N=354, 3–6y) — Emotion regulation is positively associated with all three empathy dimensions (affective, cognitive, behavioral). Lability/negativity specifically hurts *cognitive* empathy. Grounds the early empathy bands.
9. **Silkenbeumer, Schiller & Kärtner (2018) — Co- and self-regulation of emotions in the preschool setting** — Self-regulation is *built out of* co-regulation. As a child's own ER improves, adults shift from doing the regulating toward *meta-cognitive prompts* ("what could you do?"). The mechanism under the self-management progression.
10. **Lieberman et al. — Affect Labeling ("Putting Feelings Into Words," UCLA, fMRI)** — Verbally labeling an emotion measurably reduces amygdala activation and lets the prefrontal cortex regain control. The neuroscience under "name it to tame it" (Siegel) and under emotion-coaching as a regulation strategy.
11. **Conflict-resolution & peer-relationship research** — With age, children shift from coercion toward negotiation; adolescents work toward *independent* conflict resolution without adult intervention (ScienceDirect 2018; Frontiers 2023). Emotional comprehension predicts conflict-resolution strategy and peer adaptation. Grounds the relationship-skills upper bands and the conflict-resolution subdomain.

## Age-band milestones

Bands: **PreK (3–5)** · **Lower Elementary (K–2, 5–8)** · **Upper Elementary (3–5, 8–11)** · **Middle (6–8, 11–14)** · **High (9–12, 14–18)**. Each subdomain maps to a CASEL competency.

### Emotion Recognition (Self) — CASEL: self-awareness

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (≈stage ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | Uses simple then specific feeling words (happy, sad, mad → worried, proud, frustrated); begins to connect cause ("I'm mad because…") | Labels 5+ emotions; consistently connects cause to feeling; identifies pride/embarrassment; beginning self-reflection | CDC 3y/4y/5y; CASEL self-awareness |
| Lower Elementary (K–2) | Names a range of emotions and connects them to situations; recognizes emotions in the moment | Describes internal states to others unprompted; notices intensity ("a little mad vs. really mad") | CASEL example practices (early elem: name the emotion); CDC 5y |
| Upper Elementary (3–5) | Recognizes physical cues of emotion (tight chest, hot face); understands people can feel differently about the same event | Distinguishes mixed/simultaneous emotions; links feelings to values and thoughts | CASEL example practices (upper elem); Illinois SEL 1A |
| Middle (6–8) | Tracks changing emotions over time; identifies factors that influence how they feel and react | Recognizes patterns/triggers across contexts; begins integrating personal and social identity into self-concept | CASEL example practices (middle); Erikson identity onset |
| High (9–12) | Recognizes personal strengths and the resources they can draw on in hard emotional situations; links feelings, values, thoughts | Articulates a coherent sense of identity, purpose, and values; examines own biases; growth mindset about weaknesses | CASEL example practices (high); Marcia identity statuses; CASEL self-awareness subskills |

### Emotion Recognition (Others) / Perspective-Taking — CASEL: social awareness

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (≈stage ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | Notices visible distress; reads happy/sad/angry/scared in faces; by 4–5 comforts others and passes standard false-belief tasks | Identifies *why* someone feels a certain way; anticipates how actions make others feel; spontaneous perspective-taking in stories | CDC 3y/4y/5y; Wellman 2022 (ToM sequence) |
| Lower Elementary (K–2) | Reads basic social cues (bored, left out); understands others can want/believe different things | Adjusts behavior based on others' emotional states; explains a peer's feelings unprompted | CASEL social awareness; Illinois SEL 2A |
| Upper Elementary (3–5) | Takes others' perspectives; recognizes that the same event yields different emotional responses across people | Reads subtle/mixed cues; recognizes strengths in others; notices when a group norm is unfair | CASEL example practices (upper elem); CASEL social awareness subskills |
| Middle (6–8) | Empathizes with people from different backgrounds; identifies social norms (including unjust ones); reads group dynamics | Analyzes how organizations/systems shape behavior; expresses gratitude and situational awareness fluently | CASEL social awareness subskills; Illinois SEL 2A/2B |
| High (9–12) | Demonstrates empathy/compassion across diverse contexts; understands institutional and cultural influences on behavior | Actively seeks out and values differing viewpoints; identifies and names systemic/unjust norms and their impact | CASEL social awareness; Washington OSPI SEL |

### Self-Regulation / Emotion Management — CASEL: self-management

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (≈stage ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | Calms within ~10 min after a parent leaves; default frustration response is still crying/yelling (on-time); recovers faster with adult support; by 4–5 uses a strategy or two with prompting | Uses 1–2 calming strategies *with a meta-cognitive prompt* ("what could you do?"); asks for help instead of melting down; waits briefly toward a reward | CDC 3y/4y/5y; Silkenbeumer 2018; affect labeling (Lieberman) |
| Lower Elementary (K–2) | Uses a named calming strategy (breaths, walk away, ask for help) with reminders; manages most daily frustrations verbally | Uses strategies independently most of the time; names own triggers ("I get mad when…"); delays gratification for short periods | Illinois SEL 1B; CASEL self-management (managing emotions, self-discipline) |
| Upper Elementary (3–5) | Applies stress-management strategies to school demands; recovers from setbacks; stays on task with mild self-direction | Selects among strategies flexibly by situation; self-monitors and adjusts before escalation; sustained effort toward a multi-step goal | CASEL example practices (self-mgmt/self-awareness link); Illinois SEL 1B/1C |
| Middle (6–8) | Manages stress from increased academic/social load; sets short-term goals and uses planning/organization | Identifies and deploys a personal repertoire of stress-management strategies; shows self-motivation and initiative under pressure | CASEL self-management subskills; Illinois SEL 1C |
| High (9–12) | Sets personal and collective long-term goals; manages competing demands; regulates emotion in high-stakes situations | Exhibits durable self-discipline/agency; takes initiative; plans and organizes toward multi-month goals with minimal external structure | CASEL self-management subskills; Erikson industry→identity |

### Peer Play → Peer Relationships — CASEL: relationship skills

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (≈stage ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | Parallel → cooperative play; pretend play with roles; by 4–5 follows game rules and resolves some conflicts verbally | Organizes play; includes new children; leads group play and creates games with rules | CDC 3y/4y/5y |
| Lower Elementary (K–2) | Forms friendships; plays cooperative games with rules; communicates wants/needs; works in pairs/small groups | Initiates and sustains friendships; takes on group roles; helps include peers who are left out | Illinois SEL 2B/2C; CASEL relationship skills |
| Upper Elementary (3–5) | Maintains stable friendships; practices teamwork and collaborative problem-solving; navigates cliques emerging | Shows early leadership; offers and seeks help appropriately; communicates across differences | CASEL relationship skills subskills; peer-relationship research |
| Middle (6–8) | Peers become primary source of support/affirmation; sensitive to peer opinion and belonging; values friendship loyalty | Maintains healthy relationships while resisting negative social pressure; leads teams; builds cross-group relationships | Edutopia/NCSSLE middle-school SEL; CASEL relationship skills |
| High (9–12) | Sustains close, reciprocal friendships and (often) romantic relationships; teamwork with diverse groups | Demonstrates cultural competency; stands up for others' rights; models leadership and healthy relationship norms | CASEL relationship skills subskills; Washington OSPI SEL |

### Turn-Taking / Sharing → Cooperation & Teamwork — CASEL: relationship skills

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (≈stage ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | Shares/takes turns with reminders and adult mediation; by 4–5 negotiates simple turn-taking ("when I'm done") | Shares without being asked; mediates turn-taking for younger children; applies fair-play concepts | CDC 3y/4y/5y |
| Lower Elementary (K–2) | Takes turns reliably in structured games; shares materials; follows group rules | Adjusts sharing to others' needs ("she hasn't had a turn"); helps enforce fair rules | Illinois SEL 2B; CDC 5y |
| Upper Elementary (3–5) | Collaborates on group tasks; divides work; compromises to reach a shared goal | Coordinates a group toward a goal; distributes roles fairly; resolves resource disputes without adults | CASEL teamwork subskill; peer-mediation research |
| Middle (6–8) | Works productively in teams with assigned/negotiated roles; gives and receives feedback | Facilitates group work; manages differing contribution levels; keeps a team on task | CASEL relationship skills; Illinois SEL 2C |
| High (9–12) | Collaborative problem-solving in diverse teams toward complex goals; shared accountability | Leads collaborative projects; builds consensus; mentors peers in teamwork | CASEL teamwork/leadership subskills |

### Empathy & Prosocial Behavior — CASEL: social awareness

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (≈stage ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | Notices distress and comforts (pat, offer toy, "it's okay"); by 4–5 asks "what happened?" and shows early cognitive empathy | Spontaneous, specific comforting; early perspective-taking in real situations; prosocial choices toward less-preferred peers | CDC 3y/4y/5y; Simon & Nader-Grosbois 2025; Wellman 2022 |
| Lower Elementary (K–2) | Shows concern and helps when someone is hurt/sad; recognizes basic unfairness | Advocates for others ("that's not fair to her"); shows spontaneous prosocial behavior toward strangers | CDC 5y; CASEL social awareness |
| Upper Elementary (3–5) | Demonstrates empathy/compassion; shows concern for others' feelings across contexts; expresses gratitude | Recognizes strengths in others; adjusts help to what the person actually needs; includes the excluded | CASEL social awareness subskills |
| Middle (6–8) | Empathizes with peers from diverse backgrounds; sensitive to belonging and exclusion dynamics | Acts on empathy through service/advocacy; recognizes and names unjust norms affecting peers | CASEL social awareness; NCSSLE middle SEL |
| High (9–12) | Sustained compassion and civic concern; understands systemic influences on others' experiences | Leads prosocial/civic action; mentors; advocates for others' rights at a group/community level | CASEL social awareness/relationship skills |

### Conflict Resolution — CASEL: relationship skills (added for older ages)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (≈stage ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | Needs adult mediation for most conflicts; by 4–5 resolves some disputes verbally when calm | Uses words to solve simple conflicts with minimal prompting; proposes a fair swap or turn | CDC 4y–5y; conflict-resolution research (coercion→negotiation) |
| Lower Elementary (K–2) | Uses simple problem-solving steps (say the problem, listen, find a fix) with adult support | Resolves common peer conflicts independently; apologizes and repairs; uses "I" statements | Illinois SEL 2D; Frontiers 2023 (emotional comprehension → strategy) |
| Upper Elementary (3–5) | Negotiates and compromises; distinguishes conflict from bullying; seeks a win-win with adult available | Mediates minor disputes for peers; de-escalates before things blow up; separates the person from the problem | Peer-mediation research; CASEL conflict-resolution subskill |
| Middle (6–8) | Uses negotiation over coercion; manages conflict within friendships without losing the relationship | Resolves most conflicts independently; navigates group/social-media conflict; repairs ruptured friendships | ScienceDirect 2018 (adolescent strategies); Illinois SEL 2D |
| High (9–12) | Resolves interpersonal conflict independently and constructively; handles disagreement in teams and relationships | Facilitates others' conflict resolution; manages high-stakes/values-based disagreement; restorative approaches | CASEL relationship skills; conflict-resolution research |

### Responsible Decision-Making — CASEL: responsible decision-making (added for older ages)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (≈stage ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | Follows simple rules; makes basic safe/unsafe choices; by 4–5 avoids obvious dangers and changes behavior by setting | Explains *why* a choice is safe/kind; anticipates a simple consequence ("if I push, he'll be sad") | CDC 4y (avoids danger, changes behavior by setting) |
| Lower Elementary (K–2) | Identifies a problem and a couple of solutions; understands classroom/home rules and reasons for them | Weighs options against fairness and feelings; predicts consequences before acting | Illinois SEL Goal 3; CASEL RDM subskills |
| Upper Elementary (3–5) | Makes reasoned judgments after gathering information; anticipates and evaluates consequences of actions | Applies ethical criteria; reflects on impact on self *and* others; responsible use of technology | CASEL RDM subskills; Illinois SEL 3B |
| Middle (6–8) | Analyzes situations, considers consequences, and resists impulsive/risky choices; digital-citizenship reasoning | Evaluates interpersonal and community impacts; navigates peer pressure with a values-based rationale | CASEL RDM subskills; Illinois SEL 3C |
| High (9–12) | Makes constructive choices across complex situations; anticipates long-term and community consequences; reflects on role in family/community well-being | Applies critical thinking to real ethical dilemmas; evaluates institutional impacts; makes principled decisions under social/emotional pressure | CASEL RDM subskills; Washington OSPI SEL |

### Identity & Self-Concept — CASEL: self-awareness (added for older ages)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (≈stage ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | Knows own name, age, sex; describes likes/interests; simple self-descriptions | Describes self in terms of preferences, roles, and feelings ("I'm the kind of kid who…") | CDC 5y; CASEL self-awareness |
| Lower Elementary (K–2) | Concrete self-concept (what I look like, what I can do); identifies personal likes and strengths | Recognizes personal, cultural, and linguistic assets; growth-mindset language about learning | CASEL self-awareness (identity/assets); Erikson industry |
| Upper Elementary (3–5) | Compares self to peers; sense of competence tied to school/activity success (Industry stage) | Integrates strengths across domains; frames setbacks as learning; early sense of purpose/interests | Erikson (Industry vs. Inferiority); CASEL self-awareness |
| Middle (6–8) | Begins identity exploration; sensitive to peer perception; integrating personal and social identities | Actively explores values/roles (moratorium); connects interests to a developing sense of purpose | Erikson/Marcia (identity onset); Marcia moratorium; CASEL |
| High (9–12) | Explores and commits to values, career direction, and relationships; more coherent, stable identity | Identity achievement across domains; articulated purpose linking self and beyond-self goals; examines biases and integrity | Marcia identity achievement; Erikson identity vs. role confusion; CASEL self-awareness |

## Recent research

- [CASEL — What Is the CASEL Framework](https://casel.org/fundamentals-of-sel/what-is-the-casel-framework/) — Canonical list of the five competencies and their named subskills. Self-awareness now explicitly includes integrating personal/social identities, examining biases, self-efficacy, growth mindset, and sense of purpose — grounding the added Identity subdomain for older ages.
- [CASEL — Explicit SEL Instruction / developmental example practices](https://schoolguide.casel.org/focus-area-3/classroom/explicit-sel-instruction/) — The same competency looks different by stage: early elementary names an emotion; upper elementary recognizes physical cues and that people respond differently to the same event; middle school tracks emotions over time and identifies influences; high school draws on personal strengths/resources in hard emotional moments. Directly used to band the self-awareness/self-management rows.
- [Illinois State Board of Education — SEL Standards (first state K-12 SEL standards, 2004)](https://everydayspeech.com/sel-implementation/understanding-social-emotional-learning-standards-in-illinois/) — Three goals mapped to CASEL, with gradable benchmarks across five spans (K-3, 4-5, 6-8, 9-10, 11-12). Relationship-skills goal explicitly names conflict resolution; decision-making goal names problem-solving, critical thinking, and responsible technology use. Basis for the upper bands and the added Conflict-Resolution and Responsible-Decision-Making subdomains.
- [Resolving conflicts with friends: adolescents' strategies (ScienceDirect, 2018)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0140197118300186) + [Emotional comprehension and peer conflict-resolution strategies (Frontiers, 2023)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1142373/full) — With age, children shift from coercion toward negotiation; adolescents aim for *independent* conflict resolution without adult intervention. Emotional comprehension predicts which strategy a child uses and their peer adaptation. Grounds the conflict-resolution progression.
- [Identity Development Theory — Erikson & Marcia (adolescent psychology, Pressbooks)](https://wisconsin.pressbooks.pub/adolescentpsychology/chapter/identity-development-theory/) + [Identity development research: systematic review of reviews (2025)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15298868.2025.2549770) — Adolescent identity forms through exploration + commitment across ideology, career, and relationships (Marcia: diffusion → foreclosure → moratorium → achievement). Purpose is an extension of identity, enabled by abstract thinking. Grounds the Middle/High Identity and self-awareness rows.
- [How Students Typically Grow in SEL From Elementary to Middle School (Edutopia)](https://www.edutopia.org/article/how-students-typically-grow-sel-elementary-middle-school/) + [NCSSLE — Building SEL in middle school](https://safesupportivelearning.ed.gov/voices-field/how-can-schools-successfully-build-social-and-emotional-competencies-middle-school) — In middle school peers become the primary source of support, advice, and affirmation; students compare themselves to others and are highly sensitive to peer opinion and belonging. Grounds the Middle-band peer-relationship rows.
- [Wellman (2022) — Foundations of Theory of Mind in Early Childhood (Nature Reviews Psychology)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-022-00037-z) — ToM develops desires → beliefs → false beliefs; most children pass false-belief tasks between 4y–5y. Grounds the PreK/Lower-Elementary emotion-recognition-others and empathy floors.
- [Silkenbeumer, Schiller & Kärtner (2018) — Co- and self-regulation of emotions in the preschool setting](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323966518_Co-_and_self-regulation_of_emotions_in_the_preschool_setting) + [Lieberman et al. — Affect Labeling (UCLA fMRI)](https://www.scn.ucla.edu/pdf/AL%282007%29.pdf) — Self-regulation is built out of co-regulation; adults move from doing the regulating to meta-cognitive prompts as the child's ER matures. Naming an emotion reduces amygdala activation. The mechanism under the self-management progression and "name it to tame it."
