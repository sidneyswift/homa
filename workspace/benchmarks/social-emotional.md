# social-emotional

> ✅ FRESH — researched 2026-05-04

## Frameworks used

1. **CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early"** — Primary age-band reference. Social/emotional milestones at 3y, 4y, and 5y (75th percentile — what *most* children can do). Simple, observable behaviors. Used as the floor.
2. **Early Stages DC** — Local DC resource that re-packages CDC milestones with practical context. Useful because it's the framework the parent would encounter if she engaged local early intervention services.
3. **CASEL Framework (early childhood adaptation)** — Five competencies: self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, responsible decision-making. Maps well to our subdomains. Used as the structural framework for what "advanced" looks like.
4. **WonderTree / practitioner synthesis** — Breaks CDC milestones into daily-life examples with support strategies per age. Good for translating milestones into the parent's teaching context.
5. **PMC/Nature-based SR study (2024)** — Research on self-regulation development in preschoolers. Key finding: SR is malleable in early childhood, develops from co-regulated (parent helps) to self-regulated around age 3–4, and unstructured play predicts better SR years later.

## Age-band milestones

### Emotion Recognition (Self)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Uses simple feeling words (happy, sad, mad); may not always label correctly in the moment | Labels 4+ emotions accurately in self; begins to connect cause ("I'm mad because…") | CDC 3y; WonderTree |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Names basic emotions and sometimes explains why | Labels 5+ emotions; can describe what makes them feel each one | CASEL self-awareness |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Talks about feelings using specific words; beginning to understand mixed feelings | Spontaneously labels emotions in daily narration ("I was worried but then I felt better") | CDC 4y; WonderTree |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Identifies emotions in self and can explain causes | Uses nuanced emotion words (frustrated, nervous, proud, embarrassed) | CASEL self-awareness |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Names a range of emotions and connects them to situations | Describes internal emotional states to others unprompted; beginning self-reflection | CDC 5y |

### Emotion Recognition (Others)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Notices when someone is visibly upset (crying, yelling) | Identifies emotions in others from facial expressions and body language | CDC 2y (notices) → 3y emerging |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Beginning to respond to others' emotions (may pat or stare) | Identifies why someone might feel a certain way ("She's sad because her toy broke") | WonderTree |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Comforts others who are hurt or sad (hugging a crying friend) | Anticipates how actions might make someone else feel | CDC 4y milestone |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Reads basic social cues (someone looking bored, scared) | Adjusts own behavior based on others' emotional states | CASEL social awareness |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Shows empathy through words and actions; talks about how others feel | Perspective-taking in stories and real life ("How would you feel if…") | CDC 5y; CASEL |

### Self-Regulation (Emotional)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Calms down within ~10 minutes after parent leaves (e.g., daycare drop-off); still needs adult co-regulation for big emotions; may tantrum when frustrated | Begins using simple strategies with prompting (deep breaths, hugging a stuffed animal); recovers from minor frustrations in <5 min | CDC 3y milestone; PMC SR study |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Starting to transition from co-regulated to self-regulated; tantrums decreasing in frequency but still common; may cry or yell when frustrated | Uses 1–2 calming strategies with adult reminder ("take a breath"); can sometimes wait briefly for something wanted | PMC SR study; WonderTree |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Avoids obvious dangers; changes behavior based on setting (library vs playground); manages minor frustrations with verbal expression | Uses calming strategies independently (walks away, takes breaths, asks for help); can wait for a turn without adult prompting most of the time | CDC 4y milestones; CASEL self-management |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Manages most daily frustrations verbally; seeks adult help when overwhelmed rather than melting down | Identifies own triggers ("I get mad when…") and proactively uses strategies; can problem-solve through conflicts with minimal help | CASEL self-management |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Follows rules and takes turns in games; does simple chores; manages emotions in group settings | Self-monitors emotions; uses multiple strategies flexibly; begins to delay gratification for longer periods | CDC 5y; CASEL |

### Peer Play / Social Interaction

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Notices other children and joins them to play; mostly parallel play with emerging cooperative play | Initiates play with peers; engages in simple cooperative play (building together, pretend play with roles) | CDC 3y; WonderTree |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Plays alongside and increasingly with peers; beginning to take on roles in pretend play | Negotiates play themes; can sustain cooperative play for 10+ minutes | WonderTree |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Pretends to be something else during play (teacher, superhero, dog); asks to go play with specific children | Complex pretend play with multiple roles; negotiates rules; seeks out preferred playmates | CDC 4y milestones |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Sustained cooperative play; understands and follows simple game rules; resolving some conflicts verbally | Organizes play activities; includes new children; explains game rules to others | CDC 4y–5y bridge |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Follows rules and takes turns in structured games; sings/dances/performs for others | Leads group play; creates games with rules; navigates multi-child social dynamics | CDC 5y milestones |

### Turn-Taking / Sharing

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Beginning to share with reminders; takes turns with adult mediation | Shares spontaneously sometimes; takes turns in simple games with minimal prompting | CDC 30mo–3y progression |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Takes turns with reminders; shares preferred toys with specific friends | Takes turns independently in play; shares with less-preferred peers | WonderTree |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Likes to be a "helper"; shares and takes turns more consistently | Mediates turn-taking for younger children; shares without being asked | CDC 4y "helper" milestone |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Takes turns in structured games; shares materials in group activities | Negotiates sharing ("You can have it when I'm done, then it's your turn") | CDC 4y–5y |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Follows rules and takes turns in games reliably | Fair play concepts; adjusts sharing based on needs ("She hasn't had a turn yet") | CDC 5y |

### Empathy

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Notices when someone is upset; may stop playing to look or approach | Spontaneously comforts (pats, offers toy, says "it's okay") | CDC 2y → 3y bridge |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Shows concern for others' distress; may try to comfort with physical gestures | Offers specific comfort ("Do you want your blankie?"); adjusts approach based on person | WonderTree |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Comforts others who are hurt or sad (hugging, patting, kind words) | Anticipates what might help; asks "What happened?" or "Are you okay?" | CDC 4y milestone |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Empathetic responses are more consistent and varied | Perspective-taking in real situations; can explain why someone might feel a certain way | CASEL social awareness |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Shows empathy in group contexts; recognizes unfairness | Advocates for others ("That's not fair to her"); talks about feelings in stories | CDC 5y; CASEL |

## Recent research

- [CDC Milestones by 4 Years](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/4-years.html) — SEL milestones at 4y: pretend play, asking to play with peers, comforting others, avoiding danger, being a "helper," changing behavior by setting. your child already meets several of these (cooperative play, turn-taking).
- [Early Stages DC — Social-Emotional Milestones](https://www.earlystagesdc.org/page/social-emotional-milestones) — Local DC resource. Clean CDC milestone breakdown from 2mo through 5y. 3y: calms down within 10min of parent leaving, notices and joins other children. 4y: pretend play, comforting, helping, context-switching. 5y: rule-following, performing, chores.
- [WonderTree — Social Emotional Milestones by Age](https://blogs.wondertree.co/social-emotional-development-milestones-by-age-for-preschoolers/) — Practitioner-friendly breakdown. Key insight for your child: "The best time to build a skill is before a hard moment happens" — practice self-regulation strategies during calm moments, not during meltdowns.
- [PMC — Nature-Based Practices and Self-Regulation (2024)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11591543/) — SR is malleable in early childhood. Develops from co-regulated (caregiver-assisted) to self-regulated around age 3–4. Unstructured play and nature-based experiences independently predict better SR at ages 4–5 and 6–7. Hot executive function (emotionally-charged SR) benefits most from nature play.
- [Springer — Systematic Review of SR Interventions in Preschool (2023)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-023-09740-6) — 85 studies reviewed. Mindfulness, games/play-based, and curriculum-embedded approaches all show efficacy. Key finding: interventions work best when they combine explicit strategy teaching with practice opportunities embedded in daily routines.
- [ScienceDirect — Free Play Predicts Self-Regulation (2021)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0885200621001411) — Longitudinal study: more time in unstructured quiet play at ages 3–5 predicted better SR at ages 4–7, even controlling for earlier SR. Supports the parent's play-based approach.
