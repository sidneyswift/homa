# Music

> ✅ FRESH — researched 2026-07-02

## Frameworks used

1. **National Core Arts Standards — Music (2014, NCCAS)** — The authoritative U.S. PreK–12 framework. Organizes all learning under four **Artistic Processes**: **Creating** (Imagine → Plan & Make → Evaluate & Refine → Present), **Performing** (Select → Analyze → Interpret → Rehearse/Evaluate/Refine → Present), **Responding** (Select → Analyze → Interpret → Evaluate), and **Connecting** (relate music to personal experience, other subjects, history/culture). Grade-by-grade standards PreK–8 (general music); discrete high-school strands for Ensembles, Harmonizing Instruments, Composition/Theory, and Music Technology at **Novice (≈gr 5), Intermediate (≈gr 8), Proficient, Accomplished, Advanced** levels. *Use:* the spine for all age-band milestones below.

2. **NAfME (National Association for Music Education)** — Steward of the 2014 standards; publishes grade-level performance standards and Opportunity-to-Learn standards. *Use:* the practical grade-by-grade sequencing that maps the anchor standards onto real classroom expectations.

3. **Kodály method** — Singing-first, child-developmental sequencing. Voice is the first instrument; folk songs as primary material. Solfège + hand signs for pitch (sol-mi → la → do-re → full diatonic). Rhythm syllables (ta, ti-ti). Sequence: experience → notation. UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage (2016). *Use:* pitch/singing and music-literacy scope-and-sequence, especially the early bands.

4. **Orff Schulwerk** — Movement + speech + rhythm + improvisation. Pentatonic melodies, ostinato patterns, body percussion (clap/stomp/snap/pat). Orff instruments (xylophones, metallophones, drums). *Use:* rhythm, improvisation, and instrument-exposure milestones; strongest fit for kinesthetic learners. 2025 fNIRS evidence links Orff-based play to improved executive function and neural efficiency in 3-year-olds.

5. **Suzuki method (general principles)** — "Mother tongue" approach: immersion before literacy, daily listening, repetition, parent involvement. Formal instrument instruction typically starts age 3–5; many programs enroll from ~3.5. *Use:* early instrumental readiness and the listen → imitate → play → read progression.

6. **Dalcroze Eurhythmics** — Music through movement; rhythm internalized kinesthetically before it is written. Games of quick reaction and change. *Use:* rhythm and expressive-movement milestones in the early bands.

7. **Edwin Gordon's Music Learning Theory (MLT)** — **Audiation** (hearing music in the mind) as the foundation. Tonal and rhythm patterns learned through exposure before labeling; stages absorption → imitation → assimilation → audiation. Ages 0–5 treated as the critical developmental music-aptitude window. *Use:* the developmental logic under the PreK / Lower-Elementary singing and listening bands.

## Age-band milestones

Bands (used consistently below): **PreK (3–5)** · **Lower Elementary (K–2, 5–8)** · **Upper Elementary (3–5, 8–11)** · **Middle (6–8, 11–14)** · **High (9–12, 14–18)**. Each subdomain maps to one or more Artistic Processes (C=Creating, P=Performing, R=Responding, Cx=Connecting).

### Rhythm & Beat  *(P, C)*

- **PreK (3–5):** Claps/marches to a steady beat (may drift, then stabilizes). Responds to fast/slow tempo and start/stop cues. Echoes 2- then 4-beat patterns; plays a simple ostinato on unpitched percussion. Distinguishes loud/quiet. *(Kodály L1; Orff; Dalcroze; Gordon MLT)*
- **Lower Elementary (K–2):** Keeps a steady beat independently while others sing. Reads/performs iconic then standard notation for quarter notes, paired eighths, and quarter rests (ta, ti-ti, rest). Improvises and creates 4–8 beat patterns; distinguishes beat vs. rhythm; feels meter (2/4, 4/4). *(NAfME K–2; Kodály L2)*
- **Upper Elementary (3–5):** Reads and performs whole, half, dotted-half, quarter, paired-eighth, and (by gr 5) sixteenth notes plus equivalent rests in 2/4, 3/4, 4/4. Performs two-part rhythms/ostinati; conducts basic meter; syncopation introduced. *(NAfME 3–5; state model curricula)*
- **Middle (6–8):** Reads dotted and syncopated rhythms, triplets, and compound meters (6/8); uses a counting system (numbers/syllables) fluently while performing on instrument or voice. Composes multi-measure rhythmic passages. *(NAfME 6–8; NCAS Intermediate)*
- **High (9–12):** Sight-reads rhythmically complex, multi-meter/mixed-meter literature at ensemble tempo; interprets rubato and expressive timing; internalizes advanced subdivision. *(NCAS Ensemble Proficient→Advanced)*

### Pitch, Singing & Solfège  *(P, C, R)*

- **PreK (3–5):** Sings fragments then whole simple songs from memory; pitch range expands toward an octave (≈D4–D5). Matches pitch with an adult; distinguishes high/low; begins sol-mi echoes. Advanced audiation = starting a song at a comfortable pitch unaided. *(Gordon MLT; Kodály)*
- **Lower Elementary (K–2):** Sings in tune across most of a song using head voice; matches pitch reliably. Reads/sings sol-mi-la then do-re-mi with hand signs. Echoes 4–5 note melodic patterns; sings simple rounds/two-part by the end of the band. *(Kodály L1–2; NAfME K–2)*
- **Upper Elementary (3–5):** Sings independently and in tune in a group; reads simple pitch notation on the treble staff (movable-do) in major keys; sings partner songs, rounds, and beginning 2-part harmony; expanding range (≈C4–D5). *(NAfME 3–5; TN/Kansas model standards)*
- **Middle (6–8):** Sings in 2–3 parts with independence and blend; navigates the adolescent voice change (esp. changing male voices); sight-sings diatonic melodies; applies solfège/scale-degree reading. *(NAfME 6–8; NCAS)*
- **High (9–12):** Sings SATB literature with intonation, balance, and stylistic accuracy; sight-sings including chromatic alterations and modulations at the Advanced level; interprets phrasing and diction across languages/styles. *(NCAS Ensemble/Choir Proficient→Advanced)*

### Instrumental & Vocal Performance / Technique  *(P)*

- **PreK (3–5):** Explores percussion freely, then purposefully (steady beat on a drum; 2–3 note melodies on pitched percussion with proper mallet grip). Formal Suzuki instruction (violin/piano) possible from ~3.5. *(Orff; Suzuki pre-twinkle)*
- **Lower Elementary (K–2):** Plays classroom pitched/unpitched instruments with correct technique; performs simple pentatonic melodies and ostinato accompaniments; beginning Suzuki repertoire (Book 1). *(Suzuki; Orff; NAfME K–2)*
- **Upper Elementary (3–5):** Recorder/ukulele or beginning band/orchestra/general instrument instruction; reads notation for the instrument; posture, tone, and instrument-care basics; performs short pieces from notation and memory. *(NAfME 3–5; recorder instruction typically begins ~gr 3)*
- **Middle (6–8):** Formal ensemble study (band/orchestra/choir) with graded literature; scales and technical exercises; tuning, blend, articulation; individual and section rehearsal skills. *(NAfME 6–8; NCAS Intermediate)*
- **High (9–12):** Performs solo and ensemble literature of increasing difficulty; advanced technique, breath/bow control, extended range; leadership (section leader/soloist); the Advanced level prepares for collegiate study. *(NCAS Ensemble/Harmonizing Instrument Proficient→Advanced)*

### Music Literacy & Notation Reading  *(P, R)*

- **PreK (3–5):** "Reads" iconic/pictorial notation (high/low, long/short); associates sound with symbol pre-formally. *(Gordon; Kodály prep)*
- **Lower Elementary (K–2):** Reads standard rhythm notation (quarter, paired eighths, rest) and simple pitch notation (sol-mi-la) on lines/spaces; distinguishes line vs. space notes; beginning staff awareness. *(Kodály; NAfME K–2)*
- **Upper Elementary (3–5):** Reads treble-clef staff note names, keys of C/G; whole–sixteenth note values and rests; time signatures 2/4, 3/4, 4/4; dynamics and tempo markings; begins reading for an instrument (recorder → band). *(state model curricula; NAfME 3–5)*
- **Middle (6–8):** Reads treble and (as needed) bass/alto clef; key signatures, major/minor scales, intervals, and basic chord symbols; counts complex rhythms; reads standard expressive/articulation markings. *(NAfME 6–8; general music-theory scope)*
- **High (9–12):** Fluent multi-clef reading; full theory (diatonic harmony, seventh chords, secondary function, modulation) at Accomplished/Advanced; sight-reading and score study; AP Music Theory–level literacy. *(NCAS Composition/Theory; AP Music Theory)*

### Improvisation & Composition  *(C)*

- **PreK (3–5):** Improvises short rhythm patterns and pentatonic melodic fragments; invents sound effects/movement for stories; "composes" via manipulatives/icons. *(Orff; NCAS Cr PreK)*
- **Lower Elementary (K–2):** Improvises 4-beat rhythmic/melodic answers (question-answer); generates and documents simple musical ideas with iconic/standard notation; revises with guidance. *(NCAS Creating K–2; Orff)*
- **Upper Elementary (3–5):** Improvises over pentatonic/simple harmonic frameworks; composes short pieces with a beginning/middle/end using notation or notation software; evaluates and refines against criteria. *(NCAS Creating 3–5)*
- **Middle (6–8):** Improvises melodic/rhythmic ideas within a given tonality/meter; composes and arranges multi-section pieces applying form, texture, and expressive elements; uses DAW/notation tools. *(NCAS Creating 6–8; Music Technology)*
- **High (9–12):** Composes and arranges original works demonstrating command of harmony, form, orchestration/voicing, and style; improvises stylistically (e.g., jazz changes); Composition/Theory & Music Technology strands. *(NCAS Composition/Theory & Music Technology, Proficient→Advanced)*

### Music Theory  *(P, R, C)*

- **PreK (3–5):** Pre-theory: high/low, loud/soft, fast/slow, same/different, steady beat vs. rhythm. *(Gordon; NCAS)*
- **Lower Elementary (K–2):** Beat/rhythm, meter feel, dynamics (p/f), tempo terms, melodic direction (up/down/same), simple form (AB, ABA). *(NAfME K–2)*
- **Upper Elementary (3–5):** Note/rest values, treble staff, C/G major scales, simple intervals, dynamics/tempo/articulation vocabulary, form (rondo, verse/chorus, theme & variations). *(NAfME 3–5)*
- **Middle (6–8):** Major & minor scales, key signatures (circle of fifths intro), intervals by number/quality, major/minor/diminished/augmented triads and inversions, cadences, phrase structure. *(general MS theory scope; NCAS Intermediate)*
- **High (9–12):** Full diatonic and chromatic harmony, four-part voice leading, seventh chords, non-chord tones, modulation, harmonic analysis, form analysis; culminates in AP Music Theory. *(HS Music Theory / AP Music Theory curricula)*

### Responding: Listening, Analysis & Evaluation  *(R)*

- **PreK (3–5):** Has preferences; recognizes familiar music; responds emotionally/physically; identifies a few instruments by sound; describes music with feeling words. *(NCAS Responding PreK; NAEYC)*
- **Lower Elementary (K–2):** Identifies instrument timbres and families (intro); describes tempo/dynamics/mood with vocabulary; identifies simple form and repetition; states preferences with a reason. *(NCAS Responding K–2)*
- **Upper Elementary (3–5):** Analyzes elements (melody, rhythm, harmony, form, timbre, texture); identifies orchestral instrument families; evaluates performances against criteria; explains how music conveys expression. *(NCAS Responding 3–5)*
- **Middle (6–8):** Analyzes how form, harmony, and expressive elements shape meaning; evaluates using discipline-specific criteria and evidence; compares interpretations. *(NCAS Responding 6–8)*
- **High (9–12):** Critiques works and performances using sophisticated musical, structural, and contextual criteria; defends interpretations with theoretical and stylistic evidence. *(NCAS Responding Proficient→Advanced)*

### Connecting: History, Genres & Culture  *(Cx, R)*

- **PreK (3–5):** Connects music to stories, moods, and daily routines; experiences songs/dances from varied cultures. *(NCAS Connecting PreK)*
- **Lower Elementary (K–2):** Relates music to personal experiences, seasons, and other subjects; encounters music from multiple cultures and its social uses. *(NCAS Connecting K–2)*
- **Upper Elementary (3–5):** Places music in cultural/historical context; explores world-music traditions and American genres (folk, jazz roots, spirituals); makes cross-curricular links (math/rhythm, story/song). *(NCAS Connecting 3–5)*
- **Middle (6–8):** Studies Western style periods — **Medieval/Renaissance, Baroque (1600–1750), Classical (1750–1827), Romantic (1827–1900), 20th-c./contemporary** — plus jazz and world genres; connects context, function, and composer intent to sound. *(NCAS Connecting 6–8; music-history curricula)*
- **High (9–12):** Deep study of music history, theory-in-context, and diverse global/popular traditions; connects personal creative/performance choices to societal, historical, and cultural contexts; informed cultural criticism. *(NCAS Connecting Proficient→Advanced)*

## Recent research

- [Frontiers in Psychology, 2024 — "Effects of music training on executive functions in preschool children aged 3–6 years: systematic review and meta-analysis"](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1522962/full) — Meta-analysis of 10 RCTs. Music training significantly improves inhibitory control (SMD 0.38), working memory (SMD 0.35), and cognitive flexibility (SMD 0.23). Optimal dose ≥12 weeks, ≥3×/week, 20–30 min. Supports music as an evidence-based executive-function intervention in the PreK/Lower-Elementary bands.

- [Kosokabe et al., 2025 — "Self-directed music play to improve executive function in young children" (Nature Scientific Reports)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-10984-1) — RCT, 57 three-year-olds. Orff-Schulwerk music play improved inhibitory control and showed enhanced prefrontal neural efficiency on fNIRS — first imaging evidence that early music play reshapes brain-activity patterns. Reinforces the Orff/kinesthetic weighting in the early bands.

- [Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, 2025 — "Effects of a phonics-integrated music rhythm intervention on reading fluency and accuracy"](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2025.1636278/full) — Quasi-experimental (N=164, 3rd graders, 10 weeks). Rhythm skills improved (d=0.57) but did NOT causally transfer to reading despite strong cross-sectional correlations (ρ ≈ 0.20–0.35). Takeaway: treat music-literacy links as a shared auditory substrate, not a reading shortcut.

- [National Core Arts Standards — Music (nationalartsstandards.org)](https://www.nationalartsstandards.org/content/national-core-arts-standards) — Confirms the four Artistic Processes and 11 anchor standards as the PreK–12 spine; high-school strands (Ensemble, Harmonizing Instruments, Composition/Theory, Music Technology) span Novice→Advanced. This is the backbone for the age-band expansion above.

- [NAfME — Standards / 2014 Music Standards, PreK–8 General Music strand](https://nafme.org/publications-resources/standards/) — Grade-by-grade performance standards PreK–8 with Novice (≈gr 5) and Intermediate (≈gr 8) preparatory levels feeding the HS strands — the mapping used to band the milestones by stage.

- State model curricula (e.g., Tennessee, Kansas) — Concrete notation-reading progression: iconic notation (PreK–K) → quarter/eighth/rests (K–2) → whole–sixteenth values, treble staff, C/G major, 2/4-3/4-4/4 (gr 3–5) → key signatures, scales, intervals, triads (gr 6–8) → full harmony/AP Theory (9–12). Used to ground the literacy/theory bands with real classroom sequencing.
