# social-studies

> ✅ FRESH — researched 2026-07-02

Social studies is an integrated field drawing on four core disciplines — **History, Geography, Civics/Government, and Economics** — plus the inquiry and literacy skills that tie them together. This benchmark covers the full PreK–12 arc: it keeps the early "expanding environments" scope (self → family → community → world) and extends it through historical thinking with primary sources, world & US history, spatial/map reasoning, constitutional government & citizenship, and economic principles & personal finance.

## Frameworks used

1. **NCSS C3 Framework** (primary) — *College, Career, and Civic Life Framework for Social Studies State Standards* (2013). Organizes social studies into the four disciplines and an **Inquiry Arc** of four dimensions: (D1) developing questions & planning inquiries; (D2) applying disciplinary concepts & tools (civics, economics, geography, history); (D3) evaluating sources & using evidence; (D4) communicating conclusions & taking informed action. Each indicator has a suggested K–12 pathway across grade bands **K–2, 3–5, 6–8, 9–12** — these map onto the age bands below. Adopted/adapted by a majority of states.
2. **NCSS National Curriculum Standards for Social Studies** — Ten thematic strands (culture; time/continuity/change; people/places/environments; individual development & identity; individuals/groups/institutions; power/authority/governance; production/distribution/consumption; science/technology/society; global connections; civic ideals & practices), PreK–12.
3. **National Geography Standards — *Geography for Life*** — 18 standards in 6 essential elements: the world in spatial terms; places & regions; physical systems; human systems; environment & society; uses of geography. Plus five geographic skills (asking, acquiring, organizing, analyzing, answering).
4. **National Standards for Personal Financial Education** (Council for Economic Education + Jump$tart, 2021) — Six topics: earning income; spending; saving; investing; managing credit; managing risk. Benchmarks at end of grades 4, 8, 12. Paired with CEE's **Voluntary National Content Standards in Economics** (20 standards, benchmarks at grades 4/8/12).
5. **Stanford History Education Group / Digital Inquiry Group — "Reading Like a Historian"** — Historical thinking as sourcing, contextualization, corroboration, and close reading of primary sources; lessons scaffolded elementary → high school.
6. **Core Knowledge Sequence (PreK–8)** — Content scope & sequence for History & Geography; PreK–2 starts with self/family/community expanding outward.
7. **Head Start ELOF — Cognition & Social-Emotional** — Birth–5 federal framework; social studies is transdisciplinary (history/events, geography, people & environment, sense of identity/belonging), not a standalone domain.
8. **State standards as concrete exemplars** — Virginia 2023 SOL, Massachusetts 2018 HSS Framework, New York K–12 SS Framework — used to ground the typical grade-by-grade content progression ("expanding communities" then chronological history).

## Age-band milestones

Bands: **PreK (3–5)** · **Lower Elementary (K–2, 5–8)** · **Upper Elementary (3–5, 8–11)** · **Middle (6–8, 11–14)** · **High (9–12, 14–18)**. These align to the C3 grade bands (PreK/K–2 → 3–5 → 6–8 → 9–12). Each subdomain is grounded in a named framework/standard.

### Self, Family & Identity *(early scope — feeds into History & Civics)*

- **PreK (3–5):** Says first, then full (first+last) name; knows age and eventually birthday. Names immediate family members and roles. Understands basic daily/weekly routine and simple sequence (baby → now). Advanced: knows home address (street+city) and a parent phone number; describes family composition and personal history. *(CDC 3y/4y milestones; Head Start ELOF — Sense of Identity; Core Knowledge PreK.)*
- **Lower Elementary (K–2):** Knows full address + emergency contact; constructs a simple personal timeline; distinguishes home vs. school rules; describes family heritage/traditions and compares own family to others. Uses "long ago / recently" and simple calendars. *(Virginia SOL K–1; NCSS strand: individual development & identity.)*
- **Upper Elementary (3–5) and up:** Identity work folds into History (biography, personal & community history) and Civics (rights, belonging, group membership). Explicit "self & family" content tapers; skills carry forward into perspective-taking and civic identity. *(C3 D2.His / D2.Civ; NCSS.)*

### History & Historical Thinking

- **PreK (3–5):** Distinguishes past/present ("when I was a baby"); enjoys stories about long ago; orders 2–3 events in sequence. *(Head Start ELOF — history & events; CDC pretend-play → role of the past.)*
- **Lower Elementary (K–2):** Uses calendars & simple timelines; distinguishes past/present/future; knows key national holidays and the historical figures behind them (e.g., Washington, Lincoln, MLK); understands that people, tools, and daily life change over time; begins asking "how do we know?" using photos, objects, and family stories as sources. *(C3 D2.His.1–3, 9–10 K–2 pathway; SHEG intro-level sourcing; Core Knowledge.)*
- **Upper Elementary (3–5):** Reads and creates multi-event timelines; identifies causes & effects of events; distinguishes primary vs. secondary sources and compares accounts; typically studies world communities (gr. 3), state/regional history (gr. 4), and early US history (gr. 5). *(C3 D2.His.1–5, 3–5 pathway; NY/VA/MA grade scope.)*
- **Middle (6–8):** Analyzes chronology and periodization; explains multiple causes and long-/short-term effects; **sources** documents (author, purpose, audience), **contextualizes**, and **corroborates** across sources; recognizes point of view and bias. Content commonly: world history/geography (gr. 6–7) and US history to Reconstruction (gr. 8). *(C3 D2.His.6–16, 6–8 pathway; SHEG "Reading Like a Historian.")*
- **High (9–12):** Evaluates competing historical interpretations and the reliability of sources; analyzes continuity & change, contingency, and historiography; constructs evidence-based historical arguments. Content commonly: modern US history (gr. 11) and world history; often AP US/World/European. *(C3 D2.His.9–17, 9–12 pathway; SHEG; MA/NY HS scope.)*

### Geography & Spatial Reasoning

- **PreK (3–5):** Uses location/position words (near/far, above/next to); knows own city/town; points to land vs. water; recognizes a map or globe as a picture of a place; advanced: names own state/country and several of the 7 continents. *(Head Start ELOF — geography; Core Knowledge PreK; Nat'l Geography Std. 1–2.)*
- **Lower Elementary (K–2):** Names all 7 continents & major oceans; uses cardinal directions (N/S/E/W); reads a simple map with a key/legend; distinguishes globe (model) from flat map; locates own community/state/country; identifies basic physical features (mountain, river, ocean). *(Nat'l Geography Std. 1–4; Virginia SOL K–1.)*
- **Upper Elementary (3–5):** Uses grids, scale, and symbols; distinguishes physical vs. political maps; locates US states, world regions, and major landforms; describes how climate/landforms shape where and how people live (human–environment interaction). *(Nat'l Geography Std. 1, 3–4, 14–15; C3 D2.Geo.1–6 3–5 pathway.)*
- **Middle (6–8):** Uses latitude/longitude, time zones, thematic maps, and GIS-style representations; analyzes population distribution, migration, and cultural regions; explains economic interdependence and resource distribution across regions. *(Nat'l Geography Std. 1–5, 9–12; C3 D2.Geo.1–9 6–8 pathway.)*
- **High (9–12):** Applies spatial thinking and geospatial tools to analyze human & physical systems, urbanization, globalization, and sustainability; evaluates human impact on environments and vice versa; often AP Human Geography. *(Nat'l Geography Std. 6, 14–18; C3 D2.Geo.1–12 9–12 pathway.)*

### Civics & Government

- **PreK (3–5):** Understands rules exist "to keep us safe/fair"; takes turns and shares; recognizes authority figures and community helpers (firefighter, doctor, teacher, mail carrier) and what they do; emerging fairness/voting ("we pick together"). *(Head Start ELOF — people & environment; CDC 4y; NCSS strand: power/authority/governance.)*
- **Lower Elementary (K–2):** Knows why rules/laws exist and who makes them; identifies national symbols (flag, anthem), key holidays, and basic roles of leaders; understands voting as group decision-making; practices responsibilities of a good citizen. *(C3 D2.Civ.1–6 K–2 pathway; Virginia SOL K–1.)*
- **Upper Elementary (3–5):** Distinguishes local/state/national government and the three branches; explains rights & responsibilities of citizens; understands how laws are made and how citizens influence government; introduces key founding ideas & documents. *(C3 D2.Civ.1–7 3–5 pathway; NCSS civic ideals & practices.)*
- **Middle (6–8):** Explains the purposes & structures of government, the Constitution, the Bill of Rights, federalism, and separation of powers; analyzes how citizens participate (voting, juries, advocacy) and how public policy is made; compares forms of government. *(C3 D2.Civ.1–14 6–8 pathway; typically a civics course.)*
- **High (9–12):** Analyzes how the US Constitution establishes powers, responsibilities, and limits that "have changed over time and are still contested" (D2.Civ.4.9-12); evaluates landmark cases, competing constitutional interpretations, rule of law, and civic engagement; deliberates public issues and takes informed action. Often a required Government/Civics course. *(C3 D2.Civ.1–14 9–12 pathway; MA/NY HS scope.)*

### Economics & Personal Finance

- **PreK (3–5):** Distinguishes wants vs. needs (emerging); knows money buys things; understands saving in a piggy bank; people work at jobs. Best learned through pretend play (store, restaurant). *(Head Start ELOF; Tandfonline 2024 "Children understand economics too"; NCSS strand: production/distribution/consumption.)*
- **Lower Elementary (K–2):** Distinguishes goods vs. services and producers vs. consumers; understands scarcity ("the store ran out") and that choices have trade-offs; recognizes people earn income by working; introduces basic saving and spending. *(CEE Voluntary Nat'l Content Standards, gr.4 benchmarks; C3 D2.Eco.1–3 K–2; Personal Finance Std. — Earning/Saving.)*
- **Upper Elementary (3–5):** Explains scarcity, opportunity cost, and incentives; how markets set prices via supply & demand (simple); role of money, barter, and specialization; how banks help people save; basic budgeting (income vs. spending). *(CEE Econ Std. 1–8; National Standards for Personal Financial Education, gr.4 benchmarks — earning, spending, saving.)*
- **Middle (6–8):** Analyzes supply & demand, competition, markets, and the role of government (taxes, public goods); GDP/growth basics; personal finance — budgeting, saving vs. investing, interest, and intro to credit and risk. *(CEE Econ benchmarks gr.8; Personal Finance Std. gr.8 — earning, spending, saving, credit, investing, risk.)*
- **High (9–12):** Micro (markets, elasticity, firm behavior, market failure) and macro (inflation, unemployment, monetary & fiscal policy, trade); personal finance — compound interest, credit scores & debt, insurance, taxes, investing/retirement, and financial decision-making. Often Economics + Personal Finance courses (a graduation requirement in a growing number of states). *(CEE Econ benchmarks gr.12; National Standards for Personal Financial Education gr.12 — all six topics; often AP Micro/Macro.)*

### Culture, Diversity & Global Connections *(cross-cutting, all strands)*

- **PreK (3–5):** Recognizes own family celebrations; notices that other families celebrate different things; enjoys stories about children from other cultures. *(Head Start ELOF — identity & belonging.)*
- **Lower Elementary (K–2):** Names holidays from several cultures and describes traditions (food, music, clothing, language); understands people around the world live differently; respects diversity. *(NCSS strand: culture; Core Knowledge.)*
- **Upper Elementary (3–5):** Compares cultures, religions, and daily life across world communities/regions; connects culture to geography and history. *(NCSS culture & global connections; C3 D2.Geo/His.)*
- **Middle (6–8):** Analyzes how cultures develop, diffuse, and interact; examines belief systems, migration, and cultural change across world history. *(NCSS global connections; C3 D2.His/Geo 6–8.)*
- **High (9–12):** Evaluates globalization, human rights, cross-cultural conflict & cooperation, and interdependence; takes informed action on global issues. *(NCSS global connections; C3 D4 — taking informed action.)*

## Recent research

1. **NCSS C3 Framework — grade-band pathways** — Each C3 indicator has a suggested K–12 pathway across **K–2, 3–5, 6–8, 9–12**, acknowledging students' growing capacity for sophisticated ideas and demanding inquiries. As of recent counts ~38 states use standards that quote, reference, or model the C3 inquiry approach. This is the backbone for treating social studies as PreK–12 disciplinary inquiry rather than isolated content.
   - https://www.socialstudies.org/standards/c3
2. **National Standards for Personal Financial Education (CEE + Jump$tart, 2021)** — Six topics (earning income, spending, saving, investing, managing credit, managing risk) with benchmarks at end of grades **4, 8, 12**. Notably standards target concepts/principles rather than products/rules so they don't go stale — the model for the economics band progression above.
   - https://www.councilforeconed.org/policy-advocacy/k-12-standards/
3. **National Geography Standards — *Geography for Life***: 18 standards in 6 essential elements plus 5 geographic skills. Spatial thinking (maps, mental maps, spatial organization) is the through-line from PreK map-picture recognition to HS geospatial/GIS analysis.
   - https://ncge.org/teacher-resources/national-geography-standards/
4. **Stanford History Education Group — "Reading Like a Historian"** (now Digital Inquiry Group) — Historical thinking = sourcing, contextualization, corroboration, close reading; 100+ free primary-source lessons scaffolded elementary → HS. Grounds the History band from "how do we know?" (K–2) to evaluating competing interpretations (9–12).
   - https://sheg.stanford.edu/
5. **Civics grade progression** — C3 civics moves from rules/fairness (K–2) → branches & citizen roles (3–5) → Constitution, Bill of Rights, federalism (6–8) → constitutional analysis of powers "still contested" (D2.Civ.4.9-12) and informed action (9–12). Mirrors the common required civics (MS) + government (HS) course sequence.
   - https://www.socialstudies.org/sites/default/files/c3/C3-Framework-for-Social-Studies.pdf
6. **"Expanding communities" → chronological history scope** — State frameworks (NY, VA, MA) show the typical content arc: self/family (K) → neighborhoods/communities (1–3) → state/regions (4) → US history (5) → world history/cultures (6–7) → US history (8) → world & US history + government/economics (9–12). Useful for choosing *content* to pair with each band's *skills*.
   - https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/standards-instruction/ss-framework-k-8a2.pdf
7. **Early-childhood economics (Tandfonline 2024, "Children understand economics too")** — Preschoolers grasp money-buys-things, wants vs. needs, and saving; most learning happens via family exposure and pretend play, not formal instruction. Validates starting the Economics strand in PreK.
   - https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/03004430.2024.2443736
8. **NAEYC 2024 — social studies in early childhood is transdisciplinary & inquiry-based** — All children can develop citizenship/leadership; inquiry through lived experience (field trips, living books, real objects) beats didactic content delivery. Supports the homeschool method of teaching the four strands through real-world experience at every age.
   - https://www.naeyc.org/resources/pubs/yc/spring2024/social-studies-early-childhood
