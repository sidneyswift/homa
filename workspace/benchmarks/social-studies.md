## Frameworks used

1. **NCSS C3 Framework** (primary) — The College, Career, and Civic Life Framework organizes social studies into four disciplines: civics, economics, geography, and history. Dimension 1 (developing questions) through Dimension 4 (taking informed action). Used as the overarching structure for what "social studies" means at each age.
2. **Core Knowledge Sequence (PreK–2)** — E.D. Hirsch's scope and sequence for History & Geography, starting in PreK with self/family/community expanding outward. Provides specific content knowledge expectations per grade.
3. **Head Start ELOF — Cognition & Social Studies sub-elements** — Federal framework for ages birth–5. Social studies is embedded within the Cognition domain (history & events, geography, people & environment) and Social-Emotional domain (sense of identity/belonging).
4. **CDC Milestones (2022 revision)** — Developmental expectations for 3y, 4y, 5y. Social studies isn't called out explicitly but social/cognitive milestones map onto social studies readiness.
5. **Understood.org developmental milestones** — Synthesized CDC + clinical milestones for 3y through 1st grade.
6. **Virginia 2023 SOL for K History & Social Science** — State standards providing specific content expectations for kindergarten.

## Subdomains

Social studies for early childhood maps to four subdomains:

1. **Self & Family** — Personal identity, family structure, home address/phone, daily routines, personal history.
2. **Community Helpers** — Roles of workers in the community (firefighter, doctor, teacher, postal worker, farmer). Rules and responsibilities.
3. **Geography Basics** — Maps & globes, continents & oceans, cardinal directions, physical features, own community/state/country location.
4. **Cultures & Traditions** — Awareness of holidays, customs, food, clothing, music from different cultures. Respect for diversity.

## Recent research

1. **Understood.org — Developmental milestones for 3, 4, 5-year-olds, and 1st graders** — Synthesized CDC 2022 revision milestones. Social/emotional milestones overlap heavily with social studies readiness. Key insight: "pretends to be something else during play" (4y) connects to community helper understanding.
   - https://www.understood.org/en/articles/developmental-milestones-for-typical-3-year-olds
   - https://www.understood.org/en/articles/developmental-milestones-for-typical-4-year-olds
   - https://www.understood.org/en/articles/developmental-milestones-for-typical-kindergartners
   - https://www.understood.org/en/articles/developmental-milestones-for-typical-first-graders

2. **Head Start ELOF — Ages Birth to Five** — Social studies content is distributed across Cognition (history, geography, people & environment) and Social-Emotional (sense of identity/belonging). The ELOF doesn't have a standalone "social studies" domain — it's integrated.
   - https://uw.pressbooks.pub/eedutdc/chapter/1-1-head-start-elof-2/
   - https://eclkc.ohs.acf.hhs.gov/interactive-head-start-early-learning-outcomes-framework-ages-birth-five

3. **Core Knowledge Foundation — CKHG Curriculum** — K–8 comprehensive History & Geography program. PreK sequence starts with self/family/community expanding to world. The "expanding environments" approach (self → community → city → country → world) is the traditional PreK–2 social studies scope.
   - https://www.coreknowledge.org/curriculum/

4. **Virginia 2023 Standards of Learning — History & Social Science** — State standards document specific content expectations. K: self/family, community helpers, American symbols/holidays, basic geography (map/globe, continents/oceans, cardinal directions). Useful as a benchmark for "typical K" expectations.
   - https://www.doe.virginia.gov/teaching-learning-assessment/k-12-standards-instruction/history-and-social-science/standards-of-learning

5. **NCSS — National Council for the Social Studies** — The C3 Framework organizes social studies into Civics, Economics, Geography, and History with four dimensions (questioning, concepts, sources, action). Confirms social studies is integrated, not a single domain.
   - https://www.socialstudies.org/standards

6. **Wikipedia — Social Studies** — Historical context: term coined by American educators ~1900, formalized by Bureau of Education in 1916. Dewey's influence on inquiry-based learning. Social studies = integrated field drawing from history, geography, civics, economics.
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_studies_education

## Key findings for your child

1. **Geography is his standout.** Naming all 7 continents at 3y5m is typically a late-K/early-1st-grade skill (5y0m–5y6m). The world currency awareness adds economic geography depth. This is one of your child's most advanced cognitive domains — 24mo ahead is well-supported.

2. **Self/family is ahead of schedule.** Knowing both parents' phone numbers + full home address at 3y5m is exceptional. Most kids don't reliably know phone numbers until 5y+. The CDC 5y milestones include "knows full name and age"; your child knows much more.

3. **Cultures & traditions is the blind spot.** Zero data. DC is one of the most culturally diverse cities in the US, and your child already does Spanish class — there are natural hooks everywhere. This should be the easiest unknown to fill.

4. **Economics concepts are untested.** your child's currency awareness (different countries, different money) suggests economic thinking is emerging. Worth probing: does he understand wants vs. needs? Does he know people work for money?

5. **The "expanding environments" model** (self → family → community → city → country → world) describes your child's natural progression perfectly. He's already at the "world" tier for geography while still building out "community" for helpers/economics. The model suggests deepening community/economics understanding before pushing world history.
