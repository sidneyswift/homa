# self-care-independence

> ✅ FRESH — researched 2026-07-02

Life-skills / independence progression, PreK–12. Early bands cover ADL self-care (dressing, feeding, toileting, hygiene, cleanup); older bands extend into household responsibility, money & time management, cooking/nutrition, personal safety, and adolescent executive life skills — the full arc from "I can dress myself" to "I can run a household."

## Frameworks used

1. **CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early." milestones** — Primary early-childhood self-care reference. Self-care milestones at 3y, 4y, 5y are set at the 75th percentile (what most children this age can do), used here as the "typical" floor for PreK.
2. **Occupational-therapy ADL norms** — Activities-of-daily-living milestone charts (dressing, feeding, toileting, hygiene) referenced from Beery VMI, Erhardt, Exner, Mulligan, Greenspan; consolidated in OccupationalTherapy.com's updated 0–6 ADL milestones and Lurie Children's self-help/ADL chart. Clinical-grade age expectations; the anchor for the PreK and Lower-Elementary bands.
3. **AAP / HealthyChildren.org — Chores & Responsibility** — Age-banded household-chore progression (5–7, 8–10, 11–12) plus a dedicated adolescent-chores guide. Frames chores as executive-function and work-ethic builders, not just labor.
4. **CFPB "Money as You Grow" / Building Blocks of Financial Capability** — The authoritative K–12 financial-capability framework. Three developmental building blocks: **executive function (ages 3–5)**, **financial habits & norms (ages 6–12)**, **financial knowledge & decision-making (ages 13–21)**. Anchors the money & time-management subdomain.
5. **Montessori Practical Life — Care of Self** — Structured progression from simple (hand washing) to complex (dressing frames, food prep). "Sensitive period for dressing" (≈1.5–3y). Informs the PreK dressing/hygiene scaffolds and environment setup (low hooks, limited choices, realistic clothing).
6. **AAP toilet-training evidence (Blum et al., Pediatrics 1997) + STR follow-up (2024, J Pediatric Nursing)** — Toilet-training and stool-toileting-refusal (STR) norms and prognosis; the toileting subdomain floor.
7. **USDA MyPlate / kids' kitchen-skill progressions** — Nutrition literacy and a graded knife-/heat-skill progression by age (butter knife → serrated → dull metal → sharp; supervised stovetop/oven → independent) for the cooking/nutrition subdomain.
8. **AAP media guidance ("5 Cs") + home-alone readiness guidance** — Internet/media safety and home-alone readiness (most children not ready to be home alone until ~10–12y); behavior-based (not "stranger danger") safety framing. Anchors the personal-safety subdomain.
9. **CASEL SEL framework (self-management & responsible decision-making)** — K-12 developmental benchmarks (PreK, K-2, 3-5, 6-8, 9-10, 11-12) for the self-regulation and decision-making that underlie all independence. Cross-links to the SEL benchmark.

## Age-band milestones

Age bands used consistently throughout:
**PreK (3–5)** · **Lower Elementary (K–2, 5–8)** · **Upper Elementary (3–5, 8–11)** · **Middle (6–8, 11–14)** · **High (9–12, 14–18)**

### Dressing

| Band | Typical | Source |
|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | 3y: puts on some clothes (loose pants, jacket), removes simple items, manages Velcro; 4y: unbuttons some buttons, zippers up/down, shoes on correct feet; 5y: buttons buttons, ties or is learning to tie shoes, selects/lays out next-day outfit | CDC 3y/4y/5y; OT ADL norms |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Fully independent dressing including all fasteners; ties shoes reliably; chooses weather-appropriate clothing; manages belts/buckles | OT ADL (60–72mo full independence); Montessori |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Independently selects clothing for occasion/weather; basic garment care (turns clothes right-side-out, matches, hangs); begins caring for own laundry pile | AAP chores 8–10 (put away own laundry) |
| Middle (6–8) | Fully self-manages wardrobe: sorts, washes, folds, puts away own laundry; iron/steam with instruction; dresses for context (formal vs. casual) | AAP chores 11–12 (do & fold laundry) |
| High (9–12) | Complete clothing autonomy incl. stain treatment, minor repairs (button, hem), budgeting for and shopping own clothes | AAP adolescent chores; life-skills frameworks |

### Toileting

| Band | Typical | Source |
|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | 3y: daytime urine-trained, stool training solidifying (STR affects up to 22%), many still in pull-ups for nights; 4y: full daytime independence incl. wiping, uses public/unfamiliar restrooms; 5y: independent all settings, nighttime dryness typical (15–20% of 5y still have night accidents — normal range) | CDC 3y/4y/5y; AAP (Blum 1997); OT ADL |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Fully independent day and night; manages own hygiene end-to-end (wipe, flush, wash); wipes thoroughly without help | OT ADL (54–72mo); CDC bridge |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Complete independence; late-band puberty onset (menstruation can begin ~9–11) → menstrual-hygiene management taught here for early developers | AAP puberty guidance |
| Middle (6–8) | Manages puberty-related hygiene independently (menstrual products, body odor, acne routine) | AAP adolescent hygiene |
| High (9–12) | Full adult self-care; manages own supplies, appointments, and health-hygiene routines | Life-skills frameworks |

### Feeding / Utensils → Cooking / Nutrition

| Band | Typical | Source |
|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | 3y: fork/spoon with some spillage, open cup, gets simple snack; 4y: serves self, pours, spreads with knife; 5y: prepares simple no-cook food (cereal, sandwich), clears own plate; butter/child-safe knife for soft foods | CDC; OT ADL (60–66mo spreads/simple snack); MyPlate |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Makes own simple snacks and no-heat breakfast; sets/clears table; serrated plastic knife (5–7) → begins dull metal knife with supervision; reads a simple recipe; names food groups (MyPlate) | AAP chores 5–7; kids' knife-skill progression; MyPlate |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Helps make dinner; makes own lunch; dull-edged metal knife with claw grip; uses microwave/toaster independently, stovetop with supervision; understands a balanced plate and portions | AAP chores 8–10 ("help make dinner," "make own snacks"); MyPlate |
| Middle (6–8) | Cooks a simple meal with (then without) supervision; sharper knives + stovetop/oven with minimal guidance; plans and prepares a balanced meal; reads nutrition labels | AAP chores 11–12 ("cook a simple meal with supervision"); kids' cooking-skills 12–14 |
| High (9–12) | Plans a week of meals, grocery-shops to a list/budget, cooks a variety of dishes independently, adapts recipes, understands nutrition for goals (energy, cost, dietary needs) | AAP adolescent (grocery shopping w/ license); life-skills frameworks |

### Personal Hygiene

| Band | Typical | Source |
|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | 3y: washes/dries hands with reminders, begins tooth-brushing (adult finishes); 4–5y: hand/face washing independent, brushes teeth with supervision for thoroughness, blows nose, brushes hair | CDC; OT ADL; Montessori |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Independent hygiene routine with reminders; brushes teeth adequately (parent spot-checks); bathes/showers with setup help; recognizes when hands/face need washing | OT ADL; Chicago OT |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Manages full bath/shower independently; owns a morning/evening routine; puberty hygiene begins for early developers (deodorant, more frequent bathing) | AAP puberty; OT ADL |
| Middle (6–8) | Fully independent hygiene incl. puberty needs (deodorant, acne care, shaving/menstrual products); manages own supplies and self-reminds | AAP adolescent hygiene |
| High (9–12) | Complete self-management incl. restocking supplies, grooming for context, basic health self-monitoring; manages own routine health appointments | Life-skills frameworks |

### Cleanup / Household Responsibilities & Chores

| Band | Typical | Source |
|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | 3y: puts away toys with prompting, helps wipe surfaces; 4y "helper" milestone: simple chores (match socks, feed pets, water plants), follows cleanup routines; 5y: makes bed, does simple chores, tidies own space | CDC 3y/4y/5y; Montessori |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Makes own bed, sets/clears table, dusts, puts toys/books away, dirty clothes in hamper, sorts laundry, empties wastebaskets, sweeps, waters plants, brings in mail, washes plastic dishes | AAP chores 5–7 |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Vacuums, helps make dinner, makes own snacks, walks the pet, puts away own laundry and groceries; can hold a recurring daily/weekly chore | AAP chores 8–10 |
| Middle (6–8) | Cleans kitchen, changes bedsheets, unloads dishwasher, does & folds laundry, cleans bathroom, washes car, cooks a simple supervised meal, watches younger sibling with an adult home | AAP chores 11–12 |
| High (9–12) | Any household task independently: full laundry cycle, deep cleaning, yard/snow work, grocery shopping, meal cleanup, pet care, sibling care; contributes as a household member regardless of job/school load | AAP adolescent chores |

### Money & Time Management

| Band | Typical | Source |
|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | **Building block: executive function.** Recognizes coins/bills, understands you exchange money for things and that money is limited (can't buy everything), begins saving in a jar, waits/delays for a small goal | CFPB Building Blocks (exec function 3–5) |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Counts and makes simple change; distinguishes needs vs. wants; earns (allowance/chores) and saves toward a short-term goal; begins a spend/save split; tells time and follows a simple visual daily schedule | CFPB (habits & norms begin ~6); SEL |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | **Building block: financial habits & norms.** Plans ahead / saves up for a wanted item; comparison-shops; understands the cost of borrowing informally; manages a small allowance across spend/save/give; uses a checklist/planner and estimates how long tasks take | CFPB (habits & norms 6–12) |
| Middle (6–8) | Budgets an allowance or small earnings; opens/uses a savings account; distinguishes fixed vs. variable spending; understands interest and how ads persuade; manages a homework/activity calendar and multi-step deadlines independently | CFPB; AAP chores 11–12; exec-function/time-management milestones |
| High (9–12) | **Building block: financial knowledge & decision-making.** Manages a checking/debit account, earns from a job, budgets real income, understands taxes/paychecks/credit/interest, compares financial products; plans long-term goals; self-manages a full calendar, deadlines, and appointments (college-readiness executive function) | CFPB (decision-making 13–21); college-readiness EF frameworks |

### Personal Safety

| Band | Typical | Source |
|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | Knows full name and a parent's name; body-safety basics (private parts, "no/go/tell," safe vs. unsafe touch); stays with caregiver in public; basic street/car-seat rules | Body-safety curricula; CDC |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Memorizes home address and a phone number; knows how/when to dial 911 and what an emergency is; behavior-based stranger safety ("a grown-up shouldn't ask a kid for help"); basic internet rules (don't share personal info; tell an adult if something feels wrong) | Home-alone handbooks; AAP media "5 Cs" |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Short periods home alone begin to be appropriate (~10–12, child-dependent): lock doors, don't say you're alone, verify at the door, basic first aid; growing digital citizenship (passwords, privacy, don't click unknown links, report contact from strangers) | Home-alone readiness (~10–12); AAP media guidance |
| Middle (6–8) | Extended time home alone / short sibling care; kitchen/appliance/emergency safety; recognizes online risks (grooming, scams, sextortion), manages privacy settings, navigates peer pressure and consent basics | AAP adolescent; AAP online-safety resources |
| High (9–12) | Full personal-safety autonomy: driving/transit safety, substance and relationship safety, financial-fraud awareness, mental-health help-seeking and self-advocacy; can care for others in an emergency | AAP; life-skills frameworks |

### Adolescent Independence / Executive Life Skills

| Band | Typical | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Middle (6–8) | Self-manages assignments and a calendar; packs own bag/gear; wakes to own alarm; initiates and follows through on multi-step tasks; begins self-advocacy (asks a teacher for help); handles disappointment and schedule changes with growing regulation | CASEL (self-management 6–8); exec-function checklists |
| High (9–12) | The "before they leave home" set: manages meals, sleep, laundry, appointments, and money independently; time-management/organization systems that survive without reminders; self-advocacy (requests accommodations, resolves problems with adults); adapts when plans change; makes responsible decisions weighing consequences | College-readiness EF frameworks; CASEL (11–12 responsible decision-making) |

## Recent research

- **CFPB's three building blocks are developmental, not curricular** — financial capability is scaffolded on executive function (3–5), then habits & norms (6–12), then knowledge & decision-making (13–21). Teaching a 5-year-old to wait for a goal is financial education; the abstract concepts come later and only stick if the executive-function base was built early. [CFPB Building Blocks](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/educator-tools/youth-financial-education/teach/)
- **AAP frames chores as executive-function training, not labor** — organized housework builds work ethic, self-reliance, and executive functioning; part-time jobs should not exempt teens, because "the work within the home is as valuable as the work outside." Visual chore charts and clear expectations are the recommended supports. [HealthyChildren.org — Chores](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Chores-and-Responsibility.aspx)
- **"Beyond stranger danger": teach behaviors, not people** — ~90% of child-abuse cases involve someone the child knows, so effective safety education targets suspicious behaviors ("a grown-up shouldn't ask a kid for help finding a puppy") rather than the appearance of "strangers." [ED.gov Personal Safety for Children](https://www.ed.gov/sites/ed/files/parents/academic/involve/safety/personal_safety.pdf)
- **Home-alone readiness is ~10–12 and skill-based, not purely age-based** — most children aren't ready for short unsupervised periods until 10–12; readiness hinges on demonstrable skills (locking up, not disclosing being alone, knowing 911, basic first aid), which is why the personal-safety subdomain places these in Upper Elementary. [UADA Home Alone Handbook](https://www.uaex.uada.edu/life-skills-wellness/personal-family-well-being/MP571.pdf)
- **OT ADL norms peg full self-care independence at ~6** — the four core ADLs (feeding, dressing, toileting, hygiene) are expected to be independent around 60–72 months; simple meal prep and knife use for soft foods appear at 60–66 months, which is why cooking is scaffolded from Lower Elementary onward. [OccupationalTherapy.com ADL Milestones](https://www.occupationaltherapy.com/articles/updated-adl-milestones-in-children-5812)
- **Kitchen skill is a graded knife-and-heat progression** — butter/child-safe knives (2–4) → serrated plastic (4–6) → dull metal with claw grip (7–9) → sharper knives and supervised stovetop/oven (10–12) → largely independent cooking (12–14). Securing the board and step-stool positioning are the standard safety setups. [Happy Kids Kitchen — Knife Skills](https://happykidskitchen.com/teaching-knife-skills-to-toddlers-and-kids-safety-and-basics/)
- **The college executive-function gap is real: campuses assume, don't teach, EF** — the high-school independence set (self-managed meals, sleep, laundry, appointments, money, plus self-advocacy and adaptability) must be built at home before leaving; time management and organization are foundational to everything downstream. [College Success Plan — EF Guide](https://collegesuccessplan.com/blog/executive-function-guide)
- **AAP's media "5 Cs" replace screen-time counting with intentional use** — Child, Content, Calm, Crowding-out, Communication; co-viewing and ongoing conversation reduce exposure to harmful content, and digital-safety skills belong on the same developmental ladder as physical safety. [AAP Online Health & Safety Resources](https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/media-and-children/center-of-excellence-on-social-media-and-youth-mental-health/online-health-and-safety-resources/)
- **STR (stool-toileting refusal) resolves for most without intervention** — the 2024 J Pediatric Nursing follow-up found ~65% of STR children resolve within a year once pressure is removed and stool is kept soft; ~35% persist, associated with family constipation history, enuresis, and low-rhythmicity temperament. Keeps the PreK toileting floor realistic and non-alarming. [STR Follow-Up Study 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S088259632400188X)
