# literacy

> ✅ FRESH — Last researched: 2026-05-14

## Frameworks used

- **Head Start ELOF (Early Learning Outcomes Framework)** — The most detailed preschool literacy framework available, with developmental progressions for 36–48m and 48–60m across phonological awareness, print/alphabet knowledge, comprehension, and writing. Chosen as primary anchor because it maps directly to your child's age band and subdomain structure.
- **CDC Milestones 2022 revision** — Updated to reflect 75th percentile (what most children can do by each age). Literacy-specific milestones are limited but useful for gross calibration.
- **Common Core State Standards (CCSS) Kindergarten RF** — Defines end-of-K expectations for print concepts, phonological awareness, phonics/word recognition, and fluency. Useful as the "24mo ahead" target for a 3.5-year-old.
- **Indiana University Early Literacy Developmental Continuum** — Research-based continuum from birth to 5, useful for developmental staging.
- **Reading.com / StayAtHomeEducator age-based milestones** — Practitioner-facing summaries with research citations (e.g., 20% of 3-year-olds recognize a few letters; 60% of 4-year-olds know >half uppercase).
- **PMC scoping review on inferential comprehension in 3–6 year olds (Desmarais et al., 2019)** — For story comprehension subdomain: maps causal inference types to story grammar elements across ages 3–6.

## Age-band milestones

### Letter Recognition (Uppercase)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Recognizes letters in own name; sings ABC song; may identify 2–5 uppercase letters | Recognizes 10+ uppercase letters including beyond own name | Head Start P-LIT 3 (36–48m); Reading.com (20% of 3yo recognize a few letters) |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Recognizes 5–10 uppercase letters; growing awareness of letters in environment | Names most uppercase letters (15+) | Head Start P-LIT 3; StayAtHomeEducator |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Recognizes and names many (>half) uppercase letters | Names all 26 uppercase | Head Start P-LIT 3 (48–60m); Reading.com (60% of 4yo know >half uppercase) |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Names most uppercase letters (18+) | All 26 uppercase fluently + some lowercase | Head Start P-LIT 3 (by 60m: names 18 upper) |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Names all uppercase letters; recognizes most lowercase | All upper + lower fluently; automatic naming | CCSS RF.K.1d; Reading.com (end of K mastery) |

### Letter Recognition (Lowercase)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | May not distinguish upper from lower; minimal lowercase awareness | Recognizes a few lowercase (5+) | Lowercase lags uppercase by 6–12 months (Reading.com) |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Beginning to notice lowercase exists; may ID 2–5 | Recognizes 10+ lowercase | Head Start; Reading.com |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Identifies some lowercase (5–10) | Names most lowercase (15+) | Reading.com (60% of 4yo know 5–10 lowercase) |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Names many lowercase (10–15) | Names all or nearly all lowercase | Head Start P-LIT 3 (by 60m: 15 lowercase) |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Names most lowercase (15+) | All 26 lowercase fluently | CCSS RF.K.1d |

### Letter-Sound Knowledge

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | May know 0–3 letter sounds (usually first letter of name) | 5–10 letter sounds | Head Start P-LIT 3 (36–48m: awareness of alphabet letters) |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Emerging sound awareness for 3–8 familiar letters | 10–15 letter sounds | Head Start P-LIT 3 |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Produces sounds for several recognized letters (8–12) | 15–20 letter sounds; beginning consonant isolation | Head Start P-LIT 3 (48–60m); CCSS RF.K.3a |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Knows sounds for many letters (12–18) | Most/all consonant sounds + some vowels | Head Start P-LIT 3 (by 60m: knows sounds of several letters) |
| 5y0m–5y6m | 1-to-1 letter-sound for most consonants; primary vowel sounds | All letter sounds including short/long vowels | CCSS RF.K.3a (demonstrate basic knowledge of 1-to-1 letter-sound) |

### Rhyming

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Enjoys rhyming songs/books; rote imitation of rhyme | Distinguishes when two words rhyme | Head Start P-LIT 1 (36–48m) |
| 3y6m–4y0m | With support, distinguishes rhyming vs non-rhyming pairs | Identifies rhyming word from group of 3 (hat/cat/log) | Head Start P-LIT 1 |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Recognizes rhyme from group (hat/cat/log → which rhyme?) | Produces rhymes independently ("what rhymes with log?") | Head Start P-LIT 1 (48–60m); CCSS RF.K.2a |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Produces rhymes with some support | Produces multiple rhymes fluently; recognizes non-words that rhyme | Head Start P-LIT 1 (by 60m) |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Produces rhyming words independently | Manipulates onset-rime (substitute initial sound to make new rhyme) | CCSS RF.K.2a |

### Syllable Segmenting

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Emerging — may clap to music/rhythm but not segment words | Claps syllables for 2-syllable words with support | Head Start P-LIT 1 (phonological awareness progression) |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Claps/counts syllables in 2-syllable words with adult help | Segments 3+ syllable words independently | Head Start P-LIT 1 |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Counts syllables in familiar words (2–3 syllables) | Segments and blends syllables; counts in longer words (4+) | Head Start P-LIT 1 (48–60m: counts syllables) |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Segments most words into syllables independently | Deletes syllables ("say butterfly without butter") | Head Start P-LIT 1 |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Fluent syllable counting; beginning syllable manipulation | Syllable deletion and substitution | CCSS RF.K.2b (count, pronounce, blend, segment syllables) |

### Blending (Phoneme Level)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Not yet expected; may blend onset-rime with heavy support (/c/…/at/ → cat?) | Blends CVC words from isolated phonemes (c-a-t → cat) | This is genuinely rare at 3y0–3y6m |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Onset-rime blending with support (/m/ + /op/ = mop) | CVC blending from isolated phonemes | Head Start P-LIT 1 (phonemic changes awareness at 48–60m) |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Beginning to blend onset + rime; may blend 2 phonemes | Blends 3-phoneme CVC words | CCSS RF.K.2c (blend onset + rime); IU continuum (ages 3–4: phonemic awareness) |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Blends CVC words with support | Blends CCVC/CVCC words; beginning to decode simple words | CCSS RF.K.2d |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Blends CVC words independently | Blends 4-5 phoneme words; blending supports independent decoding | CCSS RF.K.2d (blend and segment 3-5 phonemes) |

### your child Comprehension / Retelling

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Recalls random details from a story; may act out with props | Retells 1–2 key events in order; identifies main character | Head Start P-LIT 4 (36–48m); PMC scoping review |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Retells 1–2 events (may be out of order); identifies characters | Retells 2–3 events in temporal order; uses "first…then" | Head Start P-LIT 4 |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Retells 2–3 key events in rough order; answers who/what questions | Retells with cause-and-effect; makes predictions; identifies problem/resolution | Head Start P-LIT 4 (48–60m); PMC review (causal inferences emerging 4–5y) |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Sequenced retelling with simple connectors; answers inferential questions with support | Tells connected multi-event stories; answers how/why about character motivations | Head Start P-LIT 4/5 (by 60m) |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Full retelling with sequence + character identification; answers detail questions | Provides story summary; answers complex inferential questions; identifies themes | CCSS RL.K.1–3; Head Start P-LIT 5 (by 60m) |

### Print Awareness

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3y0m–3y6m | Distinguishes print from pictures; asks "what does this say?" | Knows text goes left to right; identifies front/back of book | Head Start P-LIT 2 (36–48m) |
| 3y6m–4y0m | Holds book correctly; shows growing awareness of speech-print connection | Recognizes own name in print; follows left-to-right, top-to-bottom | Head Start P-LIT 2; CDC 4y milestone |
| 4y0m–4y6m | Understands print is organized differently for different purposes (list vs story) | Points to words while reading memorized text; knows words = groups of letters | Head Start P-LIT 2 (48–60m) |
| 4y6m–5y0m | Understands words = groups of letters; beginning word tracking | 1-to-1 word pointing; identifies title, author | Head Start P-LIT 2 (by 60m) |
| 5y0m–5y6m | Identifies book parts; understands sentence/word/letter hierarchy | Tracks text fluently; recognizes sentence boundaries (period/capital) | CCSS RF.K.1a–d |

## Recent research

- [Head Start ELOF — Literacy Preschool](https://headstart.gov/school-readiness/article/literacy-preschool) — Comprehensive developmental progressions for 36–60m across 6 literacy goals. Primary framework.
- [CDC Milestones 3–5, 2022 update (PEATC summary)](https://peatc.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Developmental-Milestones-3-to-5-2022.pdf) — Updated to 75th percentile threshold. Limited literacy-specific milestones but anchors gross motor/cognitive readiness.
- [Letter Recognition Milestones (StayAtHomeEducator)](https://stayathomeeducator.com/letter-recognition-milestones/) — Research-aligned developmental sequence; cites Minnesota State University thesis on letter recognition as predictor of reading success.
- [Letter Recognition Milestones by Age (Reading.com)](https://blog.reading.com/letter-recognition-milestones-by-age/) — Age-by-age breakdown with quantitative benchmarks (20% of 3yo, 60% of 4yo, K-end mastery). Cites NIH/PMC study on K-readiness threshold (≥10 letters).
- [Inferential comprehension of 3–6 year olds within story grammar (PMC/Desmarais et al.)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6681044/) — Scoping review of 16 studies mapping 6 causal inference types to story grammar development in 3–6yo. Key finding: prediction and goal inferences emerge around age 4; emotion/motivation inferences by 5.
- [Early Literacy Developmental Continuum (IU/Hall)](https://blogs.iu.edu/earlyliteracy/2025/09/05/early-literacy-developmental-continuum/) — Birth-to-5 continuum; ages 3–4 = letter recognition, phonemic awareness, sight word recognition, scribbling.
- [Children's your child Retelling as Literacy Enhancement (Dunst et al., 2012, CELLreviews)](https://www.readingrockets.org/resources/resource-library/childrens-story-retelling-literacy-and-language-enhancement-strategy) — Meta-analysis of 11 studies (687 preschoolers): story retelling improves both comprehension and expressive vocabulary.
