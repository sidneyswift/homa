# Literacy

> ✅ FRESH — researched 2026-07-02

## Frameworks used

- **Common Core State Standards (CCSS) — ELA/Literacy, Reading K–12** — The anchor framework for this template. Reading is organized into strands: Foundational Skills (RF, K–5: print concepts, phonological awareness, phonics/word recognition, fluency), Literature (RL), Informational Text (RI), and Language (L: vocabulary, morphology, figurative language). College & Career Readiness (CCR) anchor standards are identical across grades; grade-specific standards spiral in complexity K–8 and across the 9–10 and 11–12 bands.
- **Scarborough's Reading Rope (2001) / Science of Reading** — Skilled reading = two braided strands. **Word Recognition** (phonological awareness, decoding, sight recognition — becomes increasingly automatic) × **Language Comprehension** (background knowledge, vocabulary, language structures, verbal reasoning, literacy knowledge — becomes increasingly strategic). Organizing model for why the emphasis shifts from decoding (PreK–2) to comprehension/analysis (grades 3–12).
- **Simple View of Reading (Gough & Tunmer, 1986)** — RC = Decoding × Language Comprehension. Explains the common "strong decoder / developing comprehender" profile and why gains in one factor don't compensate for a deficit in the other.
- **International Literacy Association (ILA)** — Position statements and briefs on the Science of Reading, fluency, and the reading–writing connection; used to frame instructional emphasis across bands.
- **Head Start Early Learning Outcomes Framework (ELOF)** — Detailed preschool literacy progressions (36–48m, 48–60m) across phonological awareness, print/alphabet knowledge, comprehension, and writing. Primary anchor for the PreK band.
- **Hasbrouck & Tindal Oral Reading Fluency Norms (2017 update)** — Compiled WCPM (words correct per minute) percentile norms, grades 1–6. Used to ground the reading-fluency subdomain quantitatively.
- **CDC Milestones (2022 revision)** — 75th-percentile developmental milestones; limited literacy content but useful for gross PreK calibration.
- **IMSE Phonological Awareness Continuum (2025)** — Research-based teaching progression (word → syllable → onset/rime → phoneme), anchored to Moats (2020) and Kilpatrick (2015).
- **PMC scoping review on inferential comprehension in 3–6 year olds (Desmarais et al., 2019)** — Maps causal-inference types to story-grammar elements across ages 3–6.

## Age bands

- **PreK (ages 3–5)** · **Lower Elementary (K–2, ages 5–8)** · **Upper Elementary (grades 3–5, ages 8–11)** · **Middle (grades 6–8, ages 11–14)** · **High (grades 9–12, ages 14–18)**

The early subdomains below retain the fine-grained 6-month PreK progressions from the original file (the highest-resolution window for foundational skills), then fold into the five bands as skills consolidate.

## Age-band milestones

### Letter Recognition (Uppercase)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK 3y0m–3y6m | Recognizes letters in own name; sings ABC song; may identify 2–5 uppercase letters | Recognizes 10+ uppercase letters beyond own name | Head Start P-LIT 3 (36–48m); Reading.com (20% of 3yo recognize a few letters) |
| PreK 3y6m–4y0m | Recognizes 5–10 uppercase letters; growing environmental print awareness | Names most uppercase (15+) | Head Start P-LIT 3; StayAtHomeEducator |
| PreK 4y0m–4y6m | Recognizes and names >half of uppercase letters | Names all 26 uppercase | Head Start P-LIT 3 (48–60m); Reading.com (60% of 4yo know >half uppercase) |
| PreK 4y6m–5y0m | Names most uppercase (18+) | All 26 uppercase fluently + some lowercase | Head Start P-LIT 3 (by 60m: names 18 upper) |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Names all uppercase; automatic naming by end of K | All upper + lower fluently and automatically | CCSS RF.K.1d; Reading.com (end of K mastery) |

### Letter Recognition (Lowercase)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK 3y0m–3y6m | Minimal lowercase awareness; may not distinguish upper/lower | Recognizes a few lowercase (5+) | Lowercase lags uppercase 6–12 months (Reading.com) |
| PreK 3y6m–4y0m | Beginning to notice lowercase; may ID 2–5 | Recognizes 10+ lowercase | Head Start; Reading.com |
| PreK 4y0m–4y6m | Identifies some lowercase (5–10) | Names most lowercase (15+) | Reading.com (60% of 4yo know 5–10 lowercase) |
| PreK 4y6m–5y0m | Names many lowercase (10–15) | Names all or nearly all lowercase | Head Start P-LIT 3 (by 60m: 15 lowercase) |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Names most/all lowercase (15+ → all) by end of K | All 26 lowercase fluently and automatically | CCSS RF.K.1d |

### Letter-Sound Knowledge / Phonics & Decoding

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK 3y0m–3y6m | 0–3 letter sounds (usually first letter of name) | 5–10 letter sounds | Head Start P-LIT 3 (36–48m) |
| PreK 3y6m–4y0m | Emerging sounds for 3–8 familiar letters | 10–15 letter sounds | Head Start P-LIT 3 |
| PreK 4y0m–4y6m | Sounds for several recognized letters (8–12); beginning consonant isolation | 15–20 letter sounds | Head Start P-LIT 3 (48–60m); CCSS RF.K.3a |
| PreK 4y6m–5y0m | Sounds for many letters (12–18) | Most/all consonant sounds + some vowels | Head Start P-LIT 3 (by 60m) |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | K: 1-to-1 letter-sound for most consonants + short vowels; Gr1–2: digraphs, blends, CVCe/long-vowel teams, decodes 1–2 syllable words | Decodes multisyllabic words; reads on-level irregular/sight words automatically | CCSS RF.K.3, RF.1.3, RF.2.3 |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Applies grade-level phonics + word analysis to decode multisyllabic words; uses syllabication patterns and known roots/affixes | Decodes unfamiliar academic/technical vocabulary using morphology | CCSS RF.3.3, RF.4.3, RF.5.3 (syllabication, morphology, all conversions) |

### Rhyming & Phonological Awareness

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK 3y0m–3y6m | Enjoys rhyming songs/books; rote rhyme imitation | Distinguishes when two words rhyme | Head Start P-LIT 1 (36–48m) |
| PreK 3y6m–4y0m | With support, sorts rhyming vs non-rhyming pairs | Identifies rhyming word from a group of 3 | Head Start P-LIT 1 |
| PreK 4y0m–4y6m | Recognizes rhyme from a group; claps syllables | Produces rhymes independently | Head Start P-LIT 1 (48–60m); CCSS RF.K.2a |
| PreK 4y6m–5y0m | Produces rhymes with some support | Multiple rhymes fluently; manipulates onset-rime | Head Start P-LIT 1 (by 60m) |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | K: produces rhymes, blends/segments syllables and onset-rime; Gr1: isolates, blends, segments, adds/substitutes individual phonemes in CVC words | Fully proficient phonemic manipulation (deletion/substitution) — the strongest predictor of decoding | CCSS RF.K.2, RF.1.2; Kilpatrick (2015) via IMSE |

### Syllable Segmenting

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK 3y0m–3y6m | May clap to rhythm but not segment words | Claps 2-syllable words with support | Head Start P-LIT 1; IMSE Continuum |
| PreK 3y6m–4y0m | Claps/counts 2-syllable words with adult help | Segments 3+ syllable words independently | Head Start P-LIT 1 |
| PreK 4y0m–4y6m | Counts syllables in familiar 2–3 syllable words | Segments/blends syllables; counts in 4+ syllable words | Head Start P-LIT 1 (48–60m) |
| PreK 4y6m–5y0m | Segments most words into syllables independently | Deletes syllables ("say butterfly without butter") | Head Start P-LIT 1 |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Fluent counting; syllable deletion/substitution; applies syllable types to decode | Uses syllabication (open/closed, VCe, r-controlled) to decode multisyllabic words | CCSS RF.K.2b, RF.1.3, RF.2.3 |

### Blending (Phoneme Level)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK 3y0m–3y6m | Not yet expected; onset-rime with heavy support | Blends CVC from isolated phonemes (c-a-t → cat) | Genuinely rare at 3y0–3y6m; IMSE (phonemic awareness = final stage) |
| PreK 3y6m–4y0m | Onset-rime blending with support (/m/ + /op/ = mop) | CVC blending from isolated phonemes | Head Start P-LIT 1 (48–60m) |
| PreK 4y0m–4y6m | Beginning onset+rime; may blend 2 phonemes | Blends 3-phoneme CVC words | CCSS RF.K.2c |
| PreK 4y6m–5y0m | Blends CVC with support | Blends CCVC/CVCC; begins decoding simple words | CCSS RF.K.2d |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Blends 3–5 phonemes independently; blending drives decoding of unfamiliar words | Blends complex clusters; automatic word reading | CCSS RF.K.2d, RF.1.2 |

### Reading Fluency (accuracy · rate · prosody)

Fluency = reading with sufficient accuracy, appropriate rate, and expression to support comprehension. It is the bridge from decoding to comprehension (Science of Reading). WCPM figures are approximate spring 50th-percentile norms (Hasbrouck & Tindal 2017).

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Gr1 ~60 WCPM, Gr2 ~100 WCPM; reads emergent/decodable text with accuracy and support; self-corrects | Reads on-level text with automaticity and phrasing well above grade | CCSS RF.1.4, RF.2.4; Hasbrouck & Tindal 2017 |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Gr3 ~112, Gr4 ~133, Gr5 ~146 WCPM; reads on-level prose/poetry orally with accuracy, appropriate rate, expression; uses context to self-correct | Fluent, expressive reading of above-grade text; smooth phrasing and intonation | CCSS RF.3.4, RF.4.4, RF.5.4; Hasbrouck & Tindal 2017 |
| Middle (6–8) | ~150 WCPM plateau; fluency now serves stamina with longer, denser texts; silent-reading rate and comprehension grow | Fluent across genres and content-area texts with minimal effort | Hasbrouck & Tindal 2017 (Gr6 ~146); ILA fluency brief |
| High (9–12) | Automatic, effortless decoding; cognitive load fully available for analysis; a fluency gap here signals an unresolved decoding issue to remediate | Fluently reads complex, dense, discipline-specific texts | Science of Reading (fluency as automaticity); ILA |

### Vocabulary & Morphology

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | Rapid oral vocabulary growth; learns words from shared reading and conversation; uses new words in play | Uses sophisticated/rare words; asks word meanings | Head Start; Language-comprehension strand (Scarborough) |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Determines word meaning from context and simple affixes (un-, re-, -ful); sorts words into categories; understands shades of meaning | Uses grade-2+ affixes and word relationships flexibly | CCSS L.K.4–6, L.1.4, L.2.4 |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Uses context + common Greek/Latin affixes and roots (photo-, -graph, tele-) to infer meaning; interprets similes/metaphors and figurative language | Analyzes morphologically complex academic vocabulary; nuanced figurative language | CCSS L.3.4–5, L.4.4–5, L.5.4–5 |
| Middle (6–8) | Uses affixes/roots and etymology; interprets figures of speech (personification, allusion); analyzes connotation/denotation and tone | Independently unpacks technical/domain vocabulary; analyzes nuance and register | CCSS L.6.4–5, L.7.4–5, L.8.4–5 |
| High (9–12) | Determines meaning of academic/discipline words incl. figurative, connotative, technical; analyzes impact of word choice on meaning/tone; patterns of word change (analyze/analysis) | Precise control of dense academic and literary vocabulary; etymology as an analytic tool | CCSS L.9-10.4–5, L.11-12.4–5 |

### Narrative / Literary Comprehension & Analysis (Reading: Literature)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PreK (3–5) | Recalls details; retells 1–3 events (order improves with age); identifies characters; answers who/what and, later, why | Sequenced retelling with connectors; answers inferential how/why about motivation | Head Start P-LIT 4/5; PMC review (Desmarais 2019) |
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Asks/answers key questions; retells stories incl. central message/lesson; describes characters, settings, major events | Determines theme; compares versions/characters; identifies narrator point of view | CCSS RL.K.1–3, RL.1.2–3, RL.2.2–6 |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Determines theme from details (incl. how characters respond to challenges); summarizes; describes characters/settings/events using specifics; explains how chapters/stanzas build the whole | Compares themes/topics across texts; analyzes narrator/speaker point of view and its effect | CCSS RL.3.2–3, RL.4.2–3, RL.5.2–6 |
| Middle (6–8) | Cites textual evidence for explicit meaning + inferences; determines theme and analyzes its development; analyzes how dialogue/incidents propel plot and reveal character | Analyzes how structure/point of view create effects (suspense, irony); compares a text to its film/other-media version | CCSS RL.6.1–3, RL.7.1–3, RL.8.1–3 |
| High (9–12) | Cites strong/thorough evidence and where the text is ambiguous; determines two+ themes and analyzes their interaction/development; analyzes complex characters and author's structural/aesthetic choices | Evaluates multiple interpretations; analyzes an author's choices about ordering events / manipulating time (pacing, flashback) for effect | CCSS RL.9-10.1–5, RL.11-12.1–5 |

### Informational-Text Comprehension & Close Reading (Reading: Informational Text)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Identifies main topic and key details; describes connections between events/ideas/steps; uses text features (headings, glossaries) to locate information | Compares two texts on the same topic; identifies author's purpose | CCSS RI.K.1–3, RI.1.2–6, RI.2.2–9 |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Determines main idea + supporting details and summarizes; explains events/procedures using specific text evidence; uses illustrations and multiple sources | Integrates information from several texts to speak/write knowledgeably; compares/contrasts structures across texts | CCSS RI.3.2–9, RI.4.2–9, RI.5.2–9 |
| Middle (6–8) | Determines central idea and its development; traces and evaluates an argument — whether reasoning is sound and evidence relevant/sufficient; analyzes how authors on the same topic differ | Analyzes conflicting information across sources; evaluates the soundness of complex arguments | CCSS RI.6.2–9, RI.7.2–9, RI.8.2–9 |
| High (9–12) | Determines central ideas + provides objective summary; delineates and evaluates reasoning/rhetoric (incl. seminal U.S. texts); integrates and evaluates multiple sources in different media to address a question | Analyzes how style/content advance an author's purpose; evaluates rhetoric for fallacies and effectiveness | CCSS RI.9-10.2–9, RI.11-12.2–9 |

### Research & Citation Skills (Writing/Reading integration)

| Age band | Typical | Advanced (12mo ahead) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lower Elem (K–2) | Participates in shared research/writing; gathers information from provided sources to answer a question; recalls information from experiences | Locates information across multiple provided sources; begins to note where facts come from | CCSS W.K.7–8, W.1.7–8, W.2.7–8 |
| Upper Elem (3–5) | Conducts short research projects; takes notes, sorts evidence into categories, paraphrases; draws on several sources and lists them | Investigates different aspects of a topic across multiple sources; provides a basic list of sources | CCSS W.3.7–8, W.4.7–9, W.5.7–9 |
| Middle (6–8) | Conducts short and sustained research; gathers from multiple print/digital sources, assesses credibility, quotes/paraphrases avoiding plagiarism, follows a standard citation format (e.g., MLA) | Refines a self-generated research question; synthesizes conflicting sources | CCSS W.6.7–8, W.7.7–8, W.8.7–8 |
| High (9–12) | Conducts sustained research to answer/solve a self-generated question; gathers authoritative sources, assesses usefulness, integrates selectively to maintain flow and avoid over-reliance on one source; cites accurately (standard format) | Synthesizes multiple authoritative sources into an original argument; evaluates source bias and limitations | CCSS W.9-10.7–8, W.11-12.7–8 |

## Recent research

- [International Dyslexia Association — Scarborough's Reading Rope infographic](https://dyslexiaida.org/scarboroughs-reading-rope-a-groundbreaking-infographic/) — The Rope braids Word Recognition (phonological awareness, decoding, sight recognition — increasingly *automatic*) with Language Comprehension (background knowledge, vocabulary, language structures, verbal reasoning, literacy knowledge — increasingly *strategic*), explaining the PreK–2 → 3–12 shift from code-based to meaning-based emphasis.
- [Lexia — Full Breakdown of the Science of Reading Components](https://www.lexialearning.com/blog/a-full-breakdown-of-the-science-of-reading-components) — Decoding proceeds phonology → orthography → morphology; vocabulary and morphological knowledge become primary comprehension levers in the upper grades, which is why L-strand (affix/root) milestones scale sharply after grade 3.
- [Reading Rockets — Fluency Norms Chart, 2017 Update (Hasbrouck & Tindal)](https://www.readingrockets.org/topics/fluency/articles/fluency-norms-chart-2017-update) — Spring 50th-percentile WCPM: Gr1 ~60, Gr2 ~100, Gr3 ~112, Gr4 ~133, Gr5 ~146, Gr6 ~146. Growth is steepest in early elementary and plateaus around grade 6; a large fluency gap in middle/high school signals an unresolved decoding issue.
- [CCSS — Reading: Literature strand](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RL/) — Literary comprehension spirals from "identify the central message/lesson" (K–2) → "determine a theme and summarize" (3–5) → "analyze theme development and how incidents reveal character" (6–8) → "analyze two or more interacting themes and an author's structural choices" (9–12).
- [CCSS — Reading: Informational Text, Grade 8 (RI.8)](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RI/8/) — By grade 8, close reading includes delineating and evaluating an argument, assessing whether the reasoning is sound and the evidence relevant and sufficient — a measurable jump in critical-reading demand over grades 3–5.
- [CCSS — Writing, Grade 7 (W.7.7–8, research & citation)](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/W/7/) — Formal source evaluation and standard-format citation (MLA) enter as explicit standards in grades 6–8; elementary research is scaffolded through shared/short projects with provided sources.
- [CCSS — Language, Grade 6 (L.6.4–5, morphology)](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/L/6/) — Greek/Latin affixes and roots become an explicit decoding-and-vocabulary tool from grade 3 up (e.g., audience/auditory/audible), directly reflecting the Science-of-Reading morphology strand.
- [Reading Rockets — The Simple View of Reading (Gough & Tunmer, 1986)](https://www.readingrockets.org/topics/about-reading/articles/simple-view-reading) — RC = D × LC. The "strong decoder / developing comprehender" profile common in precocious early readers implies investing in language comprehension (vocabulary, background knowledge, dialogic reading) rather than more phonics.
