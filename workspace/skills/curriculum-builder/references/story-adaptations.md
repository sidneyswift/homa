# Child-Specific Adaptations

_Fill this in during onboarding based on the child's age, abilities, and preferences._

## Motor Skills
- **Writing:** For young children (under 4), skip written exercises. Do verbally or with gross motor (air writing, chalk on easel, finger paint)
- **Scissors:** Include as practice (common growth area) but always offer a tear-paper alternative
- **Drawing tools:** Thick markers, oil pastels, or chunky crayons over standard crayons for younger kids
- **Grip:** Note the child's current grip development

## Attention & Energy
- **Activity length:** Note typical focus duration per activity type
- **Movement breaks:** Build movement INTO the lesson
- **Choice:** Always offer 2-3 activity options — parent picks based on child's energy
- **Resistance:** If the child resists, pivot. Don't force it.

## Learning Style
- _Observe and note: visual, auditory, kinesthetic, or mixed?_
- _What activities does the child gravitate toward?_
- _What does the child resist?_

## Sensory Considerations
- _Any tactile sensitivities? (dirty hands, slime, textures)_
- _Sound sensitivities?_
- _Note what accommodations work._

## Assessment
- No written tests for young children. Ever.
- **Vocabulary checks:** Verbal — "Can you tell me what a portrait is?"
- **Portfolio:** Take photos of finished work for Obsidian
- **Observation:** Note what the child gravitates toward vs. resists
