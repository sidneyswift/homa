# Child-Specific Adaptations (Age 3.5)

## Motor Skills
- **Writing:** Skip written exercises. Do verbally or with gross motor (air writing, chalk on easel, finger paint)
- **Scissors:** Include as practice (growth area) but always offer a tear-paper alternative
- **Drawing tools:** Thick markers, oil pastels, or chunky crayons over standard crayons
- **Grip:** Tripod grip developing — don't force it, offer variety

## Attention & Energy
- **Activity length:** 15-20 min max per activity. 5 min for Look & Talk.
- **Movement breaks:** Build movement INTO the lesson (line hunt, body shapes, dancing)
- **Choice:** Always offer 2-3 activity options — the parent picks based on the child's energy
- **Resistance:** If the child resists, pivot. Don't force it. Especially handwriting.

## Learning Style
- **Visual first:** the child is a visual learner. Always include images/artwork to look at together.
- **Kinesthetic:** Hands-on and movement-based activities work best
- **Repetition:** He loves repeating activities and rereading books — build this in
- **Kitchen science:** Experiments and cooking activities are always a hit
- **Social:** Prefers working with the parent over independent work

## Sensory Considerations
- **Tactile sensitivity:** Doesn't like dirty hands or slime. Offer gloves for messy activities or provide clean alternatives.
- **Finger painting:** May resist — try sponge painting or brush painting first. Boafo-style finger painting is aspirational, not guaranteed.

## Assessment
- No written tests. Ever.
- **Vocabulary checks:** Verbal — "Can you tell me what a portrait is?"
- **Portfolio:** Take photos of finished work for Obsidian
- **Observation:** Note what the child gravitates toward vs. resists

## Interests (for connections)
- Paw Patrol (Skye, Marshall, Chase)
- Insects (ant farm!)
- Animals broadly
- Monster trucks
- Mondrian (art)
- Space / Buzz Lightyear
- Pete the Cat, Splat the Cat, Mo Willems books
- Dinosaurs
- Building / constructing

## What to Avoid
- Worksheets with small spaces (fine motor frustration)
- Long seated activities (>20 min)
- Activities requiring clean/precise output (perfectionism trigger)
- Slime, goo, or heavily messy sensory activities
- Forcing handwriting when he's not in the mood
