🔬 Science Center
⏱ 30 min
Chameleon Habitat
Tell children to review the chameleon's habitat. Then have them use the natural materials to assemble a mock habitat for a chameleon. Suggest that they cut out flowers and trees from construction paper and cut out a chameleon for the habitat as well, showing how the chameleon blends in with its surroundings. Talk about how blending in with its surroundings might help the chameleon survive.
🦎🌿
Materials
Pupil Edition shoe boxes (one per child) real leaves and twigs sand construction paper
📚 Literacy Center
⏱ 15 min
Changing Words
Write several initial consonants and blends on different colors of construction paper. Cut out two chameleon shapes from colored acetate. On one cutout write the phonogram -ide. On the other, write -id. Have children place the cutouts on the construction paper with consonants, blends, and digraphs to try different combinations to build and read words. Have children record some of the words they build in their journals.
🦎✏️📝
Materials
chameleon cutouts from colored acetate poster with initial consonants and blends Word Building journals pencils
💻 Technology Center
⏱ 15 min
Chameleon Composing
Have children use word processing software to compose sentences at the computer. Encourage them to write sentences to compare how the chameleon in the story is different from a real chameleon. Have children use the spell-check feature of the software.
💻📝
Materials
computer or tablet word processing software
🗺️ Social Studies Center
⏱ 30 min
Zoo Map
Tell children to review all the animals the chameleon saw at the zoo. Have each child draw a map for an imaginary zoo like the one in the story. Make sure children format their maps on letter and number grids so someone could use the map to find the flamingo pond or the elephant area.
🗺️🦩🐘
Materials
construction paper crayons or markers Pupil Edition
🎭 Drama Center
⏱ 15 min
A Day in the Life of a Chameleon
Give children party blowouts and fly cutouts. Have them use the party blowouts as tongues as they pretend to be chameleons trying to catch flies. Encourage them to engage in dialogue and act out other chameleon behaviors also, such as changing colors.
🎭🦎
Materials
party blowouts (one per child) fly cutouts
🏠 School–Home Connection
Your child is reading "The Mixed-Up Chameleon" by Eric Carle. Ask your child to tell you about the story. Then have your child use each vocabulary word in a sentence about the story. You can also ask your child to draw a picture of a chameleon and label it with descriptive words from the story.
