Day 1 — Introduce
"The Mixed-Up Chameleon" · Week of July 21
Total time: ~2 hours
7 activity blocks
📖 Read-Aloud
15 min
  1. Read "Only a Toad" aloud together. Use different voices for the characters — make the toad grumpy and the other animals proud.
  2. After reading, ask: "What was the toad's problem? How did he solve it?"
  3. Talk about a time she wanted to be something different. "Have you ever wished you could be like someone else?"
🚀 If she breezes through Ask her to retell the story in her own words. Then connect: "How is the toad like a chameleon? We'll find out this week!"
🌱 If she needs more support Focus on the pictures first. Ask "what's happening here?" for each page before reading the words together.
Materials: "Only a Toad" story (or read online)
🔤 Phonics
15 min
  1. Write these word pairs on a whiteboard: hid / hide, rid / ride, slid / slide. Read them together.
  2. Ask: "What do you notice about these words? What's the same? What's different?" Guide her to see the silent e pattern.
  3. Sort word cards into two columns: -id words and -ide words. She places each card and reads it aloud.
🚀 If she breezes through Add blends: grid, pride, bride. Can she hear the pattern in longer words? Try making up silly -ide words.
🌱 If she needs more support Start with just 3 words total. Sound out each one together slowly. Use fingers to tap each sound.
Materials: Whiteboard + markers, word cards (print or write on index cards)
✏️ Spelling Pretest
10 min
  1. Say each word clearly. Use it in a sentence. Say the word again. She writes it.
  2. Words: hid, rid, kid, lid, slid, hide, ride, side, wide, slide
  3. Check together. Circle any she missed — those are her focus words this week.
💡 This is diagnostic, not graded. Celebrate what she already knows! The words she misses become her personal focus list for the week.
Materials: Lined paper, pencil
📚 Reading
20 min
  1. Read the decodable books together: "Sid the Salamander" and "Ben and Sid." These are practice books with lots of -id words.
  2. Let her read as much as she can. Help with tricky words by pointing to the sounds — don't just say the word for her.
  3. After each book, ask one question: "What happened in this story?" Listen for her to retell the beginning, middle, and end.
🚀 If she breezes through Have her read one book independently, then tell you about it. Ask: "Which character did you like? Why?"
🌱 If she needs more support Echo-read: you read a sentence, she reads the same sentence right after you. Build confidence before independent reading.
Materials: Decodable readers (or similar books with short -id / -ide words)
✍️ Writing
15 min
  1. Ask: "What's your favorite topic to read about? Dinosaurs? Space? Animals? Princesses?"
  2. Help her write 2–3 sentences about her favorite topic. She can draw a picture alongside her writing.
  3. Read her sentences back to her. Point out what she did well: "You started with a capital letter!" Celebrate the writing.
🚀 If she breezes through Ask her to add one detail: "What's the coolest thing about [topic]?" Push for a third or fourth sentence.
🌱 If she needs more support She dictates, you write. Then she copies one sentence below yours. The goal is ideas first, handwriting second.
Materials: Lined paper, pencils, crayons
📝 Grammar
10 min
  1. Write on the whiteboard: "The chameleon" — ask: "Is this a sentence? Why not?"
  2. Now write: "The chameleon changed colors." — ask: "Is THIS a sentence? What makes it different?"
  3. Together, discover the rule: a sentence needs a naming part (who or what) and a telling part (what happened).
🚀 If she breezes through Give her 3 word groups and ask her to sort: sentence or not a sentence? Let her explain WHY for each.
🌱 If she needs more support Act it out! "I am the naming part" (stand up). "Now YOU be the telling part" (do an action). Make it physical.
Materials: Whiteboard + markers
🔬 Science Center — Chameleon Habitat
30 min
  1. Talk about where chameleons live. Look at pictures online or in a book together. What do they see? Trees, leaves, warm weather.
  2. Build a mini habitat in a shoe box: use real leaves, twigs, and sand for the ground. Add flowers from construction paper.
  3. Cut out a chameleon from green construction paper. Place it in the habitat. Can she make it blend in?
  4. Discuss: "Why do chameleons change color? How does blending in help them stay safe?"
Materials: Shoe box, real leaves + twigs, sand, construction paper, scissors, glue, crayons
