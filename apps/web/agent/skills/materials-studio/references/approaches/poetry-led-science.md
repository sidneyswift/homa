# Poetry-led science (roughly ages 4 to 7)

- Poem or story opens every lesson, not a textbook heading. The child meets a short rhyme, a character-driven narrative, or a playful read-aloud before encountering any science vocabulary. The literary form carries the concept in; the concept does not announce itself.
- Concrete-to-abstract progression through narrative. A story about Marisol noticing plants in her yard leads to "what do plants need?" A poem about a zebra drinking water leads to "what do animals need to survive?" The child reasons from a character's experience, not from a definition.
- Three literacy stages scaffold the science content. Read-aloud (teacher models), shared reading (teacher and child together), independent reading (child alone). Each stage revisits the same science concept at increasing depth while simultaneously building fluency, comprehension, and vocabulary.
- Science vocabulary emerges from context, never from a word list. Terms like "necesidades," "refugio," "luz del sol" appear inside poems and stories first. The child hears them in a meaningful sentence before seeing them isolated. Vocabulary cards and activities come after, not before, the literary encounter.
- Cross-curricular by design, not by extension. The same lesson teaches reading comprehension (identify important details), oral language (discuss with a partner), writing (respond to a prompt), and science (what living things need). These are not four separate activities bolted together; the poem or story is the single hub that connects them.
- Informational text as a genre lesson. After poems introduce the concept playfully, the unit shifts to informational text ("texto informativo") about the same topic. The child learns to distinguish genres (story vs. informational) while deepening their science understanding. The science content stays constant; the literary form changes.
- Personalization through character connection. Stories feature characters the child can identify with (Marisol, animal parents and babies). The child is asked how the character's experience relates to their own observations. This is the same personalized-question technique seen across other Aless drops, applied to science content.
- Spanish-language implementation. The Benchmark "Taller del Lector" series demonstrates this approach in full Spanish immersion, proving the technique works for bilingual and dual-language households.
