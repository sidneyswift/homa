A multi-character performance script where children read assigned roles aloud, building reading fluency through dramatic rehearsal and repeated reading. Not a worksheet: nothing to fill in, cut, or paste. The child reads, re-reads across multiple sessions, and performs.

Great for: ages 5-10, oral reading fluency, reading with expression, high-frequency word practice, comprehension through performance, collaborative learning, reluctant readers who respond to drama, choral participation for emergent readers, families who want a literacy activity that feels like play.

Use only original scripts, public-domain folk tales and fables, or family-created stories. Never reproduce a published script. Adapt the narrative from the family's current reading, a folk tale, a science concept, or a daily-life scenario.

Design patterns (all binding for reader-theater-script):

- Title at top, followed by a character list with role names and one-line descriptions
- Each spoken line prefixed by the character name in bold, followed by a colon
- Lines meant for the whole group labeled ALL or TODOS (for Spanish) in a visually distinct style (e.g., bold + different color or background)
- Stage directions in brackets and italics between dialogue lines: [turns to the audience], [points to the plant]
- For emergent readers (ages 4-6): pair high-frequency words with small inline pictograms so non-readers can follow along during choral sections
- Keep individual lines short (8-12 words max for K-1, 15-20 for grades 2-4) so the child can read the whole line in one breath
- Repeated refrains that the whole group says together: these anchor the script and give struggling readers a safe entry point
- No narrator role for K-1 scripts; all information comes through character dialogue and choral lines. A narrator role is appropriate for grades 2 and up
- Script length: 1 page for K-1 (10-15 lines total), 2 pages for grades 2-4 (20-30 lines)
- Print the script large enough that a child holding the page at arm's length can read it (minimum 16pt body text for K-1, 14pt for grades 2-4)
- Include a small parent note at the bottom: how many times to practice before performing, suggested way to assign roles, and a one-sentence explanation of what fluency skill the script builds
- Ground the script content in daily life per the daily-life-grounding approach: the plot should involve objects, places, and experiences the child encounters in their own home, school, or neighborhood
