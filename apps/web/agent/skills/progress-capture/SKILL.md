# Capture a reported ability

Keep ordinary reports easy. Save what the parent actually observed, without
turning every lesson or day recap into an assessment. The current parent
message is evidence; Homa's preceding question helps interpret it but cannot
supply an accomplishment the parent never reported.

## Resolve the report

1. Identify the child from the parent's current words and the conversation
   they are answering. A family-validated page selection is a useful hint,
   not a reason to overwrite an explicitly named sibling. If two children
   could fit a pronoun, ask who she means before recording for either.
2. Separate activity from ability. Time spent, a lesson number, finishing a
   worksheet or moving on does not establish mastery. Planner handles a
   reported dated activity. A mixed message can also contain a real ability
   claim; keep that clause instead of discarding it because she also asked
   a question or requested the next activity.
3. Use record_journal_evidence for that resolved child. The server reads the
   current canonical message and available private conversation context,
   proposes catalog claims with a model, and verifies them. Do not rewrite
   her fragment as a fuller quote, invent a catalog item, or submit a prior
   assistant sentence as something she said. An active check-in answer goes
   through progress-check-in's existing bound-question operation instead.

## Clarify only when it matters

An emerging ability is useful evidence. Help, slowness or inconsistency does
not mean nothing happened, and it does not mean independently easy. Capture
what is already supported without demanding a demonstration or a longer
answer merely to strengthen it.

Ask at most one short clarification when a missing fact would change a
supported claim, such as which child she means, what she observed, or whether
she helped. A bare yes after several questions is not enough to select one.
A parent saying a child cannot yet do something is meaningful negative
evidence; a parent declining more questions is not evidence that the child
cannot do it. Honor the decline and stop probing.

Do not interrogate time-only, lesson-number-only or moving-on reports. If she
is asking whether moving on means mastery, explain the distinction and offer
one optional observation question. Do not convert a request for advice or a
suggested activity into a completed performance.

## Read the receipt

- A progress update confirms only the abilities in that receipt. A saved
  answer may leave the displayed number unchanged; never promise movement.
- An observation/no-match receipt means her words were saved without a
  scored ability update. Say that plainly. It may reflect catalog coverage,
  age validation, ambiguity or an unavailable extraction; do not diagnose
  the cause from that generic result or repeatedly call it hoping to score.
- A conflict, refusal or unavailable result keeps its existing meaning.
  Follow its offered action, without implying that the correction succeeded.

A no-match result alone is not a reason to ask another question. Clarify only
if the conversation identifies one material ambiguity, and never repeat a
question she has declined. Do not manufacture a lower catalog match merely
because the described ability is outside the supported catalog or age bound.

When a recap names several children, preserve each child's specific report.
The tool can fan out validated claims; do not call it repeatedly for the same
message or give siblings the same accomplishment from a vague family activity.
Do not create a plan, printable, schedule, or reminder without its separate
request. Requested next steps belong to progress-next-move and its verifier.

## Examples and final check

"Yes, ask more questions. She can skip alternating legs but slowly" can
contain both a request and an emerging ability, when the child is clear.
"She can do all of that" depends on what Homa actually just described.
"We finished lesson 12" is not a competence rating. "Not more questions,
please" ends clarification; it does not record not_yet.

Before replying: the child is resolved, the quote is from this current parent
message, limits and help remain intact, the tool's receipt supports every save
claim, and any clarification is singular and useful rather than automatic.
