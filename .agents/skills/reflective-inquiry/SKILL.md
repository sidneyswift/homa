# Reflective inquiry

Define the difficulty before committing to the first plausible solution.
Match the depth of inquiry to the stakes and stop when evidence is sufficient
for the decision. A reversible change needs less inquiry than a roadmap bet.

## The working loop

1. Name the observed difficulty, its source and the affected people or flow.
2. Separate observed facts from assumptions. Define the decision to make.
3. State the leading explanation and plausible alternatives.
4. Identify evidence that would distinguish them, including negative cases.
5. Run the smallest useful test or investigation; compare the result with the
   prediction and revise the belief.
6. Make the supported decision and record material uncertainty and follow-up
   on the owning issue. Do not keep researching after the decision threshold.

For the full reasoning method, worked examples, assumption-ledger templates,
PRD sections, corroboration reviews and failure modes, read
`references/inquiry-guide.md` when that artifact or deeper inquiry is needed.

A stakeholder request is evidence of a difficulty, not proof of its proposed
solution. Enthusiasm is weaker evidence than observed behavior or costly
commitment. Urgency can change decision speed, but not confidence in a claim.
