# Onboarding cadence · the ask/give rhythm

The one idea: users tolerate extraction in proportion to what visibly
comes back. Great onboarding is not shorter than bad onboarding, it is
better paced. Duolingo asks plenty and Headspace asks plenty; neither
ever lets an answer disappear into a void. Our own first draft of the
mobile onboarding failed exactly here: seven extraction screens in a
row before the first give-back. The structure was right, the rhythm
was wrong.

## Beat vocabulary

Label every screen (or chat turn) with one beat:

- **ASK** · extracts something: a tap, a name, a form, a permission.
- **GIVE** · returns something: a reflection derived from an answer, a
  promise, a possession, a celebration, a moment of pure delight.
- **FRAME** · sets expectations before an ask run: how many questions,
  why they are worth answering, what comes after.
- **DO** · real product value happening right now, not described.
- **OFFER** · optional value the user can decline without penalty
  (widget, contacts, notifications). Always has a visible no.

A flow written as a beat string can be read at a glance:
`G G A A G G A G A F DO A A A G` (Duolingo) versus
`A A A A A A A G G G` (our failed draft).

## The laws

1. **Never open with an ask.** Spend two or three beats giving before
   extracting anything. Duolingo opens with the owl saying hello.
   Headspace makes you take one literal breath with the orb before its
   first question. The door can be an account gate and still open warm:
   promise first, buttons second.

2. **Ask runs cap at three beats.** Then something must come back.
   Back-to-back questionnaires are fine and everyone uses them, but
   only mid-flow (never at the door), always framed, always paid off.

3. **Metabolize every answer visibly.** The give-back must derive from
   the specific answer just given, not be generic praise. "How much
   French do you know?" is followed by "Since you know a few words,
   let's start at Score 10!". An answer that is never reflected back
   was extraction; an answer the product visibly uses was investment.

4. **Count the questions and say why.** Cleo: "Be honest in the next
   5 qs, Trisha. The more I know, the better I can help you." Duolingo:
   "Just 7 quick questions before we start your first lesson!"
   Duolingo ABC prints the reason under every single ask ("We'll
   personalize the experience for this age"). Named cost plus named
   reason turns a survey into a deal.

5. **A character hosts every ask.** Duo's speech bubble is on every
   screen, so four questions in a row still read as conversation.
   Headspace's orb smiles through the whole quiz. For Homa the host is
   Homa herself; a question she asks in her own voice is dialog, the
   same question in a bare form label is bureaucracy.

6. **Give a possession early.** Finch hatches the birb before asking
   anything. Duolingo ABC gives the child a character companion during
   setup. Notion turns interest picks into real workspace pages. The
   user should own something almost immediately, and each further
   answer should visibly improve the thing they own (a child card
   assembling as the parent answers is this law in action).

7. **Reciprocity beats interrogation.** Finch's bird asks for your
   name only after you named it: "Thanks for hatching me. If my name
   is Lee, what's your name?" When Homa must ask something personal,
   let her give something personal first.

8. **Celebrate heavy completions, briefly.** "Welcome, Sam Lee! Your
   profile has been successfully created." "You made it, Trisha. Your
   overview's ready." Two seconds of warmth, then move. The celebration
   lands hardest immediately after the heaviest ask (Headspace places
   its breathing moment right after the account form).

9. **The payoff is an artifact, not a message.** Ask runs resolve into
   something real and inspectable: a placement, a plan, a reveal that
   quotes the user's own answers and shows its work ("For Calm
   Explorers like you · why this recommendation"). Homa's equivalents:
   the worksheet, the child card, the week plan.

10. **Escape hatches on everything non-load-bearing.** Later, skip,
    "let me explore first". Commitment is invited, never demanded, and
    an OFFER declined costs the user nothing visible.

11. **Pre-frame the sensitive, wear opinions openly.** Lemonade
    disarms health questions before asking ("this won't affect your
    price, it's just to help you understand"). Duolingo, Headspace,
    and Cleo put a Recommended chip inside their own questions. When
    Homa has an opinion, she says so in the ask; when a question could
    feel like a test of the child, she disarms it first.

12. **Sell a permission with the message itself.** Strava and ChatGPT
    preview the actual notification as a rendered push bubble; Liven
    names the button by the benefit ("Remind me to practice"); BeReal
    promises exactly one notification and keeps the promise. Never a
    bare system dialog, never a generic bell illustration doing the
    arguing.

## The bar: what award-winning actually has

Scored against the top decile of the 60-app corpus, a merely correct
cadence is table stakes. The flows people remember all add, on top of
the twelve laws:

- **A named host with a face.** Duo, Maya, the birb, the Headspace
  orb. Not a brand tone, a character the user could describe. Homa
  already is one; the flow must let her speak on every screen.
- **One physical ritual.** A hold, a signature, a pledge button. The
  body remembers what the thumb did (see references/moments.md,
  rituals).
- **Honest theater at the wait.** Real steps named truthfully, with
  at most one wink (Lemonade's "Feeding Fluffy"). The wait is a
  stage, never a lie.
- **The charmed echo.** The payoff artifact describes the user's own
  facts with editorial warmth ("A ridiculously cute, 3-year-old Shiba
  Inu from New York"). Template praise is the tell of a mediocre flow.
- **Celebrations with a face and a subject.** Confetti made of the
  thing accomplished (Speak rains letters), or a kept collectible
  (Mindvalley's dated Day One medal). Bare confetti is wrapping paper
  on an empty box.
- **Permissions that keep promises.** The preview shown is a message
  the app really sends, at the promised frequency.

If a flow has the twelve laws but none of these, it is competent.
Award-winning needs at least three, executed in the product's own
voice, placed at the moments of highest emotion (after the heaviest
ask, at the payoff, at completion).

## How to apply

1. Write the flow as a beat string before arguing about screens.
2. Find every ask run longer than three. Break it with a metabolized
   give-back, or compress the asks onto one screen (Headspace puts its
   whole account form on a single screen rather than spreading the
   pain thin).
3. Check the openings: the flow's first two beats, and the first beat
   after any heavy ask, should be gives.
4. Check every ASK for a why-line and every run for a FRAME with a
   count. If the count would embarrass you, the run is too long.
5. Check the payoffs: does each run resolve into an artifact that
   quotes the user's answers? Generic praise fails law 3.
6. Homa specifics: the chat-first onboarding brain decides what to
   ask; the surface (native screens, web chat) owns the beats between.
   Give-backs speak in Homa's voice, which means the copy laws in the
   root AGENTS.md and homa-copy-writer apply (reassure before inform,
   warm with a little sass, never a verdict on a child). Cleo's
   profanity and Fabulous's cinematic monologues are outside Homa's
   register; steal their mechanics, not their volume.

## Anti-patterns, each observed in the wild or in our own drafts

- Seven asks in a row before the first give (our mobile wireframe v3).
- Generic praise as fake metabolism ("Great choice!" derived from
  nothing). If the give-back would read the same for any answer, it
  is not a give-back.
- A "building your plan" interstitial over no real work, or one that
  lies about what it is doing. The wait must be honest and the steps
  named (models-judge-code-verifies honesty applies to theater too).
- Delight sprinkled as decoration between every ask. The breath lands
  because it is placed after the heaviest extraction, not everywhere.
- Counting the questions and then exceeding the count.
- An OFFER with no visible decline, which is just an ASK wearing a bow.
