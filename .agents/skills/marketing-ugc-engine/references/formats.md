# Content formats

## 8 proven content types (adapted for Homa)

### 1. Discovery confession
Mom thought one thing ("she hates math"), found the real problem was
different (wrong level). The strongest format for Homa because it's
authentic and maps directly to the product promise.

### 2. Before/after reveal
Split screen or sequence: struggling with wrong-level material vs
thriving at the right level. Show worksheets/screens, never children.

### 3. "Nobody told me" series
"Nobody warned me homeschool curriculum could skip entire skill gaps."
Positions Homa as the thing moms wish they'd known about sooner.

### 4. Comment funnel
"Comment your kid's grade + hardest subject and I'll tell you what I'd
check first." Seeds the reply loop, drives engagement, builds community.

### 5. Unboxing/first experience
First time using Homa. Genuine reaction to seeing the assessment results.
Focus on the mom's face and emotions, never the child.

### 6. Tutorial/tip
Quick tip format: "3 signs the lesson is the wrong level." Educational
value that naturally leads to Homa as the solution.

### 7. Duet/stitch response
React to another creator's curriculum advice with "but first, check the
level." Riding existing conversations.

### 8. Seasonal/timely
"Before August curriculum shopping, check the level." Time-bound hooks
that create urgency.

## The 15-second formula

```
0-2s:   Hook — stop the scroll
2-4s:   Problem/context — "that's me" moment
3-7s:   Introduce product naturally
7-12s:  Demonstrate payoff with visual proof
12-15s: CTA (comment prompt, link in bio, follow)
```

## The 0.5-second rule

The first 0.5 seconds determines 90% of performance.

**What works:**
- On-screen text (large, readable)
- Movement (picking up a worksheet, turning to camera)
- Direct question ("Is your kid really at grade level?")
- Surprising stat ("70% of homeschool moms guess the wrong level")

**What kills it:**
- "Hey guys" / slow greeting
- Slow pan or zoom
- Logo intro
- Dead silence
- Corporate-looking title card

## Homa format constraints

- No child faces, names, or voices in any format
- Confession > demo (the moment it becomes a walkthrough, engagement dies)
- Proof is artifacts: worksheets, screen recordings, assessment results
- Never lead with "AI" — lead with the human discovery
- End on a comment prompt, not a hard sell
