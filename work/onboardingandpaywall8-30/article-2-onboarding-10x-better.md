# How to make your Onboarding 10x better (7 Practical Tips)

We've designed hundreds of app experiences, and one thing we've learned is
that good onboarding isn't about explaining every feature.

It's about helping users experience the value of the app as quickly as
possible.

Here's how:

## 1. Define the activation moment

First, decide what action makes users understand the value of your app.

For a fitness app, it could be completing their first workout. For a
budgeting app, it could be connecting their bank account.

Design the entire onboarding around helping users reach that moment
faster.

*Fitness App — designed by kree8.studio: this onboarding guides users
through only the information needed to create a personalised health plan,
then leads them directly to the activation moment: starting their first
mission.*

## 2. Show value before asking for effort

Don't immediately ask users to create an account, enable notifications,
enter personal details, and subscribe.

First, show them what the app can do for them.

The more effort you ask from users, the more clearly you need to
communicate the value they'll receive.

*Regen App — designed by kree8.studio.*

## 3. Ask only useful questions

Personalization can improve activation, but only when the answers
actually change the experience.

Ask about the user's goals, preferences, or challenges and then show how
their answers have shaped the app.

Users should feel like the onboarding was made for them, not like they
are filling out a form.

## 4. Keep one clear action per screen

Every onboarding screen should have one clear purpose.

Avoid long paragraphs, multiple decisions, and too many buttons on the
same screen.

Users should instantly understand what is being asked, why it matters,
and what they need to do next.

*Walkify app — designed by kree8.studio.*

## 5. Make it animated and interesting

Onboarding is the user's first interaction with your product, so don't
make it feel like a boring questionnaire.

Use purposeful animations, interactive selections, satisfying
transitions, illustrations, and subtle haptics.

Motion should make the experience easier to understand and more
enjoyable — not slow users down.

*Anything app — designed by kree8.studio.*

## 6. Make it personal

Use the user's answers to turn generic information into something that
feels specific to them.

For example, in Didit's onboarding, we ask the user's age and use it to
estimate how many years of their life they could spend on their phone.
The same statistic suddenly feels personal, emotional, and much harder to
ignore.

Personalisation helps users feel understood and gives them a stronger
reason to continue.

*Didit app — designed by kree8.studio.*

## 7. End with a meaningful result

Don't finish onboarding with a generic "You're all set" screen followed
by an empty dashboard.

Show users what was created for them: their personalised plan, first
recommendation, projected progress, or next action.

A strong onboarding ends with value — and gives users a clear reason to
continue.

Great onboarding doesn't teach users everything.

It removes uncertainty, creates excitement, and helps users experience
one meaningful result as quickly as possible.
