# Copilot Money: onboarding + paywall

Source: Appllama MCP, app_id `1447330651`. Revenue $300K/mo, 30K downloads, 4.75★ (30,042 ratings), #6 Finance (US). Category: Finance / Productivity. Apple Editor's Choice, Apple Design Award Finalist. Subtitle: "Spending, investing, net worth."

Copilot is the closest structural analog we've researched to Homa's actual decision: a parent isn't deciding whether to try a fun app, she's deciding whether to trust an unknown company with the same category of access she'd give her bank. Every other app in this research set (fitness, education, photo, meditation) asks for impulse trust ("will I use this") while Copilot has to clear "will I hand this company read access to my checking account, credit cards, and net worth." Its onboarding visibly restructures around that fact: two full screens dedicated to account security (Face ID + two-factor authentication) come *before* a single bank is linked, the phrase "we don't sell your financial data" is repeated verbatim as a persistent header across the entire linking flow, and "read-only access" is stated explicitly rather than left implied. Rocket Money ($600K/mo, higher-revenue but no captured paywall flow) was also researched and shows the identical pattern (a bottom-sheet security-info modal appears inside the Plaid connection step itself), confirming this isn't one company's idiosyncrasy but the category norm for money apps specifically.

## Paywall (1 screen)

| # | Screen | Notes |
| - | --- | --- |
| pay1 | Free Trial Paywall | Headline "Claim your free trial" under an "EDITORS CHOICE" laurel badge (an *earned* press credential, not a fabricated rating/testimonial count). The core device is a vertical **timeline, not a price tag**: "Today - Copilot will analyze your connected accounts... Δ 7 days before - you'll receive a reminder that your free trial is about to end Δ In a month - your subscription will begin, cancel anytime before." This reframes the decision from "what does this cost" to "here is exactly what happens and when," which is the paywall-level equivalent of the security screens earlier in onboarding: it substitutes procedural transparency for salesmanship. Price is shown as "Only $7.92 a month" annualized inside a bordered plan card that also states the real charge in full: "Then $95 per year of service", with both framings visible simultaneously, not just the smaller number. CTA: "Start your 1 month free trial" (trial-first, not price-first). Immediately below: "Cancel anytime, for any reason. After the free trial, this subscription automatically renews at the price selected above", auto-renewal terms stated in-line, not in fine print alone. "Restore / All Plans / Enter Referral / Skip" row gives four low-commitment exits. Below the fold: a single review card ("Simply the best," 5 stars, "This is by far the best budgeting/financial app..."), real App Store review copy used as the only social proof on the page, positioned after the commitment mechanics, not before them. |

## Verdict for our spec

- **The single most useful finding: a "what happens and when" timeline replaces a "why you should buy" pitch.** Instead of stacking benefit bullets or urgency countdowns, Copilot's paywall lays out three fixed calendar moments (today / 7 days before renewal / in a month) with plain-language descriptions of each. This directly answers the "serious decision" framing question in the brief: the mechanism for signaling "we know this is a big ask" isn't a warning or a discount, it's *removing the surprise*: showing the user, in order, every touchpoint between tapping the button and being charged. Homa's paywall could adopt this literally: a short timeline (today: full access Δ day 7: reminder before trial ends Δ day 30: card is charged) would do more trust work than a bigger badge or another feature bullet.
- **Trust is built by securing the account before asking for financial access, not by talking about security.** The Face ID / two-factor screen (position 4) and the Authenticator Setup modal (position 5) both come *before* bank linking (position 6-7). Copilot demonstrates it protects its own front door with real cryptographic tooling (TOTP, biometrics) before asking the user to trust it with a bank login. For Homa, the analog isn't literal 2FA (no bank data involved) but the sequencing principle: whatever establishes "we take security seriously" should land before the moment that actually requires trust, not during or after it.
- **The exact same reassurance sentence is repeated verbatim at every touchpoint of the sensitive ask**: "Securely give Copilot read-only access... We don't sell your financial data" appears unchanged on both bank-linking screens (6 and 7), plus "read-only" and a live Privacy & Security link on the same screen. Repetition of one fixed, legally-precise sentence (not a rotating set of taglines) is the pattern; it reads as a policy, not copywriting.
- **Real reviews, never fabricated ones, and positioned after the mechanics, not before.** The paywall's only social proof is one genuine App Store review shown below the trial timeline and pricing, consistent with Homa's own no-fabricated-testimonials constraint. The "Editors Choice" badge is likewise an earned third-party credential (Apple), not a self-reported number.
- **Two visible, low-friction exits at the highest-friction step** ("Continue without them" on the link-accounts modal; "Try demo mode" on the dashboard) signal the app isn't trying to trap the user into linking data or subscribing. A parent given an obvious way to say "not yet" is measurably more likely to come back and say "okay, now", the opposite of Homa's paywall having no visible way to decline.
- **The price is shown two ways at once, not the smaller number alone**: "$7.92 a month" is the headline badge, but "Then $95 per year of service" sits inside the same bordered card, not in a footnote. Auto-renewal language ("automatically renews at the price selected above") sits directly under the CTA in normal-weight text, not buried in a legal-only fine-print block, which is relevant to Homa's own ROSCA/CA-ARL charge-terms-near-the-button constraint.
