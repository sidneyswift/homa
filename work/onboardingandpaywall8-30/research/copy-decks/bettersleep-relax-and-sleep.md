## Reading

- First-person-plural generosity voice: the company speaks as "we" doing the user a favor at every commercial moment ("We are here for you.", "We'll remind you 2 days before your trial ends", "We offer 7 days free so everyone can try BetterSleep"). The trial is framed as inclusion ("so everyone can try"), not as a sales device, and the reminder promise lands one screen before the price does, with its own consent CTA ("Sounds good").
- The paywall itself is nearly empty (~26 words, one plan, no feature list, no visible decline) because persuasion was front-loaded: authority on the slider screen ("Doctors recommend 7 to 9 hours") and fact card ("sleep scientists from Oxford University"), and all social proof packed into the auto-advancing loader ("Trusted by over 65 million people", "4.8 stars", "APP OF THE DAY"). By the offer screen there is nothing left to argue.
- Price framing is trial-first and single-option: "$0.00 today · then renews at $69.99/year" is the only number on the screen, one line above "Try for free". No monthly/annual comparison, no strikethrough anchor, no "most popular" badge, no countdown.
- Sentences stay short and questions stay singular: one question per screen, four-ish options, every survey skippable ("Skip" top right), and primary CTAs are often an unlabeled arrow. What is omitted is as deliberate as what is said: no testimonials, no urgency copy, no discount rescue on cancellation (just "Contact Us").
