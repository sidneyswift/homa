## Flow overview

- 23 screens in the onboarding journey (5 welcome-section screens, 17 onboarding screens, 1 paywall screen), plus 15 other-tab screens (38 total in the library).
- Account gate: at the START of onboarding, immediately after the welcome screen and before any personalization questions (Onboarding positions 2-3).
- Paywall: single screen at the END of the flow, after account creation, questions, notification ask, and a guided breathing exercise (Paywall position 1). One plan, one price.
- Distinct beats: splash breathing moment (Breathe in / Breathe out), 3-dot value-prop carousel, 2 questions, a recommendations screen that doubles as the loading moment (spinner inside the CTA), notification ask, then a full guided breathing exercise before the paywall.

## Reading

- First-person-plural partnership voice: questions are phrased as joint work ("Which goals should we work toward together?") and options are completed sentence stems the user speaks in first person ("I want to... Sleep soundly"), which turns a form into a stated commitment.
- Extremely low word budgets per screen: headlines of 3-6 words, bodies of one or two short sentences, and the entire paywall carries ~35 visible words. Anything heavier (value props) is shown as a collage of real product cards instead of written out.
- Price framing: trial-first everywhere. The paywall headline is the trial ("First 14 days free"), there is exactly one plan, the annual sticker is immediately reframed per month ("$69.99 / year ($5.83/mo)"), and the CTA claims ownership of the trial ("Start my free trial") with cancel reassurance ("Cancel anytime in Settings") directly beneath.
- Notable omissions: no monthly plan or comparison table, no testimonials, star ratings, or user counts as numbers (social proof stays vague: "Loved by millions", "Join members around the world"), no urgency or countdown, and the account gate carries no persuasion copy at all — it sits before any value is shown and relies on the brand to justify itself.
