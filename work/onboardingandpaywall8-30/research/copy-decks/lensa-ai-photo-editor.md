## Reading

- Show, don't ask: onboarding asks the user nothing. Every screen is a full-bleed demo photo with a one-or-two-word headline ("Hollywood", "Smart Eraser") and a body of at most two short sentences; the images carry nearly all persuasion and the whole feature tour runs under 30 words per screen.
- The paywall sells by contrast, not adjectives: each premium benefit row carries a gray "Free: 1 photo/day / Free: Limited / Free: None" subline, so the free tier itself is the sales copy. Price appears once, plainly ($35.99 with strikethrough $99.99/year and "Save 64%"), and the 7-day trial is an opt-in toggle with the renewal disclosure ("Auto-renewing subscription starts after trial") inside the toggle card rather than in fine print.
- Compliance copy is front-loaded and granular: the first screen of the app is a two-radio legal consent (terms + photo-processing) dressed in social proof, and marketing consent appears twice with opposite polarity (opt-out on the sign-in gate, opt-in on the email form). The gate itself is soft: X to dismiss, "Sign in to continue" with no stated benefit beyond "Create more with Lensa".
- Voice is imperative second-person, zero exclamation marks, emoji as list bullets (👑 for premium rows, 😅 for "Cancel Anytime"), and even the loading state is worked copy: "The perfect shot is within reach / Lensa Pro is the missing piece" over "Breaking down the lighting layer by layer...".
