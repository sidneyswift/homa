## Reading

- The profiling flow talks in the user's first person, not the app's: each answer is replayed as a sentence fragment the user "wrote" ("I will use Goodnotes for 💼 Work", "I work in Corporate Services", "I do Engineering / Development"), each with a Change link. The survey reads like the user composing a self-description, which makes 4 B2B-style questions (use case, industry, role, company size) feel less like a marketing form; a "Why do we ask for this?" link handles the objection in four words.
- Price framing leads with the trial, not the product: the paywall headline is "Try 7 Days for $0" with a single social-proof line ("Over 30 million people use Goodnotes"), then a per-year sticker with a per-month reframe underneath ("$35.99/year" over "$3/month, billed annually"). The CTA names the plan and the free trial ("Start Your Pro Free Trial"), and "Cancel anytime" sits directly under the button. No strike-through pricing, no countdown, no discount theatrics.
- Sentences are short and functional everywhere except the two plan descriptions, which are the only benefit prose in the whole flow (9-13 words each). Feature bullets are noun fragments with counts where a claim needs one ("525 AI credits"). What is omitted: testimonials, ratings, comparison tables on the default view (pushed behind "Show all plans"), urgency, and any monthly-plan option on the first screen; annual is the only visible price.
- The account gate is absolute and silent: sign-in is screen one, offered only as OAuth ("Continue with...") with no email option, no guest mode, and no persuasion copy at all; the legal consent screen then separates the required agreement (pre-checked) from the marketing opt-in (unchecked) as two distinct rows.
