## Reading

- Second person to the parent, third person about the child by name: every question interpolates the child's name ("how many words can Morris say?", "Is Morris meeting their milestones"), so a ~20-question quiz reads as an assessment of your child, not a survey about you. Sentences are short and spoken-parent plain; the only long sentence in the whole flow is the expert quote.
- Anxiety is delegated, never voiced by the app: the scary claims arrive as a neutral statistic ("Nearly 1 in 4 kids show speech or language delay") plus a credentialed reassurance ("we can often turn things around in just a few months." — Pediatric Speech-Language Pathologist, 18+ years), and the results screen frames deficits as upside ("In 4 Months Morris can reach their full potential", "Improved areas:") rather than a verdict.
- Price is triple-framed on one screen: lead with the free window ("7 Days for Free"), reframe to the smallest number ("then $3.33/week"), then disclose the real bill in smaller type ("$14.49 billed monthly after trial"), with the trial itself re-sold twice before the paywall ("We want you to try Speech Blubs for FREE", "No Payment Due Now") and risk-reversed after ("Cancel anytime in the App Store.").
- What's omitted: no feature list or plan-comparison table anywhere (a single Monthly plan), no hard decline button on the paywall, no account creation at all, and questions never allow "I don't know" failure — the escape hatch is always soft ("Can't tell", "Maybe later", "Skip").
