## Reading

- Regulatory credibility is the voice. "CE 0123 / Cleared by FDA" is the first copy shown, efficacy numbers come with an academic citation (down to the table number), a testimonial carries a "image of model" disclosure, and the app even displays methods that outscore it (IUD 99% vs its 93%). Trust is built by conceding, not by superlatives.
- Second person, short warm sentences, every ask justified. Questions run 6-9 words with a one-line reason ("This helps us predict your future cycle patterns."), each has a no-pressure exit ("Not sure", "Haven't decided yet", Skip), and interstitials affirm rather than instruct ("You've come to the right place!").
- Price is completely absent for 45+ screens, then arrives fully loaded: three plans at once, per-month framing for the annual ($12.49/mo before $149.99/yr), the free physical thermometer as the annual clincher, and a $0 trial that states its post-trial charge in the same card. The paywall headline sells the outcome ("hormone-free birth control"), personalized with the user's name, not app features.
- What is omitted: no feature comparison table, no star ratings or download counts on the paywall, no countdown timers. Scarcity appears exactly once, softly, at the account gate ("saved for the next few minutes!"), and the paywall footer offers "delete account" as plainly as "Log out".
