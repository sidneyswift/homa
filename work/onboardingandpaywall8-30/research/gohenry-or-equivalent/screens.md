# Cozi Family Organizer: onboarding + paywall (GoHenry/Greenlight stand-in)

Source: Appllama MCP, app_id `407108860`. Revenue $400K/mo, ~40K downloads, 4.81★ (396K ratings). Category: Productivity / Lifestyle, rank #124 in US Productivity. Publisher: Cozi. Launched 2010, still updated (2026.04.17).

Neither GoHenry nor Greenlight exist in Appllama's catalog: `search_apps(query="GoHenry")` and `search_apps(query="Greenlight")` both returned zero results. Widening the search further (`"debit card for kids"`, `"allowance app for kids"`, `"kids banking app parent child"`, `"teen debit card financial literacy"`, `"Step banking teens"`, `"BusyKid"`, `"chore chart rewards for kids allowance tracker"`, `"parental control app manage child device"`) surfaced no kids'-debit-card or family-fintech app anywhere in the catalog; those queries return budgeting apps (Monarch, YNAB, EveryDollar), screen-time blockers (Opal, BePresent), and baby trackers instead. Cozi Family Organizer is the closest real app: a parent buys a family-management product, and children are set up as profiles *inside* an account the parent already owns and controls: the same buyer/onboardee split as Homa, just without a debit card attached. It is the only app across every search pass whose onboarding literally walks through parent-account-first, then add-a-child-as-a-family-member, then a family-wide paywall.

## Verdict for our spec

- **Child identity is captured strictly after the parent's own account exists, and it is optional.** The order is fixed and unambiguous: create parent account (name/email/password) → legal-guardian consent for "any user under age 13" → parent-facing welcome/testimonials → family-setup intro → parent auto-added as member #1 → *then, skippably*, "Add Family Member" for the child. A family can complete the entire onboarding and reach the paywall having added zero children. This is the cleanest real-world evidence available (in the absence of an actual GoHenry/Greenlight sample) that "parent account first, child profile second and skippable" is the default shape for this category, not an edge case.
- **Personalization and persuasion are aimed entirely at the parent, never at the child.** Every named-personalization moment ("Morris, Welcome to Cozi," the testimonial quotes, the network-diagram "we're glad you're here" copy, the "small victory" praise after the first event) addresses the adult who is setting the account up. The child is reduced to the minimum possible data footprint: first name plus a color swatch, nothing else, no separate screen of their own, no message ever speaks to or about them by name. This is a stronger, more literal version of Homa's own posture (parent as the persuaded party, child as the profile being configured) than anything found in the broader research set, and it is direct evidence against giving a child-facing screen any onboarding narrative weight at all during account setup.
- **The one warm/emotional image in the entire flow is saved for the paywall, not for child setup.** The father-and-child soccer photo appears only on the Cozi Gold offer screen, timed to make the parent feel the value of the *product* (family togetherness) right when they're asked to pay, not timed to make child-profile creation feel meaningful. For Homa, this suggests the emotional high point of onboarding is better spent adjacent to the paywall decision than "spent" on the mechanical step of entering a child's name and grade.
- **A soft upsell is seeded mid-flow, inside a functional screen, before the paywall ever appears** ("To schedule events 30+ days from now, upgrade to Cozi Gold" sitting inside the Add Event form). This primes the ceiling of the free tier through lived friction rather than through a feature list, and it lands on the parent while they are doing real work in the product: a pattern Homa could reuse once a parent is mid-task (e.g., building a lesson plan) rather than only pitching the upgrade in a dedicated paywall screen.
- **Caveat on how far this generalizes:** Cozi has no debit card, no money, and no unattended child-facing app surface at all: the child is a passive calendar/chore entry the parent manages, never someone who opens their own screen. GoHenry and Greenlight's actual structural wrinkle (a child who eventually gets *their own* app view, card, and spending choices, still supervised) is not tested by this app at all. Treat the "parent-first, child-second-and-skippable, persuasion-on-parent-only" findings above as solid, and treat anything about what happens once a child has their own surface as still unverified.
