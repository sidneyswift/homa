# Early childhood benchmarks, ages 2 to 5

> Historical research snapshot, 2026-07-06. This file preserves the evidence and analysis captured on that date. It is not current product, legal, provider, standards, or production guidance. Re-verify volatile facts and source freshness before porting any claim into Homa.

**Date:** 2026-07-06
**Lane:** Early childhood 01, part of the ages 2 to 16 national benchmark corpus (see README.md in this folder)
**Method:** Web search plus direct page and PDF fetches on 2026-07-06, going to primary sources: the CDC 2022 milestone checklists (combined PDF and live pages), the Head Start ELOF 2015 framework PDF, ASHA milestone pages, learningtrajectories.org level documents, state kindergarten readiness assessment documents, and peer-reviewed anchors (Zubler et al. 2022, Hustad et al. 2021, Crowe and McLeod 2020, Wellman et al. 2001, Piasta et al. 2012, NELP 2008, AASM 2016). Secondary clinical references (Merck consumer table, AAP HealthyChildren, pediatric OT and PT pages) fill gaps the primary frameworks do not cover.

**Key findings:**

- The CDC 2022 revision moved every milestone to a 75th percentile placement ("most children, 75% or more, do this by this age"), deliberately later than the old 50% average-age placements, so that even one missing milestone is actionable. The evidence base is Zubler et al. 2022 in Pediatrics.
- Objective intelligibility research (Hustad et al. 2021) contradicts the classic 50% at 2, 75% at 3, 100% at 4 rule of thumb: the median child reaches 50% multiword intelligibility to strangers at 34 months, 75% at 46 months, and 90% at 62 months. The paper's floor: children should be at least 50% intelligible by 48 months.
- Most English consonants are acquired by age 5;0 (Crowe and McLeod 2020, 90% criterion, 18,907 US children): plosives, nasals, glides by 3;11; v, s, z, sh, ch, j, l by 4;11; r, voiced th, zh by 5;11; voiceless th by 6;11.
- Letter knowledge: the research-optimal end-of-preschool benchmark is 18 uppercase and 15 lowercase letter names (Piasta et al. 2012), and ELOF adopted exactly that as its by-60-months indicator. State standards vary from 6 to 20 letters, no national consensus.
- Early math (Clements and Sarama): counting a set of 10 with cardinality sits at about age 4, counting to 20 plus is a 5 to 6 skill, counting to 100 about age 6; conceptual subitizing to 5 at about age 4. ELOF expects verbal counting to at least 20 by 60 months.
- Sleep (AASM 2016, AAP endorsed): 11 to 14 hours per 24 including naps for ages 1 to 2, 10 to 13 hours for ages 3 to 5.
- Toileting has a wide normal range: most US children train between 24 and 48 months, night dryness typically lands after 60 months, and at age 5 about 1 in 6 children still wets the bed (NIDDK), which the frameworks treat as within normal limits.
- Theory of mind: false-belief understanding flips from below-chance to above-chance at roughly 4 to 5 years across 178 studies (Wellman, Cross, and Watson 2001).

## 1. CDC milestone checklists, 2022 revision (24 to 60 months)

**Framework edition:** CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early." milestone checklists, February 2022 revision (in effect as of access date). Source pages: [CDC developmental milestones index](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/index.html) (accessed 2026-07-06) and the [combined printable checklist PDF](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/media/pdfs/2025/10/cdc-milestone-checklists-ltsae-english-508.pdf) (accessed 2026-07-06). CDC checklists are US government works, captured in full below.

**What changed in 2022 (FACT):** milestones are now placed at ages by which at least 75% of children would be expected to exhibit them (previously 50% average-age placement), so that even one missing milestone is actionable rather than "wait and see". Checklists were added for 15 and 30 months, giving a checklist for every AAP well-child visit from 2 months to 5 years. The evidence review and placement process is published in Zubler et al., ["Evidence-Informed Milestones for Developmental Surveillance Tools"](https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/149/3/e2021052138/184748/Evidence-Informed-Milestones-for-Developmental), Pediatrics 149(3):e2021052138, 2022 ([CDC key points page](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/key-points.html), accessed 2026-07-06).

**Reading rule:** every row below means "75% or more of children do this by this age" (FACT, quoted framing: "what most children do by this age"). CDC states the checklists are not a substitute for standardized, validated screening tools.

**Confidence: High** (primary source, full text captured).

### Intelligibility to unfamiliar listeners (FACT, objectively measured)

Hustad, Mahr, Natzke, and Rathouz, ["Speech Development Between 30 and 119 Months in Typical Children I"](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2021_JSLHR-21-00142), JSLHR 2021 (accessed 2026-07-06). Naive adult listeners orthographically transcribed speech from 538 typically developing children; ages below are model-estimated ages of reaching each threshold.

| Threshold | Multiword, 50th percentile child | Multiword, 5th percentile child | Single word, 50th percentile | Single word, 5th percentile |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 50% intelligible | 34 months | 46 months | 31 months | 46 months |
| 75% intelligible | 46 months | 61 months | 49 months | 87 months |
| 90% intelligible | 62 months | 87 months | 83 months | 120 or more months |

Key conclusions quoted from the paper (FACT): "Children should be at least 50% intelligible by 48 months." The average 36 month old was only about 50% intelligible to strangers; growth continues through 9 years for median and lower percentile children.

**INFERENCE and reconciliation note:** the classic clinical rule of thumb (about 50% intelligible to strangers at age 2, 75% at 3, 100% by age 4) comes from Coplan and Gleason 1988 parent ratings, which the Hustad group explicitly critiques; objective transcription places these thresholds substantially later. For anchoring, prefer the Hustad multiword 50th percentile row. The older rule overstates what strangers actually decode.

### English consonant acquisition (FACT)

Crowe and McLeod, ["Children's English Consonant Acquisition in the United States: A Review"](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2020_AJSLP-19-00168), AJSLP 2020, 15 studies, 18,907 US children, 90% criterion, ordered by mean age of acquisition (accessed 2026-07-06).

| Acquired during (months) | Consonants |
| --- | --- |
| 24 to 35 | b, n, m, p, h, w, d |
| 36 to 47 | g, k, f, t, ng, y |
| 48 to 59 | v, j (as in jump), s, ch, l, sh, z |
| 60 to 71 | r, voiced th (this), zh (measure) |
| 72 to 83 | voiceless th (think) |

Summary finding (FACT): most consonants are acquired by age 5;0. All plosives, nasals, and glides by 3;11; all affricates by 4;11; all liquids by 5;11; all fricatives by 6;11 (90% criterion). This echoes McLeod and Crowe 2018 across 27 languages.

## 4. Gross motor milestones by age

**Sources:** CDC 2022 checklists (75th percentile framing), AAP HealthyChildren.org milestone pages (from Caring for Your Baby and Young Child, 7th ed., 2019), Merck Manual consumer milestone table (average ages), pediatric PT references. **Confidence: High** for CDC and AAP items, **Med** for gallop and skip (fundamental-movement-skill references, not US medical bodies).

The classic "up at 3, down at 4" heuristic is consistent with, but not directly stated by, these sources.

## 7. Early literacy benchmarks

**Sources:** National Early Literacy Panel 2008 report, Piasta, Petscher, and Justice 2012, Head Start ELOF 2015 indicators, CDC 2022, ASHA. **Confidence: High** for predictors and letter benchmarks; **Med** for phonological awareness age placements (progressions are well established, exact ages vary by task).

### What predicts later reading (FACT)

The National Early Literacy Panel (NELP, 2008 report) identified six precursor skills with medium to large predictive relationships to later literacy that held after controlling for IQ and SES: alphabet knowledge (letter names and sounds), phonological awareness, rapid automatic naming of letters or digits, rapid automatic naming of objects or colors, writing or name writing, and phonological memory. Concepts about print and print knowledge were moderately correlated additional predictors. Source: [NELP executive summary](https://lincs.ed.gov/publications/pdf/NELPSummary.pdf) (accessed 2026-07-06). Alphabet knowledge correlated about .50 with later decoding across 52 studies ([Shanahan and Lonigan summary](https://www.shanahanonliteracy.com/upload/publications/52/pdf/0013189x10369172.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).

## 9. Social-emotional development

**Sources:** CDC 2022 checklists, Parten 1932 play stages (via OpenStax Lifespan Development and other summaries), Wellman, Cross, and Watson 2001 meta-analysis, ELOF 2015. **Confidence: High** for CDC and ELOF anchors and theory of mind; **Med** for play stage ages (Parten's ages are observational conventions and summaries vary by a half year).

### Play stage progression (FACT for sequence; ages are conventions)

Parten's six stages: unoccupied, solitary, onlooker, parallel, associative, cooperative. Summaries place them at: unoccupied birth to 3 months, solitary birth to 24 months, onlooker around 24 months, parallel from about 24 to 30 months (OpenStax places typical first appearance at 3.5 years, most parenting and textbook sources at 2 or older), associative 36 to 48 months, cooperative 48 months and up. Sources: [OpenStax Lifespan Development 5.5](https://openstax.org/books/lifespan-development/pages/5-5-play-in-early-childhood), [Parents.com summary](https://www.parents.com/types-of-play-6835400), [LibreTexts](https://socialsci.libretexts.org/Courses/Cerro_Coso_College/CHDV_C105_Infant_and_Toddler_Development/14%3A_Social_Development/14.07%3A_Social_Development_Through_Play) (all accessed 2026-07-06). By age 5, associative and cooperative play are the most common forms (LibreTexts citing Dyer and Moneta 2006, accessed 2026-07-06).

CDC anchors that map onto this progression (FACT): "plays next to other children and sometimes plays with them" at 30 months (parallel with emerging interaction); "notices other children and joins them to play" at 36 months; "asks to go play with children" at 48 months; "follows rules or takes turns when playing games with other children" at 60 months (all from CDC checklists, accessed 2026-07-06).

### Theory of mind (FACT)

Wellman, Cross, and Watson, ["Meta-Analysis of Theory-of-Mind Development: The Truth about False Belief"](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-8624.00304), Child Development 72(3):655-684, 2001, meta-analysis of 178 studies (accessed 2026-07-06): false-belief performance shows a consistent developmental pattern across countries and task variants, from below-chance at age 3 to above-chance by about 4 to 5, reflecting genuine conceptual change in the preschool years. The transition window is roughly 42 to 48 months in the follow-up microgenetic work (Amsterlaw and Wellman, [PDF](https://cogdevsoc.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/AmsterlawWellman.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).

## 10. Kindergarten readiness frameworks

**Confidence: High** (state primary sources). Readiness inventories measure what arriving kindergartners can do; they are administered in the first weeks of K (about 60 to 72 months old), so their domains describe the top of this lane.

### Maryland Kindergarten Readiness Assessment (KRA)

Administered statewide to incoming kindergartners in the first weeks of school; aligned to end-of-prekindergarten standards. Measures four domains: language and literacy, mathematics, social foundations, and physical well-being and motor development, combined into a single readiness score. Sources: [Maryland State Board KRA COMAR document](https://marylandpublicschools.org/stateboard/Documents/EdPolicyCommittee/20231130/Kindergarten-Readiness-Assessment-COMAR-13A.08.01.02-3-A.pdf) and [Readiness Matters 2023-2024 report](https://earlychildhood.marylandpublicschools.org/sites/default/files/files/Readiness%20Matters%20KRA%20Report%202023-2024%20(1).pdf) (both accessed 2026-07-06).

### Virginia Kindergarten Readiness Program (VKRP)

All Virginia public school kindergartners are screened fall and spring. Components: mathematics via the Early Mathematics Assessment System (EMAS, four subdomains: geometry, patterning, numeracy, computation), self-regulation and social skills via the Child Behavior Rating Scale (CBRS), and language and literacy via the Virginia Language and Literacy Screening System (VALLSS). Example indicators from the CBRS framing: listens to others, follows multistep directions, stays focused on tasks (self-regulation); cooperates in a group, expresses thoughts and emotions, resolves conflicts positively (social skills). Sources: [VDOE early childhood screening page](https://www.doe.virginia.gov/teaching-learning-assessment/early-childhood-care-education/school-readiness-and-early-childhood-assessment/school-readiness) and [VKRP measures overview PDF](https://assets.ctfassets.net/1fh9e07vdzgh/HbHr2GrOGtG5J5x415e5s/ba415efe38ed1cfcd9ac64228ef7329e/VKRP_Measures_Overview.pdf) (both accessed 2026-07-06).

### Head Start ELOF as a de facto national readiness frame

The ELOF by-60-months indicators (section 2) function as the federal preschool exit expectations: counting to 20, subitizing to 5, comparing sets to 5, naming 18 uppercase and 15 lowercase letters, producing rhymes and initial sounds, retelling stories in sequence, writing first name, self-managing routines. Source: ELOF PDF (accessed 2026-07-06).

**INFERENCE:** across these frameworks the recurring readiness domains are (1) language and literacy, (2) math, (3) social-emotional and self-regulation, (4) physical and motor, and sometimes (5) approaches to learning. This matches Homa's map domains well (literacy, phonics, math, social_emotional, executive_function, gross_motor, fine_motor, self_care).

## Dry wells and method notes

**Could not verify, deliberately left out:**

- ASQ-3 cutoff tables (the README lane description mentions ASQ-3). The Ages and Stages Questionnaires are a commercial product (Brookes Publishing); cutoff score tables are not freely published and were not scraped. A follow-up pass could cite the ASQ-3 technical report's domain structure (communication, gross motor, fine motor, problem solving, personal-social) without scores.
- MacArthur-Bates CDI vocabulary norm tables (percentile curves by month). The Wordbank open database exists but extracting percentile tables was out of timebox; the vocabulary table in section 3 therefore leans on clinical-guide numbers graded Med.
- Quantitative emotional-vocabulary counts by age (for example "names N feeling words at age 4"): no citable norm found; ELOF offers only qualitative indicators.
- A precise, primary-source age for putting on shoes, zipping, and velcro fastening. Common OT handouts say around 36 to 48 months but nothing authoritative was captured; CDC and AAP dressing items above are the verified subset.
- Tooth brushing independence: AAP lists "brushes teeth" as a 4 to 5 year milestone; the widely repeated "adults should re-brush or supervise until 7 or 8" guidance was not verified from a primary AAP or ADA page this pass, so it is marked INFERENCE in section 6.
- Zubler et al. 2022 (Pediatrics) was cited via the CDC key points page and the journal landing page, not read in full text.
- Gesell Institute norm tables are paywalled; the Gesell figures ages came from a pediatric education summary.

**Conflicts resolved (and how):**

- Intelligibility: classic Coplan and Gleason 1988 parent-report rule (50% at 2, 75% at 3, 100% at 4) vs Hustad et al. 2021 objective transcription (50% at 34 months, 75% at 46, 90% at 62, multiword, median child). Chose Hustad: larger sample, objective measurement, explicit critique of the parent-report basis. Both are recorded in section 3.
- Triangle copying: Valley Medical OT handout says 48 months; Merck, AAP, and the Beery sequence say 60 to 72. Chose 60 to 72 (three sources, including the two medical references).
- Stair alternation: sources disagree on exact ages; recorded the sequence (marking time about 24 to 30 months, up-alternating 36 to 48, down-alternating by 48 to 60) and marked the classic "up at 3, down at 4" as INFERENCE.
- Parallel play onset: OpenStax places first appearance at 3.5 years, most textbook summaries at 2 or older. Recorded both; CDC's "plays next to other children" at 30 months is the anchor to prefer for the product.
- Vocabulary at 50 words: ASHA places it at 19 to 24 months, CDC 2022 at 30 months. Not a true conflict (different percentile framings); both recorded.
- 403 on headstart.gov interactive ELOF; used the official ECLKC framework PDF instead (same content, same publisher).
- CDC live milestone pages initially returned a redesigned landing shell on fetch; the combined official 508 checklist PDF (October 2025 file) was used as the canonical text, cross-checked against search-rendered page content for the 2 year checklist.

**Coverage gaps for a follow-up pass:**

- ELOF indicator detail was captured deeply for math and literacy but only at goal level for approaches to learning, social-emotional, language, science, and motor; a second pass could table those by-60-months indicators too.
- Illinois KIDS (or another third state inventory) was surfaced in search but not verified; only Maryland KRA and Virginia VKRP are anchored, plus ELOF as the federal frame.
- No preschool music or visual-art framework was captured (NCAS has a PreK level; lane 07 territory but worth cross-referencing).
- Galloping and skipping ages rest on an Australian government FMS reference and Merck; a US pediatric PT primary source would strengthen them.
- Spanish and social_studies domains get nothing from this lane by design.
