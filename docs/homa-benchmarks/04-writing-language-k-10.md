# Writing and language benchmarks, kindergarten to grade 10

> Historical research snapshot, 2026-07-06. This file preserves the evidence and analysis captured on that date. It is not current product, legal, provider, standards, or production guidance. Re-verify volatile facts and source freshness before porting any claim into Homa.

**Date:** 2026-07-06
**Lane:** Writing and language 04, part of the ages 2 to 16 national benchmark corpus (see README.md in this folder)
**Method:** Fetched the CCSS ELA Writing and Language standards text grade by grade from the official standards site, then web-searched primary and near-primary sources for each sub-lane (ERIC records, federal WWC reports, state education department documents, publisher program pages, ASHA-journal norms) and transcribed only numbers visible in a fetched source. Grade-to-month anchors use the folder's end-of-grade convention and are always labeled as convention.

**Key findings:**

- The only quantified typing outputs in the CCSS are the page-per-sitting quotas: one page at grade 4, two at grade 5, three at grade 6 (W.4.6, W.5.6, W.6.6, verified against the standards text).
- No national keyboarding WPM ladder exists; state expectations at grade 3 span 5 WPM (South Carolina) to 15 WPM (Wisconsin rule of thumb), a 3x spread, and measured student speeds run below several state ladders.
- Handwriting speed roughly sextuples from grade 1 (19 letters per minute) to grade 9 (118 letters per minute), where it approaches adult speed (Graham, Berninger, Weintraub, and Schafer 1998).
- Gentry's classic spelling-stage age bands (conventional spelling from about age 10) describe untutored development; Gentry's own current instructional expectations put the same phases 1 to 2 years earlier, with consolidated spelling expected by end of grade 1.
- The written-conventions ladder is strikingly concentrated: grades 3 to 5 carry most of the load (parts of speech, quotation marks, all three sentence structures, tense system), while grades 8 to 10 add only voice, mood, verbals, parallel structure, and semicolon or colon control.
- Sentence length in writing grows about 1 word per T-unit per 1 to 2 grades (Hunt 1965 line: 8.5 words at grade 4, 11.3 at grade 8, 14.4 at grade 12).
- In 3-minute writing probes, average first graders produce 8 to 14 total words and 2 to 7 correct writing sequences; fifth graders produce 51 to 67 words and 46 to 63 correct sequences (Malecki and Jewell 2003).
- ASHA's public milestones stop at age 5; for ages 5 to 10 the best oral-language anchors are ASHA-journal SUGAR norms (MLU about 6.7 at age 5 rising to 9.6 by ages 9 to 10).

## 4. Spelling development stages and scope

Confidence: High for stage names and grade ranges, Medium for age bands (sources vary, see notes).

### 4a. Gentry's five stages of spelling development

Stage names are FACT from Gentry's own publications ([Gentry 1984, ERIC EJ303586](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ303586), accessed 2026-07-06) and the ERIC digest reprinted by [Reading Rockets](https://www.readingrockets.org/topics/spelling-and-word-study/articles/invented-spelling-and-spelling-development) (accessed 2026-07-06; Gentry 1982 called stage five "correct", later works say "conventional"). Age bands are from a New Zealand Ministry of Education summary chart ([TKI five stages chart](https://eesoll.tki.org.nz/content/download/35707/404674/file/summary+chart+for+five+stages+of+spelling+development.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06), the most explicit citable band table found; treat as typical, not normative.

| Stage | What the child does | Typical ages (TKI chart) | Anchor note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Precommunicative | Letter-like marks or random letter strings, no letter-sound correspondence | about 3 to 5 years | PreK edge of this lane |
| 2. Semiphonetic | Letters begin to stand for sounds, often one letter per word or syllable (U for you); "invented spelling" appears | about 4 to 6 years | K |
| 3. Phonetic | A letter or letter group for every speech sound heard (KOM for come); systematic and readable | about 5 to 7 years | K to 1 |
| 4. Transitional | Moves from sound-only to visual and structural knowledge (EGUL for eagle, HIGHEKED for hiked); conventional patterns appear | roughly 6 to 11 years, gradual | 1 to 5, wide band |
| 5. Conventional (correct) | Knows the orthographic system and base rules: prefixes, suffixes, silent consonants, irregular spellings; self-corrects | from about 10 to 11 years onward per TKI; Gentry described developmentally correct spelling as achievable by age 8 to 9 in advanced children ([NZCER Set 1983 reprint of Gentry](https://www.nzcer.org.nz/system/files/journals/Set1983_2_010_0.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06) | 3rd grade onward, instructional-level dependent |

Two caveats, both FACT: Gentry wrote that stage transitions are gradual and stages coexist in one writing sample ([Reading Rockets](https://www.readingrockets.org/topics/spelling-and-word-study/articles/invented-spelling-and-spelling-development), accessed 2026-07-06), and Gentry (1987) said the stages "normally... occur over two years, roughly between ages five and seven or eight" (quoted in [ED496700](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED496700.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06). Gentry's own current Monster Test guidance re-anchors the phases much earlier: prealphabetic expected first half of kindergarten, partial alphabetic by end of kindergarten, full alphabetic no later than middle of grade 1, consolidated (formerly transitional) by end of grade 1 ([jrichardgentry.com Monster Test](https://jrichardgentry.com/books-2/kid-writing/monster-test/), accessed 2026-07-06). So the classic age bands describe untutored development; instructional expectations sit 1 to 2 years earlier.

## 8. The 6+1 Traits model

Confidence: High. The 6+1 Trait Writing Model of Instruction and Assessment was developed by Education Northwest (formerly Northwest Regional Educational Laboratory); current rubrics were updated in 2021 and exist in two bands, K to 2 and 3 to 12 ([Education Northwest traits page](https://educationnorthwest.org/traits), accessed 2026-07-06). All FACT.

| Trait | Rubric key question (grades 3 to 12, 2021 rubric) |
|---|---|
| Ideas | Does the writer engage the reader with fresh information or perspective on a focused topic? |
| Organization | (structure of the piece; the rubric assesses lead, sequencing, pacing, and conclusion) |
| Voice | Is the voice appropriate to the task, purpose, and audience? |
| Word choice | Does the language convey precise and compelling meaning and create a vivid picture? |
| Sentence fluency | Does the writer control sentences so the text flows smoothly when read aloud? |
| Conventions | How well does the text illustrate the writer's proficiency with grade-appropriate conventions? |
| Presentation (the +1) | Is the finished piece easy to read, polished, and pleasing to the eye? |

Key questions quoted from the [6+1 Trait Writing Rubrics, grades 3 to 12, 2021 PDF](https://educationnorthwest.org/sites/default/files/resources/traits-rubrics-3-12.pdf) (accessed 2026-07-06). Trait definitions in one line each: ideas are the message and content; organization is the structure; voice is the writer's feelings and unique style; word choice is vivid, precise language; sentence fluency is smoothness and coherence; conventions are grammar, punctuation, and spelling; presentation is the appearance of the piece ([Texas Journal of Literacy Education 9(2), 2021, EJ1329970](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1329970.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06). Reach (same source, citing Coe et al. 2011): by 2009, 35 states had adopted elements of the model in their writing assessments and 22 used them in writing standards. Proficiency by band is defined by the two rubric documents rather than by grade-level numbers; the model is a qualitative scale, so no month anchors are ported from it.

## 9. Narrative and composition development

Confidence: High for the stage sequence (Applebee 1978, The Child's Concept of Story: Ages Two to Seventeen, University of Chicago Press, [Internet Archive record](https://archive.org/details/childsconceptofs0000appl_i5o9), accessed 2026-07-06), Medium for exact ages, which come from SLP practice summaries of Applebee rather than the book's own tables.

| Stage | Typical age | Months | What the story looks like | Story grammar elements |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Heaps | about 2 years | 24 | Unrelated labels and descriptions, no central theme, simple declarative sentences | 0 organized elements |
| 2. Sequences | 2 to 3 years | 24 to 36 | Events grouped around a central theme, but no plot or causality | theme only |
| 3. Primitive narratives | 3 to 4 years | 36 to 48 | Central theme with initiating event, attempt or action, and consequence; no resolution, little motivation | 3 |
| 4. Unfocused chains | 4 to 4.5 years | 48 to 54 | Cause and effect and temporal links appear, but the focus (character or theme) drifts; weak plot, ending may not follow | 4, shifting focus |
| 5. Focused chains | about 5 years | 60 | Chain of events with one character or theme held in focus throughout | 4, stable focus |
| 6. True narratives | from about 5 to 7 years | 60 to 84 | Central theme, character, and plot; clear motivation; logically and temporally sequenced events; resolution or ending | 5+ |

### 10c. Complex sentence development

FACT (sources as linked): coordinate and subordinate clauses emerge at about 36 to 48 months (Crystal, Fletcher, and Garman 1974, as cited in the [SUGAR handout](https://www.michiganspeechhearing.org/docs/SUGAR_DAY2_Handout.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06). Across ages 5 to 11, utterances containing subordination rise steadily, about 0.2 percentage points per month of age, with coordination-only and combined structures rising more slowly (Owens, Pavelko, and Hahs-Vaughn 2024, LSHSS, per the same handout): in the SUGAR model tables, out of 50 utterances, subordination-only utterances grow from about 1 at age 5;0 to about 8 at age 11;0.

INFERENCE, bridge to writing: oral words per sentence at ages 9 to 10 (about 9.7) sits close to written words per T-unit at grades 5 to 8 (8.8 to 11.3, section 7a), consistent with the standard view that oral syntax leads written syntax by a few years in elementary school and writing catches up in adolescence. Units differ (utterance vs T-unit), so treat as directional only.

## Dry wells and method notes

- Original Graham et al. (1998) article is paywalled; LPM values were taken from a peer-reviewed secondary table (EJ1377832) and a university course page rather than the primary PDF. The two secondaries agree, so confidence stays High, but a primary re-check is cheap insurance before porting item 14.
- The Beery VMI norm tables are proprietary; pre-writing ages are OT-community conventions citing Beery. Could not verify the exact 6th-edition month values. Marked INFERENCE in section 5a.
- Could not find a current state standards document publishing a full K to 8 keyboarding WPM ladder beyond South Carolina's; Utah's often-quoted 15/20/25/27 ladder appears only in archival compilations, and Utah's current standards deliberately dropped WPM targets. Treat any other WPM-by-grade table found online as vendor folklore until traced.
- Gentry's transitional and conventional stage age bands vary by source (TKI chart vs Gentry 1987 quote vs Monster Test phases). Presented all three rather than forcing one band.
- No published national norms found for compositional length by grade outside the 3-minute CBM paradigm (e.g., "expected essay length in grade 6"); state scoring guides define quality, not length. Logged as a gap rather than estimated.
- AIMSweb national aggregate writing norms (a richer percentile source than Malecki and Jewell) are behind Pearson's paywall; not used.
- ASHA public milestones stop at the 4-to-5-years band; school-age oral norms therefore come from LSHSS-published SUGAR studies (an ASHA journal), which is the closest citable equivalent.
- The 2017 SUGAR study and the 2020 extension tabulate age 7 differently (7;0 to 7;11 alone vs pooled 7;0 to 8;11); section 10b uses the pooled 2020 bands for ages 7+, noting the 2017 age-7 mean was 8.19.
- The 6+1 Traits K to 2 rubric was not separately fetched; section 8 documents the 3 to 12 rubric questions only.
- Loban's oral-language words-per-communication-unit series (the oral twin of section 7a) was not separately tabulated; the KISS compilation mixes his written data in. Follow-up pass could add Loban oral norms K to 10.
- NAEP writing achievement levels and results (last national administration 2011, computer-based) were left to lane 10 (assessment norms) to avoid duplication.
