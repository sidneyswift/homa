# Curriculum crosswalk: reading and language arts

> Historical research snapshot, 2026-07-06. This file preserves the evidence and analysis captured on that date. It is not current product, legal, provider, standards, or production guidance. Re-verify volatile facts and source freshness before porting any claim into Homa.

**Date:** 2026-07-06
**Lane:** Curriculum crosswalk 14 (ELA), part of the ages 2 to 16 national benchmark corpus (see README.md in this folder)
**Method:** Publisher sites first (allaboutlearningpress.com, logicofenglish.com, epslearning.com, hookedonphonics.com, goodandbeautiful.com, abeka.com, welltrainedmind.com, iew.com, lwtears.com, bobbooks.com), fetching scope-and-sequence, placement, and product pages directly; Cathy Duffy and homeschool review sites used only as labeled secondary sources. Level contents are transcribed from fetched pages, never from memory; grade-to-month anchors use the folder's end-of-grade convention and are always marked as convention.

**Key findings:**

- Most of the big homeschool phonics programs explicitly refuse grade labels. All About Reading and All About Spelling ("level numbers on our books do not refer to grade levels"), Logic of English Foundations (entry by skill, not age), and Explode the Code (grade bands "not meant to be followed strictly") are mastery-based with placement tests. A Homa family saying "she is in Level 2" of these programs is telling us a skill position, not a grade.
- The phonics endpoints converge. All About Reading 4, Explode the Code 8, and Logic of English Essentials all terminate at the same skill cluster: multisyllable decoding plus morphology (suffixes, roots, word parts), which is the bank's `phonics.multisyllable` (112mo) and `phonics.roots-prefixes` (130mo) rungs. "Finished the phonics program" from any of these publishers means roughly the same bank position.
- Teach Your Child to Read in 100 Easy Lessons carries a verifiable endpoint claim: the publisher states completion brings a child to "about a second-grade level" ("reading on a solid second-grade reading level" in the longer form), which maps to the 84 to 96mo band of the bank's phonics and literacy ladders.
- The Good and the Beautiful is the one grade-labeled program that positions itself ahead in writing: its own placement document states "course levels correspond to slightly advanced public school grade levels."
- Abeka's K4/K5 phonics sequence places decoding skills one or more bank rungs early: blending and one-vowel words in K4 (60mo vs the bank's `phonics.decodes-cvc` at 72), and 49 to 132 "special sounds" (digraphs, blends, r-controlled, diphthongs) in K5 (72mo vs bank anchors at 84 to 90). Its "about half a grade ahead" reputation in secondary sources matches the scope documents.
- Handwriting is where grade labels survive: Handwriting Without Tears and Zaner-Bloser are the only fully grade-labeled product lines in this lane, one workbook per grade, no placement test.
- Spelling has no direct bank ladder. All About Spelling's 7 levels (grade 1 through high school in secondary guidance) can only map to the writing ladder's convention items, a real crosswalk gap worth noting for report copy.
- Writing programs level by readiness too: Writing With Ease ships placement diagnostics and says levels "do not translate to grade levels"; IEW levels A/B/C are assigned by reading level, with publisher guidance that starting below level is normal.

## 1. All About Reading and All About Spelling

Confidence: High. All level contents below are FACT from All About Learning Press product pages unless labeled secondary.

### 1a. All About Reading (AAR): Pre-reading plus Levels 1 to 4

Structure FACT: one Pre-reading level plus four levels; "All About Reading consists of four levels (Levels 1-4), plus a Pre-reading program... After completing Level 4, your child has all the skills needed to decode just about any word" ([AAR overview](https://www.allaboutlearningpress.com/all-about-reading/), accessed 2026-07-06). Orton-Gillingham based, mastery-based, five components (phonological awareness, phonics and decoding, fluency, vocabulary, comprehension) ([Level 4 Teacher's Manual sample](https://downloads.allaboutlearningpress.com/samples/AAR-L4-TM-Sample.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).

Level does not equal grade, FACT, publisher wording: "Please note that the level numbers on our books do not refer to grade levels. Our lessons are mastery-based, not grade-level-based... The book may be labeled 'Level 1,' but this is not meant to indicate that it is appropriate for all first graders. Some kids may begin the program when they are five, while others may begin when they are older." ([AAR Level 1 page](https://www.allaboutlearningpress.com/all-about-reading-level-1/), accessed 2026-07-06)

## 2. Logic of English

Confidence: Med-High. Structure and stance are FACT from publisher pages; per-level phonogram counts beyond what the publisher states in prose were not fully itemized (dry well).

### 2b. Essentials (ages 8 and up)

FACT: "designed for students ages 8 through adult," 30 units across four volumes, five parts per unit (phonograms and rules; spelling analysis; grammar; vocabulary; review and dictation); each unit carries three difficulty levels A (beginning), B (intermediate), C (advanced), so one purchase can be taught up to three times at rising difficulty; teaches "the phonograms and spelling rules that explain 98% of English words"; placement test determines level and whether to add the Essentials Reader ([About Essentials](https://support.logicofenglish.com/hc/en-us/articles/4600606428699-About-Essentials), [Essentials page](https://logicofenglish.com/pages/essentials), [Essentials levels](https://support.logicofenglish.com/hc/en-us/articles/4600880333979-Essentials-Levels), [Essentials schedules](https://support.logicofenglish.com/hc/en-us/articles/6202642065819-Essentials-Schedules), all accessed 2026-07-06). Foundations is not a prerequisite. Level C "includes discovering Advanced Phonograms."

## 3. Teach Your Child to Read in 100 Easy Lessons

Confidence: High. Publisher is Simon and Schuster; program authors Engelmann, Haddox, and Bruner; an adaptation of the DISTAR Direct Instruction reading program.

- Endpoint claim FACT, publisher wording: "In 100 lessons... you can give your child the basic and more advanced skills needed to be a good reader" at "about a second-grade level" ([Simon and Schuster US page](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Teach-Your-Child-to-Read-in-100-Easy-Lessons/Phyllis-Haddox/9780671631987), accessed 2026-07-06). The same publisher's alternate copy: "within 100 teaching days your child will be reading on a solid second-grade reading level" ([Simon and Schuster AU page](https://www.simonandschuster.com.au/books/Teach-Your-Child-to-Read-in-100-Easy-Lessons/Phyllis-Haddox/9780671631987), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Structure FACT: 100 fully scripted lessons, about 20 minutes each; explicit phonemic awareness plus 44 letter-sound correspondences; a modified DISTAR orthography (marked letters, joined digraphs) that transitions to standard print over lessons 74 to 76, after which all print is conventional; by then the child reads stories of about 200 words with comprehension questions ([startreading.com research page](https://startreading.com/research-reviews/), the program's companion site, accessed 2026-07-06).
- Audience: publisher copy targets preschoolers through struggling K/1 readers; a secondary reviewer summarizes the typical span as ages 4 to 6 ([Numa review](https://numaschool.com/learn/glossary/100-easy-lessons), secondary, accessed 2026-07-06).
- No levels exist: it is a single-volume ladder, one lesson at a time. Placement is "start at lesson 1."

INFERENCE for the crosswalk: "solid second-grade reading level" at completion corresponds to the bank's 84 to 96mo band (`literacy.reads-short-book` 84, early `literacy.chapter-books` 96, `phonics.digraphs-cvce` 84), while the phonics coverage itself (44 correspondences, no full morphology) tops out below the multisyllable and morphology rungs.

## 5. Hooked on Phonics

Confidence: Med. The current product is app-first and reorganized; the classic 8-level box mapping is stable across sources but the primary page for it is an archival listing.

- Audience and span FACT: "designed for children ages 3-8," covering "foundational reading skills for Pre-K through second grade"; the current program has two sections, Pre-reader (alphabet and letter sounds) then Learn to Read (systematic sequential phonics, a new book after each lesson); each step is three lessons ([publisher FAQ](https://www.hookedonphonics.com/access-resources/faqs/), accessed 2026-07-06; [official scope and sequence PDF](https://res.cloudinary.com/hookedonphonics/images/v1727717499/HOP-Scope-and-Sequence/HOP-Scope-and-Sequence.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Classic 8-level structure with publisher grade/age labels (FACT for the labels, hosted secondary): Levels 1 and 2 All About Letters (Pre-K, ages 3 to 4); Levels 3 and 4 Word Families (kindergarten, ages 4 to 6); Levels 5 and 6 Beginning Phonics (first grade, ages 6 to 7); Levels 7 and 8 Intermediate Phonics (second grade, ages 7 to 8) ([GPII unified listing of the Learn to Read editions](https://ul.gpii.net/content/learn-read-various-editions), accessed 2026-07-06). Levels 7 and 8 cover long vowels with e, vowel teams, r-controlled words, y endings, diphthongs, complex blends, wr- and kn-, soft c and g, two-syllable words, and 36 new sight words (same listing).
- Placement FACT: the publisher's level selection guide places by skill, not grade: Pre-reader if letters and sounds are not mastered; kindergarten section once letters and sounds are mastered and the child is ready to read CVC words; first grade once short-vowel words are read and blends/digraphs are next; second grade once ending blends, inflectional endings, long vowels, and diphthongs are next. It explicitly recommends the skill descriptions over the grade labels, and recommends struggling grade 1 to 3 readers start at the kindergarten section ([level selection guide](https://intercom.help/hookedonphonics/en/articles/11510892-level-selection-guide), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Positioning claim FACT (marketing): the program serves children who need "a boost in order to read at (or above!) grade level" (publisher FAQ, above).

## 6. The Good and the Beautiful Language Arts

Confidence: High for structure and placement stance, Med for per-level skills (course pages not transcribed level by level, dry well).

- Course ladder FACT: Preschool (90 lessons), Kindergarten Prep (30 lessons), Level K, Levels 1 to 7, then High School 1, 2, and 3. Most levels are 120 lessons (Level 7 is 140), designed for one school year at 4 lessons per week ([choose your course page](https://www.goodandbeautiful.com/pages/choose-language-arts), [LA FAQs](https://www.goodandbeautiful.com/pages/language-arts-faqs), placement PDF below, all accessed 2026-07-06).
- Bundling FACT: every level "thoroughly cover[s] reading, writing, grammar, spelling, literature, geography, and art," so one course is the family's whole ELA block plus art; courses are parent-led through Level 3 and student-directed from Level 4 up ([choose your course page](https://www.goodandbeautiful.com/pages/choose-language-arts), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Ahead positioning FACT, publisher wording: "Course levels correspond to slightly advanced public school grade levels" ([Language Arts Course Level Placement Test PDF](https://files.thegoodandthebeautiful.com/Language-Arts/Language-Arts-Course-Level-Placement-Test_June24.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Placement FACT: start at the course matching the child's grade unless extra reading help is needed; the placement test assesses reading only (timed oral passages with error limits per level), because spelling and grammar are reviewed within each level; advanced readers are told NOT to skip levels ("Not skipping levels ensures that no foundational learning is missed"); ages 14 plus who pass the Level 6/7 reading checks start High School 1; a Grammar Prep Unit (8 lessons, free) backfills grammar before Levels 5 to 7 (same placement PDF, accessed 2026-07-06).
- Level K entry gates (FACT, same PDF): Level K requires all letters and most-common sounds solidly mastered (else Kindergarten Prep or the Preschool course); Level K includes Reading Booster A cards and books, with Booster B substituted for advanced K readers.

## 7. Abeka phonics and Language Arts

Confidence: Med-High. The special-sounds counts come from Abeka's own scope-and-sequence PDF; the reputation claims are labeled secondary.

- Structure: grade-labeled from the start (K4 for 4-year-olds, K5 for kindergarten, then numbered grades), sold as full-grade Language Arts programs; phonics is the K4/K5 core and stays integrated with reading, spelling, and grammar in grades 1 and 2 ([Rainbow Resource Abeka phonics overview](https://www.rainbowresource.com/abeka-phonics), secondary, accessed 2026-07-06). No placement test culture: placement is by grade purchase (INFERENCE from the product structure).
- Six-step sequence FACT (publisher): 1 short vowels, 2 consonants, 3 blend one letter at a time ("blend ladders," front-end blending), 4 one-vowel words, 5 long vowels and two-vowel words, 6 the "132 most commonly used letter combinations in the English language" as "special sounds," circled and marked inside words ([Abeka blog, The building blocks for reading](https://www.abeka.com/blog/the-building-blocks-for-reading-the-importance-of-phonics-for-k5-and-younger/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- K4 FACT: steps 1 to 4 plus a touch of 5, about 3 days per letter, vowels first, then blending, then one-vowel words (same Abeka blog, accessed 2026-07-06).
- K5 FACT: reviews K4, two days per letter, adds long vowels and special sounds. Regular K5 masters 49 special sounds (27 consonant blends, 5 consonant digraphs, 5 diphthongs, 12 letter groups); Advanced K5 (AK5) adds 83 more for all 132, including 11 suffixes and 5 prefixes; K5 readers progress from one-vowel words and the sight word "the" to one- and two-vowel words, 12 sight words, sentences with expression and comprehension questions ([Abeka K5 scope and sequence PDF](https://static.abeka.com/Abeka/InteractivePDF/ScopeSequence/SchoolSS/Detailed_IndividualPDFs/2023/K5_CS%20SS%2023.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06). The 132 special sounds list circulates as the Basic Sounds chart ([scan via donpotter.net](http://www.donpotter.net/pdf/a_beka_basic_sounds.pdf), secondary, accessed 2026-07-06).
- Formal-and-early reputation, labeled secondary: "Abeka is known for running slightly above grade level... roughly half a grade level above conventional school standards," with the fast K to 2 phonics named as a common dropout point for struggling readers ([Homeschool Start Guide comparison](https://homeschoolstartguide.com/blog/abeka-homeschool-curriculum), accessed 2026-07-06); "Children often read fluently by end of K5 or Grade 1" ([stealingfaith.com Abeka review](https://www.stealingfaith.com/abeka-curriculum-reviews/), accessed 2026-07-06).

INFERENCE vs the bank: Abeka K5 completion (72mo) includes skills the bank anchors at 84 to 90mo (digraphs, r-controlled, diphthongs inside the special sounds), so an on-track Abeka child will read a rung or so "early" by bank anchors, an artifact of the curriculum, not necessarily of the child.

## 8. First Language Lessons

Confidence: High for structure and grade targeting (publisher support article); grammar content detail is from a labeled secondary review.

Structure FACT: four levels, scripted, "not 'grade levels'"; each level is one year. Publisher targeting: Level 1 content targeted at first or second grade; Level 2 at second or third; Level 3 at third or fourth; Level 4 at fourth or fifth. Levels 1 and 2 are oral with only an instructor guide; Levels 3 and 4 add a student workbook and diagramming ([WTM support, Tell me about First Language Lessons](https://support.welltrainedmind.com/hc/en-us/articles/360056756933-Tell-me-about-First-Language-Lessons-Grammar), [series page](https://welltrainedmind.com/c/series/first-language-lessons/), accessed 2026-07-06). Publisher also notes FLL and WWE overlap in copywork/narration/dictation and advises choosing one source for those exercises (same support article).

Grammar scope per level (secondary, [Boldly Educated review](https://boldlyeducated.com/first-language-lessons-review-an-honest-look-at-the-classical-grammar-curriculum-for-the-well-trained-mind/), accessed 2026-07-06):

| Level | Signature grammar skills |
|---|---|
| 1 | Definitions and identification of nouns, pronouns, verbs (action and being); capitalization and end punctuation; four sentence types; poem memorization; simple copywork |
| 2 | Adds adjectives, articles, adverbs; narration from literature; longer memorization; still oral |
| 3 | Adds conjunctions, interjections, prepositions and prepositional phrases, direct objects; sentence diagramming begins; comprehensive review makes it a valid entry point for grades 3 to 4 |
| 4 | Adds indirect objects, predicate nominatives, predicate adjectives, linking verbs; completes elementary grammar; leads into Grammar for the Well-Trained Mind for middle school |

Placement approach: choose by ability and maturity; suggested start is Level 1 when the child is an emerging reader in grade 1 or 2 (publisher support article above).

## 9. Institute for Excellence in Writing

Confidence: High. All structure below is FACT from IEW pages.

- Levels FACT: Structure and Style for Students (SSS) runs in three levels by grade band: Level A grades 3 to 5, Level B grades 6 to 8, Level C grades 9 plus; each Year 1 course is 24 weeks of video instruction ([SSS info page](https://iew.com/sss-info), accessed 2026-07-06).

## 10. Writing With Ease and Writing With Skill

Confidence: High. Publisher is Well-Trained Mind Press (Susan Wise Bauer).

### 10a. Writing With Ease (WWE), levels 1 to 4

- Structure FACT: four levels built on three foundational skills, narration, copywork, and dictation, as prerequisites to original composition; weekly rhythm alternates copywork and narration days; by week 36 of level 1, copywork reaches 10 to 12 word sentences ([WTM support, Tell me about Writing With Ease](https://support.welltrainedmind.com/hc/en-us/articles/1500001427302-Tell-me-about-Writing-With-Ease), [Susan Wise Bauer program page](https://susanwisebauer.com/writing-with-ease-strong-fundamentals/), accessed 2026-07-06).

### 10b. Writing With Skill (WWS), levels 1 to 3

- Level 1 skills FACT (publisher): one- and two-level outlining; chronological narratives, biographical sketches, descriptions, sequences across the curriculum; basic literary essays on fiction and poetry; researching and documenting source material (footnotes, endnotes, in-text citations) (WWS Level 1 product page, accessed 2026-07-06).
- Level 3 FACT (publisher): extensive practice planning and carrying out independent projects, preparing for higher-level writing; suggested transcript titles Expository Writing I to III (WWS Level 3 product page, accessed 2026-07-06).
