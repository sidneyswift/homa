# Science benchmarks, kindergarten to grade 10

> Historical research snapshot, 2026-07-06. This file preserves the evidence and analysis captured on that date. It is not current product, legal, provider, standards, or production guidance. Re-verify volatile facts and source freshness before porting any claim into Homa.

**Date:** 2026-07-06
**Lane:** Science 05, part of the ages 2 to 16 national benchmark corpus (see README.md in this folder)
**Method:** Primary sources fetched and parsed directly: the NGSS combined performance-expectation PDF ("AllTopic"), NGSS Appendix F (practices) and Appendix K (model course maps), NAEP science report pages at nationsreportcard.gov (2024 grade 8, 2019 grade 4), NCES Digest table 225.45 and Condition of Education course-taking indicator, ACF Head Start ELOF, World Book Typical Course of Study (via a homeschool mirror plus the World Book pages), and Texas TEA rule pages. Every number below traces to one of these fetched sources; unverifiable items are parked in "Dry wells and method notes".

**Key findings:**
- NGSS (2013, still current) organizes every standard as a three-dimensional performance expectation coded grade-DCI-number (for example `5-PS1-3`), blending a science and engineering practice, a disciplinary core idea, and a crosscutting concept. This lane catalogs the full topic-arranged set: 72 elementary PEs (K to 5), 59 middle-school PEs, and 71 high-school PEs by my row counts.
- NGSS assigns specific PEs to K to 5 by grade, but leaves middle-school and high-school grade placement to states and districts; Appendix K publishes two sanctioned sequencing models (Conceptual Progressions and Science Domains). So "what a 7th grader should know in science" is genuinely not nationally fixed.
- NAEP science fell in 2024: 31 percent of 8th graders scored at or above NAEP Proficient (down from 35 in 2019) and 38 percent scored below NAEP Basic (the highest since the 2009 trendline began). NAEP did not assess grade 4 or 12 science in 2024, so grade 4 data is still the 2019 result (36 percent at or above Proficient).
- Homeschool-familiar scope (World Book) tracks NGSS topics loosely but sequences high school as the classic biology (grade 10), chemistry (grade 11), physics (grade 12) ladder, with earth and physical science in grade 9. Most homeschool families follow this or a Christian-curriculum variant, not NGSS.
- NCES transcript data (2019) anchors the real US high-school sequence: about 97 percent of graduates complete biology, 76 percent chemistry, 40 percent physics; biology is effectively universal and usually the grade 9 or 10 course.
- ELOF (2015) already expects observation, prediction, comparison, sorting, and simple cause-and-effect reasoning by 60 months, giving a clean PreK-to-K science bridge for the youngest Homa ages.

**How NGSS is structured (FACT,** [nextgenscience.org K-PS2 topic page](https://www.nextgenscience.org/dci-arrangement/k-ps2-motion-and-stability-forces-and-interactions), accessed 2026-07-06). Every NGSS standard is a *performance expectation* (PE): a statement of what students who understand the material can do. Each PE integrates three dimensions:

1. **Science and engineering practices (SEPs):** the 8 doing-verbs of science (question, model, investigate, analyze, use math, explain, argue, communicate). See section 5.
2. **Disciplinary core ideas (DCIs):** the content, in four domains: PS (physical science), LS (life science), ESS (earth and space science), ETS (engineering, technology, and applications of science).
3. **Crosscutting concepts (CCCs):** 7 lenses that recur across domains (patterns; cause and effect; scale, proportion, and quantity; systems and system models; energy and matter; structure and function; stability and change).

**PE coding scheme (FACT,** same source). A code such as `5-PS1-3` reads: grade or band (`K`, `1` to `5`, `MS`, or `HS`), then the DCI it draws from (`PS1` = Matter and Its Interactions), then the PE number within that topic. `MS-` marks middle-school band PEs, `HS-` high-school band PEs. An asterisk (for example `K-PS2-2*`) marks a PE that integrates engineering. "Assessment Boundary" and "Clarification Statement" notes bound each PE.

Confidence: High. Editions verified against the live NGSS site and the framework PDFs.

### What US students actually take in grades 9 and 10 (FACT, NCES)

The NCES 2019 High School Transcript Study reports cumulative science-course completion for graduates (grades 9 to 12, not broken out by grade) ([NCES Digest table 225.45](https://nces.ed.gov/programs/digest/d22/tables/dt22_225.45.asp) and [Condition of Education course-taking indicator](https://nces.ed.gov/programs/coe/indicator/sod/high-school-courses?tid=4), both accessed 2026-07-06):

| Science course completed by graduation | 2019 | 2009 |
|---|---|---|
| Biology | ~97% (96.9) | ~97% |
| Chemistry | 76% | 73% |
| Physics | 40% (39.5) | 34% |
| Earth / environmental science | 47% | 40% |
| Integrated / unified / survey science | 46% | 55% |
| Biology and chemistry (both) | 75% (74.9) | 72% |

Interpretation (INFERENCE): biology is effectively universal and, in the common ladder, is the grade 9 or 10 course; the near-universal biology completion plus the far lower chemistry and physics rates match the classic "biology, then chemistry, then physics" sequence where physics is often skipped. ## 5. The 8 science and engineering practices: progression by grade band

## 10. Non-NGSS state systems: Texas TEKS and Florida

Roughly 20 states adopted NGSS verbatim and about 24 adopted NGSS-based standards; several large states run their own systems (INFERENCE, widely reported; exact current count not re-verified here, see Dry wells). Texas and Florida are the two largest non-NGSS systems.

**Texas (TEKS), FACT** ([19 TAC Chapter 112 index](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/texas-administrative-code/19-tac-chapter-112), [TEKS review page](https://tea.texas.gov/curriculum-and-instruction/curriculum-standards/teks-review/science-teks-review), [TEA graduation toolkit 2025](https://tea.texas.gov/about-tea/news-and-multimedia/brochures/tea-graduation-toolkit-2025.pdf), all accessed 2026-07-06):
- Science TEKS were adopted 2021 for K to 8 and 2020 for the four core high-school courses (Biology, Chemistry, Integrated Physics and Chemistry, Physics). Texas has never adopted NGSS.
- **Placement difference 1:** Texas offers Integrated Physics and Chemistry (IPC) as a distinct one-credit course "recommended for students in Grades 9 and 10" ([19 TAC 112.44](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/19-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-112-44), accessed 2026-07-06). NGSS has no such single integrated physical-science course.
- **Placement difference 2:** the Foundation High School Program requires three science credits: Biology, then one of IPC/Chemistry/Physics, then an advanced science. Biology has a required end-of-course (STAAR EOC) exam. This nails biology to (typically) grade 9 or 10 in Texas, whereas NGSS fixes no grade.
- TEKS structure allots at least 40 percent of instructional time to scientific and engineering practices (FACT, 112.44), a structural echo of NGSS three-dimensionality without adopting the PE format.

**Florida, FACT partial:** Florida uses its own standards (historically the Next Generation Sunshine State Standards for science; the state adopted revised "Florida's B.E.S.T." standards for other subjects). This lane did not fetch a live Florida science standards page, so specific Florida placement differences are not verified here (see Dry wells) and should not be stated as fact until confirmed.

Confidence: High for Texas structure and IPC grade 9-10 placement. Low for Florida (unverified this pass).

- **Exact by-grade science course-taking (percent of 9th graders in biology, etc.):** NCES HSTS reports cumulative completion by graduation, not clean by-grade enrollment. The section-4 grade 9/10 assignment is inference from the completion pattern, not a directly published by-grade table. - **Appendix K exact PE-to-course assignments:** the two model course maps were read and described, but the full PE-by-course grids (Tables 1 to 6) were not transcribed. If a state's specific sequence is needed, transcribe the relevant Appendix K table.
- **HS-PS (remaining), all HS-ESS, and HS-ETS PEs:** cataloged in the NGSS PDF but not fully tabulated here because they anchor grades 11 and 12 (chemistry, physics, earth science), which sit outside the grade 9-10 focus. Codes are enumerated in section 4's parenthetical; a completeness pass could add them.
- **Microscope and specific-tool-by-grade standards:** NGSS names practices, not tools, so "student uses a compound microscope by grade X" could not be sourced to a national standard. Candidate item wording should avoid naming tools as national expectations.
- **Florida science standards:** not fetched this pass. Florida runs its own system (historically Next Generation Sunshine State Standards for science); specific placement differences are unverified and were deliberately not asserted.

- **NGSS total PE count:** row counts here (72 elementary, 59 middle, 71 high) are this lane's tallies from the topic PDF; the commonly cited NGSS total is around 200.
