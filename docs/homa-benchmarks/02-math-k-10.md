# Math benchmarks, kindergarten to grade 10

**Date:** 2026-07-06
**Lane:** Math 02, part of the ages 2 to 16 national benchmark corpus (see README.md in this folder)
**Method:** Fetched the CCSS-M standards text grade page by grade page from thecorestandards.org (the live official site, with corestandards.org as temporary mirror), CCSS Appendix A for high school pathways, nationsreportcard.gov and NCES pages for NAEP 2024 and course-taking data, learningtrajectories.org's official trajectory summary PDF for Clements and Sarama levels, Intervention Central and a 2019 peer-reviewed review for CBM fluency norms, World Book's typical course of study pages, and TEA and Florida DOE documents for state variation. Every number below was read from a fetched source on 2026-07-06.

**Key findings:**

- CCSS-M (2010, unrevised) remains the de facto national reference; the official site is now thecorestandards.org. The most report-usable spine is the required-fluencies ladder: within 5 (K), within 10 then 20 (1 to 2), one-digit facts from memory (add gr 2, multiply gr 3), standard algorithms (add/subtract gr 4, multiply gr 5, divide and decimals gr 6).
- NAEP 2024: grade 4 math, 76 percent at or above NAEP Basic and 39 percent at or above NAEP Proficient; grade 8, 59 percent and 27 percent. NCES itself warns NAEP Proficient is not "grade level", NAEP Basic is the closer proxy.
- Algebra 1 in grade 9 is still the national norm (50.1 percent of fall 2022 ninth-graders), with roughly a quarter to a third of students taking it in grade 8; that remains the single most recognized acceleration marker.
- There is no numeric national fact-fluency rate: CBM research criteria disagree by a factor of 2 or more (Deno and Mirkin vs Burns et al. vs precision teaching's 80 to 110 digits per minute), so Homa should anchor to qualitative "knows from memory" language, not a per-minute cut.
- Clements and Sarama's trajectories put counting to 100 and counting-on at typical age 6 to 7, meaning CCSS kindergarten counting expectations sit at the ambitious end of the developmental range; the counting-all, counting-on, derived-facts, known-facts strategy ladder is the most parent-observable progress signal in K to 2.
- World Book's typical course of study, the most homeschool-familiar neutral scope-and-sequence, tracks CCSS-M nearly verbatim at every grade checked, so CCSS anchors will not conflict with it.
- Texas TEKS and Florida B.E.S.T. both expect fact automaticity and standard algorithms about a year earlier than CCSS in grades 3 to 5, and both add financial literacy strands CCSS lacks; otherwise content correlates strongly.

## 6. Math fact fluency norms from curriculum-based measurement

Confidence: Med. The numbers below are verified against fetched sources, but the field genuinely disagrees about criterion levels, metrics differ (digits correct per minute, DCPM, vs problems or facts correct per minute), and timing conventions differ (1-minute vs 2-minute probes). Treat these as ranges, not cut scores. CCSS itself sets no numeric rate ("fluently" is defined qualitatively; the Publishers' Criteria describe fluent as "accurate and reasonably fast", [K-8 Publishers' Criteria PDF](https://www.thecorestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/Math_Publishers_Criteria_K-8_Spring_2013_FINAL1.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).

### Deno and Mirkin (1977), as presented by Intervention Central

FACT: these classic criteria are presented as digits correct in 2 minutes ([Intervention Central, CBM computation fluency directions PDF](https://www.interventioncentral.org/sites/default/files/workshop_files/allfiles/5_CBA_Math_Computation_Directions.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06, which labels the table "Adapted from Deno & Mirkin, 1977"):

| Grades | Performance level | Digits correct in 2 minutes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 3 | Frustration | 20 or less |
| 1 to 3 | Instructional | 21 to 40 |
| 1 to 3 | Mastery | 41 or higher |
| 4 and up | Frustration | 40 or less |
| 4 and up | Instructional | 41 to 80 |
| 4 and up | Mastery | 81 or higher |

Caution: secondary sources restate the same Deno and Mirkin criteria per 1 minute (mastery about 20 DCPM at grades 1 to 3, about 40 DCPM at grades 4 and up); a 2019 review states "Deno and Mirkin (1977) recommended 40 DCPM" as the computational criterion ([Stocker et al. 2019 review PDF](https://centralreach.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/2019-Stocker-Schwartz-Kubina-Kostewicz-and-Kozloff.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06). The per-minute and per-2-minute presentations are consistent with each other once halved; the file above flags the timing explicitly.

### Burns, VanDerHeyden, and Jiban (2006), empirically derived

FACT: from the study abstract, instructional-level fluency was empirically derived as 14 to 31 digits correct per minute for grades 2 and 3, and 24 to 49 digits correct per minute for grades 4 and 5 ([Burns, VanDerHeyden, and Jiban 2006, School Psychology Review](https://doi.org/10.1080/02796015.2006.12087975), accessed 2026-07-06):

| Grades | Frustration | Instructional | Mastery |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 to 3 | Below 14 DCPM | 14 to 31 DCPM | Above 31 DCPM |
| 4 to 5 | Below 24 DCPM | 24 to 49 DCPM | Above 49 DCPM |

### The disagreement in the field

- FACT: VanDerHeyden and Burns (2009) report that about 60 DCPM for fourth and fifth graders "may better support retention and mathematics achievement", higher than the classic criteria ([Stocker et al. 2019 review](https://centralreach.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/2019-Stocker-Schwartz-Kubina-Kostewicz-and-Kozloff.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).
- FACT: the precision teaching literature recommends 80 to 110 DCPM for simple computation (Haughton 1972; Johnson and Layng 1996; Kubina and Yurich 2012; Wood et al. 1978), roughly double the CBM criteria ([same review](https://centralreach.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/2019-Stocker-Schwartz-Kubina-Kostewicz-and-Kozloff.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).
- FACT: the same review notes national bodies (NMAP 2008, NCTM 2000, CCSS 2010) have declined to publish numeric fluency rates, likely due to lack of empirical consensus ([same review](https://centralreach.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/2019-Stocker-Schwartz-Kubina-Kostewicz-and-Kozloff.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).
- INFERENCE for product use: a safe report framing is qualitative ("knows addition facts from memory", matching 2.OA.B.2 language) rather than a per-minute rate; if a rate is ever needed internally, the Burns et al. instructional band is the most defensible, and any single cut score would overstate precision.

## 9. State variation notes

Confidence: High for the facts below; deliberately shallow per the lane brief. The two big non-CCSS systems a US homeschool product will meet are Texas and Florida.

### Texas TEKS

- FACT: Texas never adopted Common Core; 2013 House Bill 462 prohibits the State Board of Education from adopting it. The current K to 8 math TEKS were adopted in 2012 (effective 2014-15); assessment is STAAR ([EdGate Texas standards summary](https://edgate.com/standards/us/p-w/standard-tx), accessed 2026-07-06; adoption date confirmed at [Texas Administrative Code 111.5](http://txrules.elaws.us/rule/title19_chapter111_sec.111.5), accessed 2026-07-06).
- FACT, grade placement difference worth knowing: TEKS grade 3 requires students to "recall facts to multiply up to 10 by 10 with automaticity and recall the corresponding division facts" (3.4F) and to multiply two-digit by one-digit numbers including with the standard algorithm (3.4G), slightly ahead of CCSS grade 3 (memory of one-digit products only, no two-digit algorithm) ([TEA grade 3 TEKS PDF](https://tea.texas.gov/academics/curriculum-standards/teks/grade3-teks-062024.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).
- FACT: TEKS includes a personal financial literacy strand in every elementary grade (grade 3 example: standard 3.9, income, spending, credit, savings), a strand CCSS-M does not have at all ([TEA grade 3 TEKS PDF](https://tea.texas.gov/academics/curriculum-standards/teks/grade3-teks-062024.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).
- FACT: TEKS uses 7 mathematical process standards where CCSS uses 8 practice standards; a side-by-side comparison found "such a strong correlation between the two sets of standards" in content ([Kaplinsky TEKS vs CCSS comparison](https://robertkaplinsky.com/comparing-the-texas-essential-knowledge-and-skills-to-the-common-core-state-standards/), accessed 2026-07-06).

### Florida B.E.S.T. standards

- FACT: Florida adopted the Benchmarks for Excellent Student Thinking (B.E.S.T.) in 2020, replacing its Common Core-derived MAFS standards; K to 12 math classrooms implemented them in 2022-23 ([Florida DOE standards recommendations packet](https://www.fldoe.org/core/fileparse.php/5660/urlt/StandardsRecommendationsPacket.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06; [Independent Institute review](https://www.independent.org/article/2020/07/02/better-than-common-core/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- FACT, grade placement differences worth knowing (from Florida DOE's own side-by-side, [same packet](https://www.fldoe.org/core/fileparse.php/5660/urlt/StandardsRecommendationsPacket.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06):
  - Multi-digit multiplication with procedural fluency moves down to grade 4 (MA.4.NSO.2.2, four digits by one digit) vs CCSS grade 4 strategies-only.
  - The standard division algorithm moves down to grade 5 (MA.5.NSO.2.2, up to five digits by two digits "including using a standard algorithm with procedural fluency") vs CCSS grade 6 (6.NS.B.2). Florida markets this as "all basic whole number arithmetic mastered by end of grade 5".
  - A financial literacy strand exists in grades 9 to 12.
- FACT: B.E.S.T. organizes content by strand (number sense and operations K to 12, fractions 1 to 5, algebraic reasoning K to 12, measurement K to 5, geometric reasoning K to 12, data analysis and probability K to 12, functions 8 to 12) rather than CCSS domains ([Fordham Institute review of B.E.S.T.](https://fordhaminstitute.org/sites/default/files/publication/pdfs/20200609-state-sunshine-state-standards-floridas-best-edition.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).

INFERENCE for product use: the practical deltas are small and mostly about when computational fluency is expected (Texas and Florida both push standard algorithms and fact automaticity about a year earlier than CCSS in grades 3 to 5) plus financial literacy strands. A child tracked against CCSS anchors will look at most about one grade "early or late" on those specific computation items relative to TEKS or B.E.S.T. expectations, worth remembering when a Texan or Floridian family compares Homa's map to their state checklist.

That is 48 to 50 scale points across 4 grade years, so roughly 12 points of NAEP scale per school year in the middle grades (derivation: (262 - 214) / 4 = 12; (299 - 249) / 4 = 12.5). Cut scores from [NCES achievement levels page](https://nces.ed.gov/nationsreportcard/mathematics/achieve.aspx), accessed 2026-07-06. Use only as an order-of-magnitude sense of "a year"; NAEP is not vertically equated for individual growth claims.
- FACT, remediation framing from the CCSS writers: unfinished learning is best handled inside grade-level work, "the development of fluency with division using the standard algorithm in grade 6 is the occasion to surface and deal with unfinished learning about place value; this is more productive than setting aside division and backing up" ([K-8 Publishers' Criteria PDF](https://www.thecorestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/Math_Publishers_Criteria_K-8_Spring_2013_FINAL1.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06). This matches Homa's no-behind-framing law well: the standards' own authors advise against "backing up" a child.

### Remediation landmarks (where gaps most often surface)

INFERENCE, anchored to the verified standards ladder: the three load-bearing prerequisites where a gap propagates are (1) place value and the make-a-ten structure (1.NBT to 2.NBT), which carries all multi-digit arithmetic; (2) multiplication fact fluency by end of grade 3 (3.OA.C.7), which carries fractions, division, and area; (3) fraction understanding in grades 3 to 5 (3.NF to 5.NF), which carries ratio, proportion, and algebra readiness in grades 6 to 8. Each is individually cited in sections 2 and 3.
