# Reading benchmarks, kindergarten to grade 10

> Historical research snapshot, 2026-07-06. This file preserves the evidence and analysis captured on that date. It is not current product, legal, provider, standards, or production guidance. Re-verify volatile facts and source freshness before porting any claim into Homa.

**Date:** 2026-07-06
**Lane:** Reading 03, part of the ages 2 to 16 national benchmark corpus (see README.md in this folder)
**Method:** Web research against primary sources: the official CCSS standards site and full ELA standards PDF, the Hasbrouck and Tindal 2017 technical report (Behavioral Research and Teaching, University of Oregon) cross-checked against Read Naturally's reproduction, the official DIBELS 8 benchmark goals PDF (University of Oregon), MetaMetrics and CCSS Appendix A supplemental documents for Lexile, the official Fountas and Pinnell expectations chart, nationsreportcard.gov plus a state reproduction for NAEP 2024, and peer-reviewed or author-published papers for Ehri, Chall, vocabulary, silent rates, and reading onset. Every number was read from a fetched document; nothing is from memory.

**Key findings:**

- The Hasbrouck and Tindal 2017 ORF norms (grades 1 to 6, five percentiles, three seasons) were verified in full, 30 rows: the 50th percentile spring series runs 60, 100, 112, 133, 146, 146 WCPM for grades 1 to 6, and ORF stops discriminating around 145 to 150 WCPM after grade 5.
- DIBELS 8 benchmark goals (2020 update) are criterion-referenced risk cuts, not percentiles; its ORF core-support minimums sit well below the H and T 50th percentile, so the two tables answer different product questions (at risk vs where among peers).
- The four text ladders disagree in resolution: Lexile and ATOS resolve through grade 10 plus, while Fountas and Pinnell plateaus at Z in grade 7 and DRA tops out near 80 in middle school.
- CCSS foundational skills end at grade 5; from grade 2 on there is no new print-concepts or phonological-awareness expectation, and grades 4 to 5 share identical decoding text. After that, all reading growth is carried by text complexity and comprehension depth.
- NAEP 2024: 40 percent of grade 4 and 33 percent of grade 8 students scored below NAEP Basic, the worst below-Basic shares in 20 years (grade 4) and in assessment history (grade 8). NCES itself warns NAEP Proficient is not "grade level".
- Root-word vocabulary grows from about 6000 words at end of grade 2 to about 8400 by grade 5, plus an explosion of about 14,000 derived words between grades 1 and 5 (Anglin, Biemiller).
- Comprehension-based silent reading rates average 116 wpm at grade 2, 148 at grade 4, 164 at grade 6, then nearly plateau (170 at grade 8, 187 at grade 10); growth since 1960 has flattened (Spichtig et al. 2016).
- The normal onset window for reading is wide (about 5 to 7 plus): NZ evidence (Suggate et al. 2012) shows children starting formal instruction at 7 catch up to age-5 starters by age 11, directly relevant to Homa's late-blooming readers.

## 1. CCSS reading foundational skills, grade by grade (K to 5)

Confidence: High. All rows are FACT, quoted or closely paraphrased from the Common Core State Standards for ELA, Reading: Foundational Skills strand (CCSS ELA, 2010 edition, unchanged since publication), fetched grade page by grade page from the official standards site: [RF.K](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RF/K/), [RF.1](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RF/1/), [RF.2](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RF/2/), [RF.3](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RF/3/), [RF.4](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RF/4/), [RF.5](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RF/5/), all accessed 2026-07-06.

## 2. Phonics scope and sequence

Confidence: High for the skill order, Med for exact grade edges (published programs shift skills a few months either way). Primary source: UFLI Foundations (University of Florida Literacy Institute, 2022, 128 concept lessons, designed for K to 2 core instruction), whose sequence is documented at [ufli.education.ufl.edu/foundations](https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/foundations/) (accessed 2026-07-06), itemized in the [EdReports 2025 review of UFLI kindergarten](https://edreports.org/reports/detail/ufli-ela-k-2-2025/kindergarten/gateway-one) (accessed 2026-07-06), the [Flying Start Books lesson-by-lesson correlation](https://flyingstartbooks.com/curriculum-correlations/ufli-foundations/) (accessed 2026-07-06), and a district implementation that publishes the grade split ([Rocklin USD UFLI hub](https://sites.google.com/rocklinusd.org/thehub/teachers/ela/ufli-foundations), accessed 2026-07-06). Grade-level placement cross-checked against CCSS RF standards (section 1 above).

FACT, grade split used by UFLI implementations: kindergarten = lessons 1 to 68, grade 1 = lessons 35 to 110 (re-teaching 35 to 68 as review), grade 2 = grade 1 content review plus lessons 111 to 128 ([Rocklin USD hub](https://sites.google.com/rocklinusd.org/thehub/teachers/ela/ufli-foundations), accessed 2026-07-06). UFLI states the program is "designed to be used for core instruction in the primary grades or for intervention with struggling students in any grade" ([UFLI](https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/foundations/), accessed 2026-07-06).

Homa should treat placements 3 to 6 as a K-to-1 window rather than a point anchor.

## 6. Word recognition development: Ehri phases and Chall stages

Confidence: High for the frameworks themselves, Med for the age edges (both authors stress phases are gradual and overlapping, not age-locked).

### Ehri's phases of sight word learning

Framework FACT: Ehri distinguishes four phases by "the type of alphabetic knowledge used to form connections: pre-alphabetic, partial, full, and consolidated alphabetic" (Ehri, Learning to read words: theory, findings, and issues, Scientific Studies of Reading, [author PDF](https://ala.asn.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Ehri-Word-Learning.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06). Age and grade anchors below are from [Reading Rockets, Typical reading development](https://www.readingrockets.org/reading-101/how-children-learn-read/typical-reading-development) (accessed 2026-07-06) and the [95 Percent Group stages guide](https://www.95percentgroup.com/insights/the-stages-of-literacy-development-a-complete-guide/) (accessed 2026-07-06); a published critique notes Ehri (1994) tied phases loosely to school years, with the consolidated phase beginning "during the second or third year" of school ([Beech 2005 critique](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227683974_Ehri%27s_model_of_phases_of_learning_to_read_A_brief_critique), accessed 2026-07-06).

| Phase | Typical age/grade (convention anchors) | What the child does |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-alphabetic | PreK to early K (about 36 to 66mo) | Reads words by visual cues (logo, shape, a picture), not letters; recognizes "stop" on the sign but not on a card |
| Partial alphabetic | K to early grade 1 (about 60 to 78mo) | Uses some letter-sound connections, typically first and last letters ("phonetic cue reading"); cannot yet decode systematically; confuses similar spellings |
| Full alphabetic | About end of grade 1 (about 84mo) | Forms complete grapheme-phoneme connections; decodes one-syllable regular words across patterns (closed, silent e, open, r-controlled, vowel teams); orthographic mapping stores words |
| Consolidated alphabetic | About end of grade 2 into grade 3 (about 96 to 108mo) | Reads by multi-letter chunks (syllables, affixes, morphemes, rimes); decodes consonant-le and regular two-syllable words automatically; rapid fluency growth |

### Chall's stages of reading development (1983)

FACT, all rows from the Annenberg Learner reproduction of Chall's own table (Jeanne S. Chall, Stages of Reading Development, McGraw-Hill, 1983; [learner.org PDF](https://www.learner.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/RWD.DLU1_.ChallsStages.pdf), downloaded and text-extracted 2026-07-06).

| Stage | Chall's age/grade | End-of-stage capability | Reading vs listening |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0, Pre-reading ("pseudo reading") | 6 months to 6 years, preschool | Pretend-reads, retells a familiar book from pages, names letters, recognizes some signs, prints own name | Understands thousands of heard words by age 6 but reads few if any |
| 1, Initial reading and decoding | 6 to 7 years, grade 1 to beginning grade 2 | Learns letter-sound relations, reads simple text with high-frequency and phonically regular words, sounds out new one-syllable words | Can understand about 4000 or more words heard but read about 600 |
| 2, Confirmation and fluency | 7 to 8 years, grades 2 to 3 | Reads simple familiar stories with increasing fluency, consolidating decoding, sight vocabulary, and context | About 3000 words can be read and understood, about 9000 known when heard; listening still stronger |
| 3, Reading to learn the new (phase A grades 4 to 6, phase B grades 7 to 9) | 9 to 13 years, grades 4 to 8 | Reads to gain new knowledge from textbooks, reference works, trade books, generally one viewpoint | Listening still stronger at start of stage; by end, reading and listening about equal for strong readers |
| 4, Multiple viewpoints | 15 to 17 years, grades 10 to 12 | Reads widely across complex expository and narrative materials with multiple viewpoints | Reading comprehension surpasses listening for difficult material |
| 5, Construction and reconstruction | 18 plus, college and beyond | Reads for one's own purposes, integrates and synthesizes knowledge, rapid and efficient | Reading more efficient than listening |

INFERENCE: the famous "learning to read, then reading to learn" pivot is the Chall stage 2 to 3 boundary, about age 8 to 9 (grade 3 to 4, 108 to 120mo by the folder convention). Ehri's consolidated phase and Chall's stage 2 describe the same window from different angles (word recognition mechanics vs reading purpose).

## 11. Reading onset: the normal range for learning to read

Confidence: High that the range is wide; Med on exact boundaries (definitions of "reading" vary).

FACTS:

- "Children become readers step by step. By age 7, most children are reading. Some take longer than others, and some need extra help." From the parent guide Helping Your Child Become a Reader, distributed by Reading Rockets ([guide PDF](https://www.readingrockets.org/sites/default/files/guide/English.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).
- PBS Kids parent milestones state most children have "learned to read" by age 7 while still building fluency and comprehension after that ([PBS Kids reading at age 7](https://www.pbs.org/parents/learn-grow/age-7/literacy/reading), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Nemours KidsHealth places sounding out and decoding unfamiliar words in its "first and second grade (ages 6 to 7)" band and stresses that "kids develop at different paces and spend varying amounts of time at each stage" ([KidsHealth reading milestones](https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/milestones.html), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Suggate, Schaughency, and Reese (2012, Early Childhood Research Quarterly): New Zealand children starting formal reading instruction at age 5 vs age 7 (n = 287, six-year span). The age-5 group had initially superior letter naming, nonword, word, and passage reading, but "this difference in reading skill disappeared by age 11"; in a second cross-sectional sample the later starters showed similar fluency and somewhat better reading comprehension. The authors caution the design is non-experimental ([article PDF](https://www.vauxhall.school.nz/application/files/1316/5664/1699/Suggate-Schaughency-Reese-2012-ECRQ-1.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06; [ScienceDirect abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0885200612000397), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Practice guidance quoted by Parents.com: reading disabilities are often not diagnosed before age 7, and grade 3 (about age 8) is the conventional "learning to read to reading to learn" turning point ([Parents.com, When do kids learn to read](https://www.parents.com/when-do-kids-learn-to-read-8730376), accessed 2026-07-06). This matches the Chall stage 2 to 3 boundary (section 6).

INFERENCE for Homa: the defensible framing is a wide normal onset window, roughly age 5 to 7 plus, with independent decoding typically consolidating between 6 and 8 (72 to 96mo anchors). A homeschooled 6-year-old who is not yet decoding sits inside the documented normal range, and the Suggate line of evidence says a later start, by itself, does not predict weaker outcomes by late elementary. The counterweight (also FACT, section 9): US institutional expectations are earlier (CCSS has kindergartners reading emergent-reader texts), and persistent difficulty past about age 7 to 8 is when screening guidance stops attributing gaps to normal variation. Both truths belong in any product surface logic, though never in guilt-inducing language.

## Dry wells and method notes

- Hasbrouck and Tindal 2006 norms extended to grade 8; the 2017 update covers only grades 1 to 6. Grades 7 to 8 oral fluency here rests on DIBELS 8 goals instead (section 4). A follow-up pass could add the 2006 grade 7 to 8 rows (they appear in the same tech report) if wanted, clearly marked as the older edition.
- Read Naturally's chart page lists "compiled norms 2023" (grades 1 to 8) alongside 2017; I did not verify the 2023 table cell by cell in the time box. Worth a follow-up: it would restore grades 7 to 8 with current data.
- The official F and P Instructional Level Expectations chart was verified from a Pearson-hosted printing whose interval labels use southern hemisphere months; the US-labeled original sits behind free registration at fountasandpinnell.com (resource 334). Level values matched the widely reproduced US chart, so confidence stays High, but a registered pull of the US original would be cleaner.
- DRA end-of-year benchmarks are district-set; the grade 4 to 8 values (40, 50, 60, 60 to 80) come from teacher-facing correlation charts, not from the publisher (Pearson does not publish a free official DRA-by-grade table). Med confidence, marked in section 5b.
- ATOS has no published K range (Renaissance's per-grade table starts at grade 1).
- Nagy and Anderson 1984's famous "88,500 word families in printed school English" figure was not directly verified from a fetched primary source in the time box, so it is omitted from section 10a; only their morphology (60 percent) estimate, verified via the Nagy and Scott chapter, is used.
- Per-grade silent reading rate percentile tables (like Carver 1990's rauding rates by grade) were not verified; section 10b uses Spichtig et al. 2016 grade means (even grades only) plus quartile spreads from the 2019 companion paper.
- NAEP 2026 reading was administered in early 2026 but results are not published as of 2026-07-06; section 9 uses 2024, the latest released.
- Fry per-grade targets (100 per grade) trace to tutoring practice, not to a state document; I found no state standard that names Fry or Dolch counts per grade, which is itself a finding (section 7 marks the targets Med).
- CCSS grade 9 to 10 RI standards beyond RI.9-10.8 were extracted but summarized only briefly in section 8 to stay within the time box; the RL side is complete.
