# Arts, physical education, and health benchmarks, ages 2 to 16

> Historical research snapshot, 2026-07-06. This file preserves the evidence and analysis captured on that date. It is not current product, legal, provider, standards, or production guidance. Re-verify volatile facts and source freshness before porting any claim into Homa.

**Date:** 2026-07-06
**Lane:** Arts, PE, and health 07, part of the ages 2 to 16 national benchmark corpus (see README.md in this folder)
**Method:** Web search plus direct fetches of primary standards documents: nationalartsstandards.org at-a-glance PDFs (all five disciplines), NAfME 2014 music strand PDFs, shapeamerica.org 2024 PE and health standards pages (full indicator listings), the official California PFT FitnessGram HFZ tables, health.gov guidelines, AASM consensus PDF, AAP/HealthyChildren pages, and peer-reviewed motor development and music education literature. Every number was checked against a fetched source; unverifiable items are logged in dry wells rather than estimated.

**Key findings:**

- Both flagship 2024 frameworks (SHAPE PE and SHAPE health education) deliberately abandoned per-grade outcomes for grade-span indicators (PreK-2, 3-5, 6-8, 9-12), reasoning that development is "age-related and not age dependent." Arts standards (2014) still run grade-by-grade PreK to 8, so arts is the finest-grained national ladder in this lane.
- The NCAS music notation ladder is a clean, home-observable progression: rhythm-only reading in grade 1, rhythm plus melody in grade 2, full standard notation by grade 5, sight-reading simple treble or bass notation by grade 8.
- Lowenfeld's drawing stages (scribbling 2 to 4, preschematic 4 to 7, schematic 7 to 9, dawning realism 9 to 11, pseudo-naturalistic 11 to 13, decision 14+) remain the art education reference; tadpole figures at about 3, baseline and x-ray drawings at 7 to 9 are the sharpest single-age anchors.
- Motor development has a large potential-vs-observed gap: children can reach mature form in most fundamental skills by 5 or 6 with practice, but observed mastery or near mastery of the overhand throw at age 12 is only 26.7% in a 2,098-child TGMD-3 study, and overall proficiency declines after age 10.
- FitnessGram's current aerobic standards are VO2max-based and only exist for ages 10 and up; ages 5 to 9 are participation-only by design. Musculoskeletal HFZ minimums exist from age 5.
- Girls' and boys' FitnessGram standards diverge sharply in adolescence: boys' VO2max cutoff rises 40.2 to 44.1 from 10 to 16 while girls' declines to 38.9; girls' push-up minimum stays flat at 7 while boys' climbs to 18.
- Sleep (AASM 2016: 10 to 13 hours at 3 to 5, 9 to 12 at 6 to 12, 8 to 10 at 13 to 18) and AAP screens (1-hour quality limit at 2 to 5, plan-based rather than hour-based from 5 up) are the two firmest self_care anchors in this lane.
- AAP swim guidance: lessons can start at age 1, most children ready by the 4th birthday, front crawl typically mastered by 5 or 6; two-wheeler riding typically 4 to 7.

## 2. National Core Arts Standards: structure, processes, anchor standards

Confidence: High. The National Core Arts Standards (NCAS) were released in June 2014 by the National Coalition for Core Arts Standards, replacing the 1994 National Standards for Arts Education. They cover five disciplines (dance, media arts, music, theatre, visual arts), PreK to 12, organized by four artistic processes and 11 anchor standards shared across all disciplines ([nationalartsstandards.org](https://www.nationalartsstandards.org/), accessed 2026-07-06; [NCCAS N2KN brief](https://nationalartsstandards.org/sites/default/files/2023-03/N2KN%20Vol%20I%20Issue%203-%20Anchor%20Standards.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06). FACT.

Discipline-specific performance standards sit under the anchors at each grade level PreK to 8, then at three high school proficiency levels (Proficient, Accomplished, Advanced). FACT ([nationalartsstandards.org](https://www.nationalartsstandards.org/), accessed 2026-07-06).

## 6. Drawing development stages (Lowenfeld) and figure drawing milestones

Confidence: High for stage names and ages (primary text verified), Med for the finer figure-drawing ages (secondary sources). Viktor Lowenfeld's Creative and Mental Growth (first edition 1947) is the canonical art education stage framework. Stage names and age ranges below match the book's own chapter titles ([Creative and Mental Growth full text, archive.org](https://archive.org/stream/creativementalgr00/creativementalgr00_djvu.txt), accessed 2026-07-06) and the education encyclopedia summary ([StateUniversity.com Lowenfeld entry](https://education.stateuniversity.com/pages/2195/Lowenfeld-Viktor-1903-1960.html), accessed 2026-07-06). FACT. Some modern retellings shift ranges by a year or two (for example dawning realism 9 to 12); the table uses Lowenfeld's own ranges.

| Stage | Ages (years) | Approx months | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scribbling | 2 to 4 | 24 to 48 | Substages: disordered (uncontrolled marks), controlled (longitudinal repeated motions), circular, then naming of scribbles, where the child tells stories about marks. The shift to naming marks a change from kinesthetic thinking to imaginative picture thinking |
| Preschematic | 4 to 7 | 48 to 84 | First representational attempts. Circular head with lines for limbs; objects float on the page, unrelated to each other; color choice is emotional, not realistic |
| Schematic | 7 to 9 | 84 to 108 | A repeated schema (formula) for each object; baseline and skyline organize space; x-ray (see-through) drawings; important things drawn larger; little overlapping |
| Dawning realism (gang age) | 9 to 11 | 108 to 132 | Process gives way to product; overlapping and early depth appear; more detail; child becomes markedly self-critical; peer group ("gang") matters |
| Pseudo-naturalistic (stage of reasoning) | 11 to 13 | 132 to 156 | Final product focus; attempts at light, shadow, proportion, perspective; Lowenfeld splits learners into visual (draws appearances) and haptic (draws felt, subjective experience) types |
| Period of decision (crisis of adolescence) | 14 and older | 168+ | The adolescent decides whether to keep making art; without perceived skill many stop; instruction should differentiate visual vs haptic learners |

Stage descriptions synthesized from the chapter text of Creative and Mental Growth (accessed 2026-07-06 via archive.org), the StateUniversity encyclopedia entry (accessed 2026-07-06), and art education lecture summaries ([users.totalise.co.uk children's art lecture notes on Lowenfeld 1978 edition](http://www.users.totalise.co.uk/~kbroom/Lectures/children.htm), accessed 2026-07-06). FACT for structure, INFERENCE for the compressed phrasing.

## 9. Fundamental movement skills: acquisition and mastery ages

Confidence: Med-High. Motor development sources agree on the skill inventory and the broad claim, but exact mastery ages vary by study and by how mastery is defined. Two key framings:

- Gallahue's fundamental movement phase runs through initial (about 2 to 3), elementary (about 4 to 5), and proficient stages, and "the majority of available data ... suggests that children can and should be at the proficient stage by age 5 or 6 in most fundamental skills," given practice and instruction; skills are age-related, not age-determined. FACT ([comparison of Gallahue's hourglass and Clark and Metcalfe's mountain models, Advances in Physical Education 2017](https://doi.org/10.4236/ape.2017.73018), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Observed mastery is far later and lower than that potential: in 2,098 Irish children 5 to 12 assessed with TGMD-3, mastery or near mastery ranged from 16% (overhand throw) to 75.3% (run), improving to about age 10 then declining. FACT ([Behan et al., Moving Well-Being Well, Journal of Sports Sciences 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2019.1651144), accessed 2026-07-06).

The standard skill inventory (Ulrich's Test of Gross Motor Development, TGMD-2/3, validated ages 3 to 10): locomotor: run, gallop, hop, leap, horizontal jump, slide (TGMD-3 adds skip); object control: overhand throw, underhand roll, stationary dribble, two-hand strike, catch, kick. FACT ([TGMD-2 description in J Sports Sci Med](https://www.jssm.org/jssm-10-157.xml%3EFulltext), accessed 2026-07-06). Gallahue adds a third construct, stability (balancing, rolling, bending, twisting). FACT ([Rudd et al. 2015, PLoS ONE via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4607429/), accessed 2026-07-06).

## 14. Sleep and screen time guidance by age

Confidence: High. These anchor Homa's self_care domain as well.

| Age | Recommended sleep per 24 hours | Months span |
|---|---|---|

| 1 to 2 years | 11 to 14 hours, including naps | 12 to 35 |
| 3 to 5 years | 10 to 13 hours, including naps | 36 to 71 |
| 6 to 12 years | 9 to 12 hours | 72 to 155 |
| 13 to 18 years | 8 to 10 hours | 156 to 227 |

Source: [AASM consensus statement PDF](https://aasm.org/resources/pdf/pediatricsleepdurationconsensus.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06; AAP endorsement: [AAP News, AAP endorses new recommendations on sleep times](https://publications.aap.org/aapnews/news/6630/AAP-endorses-new-recommendations-on-sleep-times), accessed 2026-07-06. FACT. FACT.

## Dry wells and method notes

- FitnessGram PACER lap-count HFZ table (current standards): not published as laps; the software converts laps to VO2max. The legacy 2004 boys' table scan shows lap columns (for example age 10, 20m PACER 23 to 61 laps) but column alignment in the scan is ambiguous and no equally citable girls' lap table was found, so lap counts were not tabulated. Follow-up: obtain the Cooper Institute FitnessGram Administration Manual (5th edition) tables directly.
- California PFT HFZ PDF has two garbled cells (likely OCR): female BMI HFZ at ages 5 and 7 print as "16.8 to 16.3" and "17.9 to 16.6" (source PDF shows these ranges with the upper bound where the lower bound should be), and female percent-fat NI-Health Risk at age 12 prints as "≥ 3.5" (missing digit, progression implies about 35.5). These cells were excluded or flagged rather than reconstructed.
- WHO 2020 physical activity guideline (average 60 min/day MVPA for 5 to 17) was stated from general knowledge and marked INFERENCE in section 11; verify against who.int in a follow-up pass.
- AAP one-hour daily physical activity and 8 to 12 hour sleep figures inside the school-age media policy summary come from a slide summary of the policy hosted by the National Academies; the primary Pediatrics policy text (AAP Media Use in School-Aged Children and Adolescents, 2016) should be fetched directly next pass.
- Music: no authoritative single table for "steady beat competence age" exists; the 4 to 5 independent-accuracy claims come from a music education provider synthesis (The Music Class) and UK sector guidance, not a norm study. Gordon's music learning theory audiation stages and Rutkowski's SVDM stage cutoffs would strengthen this; not fetched this pass.
- Theatre, dance, media arts: per-grade detail exists in the fetched at-a-glance PDFs beyond what section 5 summarizes; deliberately compressed to band level per lane scope.
- NHES: the National Consensus third edition (2022/2024) performance expectations were fetched but not tabulated separately; SHAPE 2024 was used as the primary indicator set since Homa already anchors on SHAPE for PE. The two third editions differ in indicator wording.
- Puberty education timing (conventionally grades 4 to 6) is curriculum convention; no national standards document mandates a grade. Marked INFERENCE in section 13.
- Lowenfeld stage ages: the task brief suggested dawning realism 9 to 12 and pseudo-naturalistic 12 to 14; the primary text says 9 to 11 and 11 to 13. The primary text was followed.
- One-mile run: only the legacy (2004-era) HFZ time table is citable; current FitnessGram intentionally has no time cutoffs. No independent percentile-norm mile-time table for US children was found at a citable primary source this pass.
- Monkey bars: "hangs from monkey bars at 5" is from an OT clinic checklist (Australian); traversing rung-over-rung has no citable age table found this pass.
- Instrument readiness: the Delzell PDF (UW hosting) is the best single citable summary of US band/strings start grades; state practice varies (grade 4 to 6 for band by state chair report).
