# Assessment norms and the homeschool testing landscape

**Date:** 2026-07-06
**Lane:** Assessment norms 10, part of the ages 2 to 16 national benchmark corpus (see README.md in this folder)
**Method:** Web research with primary-source verification: NCES and nationsreportcard.gov for NAEP, NWEA's published 2020 and 2025 norms documents, MetaMetrics (lexile.com, hub.lexile.com, metametricsinc.com), Renaissance support pages, homeschool provider sites (Seton Testing, Academic Excellence, Homeschool Boss, achievement-test.com), state statutes and education-department pages, worldbook.com, and publisher placement pages. Every number was transcribed from a fetched page and carries an inline link with access date; nothing is from memory.

**Key findings:**

- NAEP 2024: 31% of grade 4 students scored at or above NAEP Proficient in reading and 40% below NAEP Basic; reading averages fell 5 points from 2019 at both grades 4 and 8, math fell 3 points (grade 4) and 8 points (grade 8). NAEP Proficient is explicitly NOT "on grade level."
- NWEA replaced its 2020 MAP norms with 2025 norms (116 million scores, fall 2022 to spring 2024). National means dropped, so the same RIT now earns a higher percentile: which norms edition a family's report used is load-bearing metadata.
- "A year of growth" varies by an order of magnitude across grades: typical fall-to-spring reading growth is 12 RIT in grade 1 but 1 to 2 RIT in grades 7 to 10; ORF gains compress the same way. Grade-blind "one year of growth" language is statistically wrong.
- The homeschool compliance-testing market clusters at $25 to $60 per student (CAT $25 to $37, Iowa Form E $45 with 2024 norms, Stanford 10 $45, MAP via remote proctors $45 to $60); the popular CAT Survey still uses 1986 norms, a four-decade-old reference population.
- Roughly a dozen states require testing or evaluation, with thresholds written in percentiles and stanines: North Dakota 30th percentile (assessment trigger) and 50th (monitoring), Oregon 15th, Virginia and West Virginia 4th stanine (23rd percentile).
- Grade-equivalent scores are the most misread number in test reports: a GE of 6.2 for a fourth grader describes performance on fourth-grade material, not readiness for sixth grade, per the test publishers themselves.
- Lexile publishes two different grade tables (typical reader interquartile ranges and CCSS stretch text bands); they diverge most in middle grades and must never be conflated.
- Curriculum placement tests (Saxon 54 to 87, Math-U-See Greek letters, Dimensions Math, Math Mammoth) are criterion-referenced curriculum positions wearing grade labels, useful signals but never convertible to percentiles.

## 1. NAEP achievement levels and 2024 national results

Confidence: High. Sources: [NCES, NAEP reading achievement levels](https://nces.ed.gov/nationsreportcard/reading/achieve.aspx) (accessed 2026-07-06); [NCES, NAEP mathematics achievement levels](https://nces.ed.gov/nationsreportcard/mathematics/achieve.aspx) (accessed 2026-07-06); [NCES 2021-036, Mapping state proficiency standards onto the NAEP scales, table 3](https://nces.ed.gov/nationsreportcard/subject/publications/studies/pdf/2021036.pdf) (accessed 2026-07-06); [nationsreportcard.gov 2024 reading national trends](https://www.nationsreportcard.gov/reports/reading/2024/g4_8/national-trends/) (accessed 2026-07-06); [nationsreportcard.gov 2024 mathematics results](https://www.nationsreportcard.gov/reports/mathematics/2024/g4_8/) (accessed 2026-07-06).

### 1a. How NAEP achievement levels work

FACT (from the NCES pages above): NAEP reports on a 0 to 500 scale for reading and math at grades 4 and 8. Results are reported as percentages of students at or above three achievement levels set by the National Assessment Governing Board: NAEP Basic (partial mastery of fundamental skills), NAEP Proficient (solid academic performance, competency over challenging subject matter), NAEP Advanced. Levels are cumulative. Two official caveats NCES itself prints: the levels are "to be used on a trial basis and should be interpreted and used with caution," and NAEP Proficient "does not represent grade-level proficiency as determined by other assessment standards (e.g., state or district assessments)." NAEP Proficient is a high bar, well above typical state "on grade level" cut points.

FACT: NAEP is a group-level assessment. Individual students never receive NAEP scores, so NAEP is context for national distribution, never a per-child benchmark.

NCES notes these cut scores have been maintained without change since their establishment in the early 1990s. Scores are not comparable across grades or subjects.

### 1d. Trend context since 2019 (the pandemic-era decline)

FACT, per [K-12 Dive summary of the January 2025 NAEP release](https://www.k12dive.com/news/2024-naep-reading-math-scores-drop-pre-pandemic/738535/) (accessed 2026-07-06), cross-consistent with the nationsreportcard.gov pages above: between 2019 and 2024, average reading scores fell 5 points at both grade 4 and grade 8; average math scores fell 3 points at grade 4 and 8 points at grade 8. Grade 4 math recovered about 2 points from 2022 to 2024; grade 8 math and both reading grades did not significantly recover. Declines concentrated among lower-performing students: grade 4 reading below-Basic reached 40% in 2024, versus 34% in 2019 ([Education Next analysis of NAEP 2024](https://www.educationnext.org/low-performing-students-fall-farther-behind-the-pack-naep-reading-achievement-levels/), accessed 2026-07-06).

INFERENCE: for Homa, the NAEP relevance is distributional context only: "on grade level" claims anchored to state standards sit well below the NAEP Proficient bar, and national percentages say nothing about an individual homeschooled child.

## 2. NWEA MAP Growth RIT norms

Confidence: High. Sources: [NWEA, 2025 MAP Growth norms quick reference](https://www.nwea.org/resource-center/fact-sheet/87992/MAP-Growth-2025-norms-quick-reference_NWEA_onesheet.pdf/) (accessed 2026-07-06); [NWEA, 2020 MAP Growth normative data overview](https://teach.mapnwea.org/impl/2020MAPGrowthNormativeDataOverview.pdf) (accessed 2026-07-06); [NWEA, MAP Growth norms technical manual (2025)](https://www.nwea.org/resource-center/white-paper/88182/MAP-Growth-Norms_NWEA_Technical-Manual.pdf/) (accessed 2026-07-06); [NWEA Connection, introducing the MAP Growth 2025 norms](https://connection.nwea.org/s/nwea-news/introducing-the-new-map-growth-2025-norms-MCYIDN7W53LNCONBOGLKOM2WI75Y?language=en_US) (accessed 2026-07-06).

### 2a. How the RIT scale and the norms work

FACT: MAP Growth reports on the RIT (Rasch Unit) scale, an equal-interval scale where a point means the same amount of learning anywhere on the scale, which is what makes growth arithmetic legitimate. Norms are time-continuous and assume default instructional weeks before testing: fall = week 4, winter = week 20, spring = week 32. Two norm families exist: student norms (compare a child to US students in the same grade and season) and school norms (compare a grade-level aggregate to other schools). Source: the 2020 and 2025 NWEA documents cited above.

FACT (edition history): the current norms are the 2025 MAP Growth norms, derived from about 116 million scores of 13.8 million students across 30,000+ schools, six testing terms from fall 2022 through spring 2024. They replaced the 2020 norms (3.6 to 5.5 million scores per subject-grade from 500,000 to 700,000 students in 24,500+ public schools) on all MAP reports; 2020 norms remain only in data export files. NWEA notes that under the 2025 norms "the same RIT score will now correspond to a higher percentile rank than it did under the 2020 norms" because post-pandemic national achievement fell, especially among lower-achieving students in reading. Sources: [2025 quick reference](https://www.nwea.org/resource-center/fact-sheet/87992/MAP-Growth-2025-norms-quick-reference_NWEA_onesheet.pdf/) and [NWEA Connection announcement](https://connection.nwea.org/s/nwea-news/introducing-the-new-map-growth-2025-norms-MCYIDN7W53LNCONBOGLKOM2WI75Y?language=en_US) (both accessed 2026-07-06).

INFERENCE: homeschool families who tested through providers before 2025-26 will hold reports normed on the 2020 study; the same RIT maps to a somewhat higher percentile under 2025 norms. Any Homa comparison must record which norms edition a family's report used.

## 3. Lexile and Quantile frameworks

Confidence: High for the tables below (vendor-published); Medium for currency, because the underlying reader norms study is 2010 to 2016 and MetaMetrics refreshes the interactive charts. Sources: [MetaMetrics FAQ, Lexile measures and grade levels](https://lexile.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/08/T1.4.FAQ_LM-and-Grade-Levels.pdf) (accessed 2026-07-06); [hub.lexile.com Lexile grade level charts](https://hub.lexile.com/lexile-grade-level-charts/) (accessed 2026-07-06); [MetaMetrics, how do students receive Quantile measures](https://metametricsinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/Student-Quantile-Measures.pdf) (accessed 2026-07-06); [MetaMetrics, Quantile framework FAQ](https://metametricsinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/Quantile-Framework-FAQs.pdf) (accessed 2026-07-06).

### 3a. How the scales work

FACT: the Lexile framework puts readers and texts on one developmental scale (units "L"). Reader measures below 0L carry the code BR, Beginning Reader: BR150L means 150 units below 0L, so a smaller number after BR is closer to 0L and stronger (the hub charts floor at BR400L). There is no direct correspondence between a Lexile measure and a grade; MetaMetrics publishes descriptive norm ranges only and says so explicitly. Sources: the FAQ and hub pages above.

FACT: the Quantile framework does the same for math: student measures and skill difficulty (QSC, Quantile Skill and Concept, a taxonomy of 550+ skills) share one scale (units "Q"). Below 0Q is coded EM, Emerging Mathematician: EM120Q means -120Q. The scale runs from EM codes below 0Q to above 1600Q, spanning kindergarten content through Algebra II, Geometry, Trigonometry, and Pre-Calculus. Sources: [Quantile FAQ](https://metametricsinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/Quantile-Framework-FAQs.pdf) and [NC DPI Lexile and Quantile overview](https://www.dpi.nc.gov/districts-schools/accountability-and-testing/lexiler-and-quantiler-measures-support-learning-north-carolina) (both accessed 2026-07-06).

## 4. Star Assessments (Renaissance)

Confidence: Medium (scale structure is public and verified; the actual scaled-score-to-percentile tables are not freely published). Sources: [Renaissance, Star Unified Scale support article](https://support.renaissance.com/s/article/Star-Unified-Scale-1752674163255?language=en_US) (accessed 2026-07-06); [Renaissance, Scaled Score article](https://support.renaissance.com/s/article/Scaled-Score-SS-1752697961554?language=en_GB) (accessed 2026-07-06); [Renaissance, transitioning from the Star Enterprise Scale to the Star Unified Scale](https://www.renaissance.com/product_update/transitioning-from-the-star-enterprise-scale-to-the-star-unified-scale/) (accessed 2026-07-06); [renaissance.com Star Assessments overview](https://www.renaissance.com/products/assessment/star-assessments/) (accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT: Star Early Literacy, Star Reading, and Star Math are computer-adaptive tests reporting on the Star Unified Scale, roughly 0 to 1400: Star Early Literacy scores typically fall 200 to 1100, Star Reading and Star Math 600 to 1400. The Unified Scale replaced the older Enterprise Scale (a score on one scale is not a score on the other; percentile ranks are unchanged by the scale switch). All norm-referenced scores (percentile rank 1 to 99, grade equivalent, student growth percentile 1 to 99) derive from the scaled score. A Star Reading Unified score below 615 triggers Renaissance's "probable non-reader" alert.

FACT (paywall note): the scaled-score-to-percentile conversion tables and grade-level benchmark tables live in the Star technical manuals, which are distributed to customers rather than published openly; Renaissance's public materials describe score types but not the norm tables. Third-party sites republish fragments of unclear edition and are not captured here per the no-invention rule.

INFERENCE: Star reaches homeschoolers mostly indirectly (co-ops, hybrid academies, and umbrella schools using Renaissance, plus Accelerated Reader familiarity), so Homa should expect families to bring percentile ranks and grade equivalents from Star reports rather than raw scaled scores.

Untimed administration is a selling point providers advertise explicitly (Academic Excellence sells untimed CAT with untimed-specific norms; MAP is untimed by design).

This is a concrete place where Homa's reporting layer can add honest context.

## 6. Score literacy primer (internal)

Confidence: High. Sources: [University of Minnesota Survey and Assessment Services, statewide testing glossary](https://survey.umn.edu/mstp/public-private-and-charter-schools/glossary) (accessed 2026-07-06); [Riverside Insights, WJ-IV age and grade equivalents vs standard scores and percentile ranks](https://support.riversideinsights.com/support/solutions/articles/70000620442-wj-iv-age-and-grade-equivalents-vs-standard-scores-and-percentile-ranks) (accessed 2026-07-06); [The Assessment Workshop on growth measures and grade equivalents](https://www.assessmentworkshop.com/2022/07/29/measures-of-student-academic-growth-more-problems-than-many-realize/) (accessed 2026-07-06); NWEA and Renaissance documents cited in sections 2 and 4.

### 6a. The score types, side by side

| Score type | What it is | Range and anatomy | Safe uses | Unsafe uses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Raw score | Count of correct answers | Test-specific | Nothing cross-test | Any comparison |
| Scaled score (including RIT, Star Unified, Lexile, Quantile) | Raw performance mapped to a vertical, usually equal-interval scale spanning grades | RIT roughly 100 to 350; Star 0 to 1400; Lexile BR400L to 1600L+; Quantile EM to 1600Q+ | Growth over time on the same scale; equal-interval arithmetic | Comparing across different scales without a linking study |
| Percentile rank (PR) | Percent of same-grade norming peers scoring at or below the child | 1 to 99; NOT percent correct | Standing relative to national peers at one point in time | Averaging; subtracting (not equal-interval: 50th to 60th is a smaller move than 89th to 99th) |
| Stanine | "Standard nine," the normal curve cut into 9 bands (stanine 5 straddles the median; 4th stanine begins at the 23rd percentile) | 1 to 9 | Coarse banding that damps score wobble; several state laws are written in stanines | Fine-grained claims; a stanine hides up to 12+ percentile points |
| Standard score (WJ-IV and similar) | Normal-curve transform, mean 100, SD 15 | Roughly 40 to 160 | Cross-age comparison on one instrument | Reading as an IQ or a percent |
| Grade equivalent (GE) | The grade.month whose TYPICAL student would earn this raw score (8.4 = typical for month 4 of grade 8, on a 10-month year convention) | K.0 to 12.9+ | Rough developmental flavor; year-over-year direction | Placement decisions; arithmetic of any kind; "my child should skip grades" |

### 6b. The grade-equivalent misinterpretation, spelled out

FACT: the University of Minnesota glossary states it directly: a fourth grader earning GE 6.2 on a fourth-grade reading test "demonstrates that his/her developmental level in reading is high relative to his/her fourth-grade peers. However, the test results supply no information about how well he/she would handle the material normally read by students in the early months of sixth grade. It, therefore, cannot be concluded that he/she is ready for placement in the sixth grade." GEs are not an equal-interval scale and "cannot be added, subtracted, or averaged." Source: [UMN glossary](https://survey.umn.edu/mstp/public-private-and-charter-schools/glossary) (accessed 2026-07-06). The Assessment Workshop makes the same point from the test-construction side: a GE of 6.3 for a fourth grader describes performance on FOURTH grade content, content the child was never tested beyond ([source](https://www.assessmentworkshop.com/2022/07/29/measures-of-student-academic-growth-more-problems-than-many-realize/), accessed 2026-07-06). Riverside (WJ-IV publisher) lists "they are often misinterpreted" as a caution on its own age and grade equivalents, and notes GEs are median-based while standard scores and percentiles come from a different transformation, so the two can legitimately disagree ([source](https://support.riversideinsights.com/support/solutions/articles/70000620442-wj-iv-age-and-grade-equivalents-vs-standard-scores-and-percentile-ranks), accessed 2026-07-06).

### 6c. Norm-referenced vs criterion-referenced

FACT: a norm-referenced score locates a child in a distribution of other children (percentile, stanine, GE, NCE); a criterion-referenced score locates a child against a fixed content standard (state proficiency levels, NAEP achievement levels, a phonics mastery check). The same test event can emit both (MAP reports RIT and percentile, and also projects proficiency on state tests via linking studies; NAEP reports a scale score and cut-score levels). The norming population and its year always matter for norm-referenced claims: a percentile is a claim about a specific reference population (see section 5's 1986-normed CAT).

INFERENCE (Homa design relevance): percentile and GE are the two numbers homeschool parents most often carry in from provider reports, and GE is the one most likely to be over-read. Any Homa surface that ingests test reports should translate GE into "performed like a typical grade X.Y student on grade-level material," never into placement language.

### 10b. Why these matter for benchmarking

INFERENCE: curriculum placement tests are criterion-referenced mastery checks wearing grade labels, and homeschool parents routinely quote them as evidence of level ("she placed into Saxon 76," "he's in Epsilon"). They are honest about a child's position in one curriculum's sequence and nothing else: Math-U-See's own framing (one concept per level) means "Delta" says "ready for division," not "grade 4 overall." Homa should treat placement-test outcomes as curriculum-position signals, useful and parent-volunteered, and never convert them to percentile-style claims.

## Product porting notes

Homa's shipped map bank has 15 domains: math, literacy, phonics, writing, language, science, social_studies, art, music, gross_motor, fine_motor, social_emotional, self_care, executive_function, spanish. This lane feeds the reporting layer (FR-7 dashboard, FR-8 weekly notes, months-ahead displays) rather than any single domain: it supplies the external norm systems that Homa's internal age-anchor claims can be sanity-checked against, chiefly for math, literacy, phonics, and language.

How these norms calibrate the reporting layer:

- Months-ahead displays: MAP mean RIT by grade (section 2b) gives a national ruler with roughly 12-month spacing between grade means. A child's months-ahead estimate in math or literacy can be cross-checked against how many RIT points separate adjacent grade means at that age (for example, grade 3 to grade 4 fall math means are 13 RIT apart, so "about a year ahead" should correspond to roughly one grade-mean gap, not a fixed score delta). The same logic applies with Lexile and Quantile medians (sections 3b, 3c).
- Weekly-note growth claims: the growth tables (2c, 9e) are the reality check on "a year of growth" language. Typical growth shrinks sharply with age: claiming "a year of reading growth this semester" means about 6 RIT for a 4th grader but only 2 for an 8th grader. Weekly notes should scale growth praise to the child's band, and per the anti-guilt display law (BUILD.md law 4), flat middle-school reading scores are typical and must never be framed as "behind."
- Norms-edition hygiene: any ingested MAP report needs its norms edition (2020 vs 2025) recorded, since identical RITs map to different percentiles (2a, 2d). Any ingested CAT percentile is against 1986 norms (5a). Percentile claims without a norming-population year are not comparable.
- Vocabulary translation: section 6 is the copy law source for ingesting parent-reported scores. GE never becomes placement language; percentile never becomes percent-correct; stanine bands may be usefully coarse for reassure-first display.
- Compliance awareness: for families in section 7 states, the reporting layer knows which percentile thresholds exist (ND 30th/50th, OR 15th, VA/WV 23rd), always marked "not legal advice, product re-verifies before display."
