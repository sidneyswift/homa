# Curriculum crosswalk: math

**Date:** 2026-07-06
**Lane:** Curriculum crosswalk 13 (math), part of the ages 2 to 16 national benchmark corpus (see README.md in this folder)
**Method:** Publisher-first web research: scope-and-sequence pages and PDFs, placement test pages, and catalog pages from hmhco.com, singaporemath.com, mathusee.com, mathmammoth.com, rightstartmath.com, teachingtextbooks.com, beastacademy.com, goodandbeautiful.com, abeka.com, christianlight.org, and aop.com, with Cathy Duffy and homeschool comparison sites as labeled secondary sources. Every claim carries an inline link with access date; level-to-bank mappings are marked FACT or INFERENCE.

**Key findings:**

- The ten programs use five different labeling systems (grade numbers, dual-grade fractions, A/B semester halves, Greek letters, letter levels), but their CONTENT ladders are nearly identical: times-tables closes grade 3, long-multiply closes grade 4, fractions-decimals closes grade 5 in almost every program, which makes the Homa math bank a workable universal translation layer.
- Saxon's famous "5/4 = grade 5 average or grade 4 accelerated" community reading is real, but the publisher's own placement guide treats the SECOND digit as the default: "Math 5/4 for fourth grade, Math 6/5 for fifth grade." A Saxon level name alone under-determines grade by a full year.
- The publisher of Singapore Math states its grades "align with U.S. standard grades" and never claims "half a year ahead"; the ahead-ness lives in placement reality (publisher: transferring students commonly start a level early; a recommended provider: testing "often places a student one or two levels lower") and in packaging (Dimensions Math 7 plus 8 = pre-algebra plus Algebra 1, a year early).
- Math-U-See is the only program whose ladder ORDER diverges, not just its pace: multi-digit multiplication arrives a year early (Gamma, nominal grade 3) while decimals arrive a year late (Zeta, nominal grade 6, because Epsilon is fractions only), so MUS levels need item-level mapping, never grade translation.
- Teaching Textbooks' "runs about a grade behind" reputation is a community claim the publisher never makes, but it is well documented (forum threads, comparison sites) and families act on it by placing children one level up; Beast Academy is its mirror image, with the publisher itself recommending starting "about a level 'behind' their current grade level."
- The Good and the Beautiful is the only publisher that explicitly disclaims advanced positioning ("aligns closely with standard grade levels and is not considered an advanced curriculum") and the only one to cap at Math 8 with a published exit ramp to other high school programs.
- Placement test philosophies split three ways and the difference matters for interpretation: exit tests of the prior level (Math Mammoth, Dimensions Math K to 5, Saxon's banded test), entry readiness tests (Primary Mathematics 2022, Teaching Textbooks, Horizons), and skill interviews with no grade framing at all (Math-U-See). The same "passed the level 4 test" sentence means different things in each system.
- Abeka runs about a year hot (fractions AND decimals arithmetic in grade 3), CLE modestly ahead with below-grade-first testing advice, Horizons about a half grade hot per reviewers (its publisher statement was not found this pass), and CLE's grade 7 to 8 pivot to consumer math plus pre-algebra is unique among the ten.

## 1. Saxon Math (Houghton Mifflin Harcourt)

Confidence: High for level sequence, naming convention, and placement mechanics (publisher PDF and publisher-approved descriptions); Medium for per-level topic detail (publisher-approved lesson-plan descriptions on a partner site, labeled secondary).

FACT (structure): Saxon is a spiral, incremental K to 12 program: concepts arrive in small increments, and mixed practice reviews everything all year. The homeschool sequence is Math K, 1, 2, 3, then Math 5/4, 6/5, 7/6, 8/7, then Algebra 1/2 (optional), Algebra 1, Algebra 2, Advanced Mathematics, Calculus, with Geometry available as a separate course. Saxon "has been designed for students to complete one level each year" and recommends 80% mastery before moving up. Math 5/4 through 8/7 texts have 120 lessons plus 12 investigations. Sources: [HMH Saxon Math homeschool scope-and-sequence introduction PDF](https://www.hmhco.com/~/media/sites/home/education/global/pdf/scope-and-sequence/mathematics/k-12/saxon-math-homeschool/shs_ssintroduction.pdf?la=en) (accessed 2026-07-06); [HMH Saxon Math program page](https://www.hmhco.com/programs/saxon-math) (accessed 2026-07-06). The classroom edition sells as Primary K to 3, Intermediate 3 to 5, Courses 1 to 3, and Algebra 1, Geometry, Algebra 2 (same program page); homeschool families overwhelmingly use the K to 3 plus 5/4-and-up ladder below.

FACT (the naming convention): the official Saxon homeschool placement guide states "the best placement for most students is to start the year in the textbook designed for students at that grade level (Math 5/4 for fourth grade, Math 6/5 for fifth grade, Math 7/6 for sixth grade, Math 8/7 for seventh grade, and Algebra 1/2 for eighth grade)." Source: [Saxon Homeschool Middle Grades Placement Test guide PDF, publisher document mirrored by retailers](https://chcweb.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Saxon-placement-test-middlegrades.pdf) (accessed 2026-07-06). SECONDARY (the community reading of the two digits): the two digits name the two audiences, second digit = on-pace student, first digit = student a year older who needs the review year, so 5/4 reads "grade 5 average or grade 4 accelerated"; note the publisher's own placement guide treats the SECOND digit as the default grade. Sources: [Orison Orchards, Saxon levels explainer](https://orisonorchards.com/7-saxon-math-answers-homeschool/) and [Jen Merckling, Is Saxon Math right for your homeschool](https://jenmerckling.com/saxon-math/) (both accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT (placement tests): free placement tests exist for K to 3, middle grades (one test spanning 5/4 through Algebra 1/2), and upper grades. The middle-grades test is 100 questions in level-bands of 20; scoring is by band, for example "sixteen or more correct from Questions 1-20: begin Math 6/5" and "fifteen or fewer correct from Questions 1-20 and the student is an average-to-accelerated fourth grader: begin Math 5/4." No calculator, untimed, work shown. It is "not intended for use with current Saxon students." Source: [placement guide PDF](https://chcweb.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Saxon-placement-test-middlegrades.pdf) (accessed 2026-07-06); an older Saxon Publishers edition of the same test uses 10-question bands ([mirror](https://www.homeschoolpartners.net/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Saxon-Placement-Tests-Middle-Grade-Math.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).

SECONDARY (Algebra 1/2 vs 8/7): both are pre-algebra; Math 8/7 (Hake) is the gentler middle-school on-ramp, Algebra 1/2 (John Saxon) the faster older-student version; students who do well in 8/7 typically skip Algebra 1/2 and go straight to Algebra 1. Source: [Orison Orchards](https://orisonorchards.com/7-saxon-math-answers-homeschool/) (accessed 2026-07-06).

Per-level topics below are from publisher-approved lesson-plan descriptions at [Homeschool Planet's Saxon Math page](https://homeschoolplanet.com/lesson-plan-family/saxon-math/) (accessed 2026-07-06), labeled SECONDARY (publisher-approved partner copy, not the scope-and-sequence PDFs themselves).

## 2. Singapore Math (Primary Mathematics 2022 and Dimensions Math)

Confidence: High (publisher pages fetched for structure, placement, and program relationships; per-level topics summarized at strand level from publisher program descriptions).

FACT (level structure): Singapore Math Inc. sells two core programs. Dimensions Math runs PK to 8: PKA/PKB through 5B in A/B semester halves ("1A is the material for the first half of the year, and 1B is the material for the second half"), then single-volume Dimensions Math 6, 7, 8. Primary Mathematics 2022 Edition is a K to 5 program in A/B halves (older US and Standards editions run 1A to 6B and remain on sale). "Our grades align with U.S. standard grades. For example, Dimensions Math 1A and 1B are for Grade 1." Sources: [singaporemath.com placement tests page](https://www.singaporemath.com/pages/placement-tests) (accessed 2026-07-06); [singaporemath.com homeschool FAQ](https://www.singaporemath.com/pages/faq) (accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT (method): Concrete, Pictorial, Abstract progression, number bonds, bar modeling, and heavy mental math are the signature methods across both programs ([FAQ](https://www.singaporemath.com/pages/faq), accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT (the middle-school pivot): "Dimensions Math 7 and 8 together cover Pre-algebra and Algebra 1 topics, with some geometry. Dimensions Math 6 ... is a Grade 6 text." The two are "meant to be done as a set," and after grade 8 the publisher says students move to Geometry and Algebra 2; Singapore math programs do not continue past grade 8. Source: [FAQ](https://www.singaporemath.com/pages/faq) (accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT (placement tests): free, untimed (allow at least an hour), and explicitly "not the only factor." Two different test philosophies coexist: Dimensions Math K to 5 tests (and the older Primary Mathematics US and Standards tests) are mastery tests of the level named on the test ("if a student successfully completes the 2A test, then they have likely mastered the material in 2A"), while Primary Mathematics 2022 tests are readiness tests for entering the level named. No placement tests exist for Dimensions Math 7 or 8; the publisher gives completion rules instead: finished any grade 5 program, start DM 6; finished a grade 6 program with solid understanding, start DM 7. The publisher also states the tests "were not designed to be exit tests." Source: [placement tests page](https://www.singaporemath.com/pages/placement-tests) (accessed 2026-07-06).

On the "Singapore is about a half-year ahead" claim: the publisher does NOT state a half-year figure. FACT (publisher): "It's common and perfectly acceptable for students to begin at an earlier grade level if they transition from another program. For example, a second grade student might start with level 1A or 1B" ([placement tests page](https://www.singaporemath.com/pages/placement-tests), accessed 2026-07-06). SECONDARY: Singapore Math Source (a Singapore math trainer site) states "the Primary Mathematics materials are generally one year ahead of current U.S. materials" ([source](https://singaporemathsource.com/throwback-thursday-top-10-tips-for-using-the-singapore-math-curriculum/), accessed 2026-07-06), and Singapore Math Live's placement FAQ says testing "often ... places a student one or two levels lower than their grade level" and that finishing Primary Mathematics 6 prepares a student to enter Algebra 1 directly ([source](https://singaporemathlive.com/faq/how-do-i-decide-what-level-my-child-should-register-for/), accessed 2026-07-06). INFERENCE: the honest summary is "same grade labels, more advanced content per label, transferring students commonly place one level (sometimes two) below their US grade"; the community's "half-year to a year ahead" range is better supported at the one-year end for the older Primary Mathematics editions, and the DM 7 + 8 = pre-algebra + Algebra 1 packaging is a concrete full-year-ahead artifact at grade 8.

|---|---|---|---|
| DM PKA/PKB | PreK | 60 | Counting, matching, shapes, early number sense (kit and picture based) |

## 3. Math-U-See

Confidence: High for level sequence and per-level focus (publisher pages); the grade mapping is deliberately NOT a publisher claim and is labeled secondary.

FACT (structure): 13 levels "building from an introduction to mathematics through calculus": Primer, Alpha, Beta, Gamma, Delta, Epsilon, Zeta, Pre-Algebra, Algebra 1, Geometry, Algebra 2, PreCalculus, Calculus. The publisher's own one-line focus per elementary level: Primer "An Introduction to Mathematics," Alpha "Single-Digit Addition & Subtraction," Beta "Multiple-Digit Addition & Subtraction," Gamma "Single & Multiple-Digit Multiplication," Delta "Single & Multiple-Digit Division," Epsilon "Fractions," Zeta "Decimals & Percents." Source: [mathusee.com curriculum sequence page](https://mathusee.com/products/math-u-see-curriculum/) (accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT (mastery design): each level masters one main concept before moving on, taught through the Integer Block manipulative system (Alpha to Delta), fraction overlays (Epsilon), and an algebra/decimal kit (Zeta). Levels carry no grade labels; the publisher frames placement entirely by skill readiness. The Alpha product page lists readiness criteria like "accurately count up to 9" and "write the numerals 0 to 9" rather than an age or grade. Sources: [mathusee.com Alpha page](https://mathusee.com/products/math-u-see-curriculum/alpha/) (accessed 2026-07-06); [Sonlight's Math-U-See catalog page](https://www.sonlight.com/pages/math-u-see) (accessed 2026-07-06, retailer copy of publisher descriptions).

FACT (placement): an interactive placement tool at placement.mathusee.com walks the parent through yes/no questions and worked example problems and recommends a level; classroom placement tests also exist. Sources: [curriculum page](https://mathusee.com/products/math-u-see-curriculum/) and [Demme Learning blog on mastery-based placement](https://demmelearning.com/blog/math-u-see-placement-test/) (both accessed 2026-07-06).

SECONDARY (the grade mapping everyone uses): Kate Snow's widely cited review maps Primer = K, Alpha = grade 1, Beta = grade 2, Gamma = grade 3, Delta = grade 4, Epsilon = grade 5, Zeta = grade 6, with Pre-Algebra and up following one per year ([Kate's Homeschool Math review](https://kateshomeschoolmath.com/math-u-see-review-buying-guide/), accessed 2026-07-06). This matches the corpus lane 10 note that the mapping is "approximate only (publisher stresses skills over grades)."

INFERENCE: because each level is one topic deep, a Math-U-See level signals mastery of that topic and says little about untaught strands: the publisher's mastery framing means "in Gamma" = "working on multiplication," not "grade 3 overall." Math Mammoth's own switching guidance notes MUS covers "measuring, money, clock, geometry and problem solving" later or lighter than grade-labeled programs ([mathmammoth.com placement page](https://www.mathmammoth.com/complete/placement_tests), accessed 2026-07-06), so cross-strand claims from an MUS level are weaker than from grade-labeled curricula.

## 4. Math Mammoth (Light Blue series)

Confidence: High (publisher placement page fetched; it is unusually explicit about test design and scoring).

FACT (structure): the Light Blue series is a complete curriculum with direct grade labels, grades 1 to 8, where grade 7 is pre-algebra (the placement list labels the grade 7 test "pre-algebra"); a kindergarten end-of-year test also exists. Each grade splits into A and B worktexts (publisher's standard packaging; the placement page addresses grades as wholes). Source: [mathmammoth.com placement tests page](https://www.mathmammoth.com/complete/placement_tests) (accessed 2026-07-06). Note: the corpus commission described Light Blue as grades 1 to 7; the publisher page shows tests through grade 8.

FACT (placement mechanics, the cleanest in this file): the tests are explicitly end-of-year tests "meant to be taken AFTER studying the particular grade," grouped by topic so gaps are visible per section, untimed by recommendation. Publisher scoring table: 90%+ administer the next higher test; 80 to 89% go on to the next grade; 70 to 79% probably next grade with gap-filling; 50 to 69% possibly place at the test's grade or test one lower; below 50% administer the next lower test. A concise multi-grade placement test covers grades 1 to 3 in one sitting. Gaps route to topic-specific Blue Series books (for example "long division and related word problems (grades 4-5): Division 2"). Source: same page (accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT (author-stated calibration): the publisher (Maria Miller) states that children coming from public school or other curricula commonly fail their nominal grade's test because Math Mammoth "uses multi-step word problems extensively from 2nd grade onward" and covers geometry and measurement more completely; the page presents a worked example of a nominal 7th grader placing into grade 5. Source: same page (accessed 2026-07-06).

## 5. RightStart Math (Activities for Learning)

Confidence: High for the level-to-grade table (publisher's own teaching-support page pairs each level with a grade); Medium for per-level topics (Cathy Duffy review and OpenEd summary, labeled secondary).

FACT (structure and philosophy): RightStart second edition (RS2) runs in eight levels, A to H. "The RightStart Mathematics homeschool program is set up with levels, rather than grades, so that your child can begin at the proper level and advance at their own pace"; the classroom edition of the same program uses grade labels. The method de-emphasizes counting, builds visualization of quantity on the two-sided Cotter Abacus (grouped in fives and tens), and replaces most worksheets and flash cards with math card games. Source: [rightstartmath.com, Our curriculum](https://rightstartmath.com/our-curriculum/) (accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT (publisher level-to-grade pairing): the publisher's teaching-support page lists "RS2 Level A / Kindergarten, Level B / First Grade, Level C / Second Grade, Level D / Third Grade, Level E / Fourth Grade, Level F / Fifth Grade, Level G / Sixth Grade, Level H / Seventh Grade." Source: [rightstartmath.com teaching support](https://rightstartmath.com/teaching-support/) (accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT (placement): a free placement test is offered and the homeschool program expects placement by level, not age ([RightStart for home school](https://rightstartmath.com/our-curriculum/the-rightstart-difference/rightstart-for-home-school/), accessed 2026-07-06).

## 6. Teaching Textbooks

Confidence: High for level list and placement mechanics (publisher pages and PDFs); the "runs behind" claim is community-sourced and labeled as such.

FACT (structure): levels are Math 3, Math 4, Math 5, Math 6, Math 7, Pre-Algebra, Algebra 1, Geometry, Algebra 2, Pre-Calculus, delivered as physical books or an all-digital app version with video lessons and auto-grading. The grade intent is in the names: Math 3 declines route to "a standard textbook for 2nd graders," and Pre-Algebra sits after Math 7. Sources: [teachingtextbooks.com placement page](https://www.teachingtextbooks.com/placement) (accessed 2026-07-06); [Math 3 placement test PDF](https://www.teachingtextbooks.com/media/pdf/Math3-Placement-Test.pdf) (accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT (placement tests): every level has a free printable placement test with a uniform structure: 30 problems in two 15-problem sections (section 1 easier, section 2 harder), untimed (most finish inside about 1.5 hours), no calculator, pass rule "10 or more correct on Section 1 and 8 or more correct on Section 2." The publisher tells unsure families to test the HIGHER level first, and says a student "just under the recommended score" is "almost assuredly ready for that grade level." Examples: the Pre-Algebra test covers whole numbers, fractions, decimals, percents, and measurement, and routes failures to Math 7; the Pre-Calculus test draws from Algebra 2 and Geometry. Sources: [placement page](https://www.teachingtextbooks.com/placement), [Pre-Algebra test PDF](https://www.teachingtextbooks.com/media/pdf/PreAlgebra-Placement-Test.pdf), [Pre-Calculus test PDF](https://www.teachingtextbooks.com/media/pdf/PreCalc-Placement-Test.pdf) (all accessed 2026-07-06).

COMMUNITY CLAIM, clearly labeled (the "runs about a grade behind" reputation): this is not a publisher statement. Homeschool forums and comparison sites state it repeatedly: "I do think it is generally a grade behind (so TT5 would really be 4th grade)" and "they are a whole year behind" ([Simply Charlotte Mason forum thread](https://simplycharlottemason.com/scmforum/topic/saxon-or-teaching-textbooks/), accessed 2026-07-06); a Well-Trained Mind forum thread titled "Is Teaching Textbooks on grade level or behind?" documents a CLE grade 2 student testing out of most of TT Math 3 ([WTM thread](https://forums.welltrainedmind.com/topic/502770-is-teaching-textbooks-on-grade-level-or-behind/), accessed 2026-07-06); another WTM thread reports "TT is about a year (or more) behind. I've tested my current 3rd grader and she will be using their Math 6 program for 4th grade" ([WTM thread](https://forums.welltrainedmind.com/topic/90067-talk-to-me-straight-about-teaching-textbooks/), accessed 2026-07-06); and a comparison site summarizes "Teaching Textbooks is widely noted to run about one grade level below standard" ([Homeschool Start Guide](https://homeschoolstartguide.com/blog/algebra-1-curriculum-homeschool), accessed 2026-07-06). INFERENCE: the practical community adaptation is placing a child one level above nominal grade (a 4th grader in Math 5 or 6); so "she's in TT Math 6" is weak evidence of grade 6 skill content and the placement test result carries more signal than the level name.

## 7. Beast Academy (Art of Problem Solving)

Confidence: High (publisher FAQ fetched; positioning statement is the publisher's own).

FACT (structure): a full math program for ages 6 to 13, levels 1 to 5, each level in four units (books 1A to 5D), delivered as graphic-novel-style Guide books plus Practice books and/or Beast Academy Online. ## Product porting notes

This lane exists so plans and reports can speak the parent's curriculum language instead of grade-and-standards language. A parent who says "we use Math-U-See" thinks in Greek letters; one who says "Saxon" thinks in 5/4 and 6/5. The crosswalk lets Homa translate both directions: bank position to curriculum position ("that's mid-Gamma territory") and curriculum position to bank position ("finished Epsilon" seeds the fraction items but NOT the decimal items). All parent-facing phrasings must still pass the content laws in `packages/core/src/map/content.ts` and the anti-guilt display law (BUILD.md law 4): curriculum names are reassuring precisely because they are positions in a chosen sequence, never rankings.
