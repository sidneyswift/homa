# Social studies benchmarks, kindergarten to grade 10

> Historical research snapshot, 2026-07-06. This file preserves the evidence and analysis captured on that date. It is not current product, legal, provider, standards, or production guidance. Re-verify volatile facts and source freshness before porting any claim into Homa.

**Date:** 2026-07-06
**Lane:** Social studies 06, part of the ages 2 to 16 national benchmark corpus (see README.md in this folder)
**Method:** Web searches plus full-document fetches of the primary frameworks: the C3 Framework PDF (socialstudies.org), Core Knowledge CKHG unit lists (coreknowledge.org), CEE 2010 economics and 2021 personal finance standards PDFs (councilforeconed.org, jumpstart.org), NAEP civics and US history results and achievement level pages (nationsreportcard.gov, nces.ed.gov), NCGE Geography for Life pages, ELOF and the 2010 Head Start framework, and state standards (Michigan 2019, New York K-8, Texas TEKS 2022, Virginia SOL 2023) for grade-placement evidence. Every number was read from a fetched source; grade-to-month anchors are the corpus convention.

**Key findings:**

- Social studies has no national grade-by-grade content standard: the C3 Framework (NCSS, 2013) deliberately specifies skills by band (end of grades 2, 5, 8, 12) and leaves content placement to states, so skills progressions are the safe reporting anchor and topic placement is curriculum-dependent.
- The "expanding communities" sequence (K self, 1 family/school, 2 community, 4 state, 5 US, 8 US history) is real and citable in state standards (Michigan, New York, Texas), but the variations are large: Texas does state history in grades 4 and 7, New York does hemispheres in 5 and 6, and Core Knowledge starts Mesopotamia and Egypt in grade 1.
- NAEP 2022 grade 8: civics average 150 (0 to 300), 22 percent at or above NAEP Proficient; US history average 258 (0 to 500), just 13 percent at or above Proficient and 40 percent below Basic; both declined vs 2018.
- The three branches of government: bare identification is citable at grade 4 (Texas TEKS, New York grade 4), full powers-and-limits understanding at end of grade 8 (C3 D2.Civ.4.6-8); Core Knowledge treats passing USCIS-style civics items as an end-of-grade-5 goal.
- Map skills ladder with state citations: map vs globe (K), cardinal directions and simple map making (grade 1), continents and oceans (grades 2 to 3, state-dependent), grid systems, scale, latitude and longitude (grade 4), projections and coordinate systems (grade 8, Geography for Life).
- Economics and personal finance both have national standards with grade 4/8/12 benchmarks (CEE 2010; CEE and Jump$tart 2021): opportunity cost and saving plans by grade 4, budgeting, interest mechanics, and insurance terms by grade 8. CEE published a restructured 3rd edition of the economics standards in 2025.
- Ages 3 to 5: the current federal ELOF (2015) folds social studies into identity and belonging (P-SE 9 to 11); the 2010 Head Start framework's explicit social studies domain (community helpers, reasons for rules, past/present/future) remains the best federal preschool listing.
- NCGE itself concedes most US students will not meet all 18 geography standards without college coursework: treat Geography for Life as a ceiling, not a typical bar.

## 4. The expanding communities scope and sequence, K to 10

Confidence: High for the convention's existence and the cited state placements; the convention itself is INFERENCE about "typical", never a mandate.

FACT: the "expanding communities" model (also called expanding horizons or expanding environments) has been the dominant organizing framework for US elementary social studies since the late 1930s. It "begins with the self and others in kindergarten and then, gradually, expands the purview to the family and school in first grade, the neighborhood in second grade, the community in third grade, the state and region in fourth grade, the nation in fifth grade, and the hemispheres or world in sixth grade" ([Brophy, Alleman, and Halvorsen, Powerful Social Studies for Elementary Students, 4th ed., ch. 1](https://online.flipbuilder.com/jeru/naej/files/basic-html/page34.html) accessed 2026-07-06; historical dominance since the late 1930s per [Krahenbuhl, Kappan, 2019](https://kappanonline.org/problem-expanding-horizons-model-history-curricula-krahenbuhl/) accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT: no national body mandates this sequence. The C3 Framework explicitly declines to assign content to grades (section 1). The convention persists through state standards and publishers.

### 4.2 Major variations to remember when reporting

- FACT: Texas teaches state history twice, grade 4 and grade 7, and splits US history across grade 8 (through Reconstruction) and high school (since 1877) ([TEA TEKS chapter 113](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/texas-administrative-code/19-tac-chapter-113) accessed 2026-07-06).
- FACT: New York replaces the grade 5 US history year with a Western Hemisphere course and grade 6 with Eastern Hemisphere, then spends two full years (7 and 8) on US and NY history ([NYS framework](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/standards-instruction/ss-framework-k-8a2.pdf) accessed 2026-07-06).
- FACT: Michigan begins state studies in grade 3, not 4, and begins US studies in grade 4 ([Michigan standards](https://textbooks.wmisd.org/MISSH/Standards/K.pdf) accessed 2026-07-06).
- FACT: content-rich publishers deliberately reject expanding horizons: Core Knowledge starts world history (Mesopotamia, Egypt) in grade 1 (section 5). Classical homeschool programs commonly run a 4-year chronological world history cycle instead (noted in Dry wells: not verified against a publisher page this pass).
- INFERENCE for Homa: a homeschooled child studying ancient Egypt at age 7 is not "off track"; grade placement of history content is the least standardized thing in this lane. Skills progressions (sections 2, 7, 11) are the safer reporting anchor.

## Dry wells and method notes

- NAEP civics grade 8 numeric cut scores: the NCES achievement-level page as fetched showed cut scores for grades 4 (136/177/215) and 12 (139/174/204) but an empty table for grade 8, likely a page rendering artifact after the 2023 descriptor update. Not guessed; follow-up pass should pull the NAEP civics technical documentation.
- NAEP US history grade 8 percent at NAEP Advanced in 2022: not shown in the pages fetched (below Basic 40, at/above Basic 60, at/above Proficient 13 verified). By subtraction Advanced is small but the exact figure was not verified, so it is omitted.
- Classical homeschool 4-year chronological history cycle (Story of the World, The Well-Trained Mind): asserted in section 4.2 as a known publisher pattern but not verified against a publisher page this pass; verify before citing in any report surface.
- Core Knowledge Sequence PDF itself (2023 edition) was not parsed page by page; the CKHG unit lists from coreknowledge.org/history-geography were used as the per-grade topic evidence. The Sequence PDF has finer-grained topic detail (for example specific geography spine per grade) worth a follow-up pass.
- The 10 NCSS themes' per-band expectations (early, middle, high school snapshots in the 2010 National Curriculum Standards) are behind an NCSS member wall; only the theme list and framing were verified from public NCSS pages.
- C3 Framework: the PDF served today is the June 2017 file revision (rev0617.2) of the 2013 document; treated as the 2013 edition per its own title page.
- Geography for Life full text (2nd ed., 2012) is a paid NCGE publication; standard texts and the Standard 1 grade-band details were verified from NCGE's own web pages, which reproduce them.
- 2025 USCIS civics test specifics (20 of 128, 12 to pass) were verified only from a secondary legal-services page; the USCIS study page confirms the two-version split and the 2008 rules. Re-verify the 2025 numbers on uscis.gov before use.
- NAEP geography: last administered 2018 (grade 8) and not covered here since the task scoped civics and US history results; the 2018 geography results would strengthen section 7 in a follow-up pass.
- Virginia SOL: the January 2023 draft and the April 2023 approved version differ on grade placement of continents and oceans (grade 2 draft, grade 3 approved); the approved version was used. Worth re-checking the final published edition since the approved PDF is watermarked as unedited for publication.
