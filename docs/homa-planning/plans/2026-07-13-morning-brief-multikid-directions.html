Options lab · 2026-07-13 · morning brief vs multi-kid families

The first family just rebuilt the morning brief by hand.
What do we fix?

A real 3-kid family paused the built-in morning research brief after its first delivery and had Homa create three separate 6am custom research tasks, one per kid. Searched: the task framework (kinds, store, dispatcher, dispatch channel), the brief composer and its skill, fleet research and the knowledge bank, the tasks tab, and production evidence (schedules, runs, deliveries, chat, token ledger). The pattern: the flagship feature has exactly one shape (one rotating kid, 9am, family-level), so the first parent who wanted a different shape fell off the maintained rails onto the freeform one, which quietly costs 8x the tokens, skips the shared research corpus, has no delivery window, no reply contract, and sends three long texts before breakfast.

What actually happened (production evidence, anonymized)

  1. Day 1, 9:00am. First built-in brief delivered by text, about the youngest kid only (the skill's law: one angle, one kid). Composed in 30 seconds from the shared corpus; 82k input tokens.
  2. Day 1, 10:03am. The parent replied "Yes" to the brief's offer. Homa missed the referent entirely and answered about an unrelated book club. (The reply-contract commit landed 4 hours later; custom tasks still return null contracts today.)
  3. Day 1, 10:12am. The parent asked for "that report for all the girls, every night", then settled on "a morning text about each child, at 6am, before I wake up, for a week to get a feel for it". Homa paused the built-in and created three custom tasks. Well improvised, but a fork off the rails.
  4. Day 2, 6am. One of the three texts arrived. The other two sessions died on a shared continuation token (HookConflictError, fixed next day by per-run tokens) and were reaped as failed 2 hours later. The tab said "Didn't send. Homa tries again soon.", but the schedule had already advanced: nothing retried that day. Nobody told her.
  5. Day 3, 6am. All three arrived: at 6:38, 6:38, and 6:56. Each custom run did 37 to 56 minutes of live web research at 6am; "before I wake up" delivered at 6:56. The three dispatch sessions burned 672k input tokens, about 8x the built-in morning. Formats drifted between texts (one emoji opener, mixed shapes on day 2).
  6. Meanwhile, every night. Fleet research keeps filling the shared knowledge bank (9 runs, 3 days, all green; 24 active rows) that these custom tasks never read. They researched from scratch what the corpus partly already held. They do save gated rows back (3 on day 3), so the side door half-feeds the bank while fully paying retail.
  7. Privacy posture drifted. The two-tier ruling (D-ht-1) keeps child specifics out of third-party search queries by construction. The custom prompts carry kid names and interests into sessions that run open web_search with no query-hygiene rule. Saved rows looked clean; nothing enforces it.

Family details anonymized here on purpose; the full identified timeline is in the chat session that produced this lab.

Tell the truth when a task run fails

Scopy + wiring

apps/web/app/app/tasks/task-history.tsx · apps/web/lib/tasks/store.ts (reapStaleTaskRuns)

Found in codeThe failed-run card says "Didn't send. Homa tries again soon." For a reaped run the schedule already advanced (advanceTaskSchedule ran at session start), so nothing retries until the next scheduled day. On day 2 this copy was false twice for this family.

ImpactTrust. A parent who catches the app promising a retry that never comes stops believing the History page. Change the copy to name the real next attempt ("Didn't send. It runs again tomorrow at 6:00 am."), which the row's schedule already knows.

Give custom tasks a delivery window

Swiring

apps/web/lib/tasks/kinds.ts (custom.deliveryWindowHours: null) · lib/tasks/store.ts (withinDeliveryWindow)

Found in codeBuilt-ins refuse to become afternoon texts (3-hour window, skipped runs otherwise); that guard exists because three real families once got 11pm journal emails from stale rows. Custom tasks have window null: after downtime, a "6am" text can fire at 3pm.

ImpactPrevents a whole class of wrong-hour texts for every custom task ever created. Default a 3 to 4 hour window for delivery_pref text/email customs; save-only customs can keep null.

4

A quiet heads-up when a morning goes missing

S/Mlifecycle

apps/web/lib/tasks/store.ts (reapStaleTaskRuns) · agent/channels/dispatch.ts

Found in codeOn day 2, two of three texts silently vanished. The failure landed in homa_task_runs and the founder console, but the parent learned nothing unless she opened the tab. The reaper already knows the family, the task label, and the delivery pref when it fails a run.

ImpactFailure becomes a moment of competence instead of silent flakiness: one honest text ("This morning's note about one of the girls didn't go out; it's saved on your tasks page and runs again tomorrow"). Needs a rate cap (one heads-up per family per day) so a bad morning never becomes four texts.

5

Cap and pace the morning: one proactive burst, not three

Mlifecycle

apps/web/lib/tasks/store.ts (claimDueFamilyTasks) · agent/schedules/dispatch.ts

Found in codeThe dispatcher claims all due rows per family in one tick and starts one session each; nothing anywhere counts proactive texts per family per morning. Three tasks at hour 6 = three texts, forever. Fatigue was flagged as a watch item (D-ht-2) with MJ-27 quiet mode as a future valve, but no mechanism exists yet.

ImpactOverload protection by construction, for this family and every future one: when N same-hour text tasks are due for one family, either combine their outputs into one message (see 7) or space them. Also fixes the "which question does 'Yes' answer" ambiguity by giving mornings one CTA.

Teach chat-Homa to reach for the knobs before the fork

Magent behavior

apps/web/agent/instructions.md (task table row) · agent/tools/create_task.ts (description) · agent/tools/update_task.ts

Found in codeWhen the parent asked for a per-kid version of the brief, Homa's only real options were the built-in's knobs (days, hour, delivery) or freeform customs; nothing in the instructions says "a variant of a built-in should first be offered as configuration". Custom research tasks inherit the research rails

Lgenerator

apps/web/lib/tasks/dispatch-input.ts (customMessage) · lib/tasks/brief-context.ts · agent/skills/research-brief/SKILL.md (query hygiene) · lib/knowledge/gate.ts

Found in codecustomMessage passes the parent's prompt verbatim with zero research infrastructure: no positions, no bank rows, no skill checklist, no query-hygiene line, no search cap. Result measured in prod: 37 to 56 minutes and roughly 8x tokens per morning, kid names sitting in prompts of sessions that run open web search, and format drift between days.

ImpactFreedom without the bill. Detect research-flavored customs (or add a kind), inject the same context block the brief gets (bank rows first, capped top-up second), require the skill's format and query hygiene, keep save_knowledge through the gate. Cost falls back toward corpus-read levels; D-ht-1's privacy posture holds for parent-authored research too.

Overnight pre-compute so 6:00 means 6:00

Llifecycle

apps/web/lib/knowledge/demand.ts · agent/schedules/fleet-research.ts · lib/tasks/brief-context.ts

Found in codeThe parent asked for "before I wake up"; custom runs START researching at 6:00 and delivered at 6:38 and 6:56. The built-in already splits work correctly (fleet researches overnight, family run composes in seconds), but the demand queue only carries fleet-level topics; nothing pre-stages per-family angles.

ImpactAny research-flavored morning task becomes compose-and-send at the named minute. Feed family frontier topics into the nightly queue (topic keys only, same privacy construction), or add a small per-family overnight staging pass, and the 6am run reads instead of researching. This is what makes early-hour briefs honest.

The proactive governor: budgets, trials, and quiet mode

XLnew lifecycle

new: lib/tasks/governor.ts · agent_schedules + a per-family proactive budget · MJ-27 (quiet mode, pulled forward) · conversation_open_loops reply signals

Found in codeToday the only fatigue mechanisms are the parent's own pause switch and the founder watching. Reply signals already exist (conversation_deliveries.replied_at, open loops) but nothing reads them as engagement. The one-week trial this parent invented is exactly the lifecycle the product lacks.

ImpactChanges the problem's structure: every proactive kind draws from one per-family morning budget; new setups start as named trials with an automatic day-7 "keep, change, drop" check-in; falling reply rates trigger an offered (never forced) quieter rhythm. The system notices overload before the parent has to.
