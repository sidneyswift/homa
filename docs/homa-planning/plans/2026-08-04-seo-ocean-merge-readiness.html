The mental model

From one post to a governed library

The important change is not article count. It is the ownership system around every search intent, product claim, public route, and publication decision.

Before

  • One public article on a flat blog index.
  • Minimal frontmatter and no canonical topic owner.
  • Article copy could carry its own CTA and product promise.
  • No cluster hubs, pagination, related guidance, or answer-engine summary.
→

After

  • One hundred unique parent intents with one owner each.
  • Twelve reviewed articles staged behind explicit human publication.
  • Build-time contracts for metadata, links, schema, clusters, and CTA truth.
  • Public discovery through hubs, pagination, sitemap, RSS, and AI summaries.
Parent question→ Canonical intent owner→ Evidence bundle→ Reader + editor gates→ Human approval→ Git CMS→ Build validation→ Public library
Review the behavior

Four details that are easy to misunderstand

1

Twelve finished drafts are still not public

The marketing bundles are staged artifacts. They need real hero assets, per-format human approval, and the distributor step before their MDX files enter the production content directory. Merge improves the publishing system and upgrades the one existing live article.

marketing-article-writer/SKILL.md lines 170 to 187
2

The CTA has one source of truth

Article MDX ends with its useful takeaway. The route renders one CTA using HERO_COPY.primaryAction and its current disclosure, so a landing-page trial change reaches every article without rewriting editorial content.

blog/[slug]/page.tsx lines 181 to 197
3

An empty cluster does not pretend to exist

Topic routes, sitemap entries, and AI-summary links are derived only from clusters containing published posts. A brief or staged article can belong to a future cluster without creating a thin public hub or a broken link.

blog.ts lines 86 to 92; sitemap.ts lines 23 to 38
4

Crawler access is an invitation, not a result

Named search and AI crawlers may fetch public pages while /api/ stays excluded. That configuration neither forces indexing nor guarantees a citation, recommendation, or ranking. Those remain measured outcomes.

Existing dependencies

What the system now relies on

Landing-page truth

landing-content.ts owns the current CTA label and disclosure inherited by blog pages.

Product truth

The five feature pages remain the canonical descriptions of what Homa currently offers.

Human publication

The distributor copies an approved staged bundle into apps/web/content/blog. Automation cannot perform that approval.

Build-time verification

check:seo rejects duplicate intent owners, missing staged files, broken public links, invalid metadata, and CTA drift.

Root site identity

Article schema references the existing Homa Organization node instead of inventing a second publisher identity.

No database work

This release adds no migration, table, durable write path, authentication change, or tenant-scoped query.

The operational rule

One hundred topics are a decision ledger, not one hundred publish commands. Statuses such as brief and research-required preserve the queue without manufacturing pages before the evidence and product truth are ready.
