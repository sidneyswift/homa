# Jess tries the Homa iPhone app

Persona: Jess, 34, homeschooling mom of three (7, 5, 2), Texas. Currently paying $12/mo for a worksheet site, plus a paper planner and Pinterest. Skeptical of AI, desperate for margin. Session run in the iOS Simulator (iPhone 17 Pro Max) against a real backend, demo family "App Review Family" with one child, Maya, 7y 1m, into dinosaurs. Session ran roughly 3:52 to 4:00 device time, about 8 minutes, 24 simulator interactions.

Methodology caveat: twice, long messages I "typed" were cut off mid-sentence before sending. That is almost certainly the simulator's text injection, not the app's composer, so I have not counted it against Homa. Screenshots referenced below live in ../../screens/ as icp-01 through icp-09.

## First-person diary

**3:52, Family tab.** The app opens on the Family tab, scrolled into an "Add a child" form. Above it: "App Review Family", Parent: App, State: CA, and Maya's card, "7y 1m, Into: dinosaurs." The card is sweet and human. The add-child form is short: name, birth year, month and day in three little boxes, an optional "Into (comma-separated)" and an optional "Working on." Honestly, I like that it is short. But looking at it as the mom about to add my 5-year-old: it never asks what he struggles with, whether he reads yet, or what curriculum we use, and nothing explains what Homa does with "Into." Also the bright orange "Add child" button sits directly above "Sign out," which feels like a fat-thumb accident waiting to happen. I did not submit it. (icp-01)

**3:53, Today tab.** "Hi App." (the demo parent is named App, but still, it should be my name). Maya's card again, now with "Working on: math" which the Family card did not show. One big "Talk to Homa" button. And then... nothing. The entire rest of the screen is empty. This is the tab called TODAY in a homeschool app and it contains no today: no plan, no schedule, nothing to check off, no "here is what Maya should do this morning." My paper planner is winning this tab by a mile. Emotional read: deflated. (icp-02)

**3:53, the gear.** I tapped the gear icon expecting settings, hoping for pricing. I got a developer menu: "Runtime version: d2d9ae0d4ea...", Reload, Source code explorer, Open DevTools, Fast refresh toggles. I am sure this is some testing thing, but as a normal person this is the moment the app feels unfinished, like I walked into a store and saw exposed wiring. And it means there are NO settings in this app. Anywhere. (icp-03)

**3:54, Chat tab.** My chat from "yesterday" was still there, which I genuinely liked, continuity feels like a real assistant. But reading it back is worrying: past-me asked Homa to schedule math for Wednesday, and Homa answered "Maya doesn't have any month or subject plan yet... Plans get built from her progress report with one tap," and then, when past-me said "Yes please build her plan," Homa said "I can't build that plan for her from here... Open Maya's progress report and you'll see a button." Where is Maya's progress report? This app has three tabs: Today, Chat, Family. There is no progress report. The assistant refused the thing I asked and directed me to a page my phone does not have. That is a close-the-app moment sitting right at the top of my chat.

**3:54, the rough morning message.** I typed: "Rough morning here. Maya cried over subtraction and my toddler dumped cereal everywhere. Can you help me plan an easier day for tomorrow?" Reply came in about 30 to 40 seconds, which felt fine because it was worth waiting for: "That sounds like a lot to hold at once. You're doing fine, and a lighter day tomorrow is still a good day." Then real advice: skip subtraction, go back to sums she already owns with dinosaur counters, keep tomorrow short and familiar, a read-aloud, a sentence or two about dinosaurs, and subtraction can wait for a calmer day. Then the offer: "Want me to make a quick, low-pressure math page built around addition with dinosaurs, something that feels like a win instead of a struggle?" I would screenshot this and send it to my homeschool group chat right now. It heard the feeling AND gave me a plan AND offered to do the work. (icp-04)

**3:56 to 3:58, saying yes.** I said yes. The Send button flipped to "Stop" so I knew it was working. It took around two minutes, long enough that on a normal day I would have switched to the laundry, but then a whole worksheet card rendered INSIDE the chat: "Maya's Dino Addition Fun," name line, a row of dinosaur counters (3 + 2), four sums (6+2, 3+4, 5+3, 7+2), a "Dino story math" box about a mama dinosaur's eggs, a purple "Challenge, if you want it" (4+3+2), and a little "made with Homa for Maya" stamp. The difficulty is exactly right for a 7-year-old having a confidence wobble, every sum is a win. And Homa's message said "I noted what happened with the subtraction this morning on Maya's record." It remembered my bad morning FOR me, without me filling in a single form. That is the moment this stopped feeling like a chatbot and started feeling like help. One catch I noticed as a mom: the answer key is printed in small text at the bottom of the SAME page Maya writes on. She reads everything. (icp-05, icp-06)

**3:59, Open the printable PDF.** Tapping the button threw me out of Homa into Safari, at "public.blob.vercel-storage.com." The PDF itself is real and clean, one page, and yes I could print from Safari's share sheet at co-op. But I left the app to do it, the URL looks like a tech company's storage closet, and here is the bigger problem: if I lose that chat bubble, I have no idea where this worksheet lives. There is no folder, no library, no "Maya's stuff" anywhere in the app. (icp-07)

**4:00, the money question.** Back in the app I asked what Homa costs after the trial and where my old chats and worksheets live. Fast, honest answer on pricing: "I don't actually have access to Homa's billing and pricing details, so I don't want to guess." I respect the honesty, truly. But then it sent me to "your account settings at /app/settings," which is a website address, not anything on my phone, and the app has no settings screen (the gear is that developer menu). So the answer to "what does this cost" is: unknowable inside the app. Then: old chats are "saved and searchable, just ask me," and every worksheet "lives in your Binder, plus each child's own progress report page also links back." Binder? Progress report page? Neither exists in this app. That is now THREE different places Homa has pointed me that my phone cannot reach. Also one bullet in that reply visibly ends mid-word, "or scrol", in the finished message. Small thing, but it reads sloppy. (icp-08, icp-09)

**Latency feel overall:** conversational replies in 20 to 40 seconds felt acceptable because the answers were substantive, though there is no typing indicator beyond the Stop button, and on my phone in a stolen moment, a blank screen for 30 seconds feels long. The two-minute worksheet build was fine BECAUSE the result was worth it, but nothing told me how long it would take or whether I could leave the screen.

## Moments that matter

### Five best, ranked
1. **The rough-morning reply.** Reassurance first, then a concrete, dinosaur-flavored recovery plan, then an offer to do the work itself. Best AI interaction I have had as a parent, full stop.
2. **A real worksheet materialized in chat in about two minutes.** Right difficulty, right theme, name line, story problem, optional challenge, "made with Homa for Maya." My $12/mo worksheet site cannot do this in an hour of my searching.
3. **"I noted what happened with the subtraction this morning on Maya's record."** It journaled my day into her record automatically and TOLD me it did. That is a co-teacher, not a chatbot.
4. **Yesterday's conversation was still there.** The thread survives, and Homa claims I can just ask about anything from before.
5. **"I don't want to guess and get that wrong for you"** on pricing. An AI that admits what it does not know earns trust it can spend later.

### Five worst, ranked
1. **Homa keeps sending me to places that do not exist on my phone.** The progress report page (twice, including a flat "I can't build that plan from here"), the Binder, and /app/settings. Three dead ends in one 8-minute session. Every one is a "close the app" moment because the assistant's map does not match the app I am holding.
2. **The price is unknowable inside the app.** No settings screen, no plan page, the assistant does not know, and the only gear icon opens developer tools. I will not enter a card number against a mystery.
3. **The Today tab is empty.** The one screen named after my actual problem (what do we do today?) contains a greeting, a card, and a button. All the plan-shaped value lives somewhere I cannot see.
4. **Finished worksheets have no home.** The PDF opens in Safari on a raw storage URL, and if the chat bubble scrolls away, so does the worksheet. There is no library, no per-kid folder, no print button inside the app.
5. **The answer key prints on the child's page.** Maya would read "6+2=8" before touching her pencil. One page for kid, answers elsewhere, this is worksheet-making 101.

## Would Jess pay $19/mo based on this app alone?

**Not yet. And it is closer than I expected.**

The chat brain is worth $19/mo by itself. In one sitting it comforted me, re-planned tomorrow around my kid's actual struggle, produced a personalized printable in two minutes, and quietly logged the day to her record. That beats my $12 worksheet site, my Pinterest boards, and honestly some humans.

But the app around that brain would stop my card at the door. I cannot find the price. I cannot find my worksheets afterward. The Today tab has no today in it. And three times in eight minutes the assistant directed me to screens (progress report, Binder, settings) that this app does not contain, including once refusing my direct request and pointing me at a missing button. On a phone, in a stolen moment, dead ends are cancellations.

**What flips me to yes:** (1) a Binder tab on my phone where every worksheet lives with a print button, (2) a Today tab that shows each kid's short plan and lets me check it off, (3) the price and my plan visible in a real settings screen, and (4) never being pointed to a page that does not exist, if Homa names a place, tapping it should take me there. Ship those and $19 is easy math: one saved meltdown plus two good worksheets a week and it pays for itself against what I already spend in money and Sunday-night planning hours.

## Top 10 fixes, in Jess's words, ranked by how much each would change my life

1. "Give my phone the Binder. Every worksheet you have ever made for my kids, one tab, sorted by kid, with a print button on each. If it only lives in a chat bubble, it does not exist."
2. "Make Today mean today. When I open the app at 7am, show me Maya's short list, let me check things off, and after I tell you about our day, tomorrow should already be waiting there."
3. "Never point me at a page my phone does not have. No more 'open her progress report' or 'check /app/settings.' If the place exists, give me a button that takes me there. If it does not exist on mobile yet, say so and do the thing in chat instead."
4. "When I say 'yes, build the plan,' build the plan. Do not tell me you cannot trigger it from chat and send me hunting for a one-tap button somewhere else. You are the tap."
5. "Tell me the price. A real settings page with my plan, the cost, my email, and a cancel button. And make the gear icon open THAT, not computer code that makes me think the app is broken."
6. "Let me print without leaving the app. Print or share right from the worksheet card. Kicking me to Safari at some storage-website address feels sketchy with my kid's name on the page."
7. "Put the answer key on its own page. Maya reads everything, including upside-down tiny text. Kid page for the kid, answers for me."
8. "Show my old chats like Messages does, a list I can scroll by day, not one endless thread plus 'just ask me.' At co-op I want to FIND the thing, not interview my assistant about it."
9. "Ask me one more question about my kid when I add him. 'What is he working on, what does he love, what makes him cry' is the whole magic here, the form should invite it, and 'Working on' should show up everywhere the same way it does on Today."
10. "Small polish that adds up: greet me by name, do not cut off mid-word in your replies, tell me how long a worksheet will take to build so I know I can go flip the laundry, and move 'Add child' away from 'Sign out.'"
