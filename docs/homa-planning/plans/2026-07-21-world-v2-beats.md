## The story spine

Know → Make → Loop → Always working → Everything kept.

The emotional engine is the founder's wife's aha, confirmed by the
customer corpus (confidence is the most-selected onboarding goal; "where
she's at" is the parents' own phrase): the RELIEF of finally seeing where
your child is, immediately followed by "and here's the next step." Every
beat after the report exists because the report exists; that is the
cohesion. The desire ladder the page climbs: certainty about my child →
being equipped to act → momentum I can see → a companion who never stops
working → everything held in one calm place.

## The beats

1. THE CLAIM (hero, calm morning table, scroll 1.5)
   - First viewport is a complete landing page carrying the positioning:
     H1 category-led, "The homeschool assistant that does the prep."
     Directly behind it, the category-creating support: the binder, the
     planner, the assistant, and your kid's progress, wrapped in one,
     and kids never use a screen. Body names the offer: five minutes of
     questions, a free progress report, no card needed. CTA live.
   - Sub-beats: (a) the claim; (b) the all-in-one line as the camera
     pushes toward the table.
   - Visual referents: the morning table mid-homeschool, workbooks and
     the family's own curriculum stack visible, mom present as the
     protagonist.
   - Feeling: this is a different kind of thing. Invited.
2. THE REPORT (the aha, scroll 1.3, linger)
   - The reveal as a moment: the trajectory line draws itself as you
     scroll (mirrors the new onboarding's in-session reveal). The camera
     lands on the report artifact, a clay rendering of the real chart.
   - Copy in parent vocabulary: "Where she's at, subject by subject.
     What looks solid. What comes next." Adjacent reassurance: "This is
     not a test for your child."
   - Feeling: RELIEF. The fog lifts. This is the page's emotional peak
     and it happens early, exactly like the product.
3. MADE FOR EXACTLY HERE (act on it, scroll 1.1)
   - From knowing to doing: the print studio produces the first
     worksheet pitched to her level AND her obsessions. "Not a grade
     level. Her level, in her dinosaurs." The revision loop as proof:
     "Say 'make the numbers bigger' and it comes back fixed."
   - Visual referents: a legible worksheet artifact coming off the
     press, themed to a child's interest; pages into the binder.
   - Feeling: equipped, a little delighted.
4. IT BUILDS ITSELF (the loop, scroll 1.1)
   - Teach like always. Tell her about the day in a sentence, or record
     a lesson when you choose: the hours log themselves, progress
     updates, and next week reshapes around what actually happened.
     "When life happens, the week bends."
   - Visual referents: planner week-cards visibly rearranging; the
     garden path extending a little further up the hill; a check-off.
   - Feeling: momentum. It is working and I can see it.
5. SHE NEVER STOPS (delight beat, scroll 1.1)
   - The plus material as one wonder-beat: while the family sleeps,
     Homa reads research about kids like yours and leaves a morning
     note; she spots the museum exhibit nearby and ties it to this
     week's lesson; text her, call her, email her, she answers where
     you are.
   - Visual referents: the observatory at night, telescope light on,
     tiny note cards flying toward the sleeping house; morning light
     arriving with the note.
   - Feeling: cared for; "she's really smart."
6. EVERYTHING, KEPT (finale + ask, scroll 1.6, linger)
   - The flight lands at the little home at golden hour: the binder, the
     planner, the progress, the assistant, one place, one relationship.
     Reassure before inform: "You've got this. Homa's got the rest."
     Then the ask: free progress report, about five minutes per child,
     no card needed. Primary CTA to /onboarding, sample-report door
     stays. Bridge line: "Start with a snapshot today. Watch it fill in
     from your real days."
   - Feeling: calm, held.
