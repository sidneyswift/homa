BEAT 1 · LANDING

The promise she has heard before, made specific

skeptical hope

She arrives from a group thread or from Alessa saying "thankshoma.com". She has taken Pinterest checklists before; they ended in guilt. She scans for the catch: is it a test for my kid, will it call him behind, what do they want from me.

draft copy · CTA band Know exactly where your child stands. Without ever testing them.
Answer a 5-minute checklist about what you've seen your child do. Homa maps it against national benchmarks and sends you their map, free.
[Meet your child's map] · a checklist for you, never a test for them
Front end
  • Landing keeps hero variant B; the waitlist band becomes the map CTA
  • Same page answers the two fears in one scroll: "your child never uses it" + data promise
  • Old /waitlist links redirect into the flow
Back end
  • Nothing yet; utm and referrer wait in the URL until submit
Friction risk: "free report" pattern-matches to email-farm printables. The counter is specificity: name the frameworks, show a real (sample-labeled) report page a tap away.
Delight: the sample map link. Skeptics get to inspect the exact artifact before giving anything.
events: map.landing_viewed {channel, utm}
BEAT 2 · S1

The email, asked plainly, secured instantly

guarded

The moment of maximum guardedness. She has been burned by "enter your email for the PDF" farms. We ask before the work on purpose (the lead is the floor; everything after is upside), so the ask has to carry its own why.

draft copy · email screen Where should I send the maps?
Email · first name (optional) · your state (optional)
Your email gets each child's map and your invite. That's it, and you can delete everything anytime.
Front end
  • One screen, three fields, submit is optimistic (existing retry + offline queue)
  • Honeypot rides along; state select is searchable
  • On dup email: "welcome back" + resume
Back end
  • POST /api/map/start: zod validate, rate limit, insert lead_families
  • Issue resume token (httpOnly cookie + emailed link later)
  • emit map.started
Friction risk: the old form promised "email only used for your invite". This flow's promise must be re-vetted once, then said identically everywhere (form, report, email footer).
Delight: zero password, zero verification gate. She is never blocked from the thing she came for.
events: map.started {source, utm, has_state}
BEAT 3 · S2

Naming her kids warms the whole thing up

warming

Typing "Maya" changes her posture: this stopped being a form about her email and became a thing about her daughter. Interests chips are fun to tap. The help-with question quietly tells us her real worry, the single most valuable field in the funnel.

draft copy · kids screen Who are we mapping?
Per kid: name · born (month + year) · "what's Maya loving right now?" (chips: dinosaurs, horses, space, drawing, trucks, ocean...) · "what would you love a hand with first?" (chips keyed to age)
No pressure, you can always add more later.
Front end
  • Add-kid list pattern from the onboarding spec (month + year pickers, no day)
  • Out-of-range kid gets the sage card inline, never a rejection
  • Kid cap 5, "add another" stays visible
Back end
  • POST /api/map/children per kid: insert lead_children (band computed server-side)
  • pending_band flag for unscored ages
  • emit map.child_added {band, in_range}
Friction risk: "why do you need birth month?" One line answers it: "so Maya's checklist asks about her age, not a grade." Grade is ambiguous for homeschoolers; the planning docs already ruled birth month over grade.
Delight: the moment the flow says her kid's name back to her, correctly, in the very next screen.
events: map.child_added · map.out_of_range_shown {choice}
BEAT 4 · S3

The checklist: her noticing becomes the instrument

"oh, she does do that"

One question per screen, four chips, thumb-reach. The questions are things she has watched happen at the kitchen table ("if you put 5 blocks down and ask how many, can she count and tell you?"). Around the third one she realizes she can answer from memory. That reframe, "I am the instrument, and I'm credible", is the product's emotional patent, borrowed here in miniature.

draft copy · checklist intro Maya's checklist
About 5 minutes. Answer from what you've seen her do, day to day. Skip anything, "not sure" is a real answer, and blanks are fine, they're just what's next.
a checklist for you, never a test for her ✎
Front end
  • Chips: easily · getting there · not yet · not sure (+ skip)
  • Domain intro micro-headers ("Let's talk about numbers")
  • Progress dots by domain; micro-affirmation after a streak of "easily", never after blanks
  • Every tap saved instantly; back button safe
Back end
  • POST /api/map/answer per tap: upsert lead_answers
  • Adaptive ladder: server returns next question (band-above on "easily")
  • emit map.question_answered {domain, answer_type}
Friction risk: fatigue around domain 10. Mitigations: visible progress, domains ordered to lead with likely-yes items (the early-yes bias rule from the drip design), and a "finish later" exit that keeps everything.
Delight: occasional echo of what she said: "noted, she's counting past 20, that's ahead of her band." Tiny, factual, never gushing.
events: map.question_answered · map.child_completed {duration, skips, not_sure_count} · map.abandoned {at_question}
Make-or-break moment 1

The third checkbox

The instant she realizes her ordinary noticing IS the assessment. If items read clinical, or she has to go quiz the child, we have become the thing she fled and she quietly closes the tab. Every question must pass one test in review: can a tired mom answer this from memory, tonight?

anticipation

BEAT 6 · S5

The map opens on strengths, and she exhales

the exhale

She braces before she reads, every mother does. The first thing she sees must be true and kind at once: what is strong, named plainly, with the number in gold. Growth areas come after, each holding an activity, never a verdict. Full mock in artifact 5.

Front end
  • Report renders on screen immediately (D5 = A)
  • Strengths first, always; "growing right now" after, activity attached
  • Print styles; share button; "does something look off?" link
Back end
  • Resend sends "Maya's map is ready" with the permanent tokenized link
  • Open/click webhooks recorded; emailed_at stamped
  • emit map.report_viewed {entry: instant|email}
Friction risk: a genuinely struggling child's map. This is the red-team drill from the positioning doc: run the report for the hardest honest case and read it aloud. If it cannot be read without wincing, the sentence bank is not done.
Delight: "made with Homa for Maya" in the footer, and a map that prints beautifully for the fridge or the co-op conversation.
events: map.report_viewed · map.report_shared · map.report_printed
Make-or-break moment 2

The five-second read

She judges the map the way she judges the worksheet wow: about five seconds, phone at arm's length. Pass = her shoulders drop before a single number is parsed (strengths first, warm palette, her kid's name in the title). Fail = anything that smells like a report card. Everything upstream is logistics; this screen is the product's thesis, standards positioning as validation, not judgment, rehearsed a year early.

BEAT 7 · AFTER

The quiet weeks between map and invite

wanting more

The report's close is honest about what she cannot have yet: Homa is invite-only, a few families at a time. The map is the "before" picture; the product is watching it move. She forwards the email to her husband, which is the O8 objection (household sign-off) answering itself.

Front end
  • Report CTA band: "You're on the invite list. Your invite comes by email."
  • Share card sized for the group chat
Back end
  • No drip, no sequence. Only promise-keeping emails: a pending band ships → the checklist email goes out; her invite opens → MJ-09 claim email
  • Founder dashboard: leads by state, band coverage, completion funnel
events: map.band_ready_emailed · acquisition.invite_email_sent (existing plan)
BEAT 8 · INVITE DAY

The payoff: Homa already knows her family

believing

Months later her invite arrives. She claims her account and the product does not ask her to start over: it greets her with what she told us, asks only what changed, and aims the first worksheet at the exact help-with she named. The lead magnet was secretly onboarding all along.

I kept Maya's map from July. She was counting past 20 then, shall we see what's moved? Two minutes and her first page is ready.

Back end

Persona variants worth designing for

PersonaWhat changes in the flow
P1 · validation-seekerShe wants the gold. Ahead states render proudly (gold is allowed to feel good), the band-above ladder gives her headroom to prove it, and the share card is built for her group chat. Her report close leans "watch the numbers move".
P2 · three kids, mixed agesThe whole deferral design exists for her: map the 4-year-old now, link for the 7-year-old tonight, sage-card promise for the 10-year-old. Her report emails arrive per kid; nothing ever compares them.
P3 · anxious newcomerThe baseline persona above. Extra: her state (if given) adds one reassuring line to the report footer ("Ohio lets you homeschool with a simple notice"), the seed of the state-pages SEO play.
P4 · compliance-focused fact-checkerShe will check one citation. The methodology section carries resolvable sources per domain and a "last verified" date. One wrong claim and she is gone forever; this is why the sentence bank is human-reviewed (D8 = A).
P5 · evenings-only, voice-firstShe starts on the couch at 9pm and finishes across two nights. Resume links are her rail. Checklist stays chips (voice comes with the product, not the lead magnet), each screen answerable in one thumb tap.
