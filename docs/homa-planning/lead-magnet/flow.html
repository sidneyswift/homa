S3
The checklist, one kid at a time
Intro sets the contract: "About 5 minutes. Answer from what you've seen Maya do, skip anything, 'not sure' is a real answer. This is a checklist for you, never a test for her." Then one question per screen, chips only: easily · getting there · not yet · not sure. Progress dots by domain.

Did she answer "easily" at the age band?
The adaptive ladder from the template bank: band question first, band-above unlocks on success.
Ask the band-above item

Next domain
"Not yet" costs nothing and triggers no extra questions. No drilling downward, ever: below the band we simply have less certainty, and the report says so honestly.

More kids waiting?

S4
Drawing Maya's map
Scoring is deterministic TypeScript against the citation-backed benchmark bank: answers → per-domain position (months ahead / at age / growing) + a confidence level per domain. No model call, so this takes under a second; the screen is a beat of theater ("checking her counting against national benchmarks..."), not a real wait.
POST /api/map/score → scores computed + lead_reports insert with report token · event map.report_generated
S5
The report, on screen and in her inbox
Full report renders immediately (artifact 5 is the mock). In parallel, Resend delivers "Maya's map is ready" with the permanent tokenized link, one email per completed child, links only, no numbers in the email (siblings never share a chart). Printable via the browser.
Resend send → lead_reports.emailed_at · events map.report_viewed / map.report_shared
LATER · THE BRIDGE
Invite day: the lead becomes a family
When her invite goes out (MJ-09), the claim link creates her real account, calls create_my_family(), copies each lead child into children.profile (interests, help-with, initial positions with provenance "map, parent-reported, dated"), marks the lead claimed, and drops her into onboarding with the 3-minute wow already aimed at what she told us. The map she holds becomes the "before" in her first before/after.

If, then: the edge rules

Everything that is not the happy path, as one table. Each row is a rule the build must satisfy; most reuse patterns the planning corpus already ruled on.

IfThen
She abandons after S1 (email only)She is exactly a waitlist signup, which is today's whole funnel. One email after ~24h: "Maya's map is 12 taps away", single send, never a sequence. Resume link restores the flow.
She abandons mid-checklistEvery tap was already saved. Same single reminder rule. Partial answers still enrich her family's context; the report waits until enough domains have signal.
She answers "not sure" to most itemsReport renders with honest confidence language per domain: "we haven't seen enough about writing yet to place it, that's normal", never a guess dressed as a number. Matches the product's confidence-system design.
She skips entire domainsDomain renders as "not mapped yet" with one line on what would fill it in. No penalty, no gap-shaming.
Every kid is outside scored bandsNo dead end: basics + interests captured for all, sage-card promise per kid, confirmation screen thanks her and sets the expectation, waitlist status intact. When a band ships, the checklist email goes out automatically.
A domain scores below the age bandRenders as "growing right now" in sky/sage with one concrete free activity attached (presentation law: a growth area never appears without an action). The words "behind", "delay", "below average" do not exist in the template bank.
Wifi dies mid-flow (conference hall)Existing offline queue pattern: answers queue in localStorage, flush on reconnect or next visit. UI stays optimistic, tells the truth if still offline at report time ("your map will be in your inbox").
The report email bounces or failsReport already rendered on screen (D5 = A), so nothing is lost. Send retries; failures land on the founder dashboard list.
Same email, second device"Welcome back" resume via a fresh emailed link. Never two divergent lead records for one email (unique constraint, same as waitlist).
Bot fills the formHoneypot pretends success and stores nothing (exists today); per-IP rate limits and payload caps on every route; kid cap 5; zod validation server-side.

She replies to the report emailReplies route to the support inbox, answered by hand at this scale. The email says so plainly ("reply and a real person answers").
