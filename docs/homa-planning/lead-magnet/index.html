Waitlist v2 · the context-grab lead magnet

Meet your child's map

Today the site asks for an email and gives nothing back. V2 makes the landing page's own promise ("know exactly where your child stands, without ever testing them") real before the product exists: a parent answers a short checklist per child and gets a beautiful, benchmark-grounded report by email. We keep every answer, so when their invite opens, Homa already knows their kids.

The trade

Report for context

She gives us the full family picture (kids, ages, per-domain answers, interests, what she wants help with). We give her the first honest answer to "is my kid okay?" she has ever gotten without testing the child.

The continuity

The CTA finally lands

The copy kit's CTA "Meet your child's map" was shelved in the build log because nothing existed for it to lead to. This flow is that thing. Hero, CTA, report, and future product all speak one language: the map.

Nothing wasted

Onboarding, pre-answered

Every field matches the real product schema (birth month, interests, help-with, domain positions). At invite time the 3-minute wow starts pre-loaded: Homa greets her already knowing Maya loves dinosaurs and counting is the focus.

The eight decisions

Each has a recommendation and the reasoning. Tap an option on each card (or write over it), then use the composer at the bottom to copy your answers back to me in one paste. Nothing is built until you rule.

D1

Timing against the July 13 conference

Deliverable 0 (landing + waitlist) is live and done. The question is whether v2 fronts the conference crowd. Ages 2 to 7 can ship fast because the question bank exists; ages 8 to 16 need new content behind the citation rigor bar (founder decision D3 requires it).

A · Two-stage: ship ages 2 to 7 by July 13, extend to 16 afterRecommended
Conference moms with young kids get the full map on the spot. Kids 8 to 16 are still captured (name, age, interests, help-with) with the honest sage card: "his checklist is on the way, I'll email you the moment it's ready", and that promise is kept automatically as each band ships. Riskiest content is not rushed.
B · Waitlist stands for July 13, v2 ships complete (2 to 16) a few weeks after
Zero conference risk, one launch. But the conference audience, the warmest traffic of the year, hits the thin funnel.
C · Full 2 to 16 by July 13
Nine new age bands of citation-backed content, reviewed, in 8 days, while also building the flow. Possible with parallel agents but rigor review becomes the bottleneck. Not recommended.
D2

What we call it, and what we compare against

"See where your child ranks" collides with the brand's never-say law ("behind", leaderboard framing) and "national average" implies a norm study we do not have. What we truly have is stronger: citation-backed national benchmarks (CDC, Common Core, NGSS, CASEL and friends) and a months-ahead estimate, the same math your personal Homa uses.

A · "See where your child stands" · compared against national benchmarks · months-ahead framingRecommended
Matches the shipped hero verbatim, survives fact-checkers (every domain row cites its framework), and satisfies the ranking itch honestly: gold "+14 months ahead" numbers are allowed to feel great.
B · Percentile framing ("top 12% for her age")
Punchier marketing, but we would be inventing a norm population. One "where does that percentile come from?" post in a Facebook group burns the trust channel. Could be revisited when real cohort data exists.
D3

Real accounts now, or lead records with a claim step at invite time

Your sketch says "create an account and family and save all the results." Two ways to honor it. Note the PRD lists public signup as an enforced non-goal (invite codes only), and the app deliberately holds no service-role key today, so option B changes two standing postures.

A · Lead records now, account claim at inviteRecommended
New lead_families / lead_children / lead_answers / lead_reports tables, deny-all RLS, server-only access. No passwords asked mid-funnel, no public auth surface, PRD non-goal intact. At invite, one claim flow creates the auth user + family and copies everything over (that bridge is MJ-09 work we owe anyway). To her it reads: "your account is ready, everything you told me is already inside."
B · Real Supabase Auth accounts + family rows during the flow
Literal "account exists today." Costs: password or magic-link friction mid-funnel, bots can mint auth users, family-creation policies open to the public before the M1-07 adversarial isolation suite exists, and the PRD non-goal needs a logged amendment. Doable, just heavier and earlier than it needs to be.
D4

How long is the checklist per child

The template bank asks at the child's band plus one band above, across 15 domains. Depth is the context grab; length is the drop-off risk. Everything is chip-answerable, every question skippable, "not sure" is always a valid answer, and each answer auto-saves so abandonment still captures context.

A · Full map: ~25 to 30 questions, 5 to 8 minutes, adaptive ladderRecommended
Start each domain at the age band; an "easily" unlocks the band-above question, a "not yet" skips it. Enough signal for an honest 15-domain report and a rich profile. If completion drops under ~40% we ship the C fallback.
B · Quick look: 10 questions, 2 minutes, 6 headline domains
Higher completion, thinner report, thinner context. The report loses the "78 subdomains" credibility feel your screenshots have.
C · Quick look first, then "want the full map?" upsell into A
Best completion math on paper, but two reports per kid to design and a weaker single wow. Good fallback, awkward first ship.
D5

Where the report appears

Scoring is deterministic TypeScript (no model call), so the map is ready the instant the last chip is tapped. The email exists for durability, the return visit, and forwarding to a spouse.

A · On screen immediately, plus the email with the permanent linkRecommended
Never withhold a wow that is already computed. The email subject line ("Maya's map is ready") lands while she is still looking at it, which also trains her that Homa emails are worth opening.
B · Email only ("check your inbox")
Verifies the email address harder and builds a beat of anticipation, but risks the spam folder eating the wow, and feels withholding for data she just gave us.
D6

What happens to people already on the waitlist

The current form promises "your email is only used to send your invite." A map announcement is arguably invite-adjacent, but it is a new use of the address, so it is your call, not mine.

A · One single email: "your spot now comes with your child's map"Recommended
One send, no sequence, framed as the waitlist getting better rather than a new pitch. Recovers context from every signup collected at the conference.
B · Leave them alone until their invite, which then includes the map step
Cleanest reading of the original promise; slower context capture.
D7

Kids outside the scored range (if D1 = A)

The honest-handling pattern already written into the journeys (the sage card, never a rejection) applies here. Question is how much we still ask.

A · Capture basics + interests + help-with, promise the checklist, keep the promise automaticallyRecommended
"I can map Maya today. Jacob's checklist for his age is on the way, I'll email you the moment it's ready." A flag flips when his band ships and the email goes out without anyone remembering to send it.
B · Hide unsupported ages from the flow entirely
Simpler, but it ambushes the mom of a 10-year-old mid-flow and violates the out-of-range honesty pattern the planning docs already ruled on.
D8

Who writes the sentences in the report

The numbers come from the deterministic scorer either way. The question is the prose around them, which is where claims law lives.

A · Curated sentence banks (template strings), reviewed once, zero model callsRecommended
Every sentence a parent reads was approved by a human before launch. Instant, free, no hallucination surface, honest "no AI touched your answers" line available for the AI-wary channel. Model-written personalization can layer on later behind review.
B · Model-written narrative per report
Warmer and more specific, but every report becomes a claims-law risk surface and a per-lead cost, pre-revenue.

Risks and obligations that come with this feature

Not decisions, obligations. Each needs to be true before or at launch.

Privacy policy moves up the calendar. Today we hold emails. This flow holds children's names, birth months, and developmental answers. The 1-page policy naming subprocessors (Supabase, Vercel, Resend) is a launch gate for invite #1; it becomes a launch gate for the lead magnet instead. With D8 = A we can honestly say no AI provider ever sees the answers.
The form's privacy promise changes. "Your email is only used to send your invite" becomes "we'll email you each child's map and your invite, that's it", plus a delete-anytime line on the report. The child never uses the product and the parent is the one answering, which keeps us in the parent-directed posture the planning corpus relies on, but the wording must be re-vetted.
Words with teeth. The report may never say assessment, screening, diagnose, delay, or behind. It is a map and a checklist. Below-band domains render as "growing right now" with an activity attached, in sky or sage, never red, per the anti-guilt display law and the sanctioned badge set (Ahead · On track · Growing right now).
Methodology page or the skeptics win. Facebook-group traffic arrives carrying someone else's doubt. A "how the map is made" section with resolvable citations per domain is the difference between "legit" and "AI slop quiz". It ships with v1, not later.
Abuse surface. Public writes mean bots. Honeypot (exists), per-IP rate limits, payload caps, max 5 kids per lead, zod validation on every route, and the isolation script extended to prove anon and authenticated roles can read nothing from lead tables.
Older-band face validity. Parent-observable questions get harder at 12+. Items lean on work samples ("writes a paragraph with a beginning, middle, and end") rather than quiz-the-kid prompts, and Alessa should read every new band aloud before it fronts families, same bar as the copy pass.
Pre-existing landing bug worth fixing in the same pass. The current hero shows "Loved by 12,000+ homeschooling families" and a "Start free for 14 days" CTA (ported verbatim from the design kit). Both violate standing rulings (no fabricated social proof, no pricing/trial claims). The v2 CTA band replaces that hero block anyway.
