## 1. Positioning statement

### 1a. The category decision (made deliberately)

"Homeschool tracker" is rejected by fiat (correctly): trackers log what already
happened, at $5–15/mo, and that frame caps our price and buries the efficacy loop.
Three candidates evaluated:

| Candidate | For it | Against it | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Homeschool assistant** | She already has the mental slot: "I need help." An assistant is person-shaped help, which anchors premium pricing (an assistant is obviously worth more than an app). Does not lead with "AI," which matters for the religious-wary segment (see objection O2). Matches the product brief's own one-liner and the design system's description. Room for the whole surface: planning, materials, records, discovery. | Risk of sounding admin-only, underselling the efficacy loop. Mitigated by always qualifying it in-line: "the homeschool assistant that shows you it's working." | ✅ **Recommended** |
| AI teaching companion | Signals the personalization tech. | Leads with "AI" at the exact moment trust is lowest. "Teaching" implies it teaches the child; the child is never the user (kickoff, S4), so the category name would contradict our strongest safety answer. | ❌ Rejected |
| Homeschool co-pilot (new category) | Ownable if it lands; the design system already uses "co-pilot" descriptively. | Creating a category is a multi-year, funded-marketing project; a solo founder cannot pay the education tax. "Co-pilot" is tech-industry jargon (and Microsoft's word); it does not resonate with P2 or P3. | ❌ Rejected as category; **kept as an internal metaphor** and occasional descriptive phrase inside copy |

**Decision: category = homeschool assistant**, always qualified by the efficacy claim.
We say "the homeschool assistant that shows you it's working," never bare "assistant"
in headline positions.

### 1b. The price anchor (decided)

Anchor against **curriculum spend, $300–800 per year per family**, never against
trackers. HOMA at $29/mo is about $350/yr: the cost of one boxed curriculum, except it
makes materials for your specific child all year and shows you they worked. The tracker
comparison is answered, not invited (see objection O4). Note: this document writes
pricing copy assuming flat-feeling plans; the credits-visibility call is Sidney's
(09 §call-2, ❓2).

### 1c. Canonical positioning statement (April Dunford style)

**For** homeschooling mothers of young children (preK–2 at launch, see ❓1)
**who** carry the whole school in their heads and quietly wonder "am I doing enough,
are my kids okay?"
**HOMA is a** homeschool assistant
**that** knows each child, shows you exactly where they stand against real standards
without ever testing them, makes printable materials aimed at exactly what's growing,
handles the hours and the state paperwork, and then shows you the numbers moved.

**Unlike** homeschool trackers (which log what already happened) and generic AI chat
(which forgets your child between conversations),
**because** HOMA keeps a living, compounding profile of each child, benchmarked against
citation-backed milestones, and closes the loop: this month's practice, this month's
progress, side by side. Validated by months of daily production use in the origin
family: worksheets printed every day, a 15-domain profile that compounds.

### Executive-readout variant

For homeschooling moms, HOMA is the homeschool assistant that proves the homeschool is
working: it maps each child against real standards without testing them, generates the
materials, and shows the before and after. Unlike trackers and ChatGPT, it remembers.

### Sales-enablement variant (booth, referral, husband conversation)

When a homeschool mom lies awake wondering if her kids are okay, HOMA answers with
evidence: a gentle checklist for her (never an exam for the child) becomes a map,
materials get made for exactly what's growing, and the numbers move. It costs about
what one boxed curriculum costs per year.

"Homeschool tools have always tracked the school," said [Sidney Swift], founder of

**What members get**

### Customer FAQ (10)

1. **Does my child use HOMA?**
   No. You are the user, always. HOMA briefs you, and your child gets a printed page
   and your voice. There is no child login, no child chat, no screen required for them.

2. **How can HOMA know where my child stands without testing them?**
   You answer a gentle checklist about your child: does he count to 40, what books did
   she reach for this week. It is a conversation with you, never an exam for them. Your
   answers map against published state and national milestones, and the map keeps
   updating as you talk about your days.

3. **Is this a curriculum?**
   No, and it works with whatever you use. HOMA makes supplemental practice aimed at
   what each child is ready for next, and it can compare curriculums side by side when
   you are choosing one.

4. **Is my child's information safe? Is it training AI?**
   Your family's data belongs to you. It is never used to train AI models, and that is
   contractual with every provider we use, not a promise on a webpage. We publish the
   short list of companies that process data for us. You can export or delete
   everything whenever you want. [Copy gated on executed DPAs, see §6.]

5. **Why does it cost more than my tracker?**
   A tracker records what already happened. HOMA plans the week, makes the materials,
   keeps the map, and files the paperwork. Most families spend $300 to $800 a year on
   curriculum; HOMA is about one boxed curriculum's worth, for materials made for your
   specific child all year.

6. **Can't I just use ChatGPT?**
   You can ask ChatGPT for a worksheet. It will not remember your child tomorrow. HOMA
   keeps a profile that compounds for months, checks against real milestones, turns
   voice notes into logged hours, formats state reports, and prints. The difference
   shows up in week six, not day one.

7. **What ages does HOMA cover?**
   Ages [2–7] today, with more coming. If you have older kids too, HOMA still tracks
   everyone's hours and records; the made-for-them materials arrive for older ages
   [post-GA]. We will tell you honestly what is covered before you pay.

8. **Will it satisfy my state's requirements?**
   HOMA tracks hours and builds reports in your state's format, and every rule we show
   links to the actual statute with the date we last verified it. You stay the one who
   files; HOMA makes sure nothing is missing when you do.

9. **What if I disagree with what HOMA says about my child?**
   Then you are right, and you can say so. You can correct anything on the map, and
   HOMA learns from it. You know your child; HOMA only knows what you have shared.

10. **What happens if I cancel?**
    Your records are yours. Export everything (the profile, the hours log, the
    portfolio, the reports) with one click, before or after you cancel. No lock-in, no
    guilt.

### Internal / stakeholder FAQ (5)

1. **Why will anyone pay $29 when trackers are $8?**
   Because we are not selling tracking. The efficacy loop (assess without testing →
   generate targeted materials → show the metric moved) is the origin user's stated
   "ultimate selling point" and no competitor has it. COGS supports the price
   (LLM spend well under ~$10/family/mo, ≥70% gross margin law), and the anchor is
   curriculum spend, not software. If we ever find ourselves arguing $29 vs $8, we have
   let the buyer pick the category; the fix is positioning, not price.

2. **What is our honest posture on AI + children's data?**
   Parent-directed by design: the child never touches the product, which keeps us
   outside COPPA's direct operator trigger (07-research §2), and we keep it that way as
   a design law. The 2025 COPPA amendments make AI-training disclosure a separate
   consent matter; our answer is contractual zero-retention/no-training terms with
   providers, a published subprocessor list, a written retention schedule, and
   self-serve export/delete. All launch-blocking (council S9). Marketing may not claim
   any of it before the artifacts are executed.

3. **Why doesn't ChatGPT or a big edtech player eat this?**
   The moat is the accumulating family record (hours, journal, portfolio, what-works)
   with the compounding child profile as its engine, plus a citation-backed benchmarks
   library and a print pipeline, wrapped in a trust brand a general chatbot cannot
   credibly wear with this audience. Generic AI resets every conversation; our week-six
   experience is structurally different from our day-one experience. (Full teardown in
   08-competitive-moats.md.)

4. **The asset serves ages 2–7 but the median family's kids are 5–12. What does marketing do?**
   Founder call pending (09 §call-3, ❓1). This document is written to flex: hero copy
   avoids age claims, the FAQ discloses the range honestly, and P2's out-of-range
   10-year-old gets hours/records value on day one. If the call is (a) early-years ICP,
   we say "for the early years, preK–2" proudly and sell the roadmap; if (b), copy
   needs no change, the product does.

5. **What if we can't substantiate the efficacy story?**
   We never claim causation, only shown correlation in the member's own data ("up 12
   points since the fractions set"), which is self-evidencing rather than a marketing
   claim (§6 rules). Cohort-one concierge notes hand-write real before/afters from week
   one (council #9), which builds the testimonial bank before GA. If the numbers do not
   move for a family, the honest surface is "here's what we tried, here's what we'll
   try next," which is still the assistant doing its job.

## 6. Naming & claims guardrails

### Words we never say

| Never | Because | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "diagnose," "screening," "assessment" (clinical), "delay," "disorder" | Medical/developmental claims we are not qualified or licensed to make; one misuse is a trust extinction event and a regulatory magnet | "map," "checklist," "where they stand," "what's next" |
| "behind," "ahead of grade level" as a promise, "catch up" | Violates presentation law (no deficit language, ever) and implies grade-level guarantees | "growing," "on track," "that's just what's next" |
| "tutor," "teacher," "teaches your child," "replaces" | The child is never the user; the mother is the teacher. Category confusion and objection O1 fuel | "assistant," "briefs you," "you teach, HOMA carries the bag" |
| Education-outcome guarantees ("your child will read by 5") | Unsubstantiable, FTC-actionable, and off-brand (guilt mechanics in reverse) | Show member-specific before/afters (rules below) |
| "COPPA-certified," "COPPA-compliant" as a badge | Not a certification that exists in that form; invites scrutiny of the exact claim | Concrete practices: "children never use HOMA," "no AI training on family data," "export or delete anytime" |
| "state-approved," "guaranteed accepted" (reports) | P4 churns instantly on a wrong legal claim; acceptance is the state's call | "built to your state's format, with every rule linked to its statute and a last-verified date" |
| "curriculum" (as what HOMA is) | Accreditation implications; also picks a fight with what she already owns | "materials," "practice," "works with your curriculum" |

### Efficacy-loop claim rules (legal-adjacent, binding on all copy)

1. **Temporal, never causal.** "Reading is up 12 points since the letter-sounds set" ·
   "progress alongside the work" · "here's the before and after." Never "HOMA raised
   his scores," "our worksheets caused," "guaranteed improvement."
2. **Member-specific, shown in her own data.** The product displaying her child's
   trend next to her child's work is evidence, not advertising. Marketing may quote it
   only as a consented testimonial with a "results vary by family" disclosure.
3. **No aggregate efficacy claims** ("HOMA kids improve 2x") until we have a
   methodology we would show a journalist. Cohort-one concierge before/afters build the
   testimonial bank the compliant way (council #9).
4. **Benchmarks are cited, not owned.** "Citation-backed milestones drawn from state
   and national standards," with sources visible in-product. Never imply we set or
   certify the standards.
5. **The no-movement week has copy too.** "Here's what we tried, here's what we'll try
   next" is the honest surface. Never hide flat weeks; never spin them.
6. **Data promises ship only when executed.** "Never used to train AI" appears in
   public copy the day the no-training/ZDR DPAs are signed (launch-blocking, council
   S9), not a day earlier. Same for the subprocessor list and retention schedule.
7. **The child's likeness stays out of marketing.** No member children's photos, names,
   or voices in any marketing asset, including Alessa's family, without a separate
   written consent process (09 §call-4 posture: parent is the speaker/uploader).
