# ICP research 04: how US homeschool parents relate to AI (usage, objections, trust physics)

> Historical research snapshot, 2026-07-06. This file preserves the sampled
> market, legal, and community evidence captured on that date. It is not
> current product, legal, provider, or production guidance. Re-verify volatile
> claims before reuse.

Date: 2026-07-06
Analyst: research subagent for Homa positioning ("the parent talks, Homa types")
Method: web research conducted 2026-07-06. Sources: homeschool blogs and podcasts, the Well-Trained Mind forum, vendor sites and app store listings, national surveys (Pew, PDK, Common Sense Media, Barna, Count on Mothers, MassINC, NCES), and legal analyses of the 2025 COPPA amendments. Reddit could not be crawled directly (network-blocked on this run), so forum verbatims lean on the Well-Trained Mind forum, podcast transcripts, and blog culture; treat any Reddit-specific frequency claim as unmeasured. All quotes are verbatim from the cited pages, usernames omitted, no children's names appeared in quoted material. No numbers or quotes are fabricated. FACT and INFERENCE are labeled per section.

Key findings:

- The amnesia tax is real and articulated in the wild, in almost the exact words Homa's Path C predicts: "Every single time. In every conversation. Which is exhausting and defeats the purpose of having a tool that's supposed to save you time" ([Chrisanne's Life](https://chrisanneslife.com/blog/ai-tutors-homeschool-kids/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- The dominant published position across both religious and secular homeschool voices is already Homa's position: AI for the parent's prep and paperwork, kept away from the child. "AI is your assistant, but you're still the teacher" ([Educating at Home](https://www.educatingathome.com/post/homeschooling-with-ai-yay-or-nay), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Stated privacy fear is very high (84% of parents concerned about misuse of young people's data, [Common Sense Media 2026](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/sites/default/files/research/report/commonsensemedia_generationai.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06) but revealed behavior contradicts it: moms voluntarily paste ADHD profiles into custom GPTs and upload photos of their kids' schoolwork to GPT-4o captioning apps.
- No survey segments AI attitudes by homeschool status. The religious segment's gatekeepers (HSLDA, Answers in Genesis, convention speakers, apologists) have converged on cautious permission for parent-side use, not prohibition; outright rejection is a loud minority position.
- Hallucination is the most frequently observed objection, and it has a compliance-specific horror story: a parent nearly filed an AI-invented course description with state officials ([Campfire Curriculums](https://campfirecurriculums.com/ai-part-1/), accessed 2026-07-06).

## 1. Actual ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini usage for homeschooling

Confidence: High that usage is common and spans the expected tasks; Medium on the amnesia tax's population frequency (direct articulations found, but the count of independent voices is modest).

FACT. Usage is widespread enough to have produced a mature how-to genre written by homeschool parents for homeschool parents. Documented tasks match the full expected list:

- Lesson plans, schedules, curriculum comparison, book lists. A homeschool mom's guide lists course schedules, "Generate lists of literally anything you need," building your own curriculum, and comparing curricula ([Mercer Homeschooling](https://www.mercerhomeschooling.com/homeschool-with-chatgpt/), accessed 2026-07-06). Another parent used ChatGPT to compare published fifth-grade science curricula and found it "able to pick out good options and evaluate the benefits (and negatives) of each" ([Commonplace](https://www.commonplace.org/p/ai-is-a-homeschooling-tool), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Worksheets, unit studies, assessments. "Claude is what I use for lesson planning, custom worksheet creation, curriculum design, and assessment generation" ([Skip School](https://skipschool.ai/ai-homeschool-complete-guide), accessed 2026-07-06). A mom who "was skeptical" asked ChatGPT for book club discussion questions, "assumed the questions would be generic," and now uses it for six-week unit study outlines and lesson plans ([The Organized Homeschooler](https://www.organizedhomeschooler.com/ways-for-parents-to-use-ai-in-homeschool/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- IEP-ish accommodations and learning differences. A special-needs homeschool writer calls AI "an enthusiastic, overconfident assistant" useful for drafting and research, while warning against using it for skills a struggling learner must keep fresh ([Learn Differently](https://www.learndifferently.com/2025/10/13/learning-challenges-and-ai-part-one/), accessed 2026-07-06). Parents write accommodations directly into custom GPT instructions: "He has ADHD, keep things under 5 minutes, use Minecraft examples, never say 'let's practice.'" ([Chrisanne's Life](https://chrisanneslife.com/blog/ai-tutors-homeschool-kids/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Compliance paperwork. High school course descriptions are a recurring use case on the Well-Trained Mind forum. One member found it "actually really helpful" as a starting point; another said the output "read more like a sales pitch than a description" ([Well-Trained Mind forum](https://forums.welltrainedmind.com/topic/728258-using-chat-gpt-to-help-write-course-descriptions/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Working-mom workflow. "ChatGPT helps me take my scattered notes and shape them into something I can act on quickly and share with my husband" ([Stream of Blue](https://streamofblue.com/chatgpt-homeschooling-working-parents/), accessed 2026-07-06). The podcast framing is the same: "so many of us are finding part-time or full-time work... how can the busy mom implement AI into her schedule" ([Show Me Homeschool](https://www.showmehomeschool.com/podcast/navigating-ai-in-homeschool-tools-tips-and-biblical-wisdom-with-nancy-manos), accessed 2026-07-06).
- The enthusiast pole exists too, mostly child-facing: a homeschool dad of five reports "In our house, 'Have you asked AI for assistance?' is a common refrain" ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/shielding-my-kids-from-ai-would-be-a-mistake-2025-7), accessed 2026-07-06), and voice-mode drills in the car are a recommended pattern ([Skip School](https://skipschool.ai/ai-homeschool-complete-guide), accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT. The amnesia tax (re-explaining the kid's context every session) is articulated directly:

- "It didn't know our kids... Regular AI doesn't account for any of that unless you tell it to. Every single time. In every conversation. Which is exhausting and defeats the purpose of having a tool that's supposed to save you time." ([Chrisanne's Life](https://chrisanneslife.com/blog/ai-tutors-homeschool-kids/), accessed 2026-07-06)
- An educator blog names the loop that makes people quit: "Back to square one. Re-explain everything. Get a slightly-better-than-generic response. Close the tab." ([Why Edify](https://www.jeremyajorgensen.com/stop-starting-over-how-to-build-a-permanent-ai-teaching-assistant-with-chatgpt/), accessed 2026-07-06)
- The standard community workaround is a hand-maintained context blob: "You can even create a template paragraph that includes all the basic information Chat GPT needs to know about you (homeschooler, homeschooling style, ages/grades of kids, etc.) and copy and paste that at the beginning of each chat" ([Mercer Homeschooling](https://www.mercerhomeschooling.com/homeschool-with-chatgpt/), accessed 2026-07-06). A prompt guide likewise tells parents to build a master prompt with the child's age, learning style, strengths, and struggles and reuse it in new threads ([AI Adopters Club](https://aiadopters.club/p/how-to-use-chatgpt-for-homeschool), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Even the negative case is a context-cost complaint: a veteran forum member rejects the tool because entering the context costs as much as the writing. "In the time I have told Chat GPT what it should put in there, I have written the paragraph myself" ([Well-Trained Mind forum](https://forums.welltrainedmind.com/topic/728258-using-chat-gpt-to-help-write-course-descriptions/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- OpenAI itself markets Projects to educators against this exact pain: "Projects are most useful when you want ChatGPT to stay grounded in the same materials instead of re-explaining the context every time" ([OpenAI Academy](https://academy.openai.com/en/public/clubs/k-12-education-aacga/blogs/organize-ongoing-class-work-with-projects-2026-05-21), accessed 2026-07-06).

INFERENCE. The pain is real but the frontier is moving: power users now solve it with custom GPTs and Claude Projects, a setup that takes "an afternoon" plus about five minutes a week of tweaking ([Chrisanne's Life](https://chrisanneslife.com/blog/ai-tutors-homeschool-kids/), accessed 2026-07-06). Homa's wedge is the majority who will never do that setup, plus the thing no chat workaround holds: multi-child, records-grade memory that compounds into portfolios, hours logs, and compliance documents.

## 2. Objection taxonomy, ranked by observed frequency

Confidence: Medium. Ranking reflects frequency across the roughly 40 sources collected for this file, not a population survey. Letters refer to the brief's taxonomy.

1. (d) Hallucination/accuracy. Present in nearly every source, pro and con.
   - "The textbook and resources that it included are completely made up" and "Yeah, they don't know how to not hallucinate" ([Well-Trained Mind forum](https://forums.welltrainedmind.com/topic/728258-using-chat-gpt-to-help-write-course-descriptions/), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - The compliance-grade failure: a user generated a course description for state officials that did not match the actual curriculum, which "can make homeschoolers seem like they are turning in 'course descriptions' and 'objectives,' randomly lying about what their child is learning" ([Campfire Curriculums](https://campfirecurriculums.com/ai-part-1/), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - An anti-AI homeschool blogger cites research that "ChatGPT agreed with false statements up to one-quarter of the time" ([Acorn to Oak](https://www.acorntoak.com/2025/06/10/an-argument-against-using-ai-in-homeschooling/), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - Verdict: addressable (review-before-use is already the community norm), but a deal-breaker in the legal-requirements lane unless output visibly cites sources and invites review.
2. Child dependency and thinking atrophy (spans (a) and (g), and often stands alone). The most emotionally loaded objection.
   - "AI can inhibit our conclusions, because we have missed out on the critical tasks and experiences that would have allowed us to draw those very conclusions" ([Acorn to Oak](https://www.acorntoak.com/2025/06/10/an-argument-against-using-ai-in-homeschooling/), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - 67% of parents agree that using AI will make kids less creative ([Common Sense Media 2025 summit report](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/sites/default/files/research/report/2025-common-sense-summit-report-full-web.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - Verdict: fully addressable for Homa. It evaporates when the child never touches the product; it is the strongest argument FOR the parent-only architecture.
3. (a) Replacing the mother's role/identity. Usually voiced as a boundary rather than a rejection.
   - "When it comes to the weighty task (and beautiful privilege) of teaching our own children, we should be careful who (or what) we trust for help" ([Excelsior Classes](https://excelsiorclasses.com/does-ai-belong-in-homeschool-when-technology-stops-helping/), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - "It won't ever know your child's heart, struggles, or their spark the way you do" ([Liberated to Learn](https://liberatedtolearn.com/ai-in-homeschooling/), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - The nightmare version is supplied by the AI industry itself, quoted in a Gospel Coalition episode: "we're not trying to replace Google, we're trying to replace your mom" ([The Gospel Coalition](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/podcasts/recorded/parenting-age-ai/), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - Verdict: addressable; every pro-AI homeschool source pre-answers it with the assistant framing, which is evidence the reassurance works.
4. (c) Child data privacy and training fears. Massive in surveys, quieter in homeschool forums.
   - 68% of parents did not support giving AI software access to students' grades, assessment data, or other personal information ([EdWeek on the 2025 PDK poll](https://www.edweek.org/technology/americans-grow-more-skeptical-of-ai-in-k-12-schools-poll-finds/2025/08), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - 84% of parents are concerned about AI misusing young people's data; 81% about collection without knowledge ([Common Sense Media 2026](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/sites/default/files/research/report/commonsensemedia_generationai.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - Vendor behavior shows it surfaces at signup: Homeschool Moment pre-answers with "Your children's biometric data is never collected, stored, or processed... we're homeschool parents too" ([Homeschool Moment](https://homeschoolmoment.com/), accessed 2026-07-06), and AdaptiPath's FAQ includes "Do you use my data to train your" models plus a COPPA item ([AdaptiPath FAQ](https://www.adaptipath.com/faq), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - Verdict: addressable with explicit plain-language promises; a deal-breaker only if the answer is buried or hedged.
5. (b) Screens and tech values. Strong norm, but aimed at the child's experience, not the mother's tools.
   - "The more real life activities you can do with your kids. Screen free, real books, real activities, cooking, taking care of the chickens... the better equipped they will be to use AI tools" ([Show Me Homeschool](https://www.showmehomeschool.com/podcast/navigating-ai-in-homeschool-tools-tips-and-biblical-wisdom-with-nancy-manos), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - Hard-no voices exist: "It's a NO from this mom. Loud, clear, and unapologetic" ([Orlando Mom](https://orlandomom.com/raising-one-foot-in-gen-z-and-one-in-gen-alpha-why-this-mom-is-a-hard-no-on-classroom-ai/), accessed 2026-07-06). "I have a visceral response to the motto that's being shoved down our throats right now: AI is here to stay, so adapt or fall behind" ([The Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/life/article-no-i-dont-want-my-kids-using-your-stupid-ai/), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - Verdict: addressable under the parent-tool framing ("more paper and outings" converts this objection into a selling point); deal-breaker wherever the child would use a screen.
6. (e) Worldview/doctrine contamination (religious segment).
   - "Prominent voices in Christian education, such as Answers in Genesis and The Gospel Coalition, warn that AI models are programmed by humans and heavily scrape secular data," and classical Christian critics call AI a "fraudulent shortcut" that is "taking jobs away from Christian writers" ([Medium, AI in a Homeschool Curriculum?](https://medium.com/@hillary.beth.trivette/ai-in-a-homeschool-curriculum-5e38131650f2), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - 83% of practicing Christians worry about AI misinterpreting scripture ([Barna](https://www.barna.com/research/christians-trust-ai-flourishing-spiritual-authority/), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - Working mitigation observed in the wild: a Christian homeschool mom instructs the model to keep her science lessons creation-based and reports "It honors that" ([Lamp and Light Living](https://www.lampandlightliving.com/post/i-wanted-to-be-laura-ingalls-god-had-other-plans), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - Verdict: deal-breaker for a traditionalist minority; addressable for the majority when the parent stays the doctrinal filter and the product never preaches.
7. (f) Slop quality contempt. Aimed mostly at products and content-mills, not at moms' own use.
   - A Christian curriculum founder: tired "of algorithms and hooks and scripts and AI generated content that is so predictable it feels downright condescending" ([Gather 'Round newsletter feed](https://gatherroundhomeschool.kit.com/posts/july-8-2025), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - Campfire Curriculums is "adamantly against AI in writing educational content" ([Campfire Curriculums](https://campfirecurriculums.com/ai-part-1/), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - Public school parents mock AI worksheets depicting people with "monstrously distorted facial features" as "AI slop" ([Houston Chronicle op-ed](https://eedition.houstonchronicle.com/infinity/article_popover_share.aspx?guid=67a02fb1-e676-461b-9d1b-f2a99833b053&share=true), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - Verdict: only addressable through actual output quality; one visible slop artifact torches trust, and curriculum-founder influencers amplify it.
8. (g) Plagiarism/cheating framing. Framed around students, not parents.
   - HSLDA's webinar asks "is AI a gateway to rampant academic dishonesty?" ([HSLDA](https://hslda.org/post/harnessing-the-influence-of-ai-in-your-homeschool-part-2), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - The community rule of thumb: "If it would be cheating to have a human do it for you, it's cheating to use AI to do it for you" ([Neil Shenvi](https://shenviapologetics.com/ai-and-homeschooling/), accessed 2026-07-06). The family-rule version: "We don't use AI to write things we're supposed to write ourselves" ([4onemore](https://www.4onemore.com/ai-for-homeschool-parents/), accessed 2026-07-06).
   - Verdict: mostly irrelevant to a parent-only tool; becomes relevant only if marketing blurs who is doing the child's work.

INFERENCE. Deal-breakers vs addressable, summarized: nothing in the taxonomy is fatal to a product the child never uses, except (f) slop (self-inflicted) and (d) hallucination in the compliance lane (mitigable with citations and a review step). The whole taxonomy collapses remarkably well under "parent stays the teacher; the kid gets paper and outings."

## 3. The religious/conservative segment

Confidence: Medium-High.

FACT on segment size. Among homeschooled students in 2022-23, 75% had parents rating "a desire to provide moral instruction" as important and 83% "concern about environment of other schools" ([NCES NHES 2023 summary](https://nces.ed.gov/pubs2024/2024113_Summary.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06); in 2019, 13% of homeschooled students had parents naming religious instruction the single most important reason ([NCES](https://nces.ed.gov/programs/coe/indicator/tgk/homeschooled-children), accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT. The institutional gatekeepers have landed on permission with guardrails, not prohibition:

- An apologetics essay circulating in classical Christian homeschool circles notes that "The Southern Baptist Convention's Ethics and Religious Liberty Commission, Pastor John Piper, the Archbishop of Canterbury, and the Vatican" have all affirmed AI can be used morally, and that "none of them have judged AI to be inherently wicked or ethically impermissible" ([Neil Shenvi](https://shenviapologetics.com/ai-and-homeschooling/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- HSLDA runs a two-part webinar series on handling AI "with wisdom" in the homeschool ([HSLDA part 1](https://hslda.org/post/ai-and-your-homeschool-part-1), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Great Homeschool Conventions programmed multiple 2025 AI workshops, including "AI is Here to Stay. How to Incorporate AI Into Your Homeschool" ([Great Homeschool Conventions](https://greathomeschoolconventions.com/workshops/ai-is-here-to-stay-how-do-i-incorporate-ai-into-my-homeschool), accessed 2026-07-06) and a session promising "raising adaptable, faith-filled kids who can't be replaced by robots" ([Great Homeschool Conventions](https://greathomeschoolconventions.com/workshops/how-to-prepare-your-kids-for-the-ai-revolution-future-ready-skills), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Answers in Genesis draws the line exactly where Homa does: "Parents might prompt AI to brainstorm personalized ideas for spending time as a family, but parents must not hand over their children to AI 'electronic babysitters' at the expense of personally raising, discipling, and spending time with their children" ([Answers in Genesis](https://answersingenesis.org/technology/ai-useful-tool-or-existential-threat/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- A Gospel Coalition episode titled "Not a Child-Safe Technology" lands on parent-first use: "as you're planning a birthday party, preparing for vacation or organizing your calendar... if it can be a good and useful tool for you, use it well to the glory of God" and "in time teach your kids to do that too" ([The Gospel Coalition](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/podcasts/recorded/parenting-age-ai/), accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT. Stewardship-framed softening stories are documented, always with the parent as the user:

- "Consider me Jonah. I was running from it, God wasn't letting me, so here we are" (a Christian homeschool mom who now uses AI to build her Bible-based program) ([Lamp and Light Living](https://www.lampandlightliving.com/post/i-wanted-to-be-laura-ingalls-god-had-other-plans), accessed 2026-07-06).
- "I never expected to be a homeschooling mom talking about artificial intelligence... Not by replacing me. But by drawing out what was already there" ([Family Style Schooling](https://familystyleschooling.com/2025/08/09/using-ai-in-our-homeschool/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- "We're learning to use these tools. Then we're teaching our children to use these tools. So for me, my heart is to teach moms" ([Show Me Homeschool](https://www.showmehomeschool.com/podcast/navigating-ai-in-homeschool-tools-tips-and-biblical-wisdom-with-nancy-manos), accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT. The exact target sentence "I'm not against AI, I just don't want it teaching my kids" did not surface verbatim in this run. Close variants exist:

- "AI is your assistant, but you're still the teacher" ([Educating at Home](https://www.educatingathome.com/post/homeschooling-with-ai-yay-or-nay), accessed 2026-07-06).
- "Parents should take the lead in how it is used, and some may choose to limit direct use to adults in their home" ([Homeschool Boss](https://homeschoolboss.com/2026/03/using-ai-in-homeschooling-a-practical-guide-for-parents/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- "I am not anti-technology; I am tech-intentional" ([First Fish Substack](https://firstfish.substack.com/p/what-we-need-to-know-about-edtech), accessed 2026-07-06).
- "These families aren't anti-technology; they're pro-human development" ([Michael Mount Waldorf School](https://www.michaelmount.co.za/resource-library/why-waldorf-prepares-child-for-ai/), accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT on faith-segment ambivalence. Practicing Christians simultaneously lead in optimism (66% say AI is improving their lives) and match the nation in fear (57% call AI a threat) ([Barna](https://www.barna.com/research/christians-view-ai-gift-threat/), accessed 2026-07-06). Pastors are more cautious (72% call AI a threat) yet only 13% report not using AI at all ([Gloo press release on Barna data](https://gloo.com/press/releases/majority-of-pastors-use-ai-in-ministry-amid-concerns-and-caution-new-research-finds), accessed 2026-07-06). And 34% of practicing Christians already say spiritual advice from AI is as trustworthy as a pastor's ([Barna](https://www.barna.com/research/christians-trust-ai-flourishing-spiritual-authority/), accessed 2026-07-06).

INFERENCE. Pastor and community influence functions as a permission structure, not a veto: since denominational authorities publicly model cautious use, a mom adopting a parent-side AI tool is not defying her community. The residual hard-no bloc is the classical/traditionalist wing that objects to AI-written content itself. Christian publishers already ship AI features into this market without documented revolt: Gather 'Round announced its "Gather Guide" AI bot to its list in July 2025 with the hedge "He's still in training, so answers might not be 100%" ([Gather 'Round newsletter](https://gatherroundhomeschool.kit.com/posts/july-8-2025), accessed 2026-07-06), and community criticism of that curriculum remains about academic gaps, not AI ([How Do I Homeschool review roundup](https://howdoihomeschool.com/gather-round-homeschool-curriculum-review/), accessed 2026-07-06).

## 4. The secular/crunchy segment

Confidence: Medium.

FACT. Waldorf-adjacent families object developmentally, not theologically. Waldorf schooling excludes screens through the elementary years by design ([Waldorf Academy Toronto](https://waldorfacademy.org/blog/conversation-over-chatgpt), accessed 2026-07-06) and markets low-tech childhood as the best AI-era preparation, noting Silicon Valley tech parents choose it ([Michael Mount, written by Waldorf School of the Peninsula](https://www.michaelmount.co.za/resource-library/why-waldorf-prepares-child-for-ai/), accessed 2026-07-06). An AI-for-homeschooling hub describes the accepted pattern: "Most Waldorf families using this hub will find AI useful for the parent's administrative work and research, and will keep AI almost entirely out of the child's direct experience until adolescence" ([Beginners in AI](https://beginnersinai.org/ai-for-homeschooling/), accessed 2026-07-06). The same hub notes the unschooler's inverse posture: AI as "an infinite patient research partner" for the child's rabbit holes, with the risk being crowding out books and mentors (same page).

FACT. The data-broker distrust is sharper and better informed here than in the religious segment:

- A tech-intentional education Substack cites Internet Safety Labs' finding that "96% of EdTech tools share children's personal information with third parties" ([First Fish Substack](https://firstfish.substack.com/p/what-we-need-to-know-about-edtech), accessed 2026-07-06).
- An AI-in-education series notes homeschool families using consumer platforms sit in "a data-rights gray zone where FERPA doesn't apply," and that free apps popular with homeschoolers often have the most aggressive data collection ([AI in Plain English](https://ai.plainenglish.io/the-ai-classroom-stack-episode-3-who-controls-the-algorithm-9c929ec86210), accessed 2026-07-06).
- There is an anti-quantification strand: an ML-researcher homeschool couple publicly rejected performance dashboards for their child in favor of engagement and curiosity ([DeepTrendLab, summarizing a fast.ai post](https://www.deeptrendlab.com/article/i-dont-want-a-learning-dashboard-for-my-child-677901), accessed 2026-07-06).
- The tech-insider version of screen caution: a Google AI professional raising a low-tech daughter writes that parenting in the AI age "is about teaching your little one to still think for themselves and be creative with their thoughts, within a system that makes it so easy not to" ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/ai-parenting-kids-critical-thinking-2026-7), accessed 2026-07-06).

INFERENCE. Differences from the religious segment: the crunchy objection is about childhood development, surveillance capitalism, and authenticity. There is no doctrine filter and no pastor gatekeeper; trust is won by architecture (child never touches it, data never sold, output is paper and outings) rather than by authority endorsement. Overlap with Homa's positioning is near-total. The risk is aesthetic: anything that smells like a dashboard, gamification, or "optimization" of the child alienates this segment, per the fast.ai anecdote above.

## 5. Reception of existing AI homeschool tools

Confidence: Medium. Vendor claims verified from primary pages; independent community reaction is thin for all five, which is itself a finding.

FACT, tool by tool:

- Homeschool Fox "Kit": phone-first activity logging with AI parsing and voice input, $12/mo or $99/yr family-flat, built by a homeschooling couple; premium includes 500 AI actions per month with paid credit top-ups ([Homeschool Fox](https://homeschoolfox.com/try), accessed 2026-07-06; [activity parsing docs](https://homeschoolfox.com/docs/kit/activity-parsing), accessed 2026-07-06). Site testimonial: "Kit is a game changer. I just tell it what we did and it logs everything. Saves me so much time every week" ([Homeschool Fox](https://homeschoolfox.com/try), accessed 2026-07-06). Note the framing on the same page: "like having a helpful co-teacher who handles the paperwork."
- HomeschoolPlanner.com: "an experienced homeschool administrator in your pocket," AI scheduling with auto-rebalancing plus a K-8 AI worksheet generator with per-tier monthly limits, $59.99 to $119.99/yr ([App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/homeschool-planner/id6745445641), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Homeschool Moment: photo-first documentation where "GPT-4o reads your photos" for captions and subject tags, free tier of 5 entries per month, premium $12/mo ([comparison page](https://homeschoolmoment.com/compare), accessed 2026-07-06). Its FAQ leads with privacy reassurance: no facial recognition, "Your children's biometric data is never collected, stored, or processed... we're homeschool parents too" ([Homeschool Moment](https://homeschoolmoment.com/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- AdaptiPath: K-6 AI lesson generation in a hype register ("Save 10+ Hours Weekly," "#1," exclamation-heavy testimonials), FAQ includes data-training and COPPA items ([AdaptiPath](https://www.adaptipath.com/curriculum), accessed 2026-07-06; [AdaptiPath FAQ](https://www.adaptipath.com/faq), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Homeschool Managed "Clara": AI "brain dump processing" that turns a parent's unstructured dump into schedules and per-student task views, with neurodivergent-support settings per student, free during beta with a permanent founding-family rate at $19/mo planned; positioning is anti-hype: "We are committed to Clara being a tool that actually relieves mental load" ([Homeschool Managed pricing](https://www.homeschoolmanaged.com/pricing), accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT. Searches for independent Reddit or Facebook reception of Kit, AdaptiPath, Homeschool Moment, HomeschoolPlanner, and Clara returned no substantive community threads on this run, only vendor pages and SEO comparison content (searches conducted 2026-07-06; the comparison ecosystem is largely vendor-authored, e.g. [Homeschool Fox's own comparison pages](https://homeschoolfox.com/vs/homeschool-manager), accessed 2026-07-06). No torched-in-a-group backlash case study surfaced for any of the five named tools.

INFERENCE. This category is pre-chasm: the tools are young, priced $9 to $19 per month, and validated mostly by their own testimonials. Nobody yet owns the "AI staff for the homeschool mother" position. The observed convention is to soften the AI with a named persona (Kit, Clara) plus a built-by-homeschool-parents trust marker. The absence of backlash so far is consistent with the section 2 finding: parent-side AI is socially acceptable in a way child-facing AI is not, and the tools above are all parent-side.

## 6. Broader parent-AI attitude data, 2025-2026

Confidence: High (published surveys). Caveat throughout: none of these segments by homeschool status.

- Pew Research Center (fielded Sept 25 to Oct 9, 2025; published Feb 24, 2026; 1,458 teen-parent pairs): about half of parents say their teen uses chatbots while 64% of teens report using them, and about three-in-ten parents are not sure; roughly eight-in-ten parents are OK with their teen using chatbots to search for information, but only 18% are comfortable with their teen getting emotional support or advice from a chatbot, the only use a majority opposes; roughly four-in-ten parents have never discussed chatbots with their teen ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/02/24/how-teens-use-and-view-ai/), accessed 2026-07-06). Comfort skews by income: 83% of parents in households earning $75,000+ accept information-search use vs about seven-in-ten below that line ([Pew report PDF](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/02/PI_2026.02.24_Teens-and-AI_REPORT.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).
- PDK poll (June 2025, 1,005 adults): support for teachers using AI to prepare lesson plans fell from 62% to 49% in one year; tutoring from 65% to 60%; test prep from 64% to 54%; and 68% of parent respondents did not support giving AI software access to students' grades, assessment data, or other personal information ([EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/technology/americans-grow-more-skeptical-of-ai-in-k-12-schools-poll-finds/2025/08), accessed 2026-07-06; [Educating AI Substack breakdown](https://nickpotkalitsky.substack.com/p/the-ai-reality-check-what-the-2025), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Common Sense Media (fielded Nov 2025; published Mar 2026; 1,244 parents): 84% of parents concerned about misuse of young people's data, 81% about collection without their knowledge, 78% about inaccurate information; 64% not confident AI companies prioritize teen safety; 68% want strong laws (68% of Democrats, 70% of Republicans); 75% support a government AI-safety oversight body ([Common Sense Media report PDF](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/sites/default/files/research/report/commonsensemedia_generationai.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06). An earlier wave: 43% of parents think AI will negatively affect children's learning vs 39% positive, and 67% think AI use makes kids less creative "across demographic and partisan subgroups" ([Common Sense Media 2025 summit report](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/sites/default/files/research/report/2025-common-sense-summit-report-full-web.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Count on Mothers (reported Mar 2026): only 6% of US mothers see AI as a "positive force"; about a third are open to school use with oversight; 20% want schools to limit exposure; 19% fear AI companies put profit over children's safety; 47% are anxious about impact on learning and development. Verbatim from a Massachusetts mom: "AI can help kids learn... But without strong safeguards, it can expose them to risks that can harm their safety and development" ([EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/technology/moms-across-the-political-spectrum-urge-caution-on-ai-in-schools/2026/03), accessed 2026-07-06).
- MassINC/EdTrust Massachusetts K-12 parent poll (Oct 21 to Nov 12, 2025; n=1,351): 33% positive on AI in K-12 education, 34% negative, the rest mixed or unsure; homeschoolers were only 3% of the sample ([WWLP](https://www.wwlp.com/news/massachusetts/wary-parents-divided-over-ai-use-in-schools-poll-shows/), accessed 2026-07-06; [survey PDF](https://www.wwlp.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/26/2026/01/6970f810de4e2.pdf.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Faith segmentation (Barna, with Gloo): 72% of parents are concerned about AI's impact on children and teens; 33% strongly agree they worry about the data privacy and security risks of their child using AI; just 17% actively seek out information about AI ([Barna](https://www.barna.com/research/parents-ai/), accessed 2026-07-06). Practicing Christians are simultaneously the most optimistic and among the most worried segments (see section 3). A study of 680 Catholic secondary teachers found stronger religiosity slightly reduces supportive evaluation of AI, but with practical significance the authors call "negligible" ([MDPI, Religions](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/16/8/1069), accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT. No major 2025-2026 survey breaks out homeschool parents as an AI-attitude segment. Market-size context: roughly 3.4 million US children are homeschooled per NHERI 2024-25 estimates, as cited in a homeschool-AI hub ([Beginners in AI](https://beginnersinai.org/ai-for-homeschooling/), accessed 2026-07-06).

INFERENCE. The pattern across every instrument is the same: parents accept AI as an information tool for adults and reject it as an autonomous actor toward children, and the 2024-to-2025 trend moved against child-facing AI (the PDK declines above) even as adult adoption grew. That is a tailwind for parent-side positioning and a headwind for anything that reads child-facing, including in screenshots.

## 7. COPPA 2025 amendments awareness

Confidence: High on the legal facts; Medium on community awareness, where the finding is an absence of evidence, clearly noted as such.

FACT. The FTC published amended COPPA rules on April 22, 2025 (effective June 23, 2025; full compliance required April 22, 2026). Operators must obtain separate verifiable parental consent before disclosing children's personal information to third parties for purposes not integral to the service, and the FTC stated that disclosures "to train or otherwise develop artificial intelligence technologies" are never integral ([Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2025/05/coppa-rule-ftc-amended-childrens-privacy), accessed 2026-07-06; [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/ftc-publishes-updates-to-coppa-rule), accessed 2026-07-06; [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.dataprotectionreport.com/2025/06/ftcs-coppa-rule-changes-include-ai-training-consent-requirement/), accessed 2026-07-06). Personal information now includes biometric identifiers, and indefinite retention is banned; an FTC commissioner's concurrence states "claims from businesses that data must be indefinitely retained to improve algorithms do not override legal bans on indefinite retention of data" ([Public Interest Privacy Center](https://publicinterestprivacy.org/coppa-rule-training-algorithms/), accessed 2026-07-06; [pixelOS parent guide](https://www.pixelos.app/blog/what-is-coppa-parents-guide), accessed 2026-07-06). Known gap: first-party internal AI training on children's data is not clearly covered by the separate-consent requirement ([Public Interest Privacy Center](https://publicinterestprivacy.org/coppa-rule-training-algorithms/), accessed 2026-07-06; [Promise Legal](https://blog.promise.legal/coppa-verified-parental-consent-methods/), accessed 2026-07-06).

FACT. I found no organic homeschool-community discussion naming COPPA or the AI-training consent requirement on this run (searches conducted 2026-07-06). Coverage lives in law-firm alerts, privacy nonprofits, and parent-explainer blogs. Parent worry expresses itself instead as generalized distrust: 84% concerned about data misuse ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/sites/default/files/research/report/commonsensemedia_generationai.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06), while vendors pre-answer training questions in FAQs ([AdaptiPath FAQ](https://www.adaptipath.com/faq), accessed 2026-07-06; [Homeschool Moment](https://homeschoolmoment.com/), accessed 2026-07-06).

INFERENCE. "COPPA" is an elite and vendor concern; "is my kid's data training your robot" is a mass emotion without statute citations. Marketing should answer the emotion in plain words (never trained on, never sold, delete anytime) rather than lead with regulatory vocabulary. Separately: a parent-inputs-only architecture (the mother describes; the child never uses the product) changes the COPPA surface materially, since the data is parent-provided rather than collected from a child online. That is a question for counsel, not a marketing claim to improvise.

## Verdicts on tested assumptions

- A8 ("less than ~30% of faith-based-curriculum moms reject AI outright once framed as parent stays the teacher; it makes materials"): MIXED, leaning supports. No survey measures this population at this threshold, so there is no direct evidence either way. Indirect support: 66% of practicing Christians say AI is improving their lives ([Barna](https://www.barna.com/research/christians-view-ai-gift-threat/), accessed 2026-07-06); only 13% of pastors avoid AI entirely ([Gloo](https://gloo.com/press/releases/majority-of-pastors-use-ai-in-ministry-amid-concerns-and-caution-new-research-finds), accessed 2026-07-06); every major gatekeeper endorses guarded parent-side use (section 3); a Christian publisher shipped an AI assistant to its list without documented revolt ([Gather 'Round](https://gatherroundhomeschool.kit.com/posts/july-8-2025), accessed 2026-07-06). Indirect contra: national support for even teacher-side AI lesson planning fell to 49% ([EdWeek/PDK](https://www.edweek.org/technology/americans-grow-more-skeptical-of-ai-in-k-12-schools-poll-finds/2025/08), accessed 2026-07-06), and a vocal traditionalist wing rejects AI-written materials on principle ([Campfire Curriculums](https://campfirecurriculums.com/ai-part-1/), accessed 2026-07-06). Recommended: settle it with a short survey of faith-based-curriculum buyers before scaling spend against this assumption.
- A9 ("moms will hand a child's developmental data to an AI company; the privacy objection is manageable at signup"): MIXED. Stated attitudes are strongly contra: 68% of parents oppose AI access to student data ([EdWeek/PDK](https://www.edweek.org/technology/americans-grow-more-skeptical-of-ai-in-k-12-schools-poll-finds/2025/08), accessed 2026-07-06); 64% distrust AI companies on child safety ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/sites/default/files/research/report/commonsensemedia_generationai.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06). Revealed behavior supports: parents paste ADHD learning profiles into custom GPTs ([Chrisanne's Life](https://chrisanneslife.com/blog/ai-tutors-homeschool-kids/), accessed 2026-07-06) and upload photos of their kids' schoolwork to a GPT-4o captioning app ([Homeschool Moment](https://homeschoolmoment.com/compare), accessed 2026-07-06). The objection is manageable at signup only when the product makes plain-language promises up front (no training, no selling, no facial recognition, delete anytime), which is exactly what incumbent tools already do defensively ([Homeschool Moment](https://homeschoolmoment.com/), accessed 2026-07-06; [AdaptiPath FAQ](https://www.adaptipath.com/faq), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Path-C claim ("the ChatGPT amnesia tax is real and articulated by working homeschool moms"): SUPPORTS. Direct articulation by a homeschooling parent ("Every single time. In every conversation. Which is exhausting," [Chrisanne's Life](https://chrisanneslife.com/blog/ai-tutors-homeschool-kids/), accessed 2026-07-06) and by an educator ("Back to square one. Re-explain everything," [Why Edify](https://www.jeremyajorgensen.com/stop-starting-over-how-to-build-a-permanent-ai-teaching-assistant-with-chatgpt/), accessed 2026-07-06); a documented copy-paste workaround culture ([Mercer Homeschooling](https://www.mercerhomeschooling.com/homeschool-with-chatgpt/), accessed 2026-07-06; [AI Adopters Club](https://aiadopters.club/p/how-to-use-chatgpt-for-homeschool), accessed 2026-07-06); a context-cost refusal from a veteran forum member ("In the time I have told Chat GPT what it should put in there, I have written the paragraph myself," [Well-Trained Mind forum](https://forums.welltrainedmind.com/topic/728258-using-chat-gpt-to-help-write-course-descriptions/), accessed 2026-07-06); and OpenAI marketing Projects against the same pain ([OpenAI Academy](https://academy.openai.com/en/public/clubs/k-12-education-aacga/blogs/organize-ongoing-class-work-with-projects-2026-05-21), accessed 2026-07-06). Caveat: sophisticated users partially solve it with Custom GPTs and Projects, so the durable wedge is multi-child, records-grade memory with zero setup, not memory alone.

## Implications for Homa positioning (all INFERENCE)

- Lead with the mother's authority, not the technology. The community's own accepted sentence is "AI is your assistant, but you're still the teacher" ([Educating at Home](https://www.educatingathome.com/post/homeschooling-with-ai-yay-or-nay), accessed 2026-07-06). "The parent talks, Homa types" sits inside an argument the community has already settled in Homa's favor.
- Make the privacy promise on the first screen, in mom words, not COPPA words. The surviving vendors in this niche already do this defensively; matching it is table stakes, beating it (deletion controls, no-training pledge, parent-only inputs) is differentiation.
- Never ship visible slop. The contempt reflex attaches to products permanently and is amplified by curriculum-founder influencers who are on record against AI content.
- Treat the compliance lane as the highest-trust, highest-stakes surface. Pair any legal-requirement output with sources and a review step, because the community already circulates a cautionary tale about AI-invented course descriptions ([Campfire Curriculums](https://campfirecurriculums.com/ai-part-1/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- "More paper and outings for the kid" is not only religious-friendly; it is the documented Waldorf/crunchy accepted pattern (section 4), so one product story works across both segments.
