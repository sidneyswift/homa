- **Key findings:**
  - The high-burden tier is small but sharp: MA, NY, PA, RI (plus VT on the boundary) total roughly 117,000 state-registered homeschool students, about 3-4 percent of the national homeschool population, but NY and PA alone carry the country's heaviest recurring filing cadences.
  - The bigger paperwork market is the assessment-moderate belt plus ESA compliance: FL, NC, VA, MD, MN, WA and peers require an annual artifact from every family, and ESA programs (FL PEP alone: 76,274 funded students) add receipts, learning plans, and testing on top, voluntarily accepted for money.

  - The ESA wave is real and homeschool-accessible in AZ, FL, AR, WV, UT, NH, AL, TX (small), GA (narrow), WY, plus tax credits in ID and OK. ClassWallet, Odyssey, Step Up (EMA/MyScholarShop), and Student First (EMA) are the four payment rails; vendor onboarding is cheap (weeks, not quarters) once a state approves the category.
  - Regulation is trending in both directions at once: Connecticut passed the first new homeschool oversight law in decades (May 2026), Illinois' registration bill died but is expected back, while New Hampshire is moving to deregulate; ESA programs face fraud headlines, audits, and live constitutional litigation (UT, WY) that can freeze family funds mid-year.

## 1. Burden tiers: how the 50 states + DC sort

FACT. HSLDA's four-tier scheme is the industry-standard classification: no notice, low (notification only), moderate (notification plus test scores and/or professional evaluation), high (all of that plus extras like curriculum approval, teacher qualifications, or home visits) ([HSLDA legal map](https://hslda.org/legal), accessed 2026-07-06). A 2026 tracker applying that scheme with per-state primary sources counts 9 no-notice states, 26 low, 11 moderate, and a high tier of MA, NY, PA, RI, with VT on the high/moderate boundary after its 2023 simplification ([Every Homeschool 50-state guide, retrieved April 2026](https://everyhomeschool.com/guides/state-laws-2026), accessed 2026-07-06).

- **No notice (9):** AK, CT, ID, IL, MI, MO, NJ, OK, TX ([Every Homeschool](https://everyhomeschool.com/guides/state-laws-2026), accessed 2026-07-06). Connecticut leaves this tier in stages starting 2027-28 under its new law (section 3).
- **High (4-5):** MA, NY, PA, RI, with VT the boundary case ([Every Homeschool](https://everyhomeschool.com/guides/state-laws-2026), accessed 2026-07-06; [U.S. Career Institute summary listing all five](https://www.uscareerinstitute.edu/blog/homeschool-requirements-by-state), accessed 2026-07-06).
- The remainder are low or moderate; the full 51-row assignment is in the master table (section 5).

INFERENCE, flagged. Tier labels understate paperwork in several "low" states. Florida requires an annual educational evaluation filed with the district, North Carolina requires an annual nationally normed test kept on file, Washington requires an annual test or assessment plus a parent qualification rule, and New Hampshire requires an annual evaluation retained at home. For Homa's purposes (who touches paper every year), those states behave like moderate states regardless of label. The master table carries a "recurring artifact" column so the pain analysis does not depend on tier labels.

Confidence: High on the tier scheme and high-tier membership; Medium on individual contested rows (FL, NC, WA, NH, CO), which is why requirements are sourced per state.

## 2. The high tier, itemized (and the two persona anchors verified)

Confidence: High. All statements below are FACT sourced to statute, regulation, or the state education agency.

**New York (high; persona P4's state).** Under 8 NYCRR 100.10 ([regulation text](https://nyrules.elaws.us/nycrr/title8_chapterii_subchaptere_part100_s100.10), accessed 2026-07-06; [NYC DOE home schooling office](https://www.schools.nyc.gov/enrollment/enrollment-help/home-schooling), accessed 2026-07-06), the annual cycle per child is:

- Letter of intent to the district each year.
- An Individualized Home Instruction Plan (IHIP) within roughly four weeks, per child, per subject, with materials or a plan of instruction and the names of instructors.
- Four quarterly reports on IHIP-specified dates (NYC's calendar: Nov 15, Jan 31, Apr 15, Jun 30), each with hours of instruction, material covered, and a grade or narrative per subject.
- An annual assessment filed with the fourth quarterly report: a listed norm-referenced test at least every other year in grades 4-8 and every year in grades 9-12, narratives allowed otherwise ([100.10(h)](https://nyrules.elaws.us/nycrr/title8_chapterii_subchaptere_part100_s100.10), accessed 2026-07-06).
- A score below the 33rd percentile without a year's growth places the program on probation with a required remediation plan ([Homeschool Start Guide explainer on the 33rd percentile rule](https://homeschoolstartguide.com/blog/new-york-homeschool-annual-assessment), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Net: up to 6+ filings per child per year, the heaviest recurring cadence in the country.

**Pennsylvania (high).** 24 P.S. 13-1327.1 ([statute text](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/WU01/LI/LI/US/HTM/1949/0/0014.013.000.027.001..HTM?68=), accessed 2026-07-06) requires:

- A notarized affidavit (or unsworn declaration) to the superintendent by August 1 each year ([PA Home Education and Private Tutoring Guide](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/education/documents/instruction/home-education-and-private-tutoring/home%20education%20and%20private%20tutoring%20guide.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).
- A portfolio containing a log "made contemporaneously with the instruction" that lists reading materials by title, plus samples of writings, worksheets, and creative work ([statute](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/WU01/LI/LI/US/HTM/1949/0/0014.013.000.027.001..HTM?68=), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Nationally normed test results in grades 3, 5, and 8 (PDE-approved list or the state PSSA; the parent may not administer) ([PDE Home Education Program BEC with the approved test list](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/resources/policies-acts-and-laws/basic-education-circulars-becs/purdons-statutes/home-education-program), accessed 2026-07-06).
- An annual written evaluation by a qualified third party (licensed psychologist, PA-certified teacher with 2 years grading experience, or nonpublic teacher/administrator; never the parent or spouse), based on portfolio review and a child interview, with the certification submitted to the superintendent by June 30; failure risks a district hearing and loss of the homeschool option for up to a year ([HSLDA PA compliance guide](https://hslda.org/post/how-to-comply-with-pennsylvanias-homeschool-law), accessed 2026-07-06; [PDE BEC](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/resources/policies-acts-and-laws/basic-education-circulars-becs/purdons-statutes/home-education-program), accessed 2026-07-06).

**Massachusetts (high).** The only prior-approval state: the district must approve the home education plan in advance under G.L. c. 76 § 1 and Care and Protection of Charles (1987); districts review content, materials, hours, and assessment method, and may require periodic progress reports, though they cannot require home visits (Brunelle v. Lynn, 1998) ([Mass. DESE home schooling page](https://www.doe.mass.edu/homeschool/), accessed 2026-07-06; [Blue Folder MA summary of Charles/Brunelle limits](https://bluefolder.app/states/massachusetts), accessed 2026-07-06).

**Rhode Island (high).** The local school committee must approve each program as "substantially equal" under R.I. Gen. Laws 16-19-2; committees set their own documentation and assessment expectations, and an attendance register is required, producing district-by-district variation ([Every Homeschool RI entry](https://everyhomeschool.com/guides/state-laws-2026), accessed 2026-07-06; [U.S. Career Institute note on the attendance register](https://www.uscareerinstitute.edu/blog/homeschool-requirements-by-state), accessed 2026-07-06; [HSLDA Rhode Island page](https://hslda.org/legal/rhode-island), accessed 2026-07-06).

**Vermont (boundary).** Annual enrollment notice to the Agency of Education with a minimum course of study; an annual assessment (test, certified-teacher evaluation, or portfolio) must still be completed each year but since H.461 (June 2023) is retained at home rather than submitted ([Every Homeschool VT entry and H.461 change](https://everyhomeschool.com/guides/state-laws-2026), accessed 2026-07-06; [Homeschool Start Guide VT overview](https://homeschoolstartguide.com/blog/vermont-homeschool-laws), accessed 2026-07-06).

**The "old high list" states the prompt asked about (WA, MN, ND) are now moderate.** Washington: annual declaration of intent by Sept 15, a parent qualification rule (45 college quarter credits, a qualifying course, certificated-teacher supervision, or superintendent approval), and an annual standardized test or written assessment by a certificated person, retained in the child's records, with a "remedy any deficiency" duty ([RCW 28A.200.010](https://apps.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=28A.200.010), accessed 2026-07-06; [OSPI "pink book"](https://ospi.k12.wa.us/sites/default/files/2023-08/pinkbook.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06). Minnesota: annual report to the superintendent plus annual nationally normed testing ([Every Homeschool MN row](https://everyhomeschool.com/guides/state-laws-2026), accessed 2026-07-06; [HSLDA Minnesota page](https://hslda.org/legal/minnesota), accessed 2026-07-06). North Dakota: annual notice plus testing in grades 4, 6, 8, 10 ([Every Homeschool ND row](https://everyhomeschool.com/guides/state-laws-2026), accessed 2026-07-06; [HSLDA North Dakota page](https://hslda.org/legal/north-dakota), accessed 2026-07-06).

**Missouri verified (persona P2's state).** FACT, all from statute:

- No notice, registration, testing, or approval is required to homeschool in Missouri; a declaration of enrollment is optional ([RSMo 167.042 via HSLDA Missouri guide](https://hslda.org/post/how-to-comply-with-missouris-homeschool-law), accessed 2026-07-06).
- A home school must offer at least 1,000 hours of instruction per school term, at least 600 in reading, language arts, mathematics, social studies, and science (or related academic courses), with at least 400 of those 600 at "the regular home school location" ([RSMo 167.012](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?bid=57174&section=167.012), accessed 2026-07-06; [RSMo 167.031](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=167.031&bid=50227), accessed 2026-07-06).
- The parent must maintain a plan book, diary, or other written record of subjects taught and activities, plus a portfolio of the child's work and a record of evaluations, or equivalent credible evidence ([RSMo 167.012](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?bid=57174&section=167.012), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Production of a daily log showing a compliant course of instruction is an explicit statutory defense to prosecution and to educational-neglect charges, and enforcement is reviewable only by the local prosecuting attorney ([RSMo 167.012(4)](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?bid=57174&section=167.012), accessed 2026-07-06). HSLDA's practical guidance is to keep a daily hours log for exactly this reason ([HSLDA Missouri guide](https://hslda.org/post/how-to-comply-with-missouris-homeschool-law), accessed 2026-07-06).
- 2024's SB 727 reorganized the law into 167.012 and loosened "and" to "or" across core subjects ([Ellinia MO summary](https://elliniahomeschool.com/resources/mo), accessed 2026-07-06).

INFERENCE: Missouri is the only state whose entire legal exposure reduces to one artifact, the hours log. Nothing is ever filed, so the pain is not deadlines; it is the year-round bookkeeping and the quiet fear of an audit-by-prosecutor. That is precisely Homa's ambient-hours wedge.

## 3. Recent and pending changes, 2024-2026

Confidence: High on enacted items, Medium on trend reads.

- **Illinois (tightening attempt, failed).** HB 2827, the "Homeschool Act" (introduced Feb 2025), would have required an annual Homeschool Declaration Form with truancy penalties for non-filers; it drew a record 40,000+ opposition witness slips, stalled on the House floor in April 2025, and died when the session adjourned June 1, 2025; it was not revived in the fall veto session and had not been reintroduced as of mid-2026, though HSLDA expects another attempt ([bill text and synopsis](https://ilga.gov/ftp/legislation/104/HB/10400HB2827.htm), accessed 2026-07-06; [HSLDA HB 2827 tracker](https://hslda.org/post/illinois-house-bill-2827), accessed 2026-07-06; [HSLDA "dead in Illinois" post](https://hslda.org/post/bad-bill-officially-dead-in-illinois), accessed 2026-07-06).
- **Connecticut (tightening, enacted).** HB 5468 was signed May 26, 2026 as Public Act 26-37, the first homeschool oversight law in state history: in-person withdrawal signing (2027-28), a DCF records check on all household adults before withdrawal with withdrawal blocked if anyone is on the abuse registry or under active investigation (July 2027), and an annual intent-to-educate form by Oct 1 for all families (2028-29), with district follow-up duties for non-filers ([AP coverage of passage](https://apnews.com/article/connecticut-first-homeschool-requirements-republican-opposition-e944a7ede9be00ed28ac36a90591c1fe), accessed 2026-07-06; [Pullman & Comley 2026 session summary of PA 26-37 effective dates](https://www.pullcom.com/education-law-notes/legislature-has-spoken-highlights-2026-session-connecticut-general-assembly-connecticut-schools), accessed 2026-07-06; [CRHE announcement](https://crhe.org/connecticut-victory/), accessed 2026-07-06; [HSLDA opposition page confirming signing](https://hslda.org/post/hb-5468), accessed 2026-07-06).
- **Ohio (loosening, enacted 2023, now settled law).** HB 33 replaced the old excusal regime with ORC 3321.042: a three-element annual notice by Aug 30, exemption effective on receipt, and no assessments, hour minimums, curriculum submission, or parent qualifications ([ORC 3321.042](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3321.042), accessed 2026-07-06; [Homeschool Start Guide HB 33 explainer](https://homeschoolstartguide.com/blog/new-ohio-homeschool-law-hb-33), accessed 2026-07-06).
- **Vermont (loosening, 2023).** H.461 ended submission of end-of-year assessments to the Agency of Education; assessment still required, retained at home ([Every Homeschool change log](https://everyhomeschool.com/guides/state-laws-2026), accessed 2026-07-06).
- **New Hampshire (loosening, pending).** The House-passed "Home Education Freedom Act" (HB 1268) would eliminate notification, record-keeping, and annual evaluation requirements entirely and got a warm Senate hearing in April 2026; a stated motivation is insulating non-EFA homeschoolers from future ESA-driven regulation ([NHPR, April 21, 2026](https://www.nhpr.org/nh-news/2026-04-21/nh-newhampshire-homeschool-homeschooling-regulations), accessed 2026-07-06).
- **The oversight push has organized infrastructure.** The Washington Post's 2023 series documented homeschooling as the fastest-growing form of education and spotlighted abuse cases in no-notice states, fueling regulation campaigns ([WaPo data analysis](https://www.washingtonpost.com/education/interactive/2023/homeschooling-growth-data-by-district/), accessed 2026-07-06; [WaPo Michigan abuse investigation](https://www.washingtonpost.com/education/interactive/2023/homeschooling-child-abuse-torture-roman-lopez/), accessed 2026-07-06). The Coalition for Responsible Home Education's model "Make Homeschool Safe Act" (annual notification, annual evaluation, parent diploma requirement, abuse-history bars) is the template circulating in statehouses; HSLDA opposes it; Illinois, New Jersey, and Virginia have all seen tightening bills in recent years ([Independent Women overview, April 2026](https://www.independentwomen.com/2026/04/21/homeschoolers-grapple-with-varying-notification-laws/), accessed 2026-07-06; [AP/Stateline via Yakima Herald on the post-abuse-case legislative wave and the CT child advocate data](https://www.yakimaherald.com/news/nation_and_world/these-kids-are-invisible-child-abuse-deaths-spur-clash-over-homeschool-regulation/article_32c55ca6-4bc5-4e48-91e0-1cded23e4d78.html), accessed 2026-07-06).
- **Kentucky (freedom-side win).** Constitutional Amendment 2, which would have allowed public funds for non-public education, was defeated statewide in Nov 2024 ([Every Homeschool change log](https://everyhomeschool.com/guides/state-laws-2026), accessed 2026-07-06).

INFERENCE: the trend is bifurcation, not a single direction. Blue-state legislatures are converging on notification + child-welfare checks (CT is the proof of concept; IL will retry), red states are deregulating homeschool law while attaching compliance strings to money instead (ESA testing/receipt regimes). Either direction increases demand for a tool that keeps records effortlessly current: new filers need hand-holding, ESA participants need receipts and artifacts.

## 7. ESA as a channel: how a software subscription gets paid with ESA money

FACT, per platform (all links this section accessed 2026-07-06):

- **ClassWallet (AZ, AR, AL, NH, SC, NC ESA+, ID microgrants).** Two rails: DirectPay service providers (invoice-based payments) and Marketplace e-commerce vendors (integrated catalog). The state program approves the provider first, then ClassWallet onboarding is banking paperwork (W-9, EIN, bank link) ([SC ESTF Service Provider Guide](https://classwallet.com/programs/southcarolinaestf/assets/SC-ESTF-Service-Provider-Guide.pdf); [Alabama CHOOSE ESP guide](https://www.classwallet.com/alchoose/assets/CHOOSE_ACT_education_service_provider_guide_2026-2027.pdf); [DirectPay enrollment steps](https://classwallet.my.site.com/classwallet/s/article/Enroll-DirectPay-Vendor)). Idaho publishes concrete timing: decision within 14 days of application, ClassWallet registration email within 3 business days of approval ([Empowering Parents vendor page](https://empoweringparents.idaho.gov/vendors)). Alabama's ESP application is open year-round ([ESP guide](https://www.classwallet.com/alchoose/assets/CHOOSE_ACT_education_service_provider_guide_2026-2027.pdf)).
- **Odyssey (TX, UT, IA, LA, GA, WY).** Separate application per state program (about 10 minutes each), business docs (W-9, EIN, formation), review in 2-4 weeks, then a per-state vendor portal; offerings are reviewed for program compliance before listing ([vendor experience](https://support.withodyssey.com/hc/en-us/articles/38770320084635-The-Vendor-Experience); [application instructions](https://support.withodyssey.com/hc/en-us/articles/24272969415835-How-Can-I-Apply-to-Be-A-Vendor)). Texas additionally requires registration with the Texas Secretary of State and franchise-tax good standing, with the Comptroller co-reviewing all offerings ([TEFA vendor requirements](https://support.withodyssey.com/hc/en-us/articles/44035042254875-Who-Can-Become-a-Vendor-and-What-are-the-Requirements); [TEFA providers page, rolling applications](https://educationfreedom.texas.gov/providers-vendors/)).
- **Step Up For Students (FL).** Product vendors join MyScholarShop via a supplier interest form, vetting, onboarding call, then SAP Ariba catalog integration; service providers create EMA business accounts; direct-pay invoices typically pay out in 1-2 weeks; all items are pre-reviewed against approved ESA categories ([vendor partnerships page](https://www.stepupforstudents.org/schools-and-providers/vendors/); [provider onboarding](https://www.stepupforstudents.org/schools-and-providers/become-a-service-partner-empowerment/); [GetESAPaid FL vendor guide](https://getesapaid.com/florida/become-an-esa-vendor)).
- **Student First Technologies (WV Hope).** Providers register on the EMA platform after Hope board approval; families submit purchase requests and receipts through the parent dashboard ([EMA parent manual](https://hopescholarshipwv.com/Portals/HopeScholarship/content/Documents/ParentGuardian%20EMA%20User%20Manual%20-%20WVA%20Hope%20Scholarship.pdf?ver=ubTZS4moUQ2e4TlMXVYqmg%3D%3D); [ArgoPrep WV guide](https://cwargoprep.com/blogs/esa/west-virginia-hope-scholarship-curriculum)).
- **Merit (OH ACE).** Reference only: Ohio's Merit-run ACE ESA closed permanently Oct 15, 2025 ([aceohio.org](https://www.aceohio.org/)).

**Precedents for software subscriptions.** Online curriculum SaaS is an established approved category: Miacademy is a Step Up provider across FTC/FES/PEP ([Miacademy Step Up page](https://miacademy.co/homeschool-curriculum/state-funding-for-miacademy/step-up-for-students/)); Compass Classroom is an approved ClassWallet vendor in the AZ ESA ([Compass Classroom](https://compassclassroom.com/blog/how-to-use-arizona-esa-for-homeschooling/)); Arizona's handbook and guides list "educational software and subscriptions" among approved homeschool uses ([Starpath AZ guide](https://www.starpathlearning.com/library/arizona-esa-homeschool-guide)). The HomeStudyPath claim checks out only partially: its site advertises "ClassWallet-ready quarterly scaffolding," which on inspection is report formatting matched to AZ's funding cadence, not approved-vendor status ([homestudypath.com](https://homestudypath.com/)); treat "ClassWallet-ready" claims by tools as marketing, not channel proof.

**What it takes for a family to pay for Homa with ESA money.**

1. Homa registers per program: entity docs (W-9, EIN, formation), the state or program application, then platform onboarding (bank link, portal account). Timelines above suggest 2-6 weeks per program end to end, applications open year-round.
2. Homa's offering passes category review, most plausibly as "educational software / online learning program / curriculum-adjacent subscription" ([AZ approved-use categories include educational software and subscriptions](https://www.starpathlearning.com/library/arizona-esa-homeschool-guide), accessed 2026-07-06).
3. The family pays via DirectPay or the marketplace, or fronts cash and seeks reimbursement where the program allows it (AZ supports reimbursements, with auditor scrutiny rising, [AZPM](https://news.azpm.org/p/news-articles/2026/5/14/229766-state-auditors-flag-purchase-review-problems-in-arizonas-school-voucher-program/), accessed 2026-07-06).

INFERENCE: the open risk is category fit. Homa is an assistant and record-keeper, not a curriculum; approval lists are built around instructional content. Positioning the sellable SKU as "planning + curriculum materials + assessment reports" (an instructional product with compliance features, not a compliance product) is likely necessary. One or two concierge reimbursement attempts with real AZ/FL families would settle adjudication risk for a few hundred dollars. At $29-49/mo ($348-588/yr), Homa is 5-8 percent of an AZ award and about 17-29 percent of Alabama's $2,000 homeschool credit: affordable inside every program's envelope.

Confidence: High on process mechanics; Medium on category-fit judgment (INFERENCE, untested for an assistant-type product).

## 8. Second-order effects: ESA money is manufacturing Homa's customer

FACT, the published data points:

- Arizona's universal expansion was initially dominated by families already outside public school: 71.2 percent of 2022-23 universal enrollees had not attended public school ([Learning Policy Institute](https://doi.org/10.54300/682.951), accessed 2026-07-06); the Grand Canyon Institute estimates ~75-80 percent cumulatively never attended district or charter school ([GCI, Feb 2026](https://grandcanyoninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/GCI_Policy_Cost-of-the-Universal-ESA-Program_Feb_9_2026.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).
- The mix is now shifting toward converts: 57 percent of new universal-eligibility enrollees in grades 1-12 in FY2025 came directly from public school, up from 21 percent in FY2023 ([ADE FY25 Q3 report](https://www.azed.gov/sites/default/files/2025/06/ESA%20FY25%20Q3%20Executive%20%26%20Legislative%20Report%20Final%20submitted%205.30.25.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06). These are families with zero homeschool experience arriving with money and reporting duties.
- Florida's PEP went from zero (created 2023) to 59,925 students in 2024-25 and 76,274 funded in 2025-26; 2024-25 PEP spending was $270M, dominated by curriculum and instructional materials ($223M) ([Step Up PEP fast facts](https://staging.stepupforstudents.org/wp-content/uploads/26.1-PEP.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06; [ESA Tracker FL profile](https://www.esatrackerbystate.com/states/florida), accessed 2026-07-06).
- When Florida's universal voucher expansion opened in 2023, only 13 percent of 122,895 new scholarship students came from public schools ([NCPE analysis](https://www.ncpecoalition.org/voucher-recipients), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Homeschooling itself keeps growing: +4.9 percent on average across reporting states in 2024-25, with 10 states at all-time highs ([JHU Homeschool Hub](https://education.jhu.edu/edpolicy/policy-research-initiatives/homeschool-hub/homeschool-growth-2024-2025/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Counting is fragmenting as money reclassifies students: New Hampshire's homeschool count fell as EFA students left the homeschool ledger ([JHU 2023-24 growth note](https://education.jhu.edu/edpolicy/policy-research-initiatives/homeschool-hub/homeschool-growth-2023-2024/), accessed 2026-07-06), and West Virginia's count dropped from 24,502 to 19,621 in one year as Hope scaled ([WVDE snapshot](https://wvde.us/media/8742/education-snapshot-booklet-2024-2025), accessed 2026-07-06).

INFERENCE (Confidence: Medium). Three consequences for the ICP: (1) a fast-growing "funded home learner" segment now exists that accepted testing, learning plans, and receipts as the price of $2,000-11,950 per child per year: these families are compliance-native by construction, less ideologically anti-oversight than legacy homeschoolers, and newer to the craft (public-school switchers are the fastest-growing AZ inflow). (2) Their tool expectations shift from "lesson planner" toward "spend tracker + artifact generator": SLPs, portfolios, receipts, test logistics. Homa's ambient logging and report generation map directly onto that. (3) State homeschool registries increasingly undercount the true home-learning market, so Homa's TAM math should add ESA home-learner counts (AZ 100k+, FL PEP 76k, WV ~20k, UT ~15k, NH ~10k, AR ~14k) to registered homeschool populations rather than treating them as included.

## 9. Risk scan: what could shift mid-launch

Confidence: High on events, Medium on impact reads. All FACT unless noted.

- **ESA fraud headlines are recurring and escalating.** Arizona: a $600k+ ghost-student ring run partly by former ESA staff (guilty pleas 2024-2025, one acquittal), and a Colorado couple who invented children to collect $110k (guilty pleas Aug 2025) ([AZ Law Now case summary](https://azlawnow.com/investigations/esa-fake-children-600k-grand-jury-indictment/), accessed 2026-07-06; [12News](https://www.12news.com/article/news/investigations/i-team/education-impact/couple-created-ghost-children-defraud-arizona-empowerment-scholarship-program-plead-guilty-felony-charges/75-b18d934e-9b1c-4f02-a905-8916764b70e2), accessed 2026-07-06; [AG indictment PDF](https://www.azag.gov/sites/default/files/2025-06/95%20SGJ%2076%20-%20TRUE%20BILL%20-%20JOHNNY%20BOWERS%20ET%20AL.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06). The Auditor General found ADE ignored the 2024 risk-based-audit law for months and, in May 2026, flagged auto-approval of sub-$2,000 transactions and $102M of under-scrutinized debit/reimbursement spend ([AZ Mirror](https://azmirror.com/2025/07/29/arizona-school-voucher-program-ignored-state-audit-law-for-nearly-a-year-officials-say/), accessed 2026-07-06; [AZPM](https://news.azpm.org/p/news-articles/2026/5/14/229766-state-auditors-flag-purchase-review-problems-in-arizonas-school-voucher-program/), accessed 2026-07-06). INFERENCE: expect tighter category policing (bad for marginal expense types, good for clearly educational subscriptions) and periodic "voucher waste" news cycles that Homa's marketing should never be adjacent to.
- **Live constitutional litigation can freeze money mid-year.** Utah's program was ruled unconstitutional at district court and awaits the Utah Supreme Court ([timeline](https://www.utaheducationfitsall.org/be-informed/), accessed 2026-07-06); Wyoming families had ESA funds blocked for most of 2025-26 before the injunction was lifted in May 2026 ([WDE](https://edu.wyoming.gov/parents/education-savings-accounts/), accessed 2026-07-06). INFERENCE: never build revenue plans on a single program's continuity.
- **Oversight campaigns are winning their first states.** Connecticut's PA 26-37 (section 3) is the template win for the CRHE side; the model "Make Homeschool Safe Act" is circulating; Illinois-style registration fights will recur, amplified by investigative journalism (Capitol News Illinois/ProPublica prompted the IL bill) ([Yakima Herald/Stateline](https://www.yakimaherald.com/news/nation_and_world/these-kids-are-invisible-child-abuse-deaths-spur-clash-over-homeschool-regulation/article_32c55ca6-4bc5-4e48-91e0-1cded23e4d78.html), accessed 2026-07-06). INFERENCE: ESA-channel revenue is real but rationed; treat it as a distribution assist, not the business model.

## 10. Verdicts (cited)

**Persona placement check: PASS on both, with one enrichment.**

- Missouri is the right home for the "hours-log pain, median mom" archetype (P2). The ~1,000-hour claim is exactly right in statute (1,000 total, 600 core, 400 at home) and the daily log is literally the family's statutory legal defense ([RSMo 167.012](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?bid=57174&section=167.012), accessed 2026-07-06; [RSMo 167.031](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=167.031&bid=50227), accessed 2026-07-06). No other state ties a numeric annual hours quota to a log-as-defense mechanic. The nearest alternative shape is Pennsylvania's 180-day/900-990-hour requirement inside its portfolio system ([PA guide](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/education/documents/instruction/home-education-and-private-tutoring/home%20education%20and%20private%20tutoring%20guide.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06), which already belongs to P4's world. Keep P2 in Missouri.
- New York is the right home for the "compliance-extreme veteran" (P4). IHIP + 4 quarterlies + annual assessment with a percentile floor is the heaviest recurring cadence in the country ([NYC DOE](https://www.schools.nyc.gov/enrollment/enrollment-help/home-schooling), accessed 2026-07-06; [8 NYCRR 100.10](https://nyrules.elaws.us/nycrr/title8_chapterii_subchaptere_part100_s100.10), accessed 2026-07-06). Pennsylvania remains the correct stated alternate: heavier gatekeeping (notary, evaluator), fewer recurring filings. Keep P4 in New York with the PA fallback.
- Enrichment (INFERENCE): the cast lacks the third regulatory archetype the ESA wave is mass-producing, the "funded home learner" juggling SLPs, receipts, and program tests (FL PEP, AZ ESA, WV Hope). Worth a persona note even if no new persona is added.

**A13 context: what fraction of US homeschool families face genuinely burdensome annual paperwork?** INFERENCE from sourced counts (Confidence: Medium):

- High tier: NY 53,967 + PA 44,692 + MA 12,203 + RI 3,086 + VT ~3,450 is ~117k students, roughly 3-4 percent of the ~3.4M national homeschool population ([NHERI counts and national estimate](https://nheri.org/how-many-homeschool-students-are-there-in-the-united-states/), accessed 2026-07-06; [Empire Center NY count](https://www.empirecenter.org/publications/migrant-influx-helps-curb-new-yorks-k-12-enrollment-decline/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Adding moderate-tier states where every family must produce or submit an annual artifact (FL 152.9k, NC 165.2k, GA 89.5k, VA 62.8k, MD 42.2k, SC 39.8k, MN 31.2k, WA 30.4k, LA 26k, WV 19.6k, ME 11.8k, SD 11.5k, ND 5k, plus HI/OR/CO/TN Category I where counts are unreported) gives roughly 700-800k registered students under recurring artifact duties, ~20-25 percent of the national population (state counts per [NHERI](https://nheri.org/how-many-homeschool-students-are-there-in-the-united-states/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Layering voluntary ESA compliance regimes (AZ 100k+, FL PEP 76k, AR ~14k, UT ~15k, NH ~10k, WV Hope 20k+ applicants in the first six weeks of universal eligibility, [Every Homeschool ESA guide](https://everyhomeschool.com/guides/esa-by-state-2026), accessed 2026-07-06) pushes the total to roughly **a quarter to a third of US homeschool families handling real education paperwork every year, and rising**, because the fastest-growing funding programs all carry strings.
- Caveat: the median homeschooler still lives in a no-notice or notice-only state. Compliance is a sharp wedge and a growing one, not the whole market.

**Channel verdict: yes, start ESA/ESP approval during launch prep, narrowly.**

- The cost is low: a few weeks of paperwork per program, rolling applications ([Odyssey 2-4 weeks](https://support.withodyssey.com/hc/en-us/articles/38770320084635-The-Vendor-Experience), accessed 2026-07-06; [Idaho ClassWallet 14 days + 3 days](https://empoweringparents.idaho.gov/vendors), accessed 2026-07-06; [Step Up rolling](https://www.stepupforstudents.org/schools-and-providers/vendors/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Recommended order: (1) **Arizona** (largest funded homeschool-style base, ClassWallet, educational-software category precedent, year-round applications), (2) **Florida PEP via Step Up** (76k homeschool-ESA families whose mandated SLP and testing artifacts are literally Homa's output), (3) **West Virginia Hope** (universal for 2026-27, homeschool-native IIP pathway, smaller but perfectly shaped).
- Defer: Texas (homeschool tier only $2,000 and lottery-bound), Louisiana (forces exit from home-study status), Alabama (income-capped until universal 2027-28; revisit then).
- Precondition (INFERENCE): run 1-2 concierge reimbursement tests with real AZ/FL families first to settle whether an assistant-plus-materials subscription clears category review, before spending brand equity on "ESA-eligible" claims. HomeStudyPath's "ClassWallet-ready" phrasing shows how easily tools overclaim here ([homestudypath.com](https://homestudypath.com/), accessed 2026-07-06).

Confidence: High on placement verdicts (statute-verified), Medium on the A13 fraction (count-based estimate with acknowledged undercounts), Medium-High on the channel verdict (process facts firm, category fit untested).
