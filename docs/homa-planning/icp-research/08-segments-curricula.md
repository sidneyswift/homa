**Key findings**

- The religious share is shrinking as a fraction while staying large in absolute terms: "religious instruction" as a motivation fell from 64% (2012, NHES) to 51-53% (2016/2023, NHES), and a 2023 Washington Post panel poll of the newer population found 34%.

The planning table's landmines mostly survive contact with evidence, with two corrections (Charlotte Mason terminology, religious-segment AI framing).
- Beachhead verdict: the secular-leaning eclectic segment (with school-at-home newcomers as the perennial inflow channel). It is the largest slice, it already assembles per-child stacks, it adopts AI fastest, it is structurally underserved by the Christian convention distribution channel, and its three chronic pains map one-to-one onto Homa's shape.

## 1. Market baseline: how big is the pie

FACT. The federal baseline: in school year 2022-23, 3.4% of US students ages 5-17 were homeschooled and 5.2% received instruction at home including full-time virtual, per the [NCES Parent and Family Involvement survey, 2023](https://nces.ed.gov/pubs2024/2024113_Summary.pdf) (accessed 2026-07-06). Against roughly 54 million school-age children that implies about 1.8-1.9 million homeschooled students.

FACT. The advocacy-side estimate: NHERI estimates [3.408 million K-12 homeschool students in 2024-25 (6.26% of school-age population), range 3.07-3.75 million](https://nheri.org/how-many-homeschool-students-are-there-in-the-united-states/) (accessed 2026-07-06). NHERI is homeschool-advocacy adjacent and its estimates run consistently higher than NCES; treat as the ceiling.

FACT. Growth is real and post-pandemic durable: homeschooling grew about 4.9-5.4% in 2024-25, with 80% of reporting states up and 36% at all-time-high enrollment, per the [Johns Hopkins Homeschool Hub](https://education.jhu.edu/edpolicy/policy-research-initiatives/homeschool-hub/homeschool-growth-2024-2025/) (accessed 2026-07-06). [EdChoice summarizes](https://www.edchoice.org/2026-how-homeschooling-became-the-fastest-growing-education-model/) the share roughly doubling from ~3% pre-pandemic to ~6%, citing the Washington Post's "fastest-growing form of education" analysis (accessed 2026-07-06).

INFERENCE. Working range for all sizing below: 1.9M (federal floor) to 3.4M (NHERI ceiling) students.

Confidence: High.

## 2. Segment sizing: what share follows each approach

### The honest state of the data

No federal survey asks "which named philosophy do you follow." The closest instruments are (a) one NHERI-published survey with philosophy labels, (b) the NHES teaching-style question (formal vs informal curriculum), and (c) SEO-grade community charts of unknown provenance. Everything here is labeled accordingly.

FACT (unscientific sample, the best direct measurement that exists). A survey of 1,171 homeschool parents published in NHERI's Home School Researcher found: [eclectic 68% (798), classical 14% (160), unschooling 13% (153), traditional boxed curriculum 5% (60)](https://nheri.org/home-school-researcher-parent-perspectives-curriculum-and-homeschooling-approaches/) (accessed 2026-07-06). Self-selected online sample, 2017 publication; directional only. One respondent verbatim from that study: "I know no one who strictly follows a boxed curriculum. The huge majority of homeschoolers in my experience would describe themselves as eclectic (another choice on your survey)." ([source](https://nheri.org/home-school-researcher-parent-perspectives-curriculum-and-homeschooling-approaches/), accessed 2026-07-06)

FACT (federal proxy). The NHES 2019 codebook's teaching-style item (HSSTYL) gives weighted shares of all students; recomputed among homeschoolers only: strictly formal curriculum ~27%, mostly formal plus informal ~55%, mostly informal ~13%, always informal (never formal curriculum) ~5%. Raw weighted percents 0.86 / 1.74 / 0.43 / 0.16 are in the [NHES:2019 PFI public-use codebook](https://nces.ed.gov/nhes/data/2019/pfi/cbook_pfi_pu.pdf) (accessed 2026-07-06); the within-homeschool shares are my computation (INFERENCE). The modal homeschooler blends formal and informal, which is what "eclectic" means in practice.

FACT (federal, 2016). Families use many curriculum sources at once: about two-thirds reported the public library as a source and roughly 6 in 10 reported bookstores, websites, and homeschool catalogs/publishers, simultaneously, per [NCES web tables, table 6](https://nces.ed.gov/pubs2020/2020001.pdf) (accessed 2026-07-06).

CAUTION (do not cite as data). SEO sites circulate other numbers, e.g. "21% classical according to 2026 NHERI survey updates" and "about 36% eclectic" at [homeschoolpicks.com](https://homeschoolpicks.com/homeschool-methods/homeschool-methods-comparison-chart/) (accessed 2026-07-06). No underlying NHERI publication matching those figures was findable; the published NHERI-hosted study says 68% eclectic and 14% classical. Treat the SEO figures as unverified.

### Religious vs secular split and trend

- FACT. NHES trend on "desire to provide religious instruction" as an important reason: [64% (2012) to 51% (2016)](https://nces.ed.gov/pubs2020/2020001.pdf) (accessed 2026-07-06), and [53% in 2023, with moral instruction at 75%](https://www.educationnext.org/what-state-policymakers-should-know-about-homeschoolers-misconceptions-who-chooses-home-education/) (accessed 2026-07-06).
- FACT. The 2023 Washington Post-Schar School poll (SSRS panel, 504 homeschool parents, MoE 6 pts) found only [34% cite religious instruction, vs 56% on the equivalent 2019 NHES item printed in the same topline](https://www.washingtonpost.com/documents/6fbcc249-940f-4a01-aee8-b48c82a5a242.pdf) (accessed 2026-07-06); the Post's headline finding: home schooling is now [less religious and more diverse](https://www.washingtonpost.com/education/2023/09/26/home-schooling-vs-public-school-poll/) (accessed 2026-07-06).
- FACT. Yet religion still saturates the population: in the same poll, [69% of homeschool parents say religion is very or somewhat important in their lives](https://www.washingtonpost.com/documents/6fbcc249-940f-4a01-aee8-b48c82a5a242.pdf) (accessed 2026-07-06). INFERENCE: many families are religious without homeschooling for religion, which is exactly the gap a worldview-neutral, parent-controlled product can serve.
- FACT. Secular infrastructure has scaled: SEA Homeschoolers (Secular, Eclectic, Academic) reports a community of [100,000+ families, four online conferences a year, and a vetted secular store](https://seahomeschoolers.com/) (accessed 2026-07-06). The "fastest growing education segment" framing for secular homeschooling comes from vendors like [Schoolio](https://schoolio.com/secular-homeschooling/) (accessed 2026-07-06) and is plausible but not independently measured: UNVERIFIABLE as a growth-rate claim.

### Verdict: eclectic-is-the-plurality

**CONFIRMED.** Three independent lines agree: the 68% self-ID poll ([NHERI-hosted study](https://nheri.org/home-school-researcher-parent-perspectives-curriculum-and-homeschooling-approaches/), accessed 2026-07-06, unscientific), the federal teaching-style distribution where blended is modal ([NHES 2019 codebook](https://nces.ed.gov/nhes/data/2019/pfi/cbook_pfi_pu.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06), and the scholarly consensus that "after a year or two of assiduous effort to mimic formal schooling at home, new homeschooling mothers gradually move toward a less-structured, more eclectic approach" ([Kunzman & Gaither](https://www.icher.org/files/Kunzman_and_Gaither_An%20Updated_Comprehensive_Survey.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06). No precise percentage should be quoted as fact; "plurality, probably majority" is defensible.

Confidence: Medium (directionally High, numerically Low).

## 3. Trend direction per segment, 2023-2026

- **Charlotte Mason / gentle / Wild + Free aesthetic: growing.** FACT: Wild + Free began as an Instagram account in 2014 and grew into a community described as [177,000+ members by ~2020](https://homeschoolingoutsidethebox.libsyn.com/reclaiming-a-wild-free-childhood-with-ainsley-arment) (accessed 2026-07-06), with conferences, a retreat property, and monthly paid bundles ([Homeschool Compass interview](https://homeschoolcompass.com/the-call-of-the-wild-free-an-interview-with-ainsley-arment/), accessed 2026-07-06). The Good and the Beautiful, a CM-influenced open-and-go curriculum, is called [one of the fastest-growing homeschool curricula in the US](https://homeschoolfox.com/curriculum/the-good-and-the-beautiful) (accessed 2026-07-06). INFERENCE: the gentle aesthetic is the strongest style current in the 2-8 age band Homa targets. Note the crossover: Christianbook's homeschool editorial arm courts Wild + Free directly ([interview host](https://homeschoolcompass.com/the-call-of-the-wild-free-an-interview-with-ainsley-arment/), accessed 2026-07-06), so gentle is not a secular-only signal.
- **Classical: growing, with charter spillover.** FACT: 1,551 classical schools enrolled ~677,500 students in 2023-24, 39% of them home-based (co-ops, online, microschool), projected to [1.4M students by 2035](https://www.discovery.org/education/2024/03/06/the-surging-growth-of-k-12-classical-education/) (accessed 2026-07-06); sector growth ~5%/yr ([AEI report on the Arcadia analysis](https://www.aei.org/research-products/report/the-achilles-heel-ofclassical-education/), accessed 2026-07-06). Classical Conversations self-reports [45,000+ families in 2,400+ communities across 50 states and ~50 countries](https://classicalconversations.com/community-search/) (accessed 2026-07-06) and ~[50,000 families per its CEO](https://www.davebondynews.com/p/classical-conversations-ceo-says) (accessed 2026-07-06). Hillsdale's classical curriculum now reaches [88 K-12 schools with 12,000+ students on waitlists](http://eu.detroitnews.com/story/news/local/michigan/2025/07/30/hillsdale-college-spreading-gospel-of-classical-education-to-k-12-schools/82747604007/) (accessed 2026-07-06). INFERENCE: classical charter growth normalizes classical vocabulary among parents who later homeschool.
- **Secular: composition shifting toward it.** FACT: the religious-motivation decline in §2 plus SEA's 100K+ scale. INFERENCE: secular/pluralist is the fastest-compounding identity within homeschooling even if no one has isolated its growth rate.
- **Microschool/hybrid overlap: material and rising.** FACT: an estimated [750,000 to 2.1 million students](https://hechingerreport.org/are-microschools-a-solution-to-falling-public-school-enrollment-one-district-thinks-so/) attend microschools (RAND estimate, accessed 2026-07-06); [53% of microschools operate as learning centers for families following state homeschool requirements](https://microschoolingcenter.org/hubfs/American%20Microschools%202025.pdf) (National Microschooling Center 2025 sector analysis, accessed 2026-07-06); most hybrid students are [homeschooled about half the week](https://hechingerreport.org/are-microschools-a-solution-to-falling-public-school-enrollment-one-district-thinks-so/) (accessed 2026-07-06). INFERENCE: "homeschooler" increasingly means "home 2-4 days a week," which raises the value of a tool that keeps the record coherent across settings.

Confidence: Medium-High.

## 5. Heggerty check (founder decision D10)

**Verdict: CONFIRMED. "Hegg" = Heggerty is the right reading.**

- FACT. Heggerty Phonemic Awareness is a real curriculum: 35 weeks of daily, scripted, oral phonological/phonemic awareness lessons, 10-12 minutes a day, designed as "the perfect supplement to an existing literacy curriculum" ([Heggerty kindergarten product page](https://heggerty.org/product/kindergarten-phonemic-awareness-curriculum-english-2022-edition/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- FACT. Publisher: created by Dr. Michael Heggerty and published by Literacy Resources, Inc. of River Forest, Illinois, now branded simply Heggerty ([curriculum documentation](https://content.schoolinsites.com/api/documents/2edd8ebd97ca4feba039c67f0d38a7c0.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).
- FACT. Price: [$89 per manual](https://shop.heggerty.org/collections/phonemic-awareness) across Early Pre-K, Pre-K, Kindergarten, Primary, and Bridge the Gap intervention editions; bundles to $179 (accessed 2026-07-06). Manuals are non-consumable and reusable across children.
- FACT. Homeschool moms, especially former classroom teachers, use it in preschool: "Heggerty is a curriculum that teaches kids phonemic awareness in about 3-5 minutes a day" ([a homeschool preschool curriculum roundup](https://themilleracres.com/homeschool-preschool-curriculum-picks/), accessed 2026-07-06); "When I started homeschooling, I knew I had to get this, although it is an investment" ([a homeschool review blog](https://rangelacademy.wordpress.com/2016/09/11/product-review-michael-heggerty/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- INFERENCE. Plausibility of "Hegg" as shorthand: High. The brand is a single surname commonly spoken as "the Heggerty book"; a clipped "Hegg" in a parent brief is a natural truncation, and no competing curriculum with a similar name surfaced in any search.
- FACT, provenance detail for the practice library: the teacher-facing ecosystem treats Heggerty as near-infrastructure ("LETRS trainers promoted Heggerty by name"), and its evidence base is contested: reviewers note studies of the original oral-only design showed weak reading-outcome effects, after which "Heggerty added letters into its lessons" ([Curriculum Insight Project](https://curriculuminsightproject.substack.com/p/educators-were-sold-a-story-about), accessed 2026-07-06). If Homa's comparison engine cites efficacy, it must distinguish editions.
- CORRECTION for F7 (curriculum comparison journey). The planning docs frame the demo question as "Heggerty or Logic of English for phonics?" ([04-feature-journeys F7](../journeys/04-feature-journeys.md)). Heggerty is an oral phonemic awareness supplement, not a phonics program ([its own product copy: "the perfect supplement to an existing literacy curriculum"](https://heggerty.org/product/kindergarten-phonemic-awareness-curriculum-english-2022-edition/), accessed 2026-07-06). A truthful comparison engine should sometimes answer "these are complements, not substitutes," and that honesty is itself a differentiator. Keep the pair as the demo, but let the answer model complements.

Confidence: High.

## 6. Buying behavior: box vs assembly, switching, resale

- FACT. Assembly dominates: in the 1,171-parent study, only [21% used a curriculum package at all, and only 5% used one package as their sole source](https://nheri.org/home-school-researcher-parent-perspectives-curriculum-and-homeschooling-approaches/) (accessed 2026-07-06, unscientific sample). Federal data agrees directionally: families report 4-6 concurrent curriculum sources ([NCES table 6](https://nces.ed.gov/pubs2020/2020001.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).
- FACT. Mid-year switching is normalized in community guidance: "mid-year changes are completely acceptable, and sometimes necessary," with major curriculum changes "every 2-3 years ... common in early homeschool years" ([numaschool adapting guide](https://numaschool.com/learn/methods/eclectic/adapting-over-time), accessed 2026-07-06); veteran bloggers treat it as routine ([Confessions of a Homeschooler](https://www.confessionsofahomeschooler.com/blog/2014/10/homeschooling-101-switching-curriculum-mid-year.html), accessed 2026-07-06). No national statistic on mid-year switch frequency exists: UNVERIFIABLE as a rate.
- FACT. The resale market is large, organized, and priced: used curriculum typically trades at [40-60% of retail, complete clean sets up to 70%, written-in consumables 10-20%](https://www.cleverhomeschool.com/how-to-buy-and-sell-used-homeschool-curriculum-a-parents-guide-to-saving-money-tips-on-where-to-find-deals-and-how-to-recoup-your-costs-at-the-end-of-the-year/) (accessed 2026-07-06). Venues: [homeschoolclassifieds.com, the oldest dedicated marketplace, free to list](https://homeschoolstartguide.com/blog/homeschool-classifieds) (accessed 2026-07-06); Facebook groups as the dominant channel, including per-curriculum BST groups ("Sonlight ... USED books sell/swap/share," "Math-u-see ... buy/sell," "Simply Charlotte Mason Curriculum Marketplace," "Secular Homeschool Curriculum Marketplace") ([directory of groups](https://homeschool-planning.com/used-curriculum/), accessed 2026-07-06); eBay for out-of-print; spring used-curriculum sales at conventions and co-ops ([TheHomeSchoolMom](https://www.thehomeschoolmom.com/homeschool-help/used-homechool-curriculum-sources/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- INFERENCE. Switching cost math: a failed $150-$1,100 purchase recovers 40-60% if non-consumable and near-zero if consumable, so a wrong pick costs roughly $60-$600 plus the sunk weeks. This is the anxiety Homa's fit-based comparison and sample-week trial (F7) directly prices against.

Confidence: Medium-High.

## 7. Segment cards: what each philosophy demands or rejects in an AI assistant

Context stat for all cards. FACT: homeschool educators adopt AI faster than classroom teachers: [44% vs 34% reported using ChatGPT](https://www.the74million.org/article/homeschoolers-embrace-ai-even-as-many-educators-keep-it-at-arms-length/) in a 2023 Age of Learning survey (accessed 2026-07-06); over a quarter had used it to help with homework and the average acceptable start age was 11 ([Deseret coverage](https://www.deseret.com/2023/9/1/23770495/what-do-home-school-educators-think-about-chatgpt/), accessed 2026-07-06).

### Card 1: Classical

- Wants: structured sequence, mastery, memory work, Latin/logic in the taxonomy, rigor signals. FACT: the segment's own market is built on trivium staging and memory work ([CC](https://classicalconversations.com/community-search/), [Memoria](https://www.memoriapress.com/), accessed 2026-07-06); research finds families choose classical because "its rigor will prepare their children well for college" ([Kunzman & Gaither](https://www.icher.org/files/Kunzman_and_Gaither_An%20Updated_Comprehensive_Survey.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Rejects: outsourced thinking, not just fluff. Verbatim, classical educator: "AI can place information in front of a student, but it cannot build the internal structures that allow a student to generate, evaluate, or apply that information independently." And: "Judgment cannot be downloaded. It must be trained." ([CiRCE Institute](https://circeinstitute.org/blog/education-is-more-than-access/), accessed 2026-07-06). CiRCE's founder goes further: we should "never name anything technological with the words 'artificial intelligence'" ([CiRCE](https://circeinstitute.org/blog/artificial-intelligence-is-dumb/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Where she shops and pays: CC community fees $600-$1,600+ plus books, Memoria boxes $513-$625 ([CC comparison page](https://classicalconversations.com/memoria-press/), accessed 2026-07-06). INFERENCE: she is the highest-budget segment and already pays for accountability structure, so $49/mo is not the objection; vocabulary is. Homa must know what Foundations memory work and Latin recitation are without being asked.
- **Planning table row: CONFIRMED**, with a sharpening: the landmine is not only "fluffy play framing reads as unserious," it is any hint that the AI does the child's thinking. Homa is safe here only because it serves the parent, never tutors the child.

### Card 2: Charlotte Mason

- Wants: living books over textbooks, short lessons, narration, nature study, atmosphere language. FACT: canonical sources define the method exactly this way ([Simply Charlotte Mason](https://simplycharlottemason.com/blog/what-is-twaddle/), accessed 2026-07-06; [living books explainer](https://homeschoolpicks.com/homeschool-methods/what-are-living-books-charlotte-mason/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Rejects: twaddle. Verbatim definition from the segment's flagship publisher: "I would define twaddle as dumbed-down writing, usually with short choppy sentences." ([Simply Charlotte Mason podcast](https://simplycharlottemason.com/blog/help-my-child-wants-twaddle/), accessed 2026-07-06). Mason's own target was "the feeble, much-diluted stuff that is too often put into children's hands" ([Simply Charlotte Mason](https://simplycharlottemason.com/blog/twaddle-too-often/), accessed 2026-07-06). A CM-community writer's sharper cut: "Twaddle leaves the mind stupefied and in need of recovery." ([Afterthoughts blog](https://afterthoughtsblog.net/2014/10/myth-twaddle-and-light-reading-are-same.html/), accessed 2026-07-06)
- Where she shops and pays: free AmblesideOnline-style book lists, TGTB free PDFs, BYL $30-$60 guides, Wild + Free $19/mo bundles (§4). INFERENCE: she is price-sensitive on curriculum but demonstrably pays monthly for beauty and belonging (Wild + Free), which is the exact posture Homa's subscription needs; the printable that wins her is a nature-study log or narration card, not a drill sheet.
- **Planning table row: CONFIRMED with a terminology correction.** "Worksheet-heavy output reads as twaddle" is directionally right but imprecise: twaddle names dumbed-down reading content, not worksheets per se. The precise offense is diluted content and busywork displacing living ideas and narration. Homa materials for CM families should lead with book suggestions, narration prompts, and nature-study logging; when a printable is offered it must carry a real idea, not fill-in-the-blank drill. Note the practical reality: "most Charlotte Mason families combine the two, using a workbook for math while leaning on stories for everything else" ([living books explainer](https://homeschoolpicks.com/homeschool-methods/what-are-living-books-charlotte-mason/), accessed 2026-07-06), so the allergy is aesthetic-ideological, not absolute.

### Card 3: Unschooling / child-led

- Wants: interest-capture, life-as-evidence, zero assignments. FACT verbatims: "Only school chops learning into subject areas, periods, and school years." ([Unschooling To University](https://unschoolingtouniversity.com/2020/02/04/15-problems-with-testing-unschoolers-and-homeschoolers/), accessed 2026-07-06). "The idea of being 'behind' only works inside a system where everyone is expected to move at the same pace." ([Unschooling Mom2Mom](https://www.unschoolingmom2mom.com/do-unschoolers-fall-behind), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Accepts, and this is the wedge: compliance automation. FACT: an unschooling co-op mother in North Carolina uses "a website that uses AI to detail what activities their kids have done and auto-completes all of the relevant North Carolina educational standards. 'I thought that was a genius tool.'" ([The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/homeschoolers-embrace-ai-even-as-many-educators-keep-it-at-arms-length/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Where she shops and pays: mostly nowhere; unschooling is definitionally low-spend, though even advocates concede documentation is the hard part ("harder to document for record-keeping requirements," [methods comparison](https://homeschoolpicks.com/homeschool-methods/homeschool-methods-comparison-chart/), accessed 2026-07-06). INFERENCE: she will not pay for materials or benchmarks; she might pay for evidence-without-ceremony in the compliance states.
- **Planning table row: CONFIRMED.** Benchmarks and grade levels read as the system she fled; opt-in framing is mandatory. The evidence adds a positive: ambient logging that turns life into paperwork is not merely tolerated, it is celebrated, even by unschoolers.

### Card 4: Eclectic (the plurality)

- Wants: per-child, per-subject flexibility, curriculum comparison, permission to change mid-year. FACT: the segment definitionally mixes "boxed curriculum, homemade curriculum, and/or individualized curriculum" ([NHERI-hosted study](https://nheri.org/home-school-researcher-parent-perspectives-curriculum-and-homeschooling-approaches/), accessed 2026-07-06); community guidance normalizes mid-year change ([numaschool](https://numaschool.com/learn/methods/eclectic/adapting-over-time), accessed 2026-07-06). The SEA community brands the whole identity as "handcraft your student's journey through learning" ([SEA Homeschoolers](https://seahomeschoolers.com/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Rejects: being forced into one method's vocabulary. FACT: even Wild + Free, an aesthetic community, leads with "We don't all homeschool the same way ... if you take an eclectic approach by doing a little of everything, that's fine by us." ([Wild + Free members page](https://members.bewildandfree.org/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Where she shops and pays: everywhere at once (4-6 concurrent sources, [NCES table 6](https://nces.ed.gov/pubs2020/2020001.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06): per-subject picks like Singapore $169 + AAR $160 + a $45 PDF spine, topped up from BST groups at 40-60% of retail (§6). INFERENCE: her real annual spend often rivals a box, just spread across a dozen decisions; each decision is a research spiral Homa can shorten, and shortening it is felt value.
- **Planning table row: CONFIRMED**, including the plurality claim (§2 verdict).

### Card 5: Religious (overlaps all of the above)

- Wants: worldview control, content that never contradicts without consent, community trust. FACT: the discipleship framing is the segment's own language: "Our mission is to equip Christian homeschool families to fulfill God's calling within their homes" and "Our events are distinctly Christian, from the speakers to the exhibitors" ([Teach Them Diligently](https://teachthemdiligently.net/about/) and [TTD convention pitch](https://teachthemdiligently.net/7-reasons-why-all-homeschool-moms-should-attend-a-homeschool-convention/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- On AI, the caution is articulated but not absolute. FACT verbatim (Reformed apologist writing for homeschoolers): "When it is used as nothing more than a 'homework machine,' ChatGPT will destroy our students' ability to read, write, and think clearly," in the same essay noting that major Christian bodies have "all made statements affirming that AI can be used morally" ([Shenvi, AI and Homeschooling](https://shenviapologetics.com/ai-and-homeschooling/), accessed 2026-07-06). Catholic homeschool publishers run how-to-use-AI guides while warning against letting it "quietly hollow" children out ([Homeschool Connections](https://homeschoolconnections.com/pope-leo-magnifica-humanitas-homeschooling/), accessed 2026-07-06; [their ChatGPT planning guide](https://homeschoolconnections.com/chat-gpt-homeschool-planning/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Where she shops and pays: the convention floor and legacy publishers, at the market's highest prices (Abeka to $1,884 with video; Sonlight ~$1,000; §4), plus "You don't have to worry about stuff being hidden in any curriculum you buy there" as the stated reason for the channel ([TTD attendee verbatim](https://teachthemdiligently.net/7-reasons-why-all-homeschool-moms-should-attend-a-homeschool-convention/), accessed 2026-07-06). INFERENCE: "nothing hidden" is the purchase criterion; Homa earns it with content controls and provenance, never with a statement of faith it does not hold.
- **Planning table row: CORRECTED.** Worldview control and secular-content landmines: confirmed. "The deepest AI skepticism lives here": overstated as written. The evidence shows the most theologically articulated caution lives here, aimed at formation (who shapes the child) and content control, while actual adoption is pragmatic and high (44% overall usage stat above, Christian publishers teaching prompt-craft). The row should read: deepest need for worldview control and provenance transparency, not lowest willingness to use AI.

### Card 6: School-at-home (newcomer default)

- Wants: schedules, checklists, grade-level language, a box. FACT: the boxed-traditional style is the recognized newcomer archetype in every taxonomy ([NHERI-hosted study definitions](https://nheri.org/home-school-researcher-parent-perspectives-curriculum-and-homeschooling-approaches/), accessed 2026-07-06), and only ~5% of established homeschoolers still identify with it, because people leave it.
- Burns out on schedule. FACT: the research literature: "after a year or two of assiduous effort to mimic formal schooling at home, new homeschooling mothers gradually move toward a less-structured, more eclectic approach," with the shift functioning to "avoid burnout" ([Kunzman & Gaither summarizing Lois's ethnography](https://www.icher.org/files/Kunzman_and_Gaither_An%20Updated_Comprehensive_Survey.pdf), accessed 2026-07-06). The February timing is community folklore, but well-attested folklore: "Everyone wants to quit in November and February" (attributed to Susan Wise Bauer, [Simple Homeschool](https://simplehomeschool.net/everyone-wants-to-quit/), accessed 2026-07-06); "February is a really, really common exhaustion point" ([a veteran homeschool blogger](https://jessconnell.com/2016/02/03/did-you-know-february-is-homeschool-burnout-month/), accessed 2026-07-06); "Homeschool burnout is most common in February and November" ([Homeschool.com](https://www.homeschool.com/articles/were-declaring-this-month-dont-quit-february/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- Where she shops and pays: the box and the subscription, because both promise "someone else planned this": crisis-onboarding guidance literally prescribes "a boxed traditional curriculum for the first semester to rebuild rhythm" ([methods chart](https://homeschoolpicks.com/homeschool-methods/homeschool-methods-comparison-chart/), accessed 2026-07-06), and the online programs (Time4Learning $39.95, Miacademy $48, Power Homeschool $99) are built for her (§4). INFERENCE: she is the easiest to acquire and the fastest to churn; Homa's job is to be the thing she keeps when she drops the box.
- **Planning table row: CONFIRMED.** The product implication in the table ("the tool must help her relax the model, not cement it") matches the documented trajectory: the tool should walk her toward eclectic, which is both where she is going anyway and where Homa's retention lives.

INFERENCE, composed from the cards above. The personas doc calls the benchmark mechanic philosophy-agnostic in principle, philosophy-charged in presentation ([01-personas-jtbd](../journeys/01-personas-jtbd.md)); the evidence supports one presentation rule per segment:

Confidence: High on verbatims and directional claims; Medium on anything quantitative.

## 8. Faith-based distribution and where Homa can enter credibly

- FACT. The national convention circuit is explicitly Christian. Great Homeschool Conventions describes itself as "a conservative Christian organization and avowedly 'young-earth'" that will not platform anything "anti-Christian" ([GHC convention philosophy](https://greathomeschoolconventions.com/about/convention-philosophy), accessed 2026-07-06). Teach Them Diligently is discipleship-branded end to end (§7 card 5 verbatims). State-org events are described as operating "with overtly Christian frameworks," with secular families "finding a large portion of the curriculum floor irrelevant" ([secular conventions guide](https://homeschoolstartguide.com/blog/secular-homeschool-conventions), accessed 2026-07-06).
- FACT. The dominant legacy publishers are worldview products (Abeka, BJU, Sonlight, MFW, CC; §4 table), and Christian retail (Christianbook's Homeschool Compass) courts even the gentle-aesthetic communities ([Wild + Free interview](https://homeschoolcompass.com/the-call-of-the-wild-free-an-interview-with-ainsley-arment/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- FACT. The secular minority routes around all of it, online: SEA's 100K+ family community, four annual online conferences, and a vetted secular bookstore ([SEA](https://seahomeschoolers.com/online-conference-series/), accessed 2026-07-06); direct-to-consumer PDF curricula (BYL, Torchlight, Blossom & Root, §4); a secular spinoff of the biggest Christian box (BookShark, [created "specifically as a secular alternative to Sonlight"](https://homeschoolfox.com/curriculum/bookshark), accessed 2026-07-06); and secular BST groups ([group directory](https://homeschool-planning.com/used-curriculum/), accessed 2026-07-06).
- INFERENCE. Implication for Homa: the convention channel demands worldview signaling (statements of faith, young-earth alignment) that a worldview-neutral product cannot honestly perform, and should not fake. The secular/eclectic online channel has no gatekeeper, matches Homa's parent-controlled neutrality, and its members are the heaviest online-tool buyers. Enter there first; serve religious families from day one via parent-controlled content settings (the D-queue "never surprise her" baseline), and treat explicit faith-aware packs as a later, opt-in expansion once trust exists. The 69%-religion-important vs 34%-homeschool-for-religion gap (§2) says worldview-respect without worldview-branding serves the actual middle of this market.

Confidence: Medium-High.

### Beachhead

**The secular-leaning eclectic segment, with school-at-home newcomers as the acquisition inflow.** Reasons, all sourced above:

1. It is the largest slice (§2) and the only one with no philosophy-vocabulary landmine (§7 card 4).
2. Its defining behavior is per-child stack assembly (79% use no single package, §6), which is exactly what per-child personalized materials plus curriculum comparison serve.
3. It adopts AI tools fastest (44% context stat, §7) and buys online without convention gatekeepers (§8).
4. Its three chronic pains are Homa's three features: assembly cost (personalized materials), am-I-doing-enough anxiety (gentle observational benchmarks), and state paperwork (automation), with the unschooling-adjacent evidence showing even the most benchmark-averse families celebrate compliance automation (§7 card 3).
5. The newcomer segment arrives every September, defaults to school-at-home, and predictably burns out toward eclectic by February (§7 card 6); a tool whose job is "help her relax the model" converts that churn moment into Homa's retention story rather than a cancellation.

The runner-up (gentle/CM Christian eclectic, the P2 shape) is reachable with the same product if materials respect the twaddle correction, but it should not be the first-dollar target because its distribution runs through worldview-gated channels (§8).
