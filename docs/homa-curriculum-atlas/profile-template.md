`methodology.md` and `taxonomy.md`. Length guidance by tier: flagship 250+
lines, deep 120 to 200, standard 60 to 120, stub 25 to 40 (stubs may stop
after Reputation and rankings plus Official samples and links).

# {Name}

## Snapshot

Three to six sentences. What it is, who makes it, who it is for, what makes
it distinctive, and roughly how big its mindshare is. A person who reads
only this section should be able to hold a competent conversation about the
curriculum.

## Reputation and rankings

Awards won (Cathy Duffy Top Picks, Practical Homeschooling Reader Awards,
Old Schoolhouse, homeschool.com), reviewer verdicts, and honest community
sentiment including the critical threads. Note whether its trajectory is
rising, stable, or fading, with evidence.

## Pedagogy and method

The teaching model and why its fans believe it works. Mastery or spiral,
conceptual or procedural, scripted or flexible, parent role, session length,
assessment philosophy. Name the intellectual lineage if there is one
(Singapore MOE research, Orton-Gillingham, trivium, Mason's principles).

## Structure and scope

Levels and what defines them, components per level (student book, workbook,
teacher guide, tests, manipulatives), lessons per level, expected pace, and
a strand-level summary of scope and sequence. Topic outlines are facts and
may be summarized; link the publisher's own scope and sequence documents.
If the repo's benchmarks crosswalk already mapped this program's levels
(math and ELA programs), link it rather than redoing it.

## A lesson up close

The heart of the profile. Walk through what one actual lesson or day looks
like from the parent's and child's seat: how the teacher material is
organized, what the parent says or does, what the child sees on the page,
how practice is structured, how long it takes, what a completed day
produces. Describe the worksheet or workbook page anatomy: layout, item
types, item counts, difficulty ramp, illustration style, white space. This
section describes and links; it never reproduces content (see methodology
copyright policy).

## Materials and visual design

The physical and visual reality: page size, color vs black and white, paper
and binding quality, illustration character, typography feel, manipulatives
and their build quality, how heavy the box is on the shelf. What the
aesthetic signals to the buyer (Abeka's flag-and-flagpole traditionalism,
TGTB's fine-art softness, Beast Academy's comic energy).

## Pricing

What a year actually costs at each level of buy-in (minimum viable, typical,
everything). Consumable vs reusable economics for multi-kid families.
Resale behavior if notable. Date every number and link the price page.

## Why families choose it

The honest pull factors, in the buyer's own logic.

## Why families leave it

The honest churn factors: where it breaks down, for which kids, at which
level. Curriculum switching is normal in this market; name the common
destinations ("families leaving X for more conceptual depth typically move
to Y").

## Competitive notes for Homa

What this curriculum teaches Homa: materials patterns worth learning from,
the gap it leaves that Homa can fill, how Homa should behave when a family
says they use it (vocabulary to know, levels to understand, supplements it
typically needs), and any direct competitive overlap with Homa's own
surfaces.

## Official samples and links

Bulleted links to the publisher's free samples, look-inside pages, scope
and sequence PDFs, placement tests, demo videos, catalogs. This is how the
atlas captures "what it actually looks like" without copying it.

## Sources

Bulleted list of every source used, with URLs. Note access date once at the
top of the list (normally the front matter last_reviewed date).
