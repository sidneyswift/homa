# What the atlas means for Homa

Synthesis of wave 1 (2026-07-13): 87 curriculum profiles, 8 category
comparisons, the market overview, and the TPT marketplace profile,
researched by parallel agents against live sources and validated against
the methodology. This file is the product-facing summary; every claim
below traces to a profile or comparison doc, and category evidence lives
in `comparisons/`.

This is dated strategy input, not policy. The PRD and founder rulings
still govern; where this file suggests product direction it feeds those
authorities.

## The thesis

The atlas set out to map Homa's competition and found something more
useful: the competition is not any curriculum, it is the unmanaged stack.
The plurality family assembles 4 to 6 sources, switches mid-year as a
normal act, and pays in unpriced labor (planning, sourcing, adapting,
record-keeping) for the privilege. Every category's churn drivers and
every category's structural gaps point at the same assistant-shaped hole,
and no publisher can fill it because each publisher is one piece of the
stack. Homa's layer is the stack itself. Every implication below is a
variation on that theme.

## The market's product laws

Recurring across all eight categories and the marketplace:

1. Open-and-go is the killer feature word. The chronic constraint is
   mom's prep time, not money. TPT sells "NO PREP" as a genre name; TGTB
   built the fastest-growing brand of the decade on the same promise.
2. Independence is the second currency: anything the child can self-run
   (Teaching Textbooks, CLE light units, courseware) wins multi-kid and
   working-parent households, at a known cost in depth they then shop to
   patch.
3. Free-plus-beautiful is the winning acquisition model: TGTB's free
   PDFs, Ambleside's free commons, Khan's free courseware, free placement
   tests everywhere. Free is the funnel; beauty and execution are the
   product.
4. Philosophy is the store layout. Moms are taught to identify an
   approach before comparing products, and each philosophy has vocabulary
   landmines (twaddle, trivium stages, narration). A product that speaks
   the vocabulary wrong reads as ignorant; the atlas front matter is
   Homa's fluency layer.
5. Structure outsells content. CC charges four figures for community
   accountability with thin materials; Memoria charges hundreds for
   checkbox daily manuals; Timberdoodle monetizes schedule curation of
   other vendors' products. Families pay most for the orchestration
   layer, which is Homa's layer.
6. Human-quality feedback is the willingness-to-pay peak (Outschool
   teachers, Mr. D live help, Night Zookeeper tutors, Oak Meadow's $2,870
   teacher track), while pure automation races to free. Homa's generated
   feedback must clear the human-quality bar, not the courseware bar.

## The boundary Homa must respect

The classical and CM research sharpened the AI landmine into a usable
rule: these philosophies locate education in the child's own act, so
tools that touch the child's work are rejected on principle, while the
parent's clerical layer is resented and happily outsourced. The online
category proved the same rule from the failure side: child-facing AI
keeps failing on engagement and trust (Khanmigo's non-event,
Time4Learning's 2026 AI-grading backlash), while parent-serving
automation is celebrated even by unschoolers. This is the strongest
external validation yet of Homa's parent-first architecture: Homa serves
the parent's clipboard, never the child's mind, and every surface should
be checkable against that sentence.

## Worldview is first-class configuration

Science splits four ways (young-earth, Christian-but-neutral, neutral,
secular) and the neutral middle is attacked from both flanks. History has
no neutral middle at all (secular vs providential). Secular buyers apply
bright-line tests and treat SEA vetting as certification; religious
buyers demand provenance ("nothing hidden"). Beautiful Feet already sells
charter packs with religious titles removed: a productized worldview
toggle. Implication: a per-family worldview setting, inferred at
onboarding from the named curriculum and confirmed with the parent, must
gate generated content in science and history especially. Expect Homa's
own materials to be vetted by both flanks; visible provenance is the
answer to both.

## The recurring gaps are Homa's feature map

Seven gaps recur across categories, each documented in the comparisons:

1. Permission to prune. Every all-in-one's churn section is the same
   burnout-and-drift lifecycle; veterans preach "skip pages" but nobody
   tells a specific mom what is safe to skip for her specific child.
   Cheapest wedge into the boxed-curriculum segment.
2. The practice layer each core lacks. Saxon families need less review,
   Beast and Dimensions families need more spaced fluency, TT and CTC
   families need paper and word problems. Generated practice tuned to the
   family's declared curriculum, in its visual grammar, serves every core
   without asking anyone to switch.
3. One-lesson-many-levels. Family-style teaching is the norm in history,
   science, and unit studies, and the static implementations (MOH's three
   tracks, Berean's three-level review, Gather Round's response ladder:
   trace, word bank, sentence, paragraph, essay) are the proven shapes.
   Homa can do dynamically what they do statically: possibly the single
   strongest materials opening in the atlas.
4. Records that survive the stack. Oral pedagogies produce no paper by
   design while compliance states demand paper; courseware takes the
   record hostage at cancellation; publishers monetize accreditation
   lock-in. A curriculum-neutral record that turns logged life and oral
   work into portfolio-ready evidence, and survives every switch, is the
   unclaimed generalization. Homa's ChildProgress layer is already shaped
   for it.
5. Scheduled transitions and placement translation. Every program has a
   structural exit (Dimensions and TGTB math end at 8, Mystery Science at
   5, MWC at 6, Beast at 5) and the level languages are mutually
   unintelligible (Greek letters, dual-grade fractions, A/B halves). The
   benchmarks crosswalk already maps them; parent-facing translation and
   bridge planning at the documented exit moments is an ownable job.
6. Logistics nobody prices. Science's experiment-supply problem makes it
   the most-skipped subject; the PDF-plus-library economy leaves sourcing
   30 to 50 books per year as unpriced labor. Week-ahead supply and
   library lists generated from the family's actual position are cheap,
   high-frequency value.
7. Complements-not-substitutes honesty. The curriculum-vs-supplement
   confusion is systemic (IXL, Khan, Power Homeschool discover it at
   tears, stall, or transcript time), and the market has no honest
   adjudicator. The Heggerty correction in the repo's own research is the
   pattern: sometimes the right answer is "these are complements." An
   assistant that says so earns the trust the platforms cannot.

## Materials studio quality bar

What generated materials must match, from the flagship anatomy, the TPT
data, and the category patterns:

- Per-page difficulty ramps as a house pattern (Dimensions' three-band
  workbook exercises; TPT's evergreen differentiation sellers; Gather
  Round's response ladder).
- Ritual formats, not just one-offs: morning work, spiral review,
  paragraph of the week. Recurring formats are what installs a habit.
- Evidence labels: the science-of-reading wave trained buyers to shop by
  pedagogical provenance (decodable stage, OG lineage, bar-model stage).
  Homa materials should carry their pedagogy visibly.
- Ambient evidence shapes for the gentle segments: narration prompts,
  nature logs, story maps. Never quiz-shaped unless the family's
  philosophy welcomes it.
- Aesthetic floor: TGTB and Beast prove page beauty creates loyalty that
  survives pedagogical doubts; TPT's winners are visually consistent
  brands. Homa's design system is an asset; "AI generic" is disqualifying.
- Print-at-home is normalized (TGTB's side effect: families own the
  printer habit), and per-child printables delete the consumables tax
  that boxed publishers charge multi-kid families.
- Never clone: patterns are unowned, expression is owned. The methodology
  copyright policy governs.

## Onboarding and product data

- "What curriculum do you use?" is the highest-information onboarding
  question in the product. One answer (Dimensions Math 2A, CC Foundations
  cycle 2, AO Year 4 Term 2) implies philosophy, worldview posture,
  parent load tolerance, level position, and likely gaps. The atlas front
  matter is the lookup table; wave 3 wires sequences into
  `packages/taxonomy/` skill states.
- Model cycle position as family state, not just grade: "year two of the
  history cycle" is a complete progress statement in half the market.
- The methods glossary already flagged the product's style enum gaps;
  atlas approach tags roll up to whatever that enum becomes.
- Billing legibility is a product feature, not plumbing: Power
  Homeschool's price-identity collapse, Outschool's credit-abstraction
  churn, and auto-renewal resentment at IXL and ABCmouse say flat,
  cancel-safe pricing is a differentiator in this market.
- Price anchors: parents will compare Homa to Time4Learning ($39.95/mo)
  and the $0-200 assembly universe. The segments research's positioning
  answer stands: Homa is not courseware that teaches the child, it is the
  assistant that serves the parent's own stack.

## What Homa should not build

- A child-facing AI tutor (the documented failure pattern and the
  philosophy landmine, in one).
- Teacher-life products (planners, decor): TPT's turf, off-mission.
- A curriculum clone of any publisher's expression.
- Worldview signaling Homa does not hold (statements of faith): serve
  worldview control with provenance and settings, never with borrowed
  identity.
- A review-scores site: Cathy Duffy and the award circuits own ranking
  authority; Homa's authority is fit-for-this-family, not stars.
