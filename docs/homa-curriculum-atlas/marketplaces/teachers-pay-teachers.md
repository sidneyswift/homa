# TPT (Teachers Pay Teachers)

Not a curriculum: the dominant marketplace for teacher-created printable
and digital resources, and competitively the closest existing thing to
Homa's materials studio. Profiled at marketplace level; the atlas treats
marketplaces as their own entity type (see `taxonomy.md`).

## Snapshot

TPT is the largest marketplace of educator-created learning materials:
worksheets, units, centers, decor, planners, and digital activities sold
by individual "Teacher-Authors" who set their own prices. Founded in 2006
by a New York City teacher (Paul Edelman), it was acquired by IXL Learning
on 2023-03-02 for an undisclosed price, joining Rosetta Stone, Wyzant,
Education.com, ABCya, and Vocabulary.com in IXL's portfolio. The company
claims more than 7 million resources, more than 7 million educator users
including 85% of US PK-12 teachers, and over 1 billion all-time downloads.
For homeschool families it is the infinite supplement aisle: the place a
mom goes to patch a gap her curriculum left, find a seasonal unit, or
print practice for a skill her kid has not locked in yet. That job
description overlaps Homa's materials studio almost exactly, which makes
this profile load-bearing for product strategy.

## Scale and ownership

- Publisher claims, 2023 acquisition press release: 7M+ resources, 85% of
  US PK-12 teachers, 1B+ downloads
  ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ixl-learning-acquires-teachers-pay-teachers-the-worlds-largest-platform-for-educator-created-content-301760400.html)).
- Independent academic accounting (data through Sept 2019): 4,018,173
  resources across 208,748 seller stores, 1.5B all-time downloads, $3.9B
  in cumulative sales; 69% of all downloads were free items; 81% of total
  sales went to the top 1% of sellers (about 1,500 stores)
  ([Koehler et al. 2021](https://www.matt-koehler.com/publications/Koehler_et_al_2021.pdf)).
- Over 300 sellers had earned $1M+ lifetime as of 2022, while the average
  seller profits in the low single-digit thousands per the then-CEO
  ([K-12 Dive](https://www.k12dive.com/news/ixl-learning-acquires-teachers-pay-teachers/644562/)).
- District product: TPT School Access (site-license subscription) plus
  Easel, its digital-activity layer.

The concentration number is the strategic one: this is a hits business
where a tiny elite of seller-brands owns the demand, which is why the
patterns of those top stores (below) define what "good" means for
printable materials in the US.

## How the marketplace works

Buyer side: free account, per-resource purchases, PDF (and increasingly
digital/Easel) delivery, licenses are per-teacher with additional-license
pricing for sharing. Free resources are the top-of-funnel: sellers give
away samplers to build followings.

Seller side, per the publisher's fee schedule
([Seller fees and payout rates](https://help.teacherspayteachers.com/hc/en-us/articles/360044219891-Seller-Fees-and-Payout-Rates),
as of Jul 2026):

- Basic Seller: $29 one-time fee, 55% payout, $0.30 transaction fee per
  resource.
- Premium Seller: $59.95 per year, 80% payout, $0.15 fee only on orders
  under $3.
- Publisher accounts (entities selling content they did not author):
  50/50 split.

The platform runs on sale culture: crossed-out anchor prices, flash
sales, bundle "value" framing (a $19.99 bundle presented against a $135
sum of parts, a $25 "lifetime vault" against a $525 value), and seasonal
purchase spikes around back-to-school. Sellers are their own marketers;
the durable winners operate like small media companies with email lists,
Pinterest funnels, and Instagram followings.

## What wins there (observed patterns)

From the all-time best-rated browse listing and top store pages, scraped
2026-07-13 (sorted by rating count, the de facto all-time best-seller
view). Categories and framing that dominate:

- Science of Reading alignment: decodable passages, phonics fluency
  packets, and reading intervention binders explicitly tagged "SOR
  aligned." The science-of-reading wave is the strongest merchandising
  current on the platform right now.
- "NO PREP" as a genre name: print-and-go packets are labeled NO PREP in
  the title, the same open-and-go promise the curriculum market sells.
- Ritual formats: morning work, spiral review, paragraph of the week,
  problem of the day. Products that install a daily routine outsell
  one-off activities.
- Seasonal packets: summer review, back to school, holiday units. The
  calendar is a merchandising engine.
- Differentiation and special needs: adapted work binders, editable
  levels, visual schedules. The special-education niche produces some of
  the platform's biggest all-time sellers.
- Teacher-life infrastructure: editable planners, classroom decor
  bundles, labels. Not instructional at all, but among the top sellers,
  which says the buyer is shopping for her own workflow, not just the
  child's learning.
- Bundles: the unit of serious spending. Individual resources $3-10;
  bundles $16-36 after discounts; "growing bundles" and lifetime vaults
  reward early buyers.

Example top stores (metadata only): The Moffatt Girls (early-elementary
packets, seasonal and NO PREP lines), Miss Giraffe (K-2 phonics and math),
One Stop Teacher Shop (spiral review and editable planners). Example
mega-sellers observed on the best-rated list include decodable passage
bundles, a special-needs adapted morning work binder, back-to-school
activity bundles, and first-grade math worksheet bundles (links resolve
from the browse page; product IDs are stable).

## The quality problem

TPT has no peer review or vetting; ratings measure buyer satisfaction,
not accuracy or pedagogy. The research record is consistent and blunt:

- Best-selling US history resources: 70% rated moderate or low quality,
  30% judged potentially harmful to students
  ([Harris, Archambault, Shelton 2021](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/357198580_Issues_of_quality_on_Teachers_Pay_Teachers_an_exploration_of_best-selling_US_history_resources)).
- Highly rated middle school science products: only 2 of 33 showed key
  NGSS design features; most propagated the "one scientific method" myth
  ([Research in Science Education 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11165-023-10146-1)).
- Free K-3 reading lessons: mostly consistent with research-supported
  practice but requiring real teacher expertise to use well ("let the
  buyer beware", Boise State analysis).
- Users believe TPT materials are high quality, especially ones they paid
  for, and often do not realize nothing is vetted; more experienced
  teachers drift away from the platform
  ([CITE Journal 2025](https://citejournal.org/volume-25/issue-2-25/current-practice/tensions-and-opportunities-early-career-elementary-teachers-perspectives-on-supplementing-curriculum-with-teachers-pay-teachers/)).

The market's own answer to quality is brand: buyers learn to trust
specific seller-brands (the top 1%) rather than the platform, which
recreates the curriculum market's philosophy-brand dynamic one layer
down.

## How homeschool families use it

TPT is classroom-teacher-first (audience filters exist for homeschool,
but the design center is the classroom). Homeschool use rides the
supplement pattern the repo's ICP research already established: families
assemble 4 to 6 concurrent sources, and TPT is the long tail of that
assembly, used for gap-filling, seasonal enrichment, extra practice on a
stuck skill, unit-study raw material, and cheap centers for littles.
Two frictions are specific to the homeschool buyer: classroom formatting
(30-desk assumptions, station language) has to be adapted to a kitchen
table, and search cost is high (the winning products are optimized for
teacher search terms, not "my 7-year-old confuses b and d").

## Competitive notes for Homa

TPT is the incumbent for exactly the job Homa's materials studio does,
so the lessons are direct:

1. The search cost is Homa's wedge. A TPT session is 45 minutes of
   scrolling, filtering, and preview-squinting to find a worksheet that
   is almost right. Homa generates the exactly-right one from the
   child's actual state in seconds. "Stop scrolling TPT at 11pm" is a
   marketable sentence.
2. NO PREP and ritual formats are the proven demand shapes. Morning
   work, spiral review, paragraph of the week: Homa should generate
   these recurring formats natively, personalized per child, rather
   than one-off worksheets only.
3. The SOR merchandising wave says buyers now shop by evidence label.
   Homa's materials carrying visible pedagogical provenance (decodable
   progression stage, bar-model stage) meets a demand TPT trained.
4. Quality-plus-trust is an open flank: the platform's own research
   record shows best-sellers are frequently weak, and trust concentrates
   in seller-brands. Homa's counter is generation with stated pedagogy
   and per-family consistency, the thing a 7-million-item bazaar
   structurally cannot promise.
5. Aesthetics are table stakes: the winning stores win partly on
   consistent, warm, legible visual design. Homa's design system is an
   asset here; generated materials must not look "AI generic" next to a
   Moffatt Girls page.
6. Differentiation sells: adapted binders and leveled bundles are
   evergreen top sellers. Homa's per-child tiering (one topic, multiple
   response levels) is the personalized version of the pattern.
7. Do not compete for the teacher-life category (planners, decor): it is
   TPT's turf and off Homa's mission.
8. Acquisition note for the study library (founder decision, tracked in
   `backlog.md`): buying a handful of top-selling bundles from the
   dominant stores for internal study is cheap ($20-40 each) and would
   ground the materials studio's quality bar in what actually wins.
   Purchased files stay outside the repo per the methodology's copyright
   policy.
