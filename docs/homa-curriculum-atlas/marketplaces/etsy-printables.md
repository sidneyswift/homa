# Etsy (homeschool printables economy)

Not a curriculum and not even an education company: Etsy is the general
handmade-and-digital marketplace, and inside it lives a large, distinct
homeschool printables economy. This profile covers that niche at
marketplace level, the same adapted treatment as
[teachers-pay-teachers.md](teachers-pay-teachers.md). All listing
observations are metadata only (titles, prices, review counts), gathered
from public search and listing pages on 2026-07-13.

## Snapshot

Etsy is where homeschool moms buy printables when they are shopping as
moms rather than as teachers. The niche's staple genres are busy books
and busy binders for toddlers, morning binders and morning menus,
flashcards and Montessori three-part cards, themed unit studies, and
homeschool planners, almost all sold as instant-download PDFs for a few
dollars each. The buyer prints, laminates, cuts, and velcros the product
into a physical object, and that assembly ritual is part of what she is
buying. Where TPT's design center is the classroom teacher and the
standards search, Etsy's is the home: aesthetic-first merchandising,
theme and problem search ("toddler busy book for road trip"), and zero
pretense of educator review. It is the second pillar of the printable
supplement economy alongside TPT, and since 2024 it is also the market
most visibly hit by the AI-generated content flood, which is reshaping
what buyers trust.

## Scale and ownership

- Etsy, Inc. (NYSE: ETSY) 2025 10-K: Etsy marketplace GMS of $10.46B
  (consolidated $11.92B including Depop), 86.5M active buyers, 5.6M
  active sellers, 74% of GMS from US buyers, $121 GMS per active buyer
  ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm)).
- Active buyers declined 3% year over year in 2025; the company frames
  stabilization late in the year
  ([Q4 2025 press release](https://investors.etsy.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/218/etsy-inc-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-results)).
- Digital downloads are not broken out anywhere in Etsy's reporting, so
  the size of the printables economy, let alone the homeschool slice, is
  unverifiable. Observable markers instead: a single niche query like
  "toddler busy binder" returns 5,000+ listings, and the top shops in
  the niche show review counts and sales counts in the thousands to tens
  of thousands (see What wins there).
- No education division, no education vetting, no curriculum ambitions.
  The 10-K discusses AI mostly as a fraud amplifier and as an emerging
  discovery channel it is partnering into.

## How the marketplace works

Buyer side: per-item purchases with instant PDF delivery, personal-use
licenses (shops routinely prohibit redistribution and commercial use),
and consumer trust signals rather than educator ones: star ratings with
a recency-weighted item average, shop review counts, years on Etsy,
"Star Seller" badges for service quality, and "in 20+ carts" urgency
labels. Nothing anywhere in the funnel involves a teaching credential.

Seller side, per the publisher's fee policy
([Fees and payments](https://www.etsy.com/legal/fees/), as of Jul 2026)
and standard fee math
([Anlyzo fee table](https://anlyzo.com/blog/etsy-fees-digital-products-what-you-pay)):

- $0.20 listing fee that re-charges on every unit sold (digital
  listings renew per sale), a 6.5% transaction fee, and US payment
  processing of 3% plus $0.25.
- All-in take on typical digital price points: about 18.6% of a $5
  item, 14% of a $10 item, 11.8% of a $20 item. Cheap items pay the
  highest effective rate, which quietly punishes the race to the
  bottom.
- Offsite Ads add 15% on attributed orders (12% once a shop passes
  $10,000 in trailing-365-day sales, at which point participation
  becomes mandatory for the life of the shop).

Compare TPT, which keeps 20% (Premium sellers) to 45% (Basic): Etsy's
roughly 11-14% take is the main reason established teacher-sellers
cross-list their TPT catalogs on Etsy. The platform runs the same sale
culture as TPT but hotter: essentially every serious printable listing
shows a crossed-out anchor price with 40-75% off, permanently.

A physical tier exists alongside the PDFs: sellers print, laminate, and
velcro the same busy binders and ship them "fully assembled" at $40-70,
a labor-arbitrage product for the mom who wants the object without the
craft evening.

## What wins there (observed patterns)

From public search pages and listing pages for the niche's core terms,
observed 2026-07-13, metadata only:

- Busy books and busy binders are the flagship genre: laminated
  matching, sorting, counting, and tracing pages, velcro pieces, dry
  erase reuse, marketed for ages 2-5, for quiet time, travel, church,
  and "the sibling hour" during an older kid's lessons. Example scale:
  a 23-binder printable mega bundle listed at $192 anchored and $96 on
  sale from a shop showing 11.4k reviews; a single busy book at $28.59
  anchored, $10.01 on sale (65% off) from a shop with 726 reviews; a
  race-to-the-bottom entry at $9.41 anchored, $2.35 on sale (75% off).
- Morning binders and morning menus: calendar, weather, emotions, name
  writing, and letter-sound pages slipped into page protectors or
  restaurant menu covers and reused daily with dry erase markers. A
  "Complete Morning Basket Kit" line sells age-banded variants (2-3,
  3-5, 6-8) at $44.40 each. This genre is where the homeschool identity
  is most explicit, and it is dominated by mom-brand shops.
- Flashcards and Montessori nomenclature cards: three-part cards, parts
  of a tree, yoga poses, feelings. Cheap ($2-8), enormous volume, long
  tails. One card shop shows 33.4k sales over 13 years on Etsy.
- Unit studies and seasonal packs: themed bundles (ocean, Australia,
  apples, Christmas) at $7-30, often Charlotte Mason flavored, with
  watercolor art and curated book lists. Seasonal merchandising mirrors
  TPT's calendar engine.
- Planners: dated and undated homeschool planners, reading logs, and
  schedule cards, the teacher-life-infrastructure equivalent for moms.
- Personalization sells: name-tracing workbooks and custom-name morning
  pages are a standing genre (one personalized toddler workbook shop
  shows 9.5k reviews at $19.97), which TPT structurally does not offer.
- The winning aesthetic is domestic, not classroom: watercolor,
  botanical accents, sage and cream palettes, "beautiful" as a first
  order merchandising word. Listing photos are styled flat lays of the
  assembled object on a wooden table, not screenshots of worksheets.

## The quality problem

Etsy has even less pedagogical infrastructure than TPT: no vetting, no
educator buyer base to socially filter products, and ratings that
measure delight with the transaction (cute, printed fine, kid liked it),
not whether the phonics sequence is sound. There is no research
literature on Etsy educational product quality the way there is for TPT;
the quality signal is entirely shop reputation.

The AI flood is the defining quality story since 2024:

- Etsy's July 2024 Creativity Standards reorganized every listing into
  "Made by", "Designed by", "Sourced by", or "Handpicked by" a seller.
  Seller-prompted AI creations are explicitly allowed under "Designed
  by" and must disclose AI use in the listing description; selling AI
  prompt packs is banned
  ([Creativity Standards](https://www.etsy.com/legal/creativity),
  [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/etsy-new-seller-policy-2024-generative-ai/)).
- Seller-community reporting describes printable categories (wall art,
  planner inserts, SVGs, and educational printables adjacent to them)
  as the heaviest-flooded, with prices in flooded segments crashing
  toward $1-3 and buyers increasingly avoiding AI-labeled visual
  products. One 2026 industry blog, citing Digital Commerce 360,
  reports human sellers in digital print categories lost roughly 40% of
  monthly views between Jan 2025 and Jan 2026. Treat the number as
  trade-press reported, not independently verified
  ([FindSkill audit post](https://findskill.ai/blog/etsy-sellers-ai-listings-flood-3-question-audit/),
  [Anlyzo AI art analysis](https://anlyzo.com/blog/can-you-sell-ai-art-on-etsy)).
- The seller-side counter-move is an explicit "human touch" premium:
  hand-drawn elements, imperfect textures, personal voice, and shop-
  age-plus-review-count archaeology by buyers. Trust is re-concentrating
  in named shops, the same brand dynamic TPT exhibits, now with
  "verifiably human" as part of the brand promise.

For education specifically the flood is worse than for wall art: an
AI-generated busy book with a subtly wrong letter-sound pairing looks
identical to a good one in a thumbnail, and the Etsy buyer, unlike the
TPT buyer, is usually not a trained teacher who would catch it. Nobody
in this loop has pedagogy credentials. That inference matters for Homa
positioning (below).

## How homeschool families use it

Etsy is the mom-identity aisle of the 4-6 concurrent source assembly
pattern documented in the repo's ICP research. Distinct jobs observed in
listings, shop copy, and reviews:

- Occupy the toddler: the busy binder is bought so the 2-year-old has
  "school" during an older sibling's lessons, plus restaurants, road
  trips, church, and waiting rooms.
- Install a morning ritual: morning menus and morning basket kits give
  the day a warm opening liturgy, calendar, weather, a verse or a poem,
  name writing. The faith-based segment is heavily represented here.
- Theme the season: unit studies and holiday packs feed the morning
  basket and keep the Instagram-native aesthetic of the homeschool
  going. This is the same blog-to-printable culture the atlas documents
  in [../comparisons/preschool.md](../comparisons/preschool.md).
- Patch a skill cheaply: flashcards and single-skill games at $2-5.
- Then she assembles: printing on cardstock, laminating, cutting,
  velcro dots, binders, and sleeves. Sellers include supply lists and
  assembly guides; the labor is understood and, for a real segment of
  buyers, enjoyed as craft. The "fully assembled" tier exists precisely
  because another segment hates it.

Two frictions define the experience: search cost (5,000+ listings for
one query, near-identical titles stuffed with the same keywords, no
meaningful skill or level filters) and the print-laminate-cut labor tax,
including ink cost, which sellers now market against with "low ink"
versions.

## Competitive notes for Homa

1. The search-cost wedge from the TPT profile extends here and gets
   sharper: Etsy adds keyword-stuffed near-duplicates and AI-slop
   anxiety on top of the 45-minute scroll. Homa generating the exactly
   right material from the child's actual state competes with both
   marketplaces at once.
2. Etsy proves the homeschool materials buyer is aesthetic-first. The
   bar for Homa's generated materials is an Etsy shop's watercolor
   warmth, not a worksheet mill. Homa's design system is the moat here,
   and "AI generic" visuals would place Homa on the wrong side of the
   platform's own trust crisis.
3. The ritual containers are the product, not the pages: busy binder,
   morning menu, seasonal unit. Homa should generate into these
   recurring formats natively (a personalized morning menu per child
   per season), the same lesson TPT's morning-work genre teaches.
4. Assembly labor is a real tax Homa can absorb: print-ready, low-ink,
   no-cut formats by default, with cut-and-laminate versions as an
   option for the moms who want the craft. Do not assume everyone wants
   zero assembly; a visible segment buys the ritual.
5. Personalization is already a paid-for feature on Etsy, but it is
   name-deep only. Homa personalizes to the child's skill state, which
   no listing can, and should say so plainly.
6. The AI flood is trust headwind and tailwind at once: buyers are
   learning to distrust anonymous generated content while trusting
   named brands. Homa's counter is provenance: generated by a system
   with stated pedagogy, for this specific child, under a brand the
   family already trusts. Anonymous-generic is the failure mode to
   engineer away from.
7. Watch the faith split: scripture morning menus are a top genre.
   Homa's generated materials stay worldview-neutral by default and
   accommodate faith content on explicit request, consistent with the
   atlas posture recorded for neutral publishers.
8. Acquisition note for the study library (founder decision, tracked in
   `backlog.md`): 3-5 representative purchases (a top busy binder, a
   morning menu kit, a seasonal unit study) would cost under $60 total
   and ground the materials studio's aesthetic bar in what actually
   wins with the mom buyer. Purchased files stay outside the repo per
   the methodology's copyright policy.
