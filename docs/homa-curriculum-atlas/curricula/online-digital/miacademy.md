# Miacademy

## Snapshot

Miacademy is the gamified K-8 courseware that has become the most common
"we left Time4Learning for..." destination: a subscription platform from
Miaplaza, Inc. with 3,000+ video-and-practice lessons across 80+ courses,
wrapped in a moderated virtual world (My World) where kids spend gold
coins earned by doing schoolwork. Its identity is engagement plus parent
control: short videos, interactive practice, auto-grading, and a reward
economy the parent can throttle. The brand family matters: Always
Icecream and Clever Dragons are the same content skinned for girls and
boys, and MiaPrep extends the system to grades 7-12, with MiaPrep Online
High School (MOHS) as a fully accredited (ACS-WASC) teacher-supported
school on top. Trustpilot sentiment runs strongly positive (4.7/5 across
591 reviews per a 2026 roundup), unusual for this category.

## Reputation and rankings

- Community reputation is the strongest of the paid all-subjects
  courseware group: praised for flexibility, engagement, parent
  controls, and neurodivergent-friendly features (text-to-speech,
  untimed tests, dark mode) ([Miacademy homepage](https://miacademy.co/),
  [logicarticles review roundup](https://logicarticles.com/miacademy-reviews/)).
- Families of ADHD and autistic kids report success with the short
  video segments and reward structure; reviewers frequently frame it as
  the burnout-relief pick ([Homeschool Tools guide](https://homeschooltools.net/offering/miacademy-homeschool-curriculum)).
- The critical threads: academic depth ("I personally don't see it as a
  complete curriculum for every subject" is representative r/homeschool
  sentiment), science and social studies units that finish in under a
  semester, formulaic video-then-quiz rhythm, frequent interface
  changes that force relearning, and occasional glitches
  ([Brighterly review mining](https://brighterly.com/blog/miacademy-reviews/),
  [logicarticles](https://logicarticles.com/miacademy-reviews/)).
- The harshest published take, for calibration: "Just watch a video and
  click a box. It's basically the least you could possibly do and call
  it school" ([captured in Homeschool Tools](https://homeschooltools.net/offering/miacademy-homeschool-curriculum)).
- Worldview positioning is deliberately in-between: core content is
  religion-free, optional Biblical Studies electives exist, and secular
  reviewers note evolution is not clearly present in K-8 science. This
  "neutral" posture widens the market and generates complaints from
  both edges ([logicarticles](https://logicarticles.com/miacademy-reviews/)).
- Trajectory: rising; it is the momentum brand in K-8 courseware and
  appears as the named alternative in competitors' complaint threads.

## Pedagogy and method

Video-taught, self-directed courseware with a token economy. Lessons are
short instructional videos followed by interactive practice and quizzes;
students earn gold coins for completed work and spend them in My World
(avatar items, games, a moderated social space). Parents set the
exchange rate in practice: the parent portal can require a quota of
schoolwork before play unlocks, adjust grade levels per subject, assign
lessons, and view auto-graded progress ([logicarticles](https://logicarticles.com/miacademy-reviews/)).
Leveling is per subject rather than per grade band, so a child can sit
at different levels across math and language arts. Assessment is
quiz-based with automatic grading and report generation for attendance
and progress ([Miacademy homepage](https://miacademy.co/)). There is no
live instruction in the base product; MOHS adds teacher grading, office
hours, and clubs at the high school tier.

## Structure and scope

- K-8 on miacademy.co: math, language arts, science, social studies,
  electives, and world languages, all included in one subscription;
  80+ courses, 3,000+ lessons, grouped K-2, 3-5, 6-8
  ([homepage](https://miacademy.co/)).
- Sister sites Always Icecream (girls) and Clever Dragons (boys) carry
  identical content with different theming ([FAQ](https://miacademy.co/homeschool-curriculum/faq/)).
- MiaPrep (miaprep.com): grades 7-12 curriculum, same model, $54/mo.
- MiaPrep Online High School: accredited ACS-WASC, teacher-graded
  cumulative assignments, small-group tutoring, advising, clubs,
  diploma, and an Arizona State University college-credit pathway for
  grades 10-12 ([high school page](https://miacademy.co/homeschool-curriculum/high-school-curriculum/)).
- No accreditation at the K-8 tier; parents keep records, though the
  platform generates progress and attendance reports.

## A lesson up close

The student logs in to a colorful dashboard, opens the assigned lesson,
watches a short video (live-action teachers and illustrated segments,
recently offered in a distraction-reducing full-screen mode), then works
a practice set that grades itself. Completing work credits gold coins;
the child then visits My World to spend them, budgeted by whatever gate
the parent configured. Multiple practice formats break the monotony
relative to older courseware, but the deep rhythm is still
video-then-quiz, which is exactly what critics name. A day produces
dashboard progress, coin balance, and printable reports when needed;
no paper artifacts by default.

## Materials and visual design

Bright, contemporary, game-adjacent UI: avatars, coins, a virtual world
rendered in friendly cartoon style. Compared to Time4Learning's aging
elementary animations, Miacademy reads a generation newer, which is a
real conversion factor for kids who called T4L babyish. The moderated
My World space (no ads, human-monitored chat) is a trust feature parents
cite. The aesthetic signals "learning app that respects that kids like
games," and its risk is the same signal: families who distrust reward
economies see a slot machine bolted to school.

## Pricing

- As of Jul 2026 ([FAQ](https://miacademy.co/homeschool-curriculum/faq/),
  [homepage](https://miacademy.co/)): $48/mo, $480/yr (works out to
  $40/mo), lifetime $1,120 per child, family lifetime $2,520 for up to
  4 children. First month $1.99 trial offer; seasonal sales are routine.
- MiaPrep: $54/mo, $540/yr, lifetime $1,060, family lifetime $2,280.
- MiaPrep Online High School: $54 curriculum + $340 discounted tuition,
  about $394/mo total ([high school page](https://miacademy.co/homeschool-curriculum/high-school-curriculum/)).
- The lifetime tiers are unusual in this category and change family
  economics: multi-kid families amortize $2,520 across four children
  and many years, undercutting every monthly competitor long-term.
- Repo price check note: the 2026-07-06 snapshot recorded the same $48
  and $480 figures ([08-segments-curricula](../../../homa-planning/icp-research/08-segments-curricula.md) §4).

## Why families choose it

- The kid actually likes it: the coin economy converts school
  resistance, which for a burned-out parent is the whole purchase.
- Parent control depth: throttle play, set per-subject levels, assign
  work, all without teaching.
- ND accommodations built in rather than bolted on.
- Lifetime pricing rewards big families and long-haul homeschoolers.
- Escape hatch from Time4Learning with the same low-involvement shape.

## Why families leave it

- Depth ceiling: families targeting rigorous or college-prep academics
  outgrow it, adding or switching to dedicated math (Beast Academy,
  Saxon) and writing programs; science and social studies thinness
  forces supplementation ([logicarticles](https://logicarticles.com/miacademy-reviews/)).
- Reward-economy fatigue: some kids optimize for coins, not learning,
  and some parents tire of policing the game gate.
- Format monotony for non-video learners; retention complaints appear
  in Reddit threads ("her kids were not retaining as much information,"
  [Brighterly capture](https://brighterly.com/blog/miacademy-reviews/)).
- Platform churn: frequent UI updates frustrate routine-dependent
  households, a notable overlap with its ND-family strength.
- Worldview edge cases: strictly secular families leave over evolution
  gaps; some Christian families want more than an elective.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Second price anchor after Time4Learning: $48/mo for all-subjects
  courseware. Homa priced near it must again sell the category
  difference (assistant to the parent vs screen that teaches the kid),
  per the positioning rule in
  [08-segments-curricula](../../../homa-planning/icp-research/08-segments-curricula.md) §4.
- The lifetime plan is worth studying: it converts churn-prone monthly
  buyers into sunk-cost loyalists and reads as founder-confidence. Homa
  should at least understand its pull when families compare.
- Vocabulary to know: My World, gold coins, Always Icecream and Clever
  Dragons (same product), MiaPrep vs MOHS (curriculum vs accredited
  school), per-subject level setting.
- When a family says they use it: expect strong engagement, thin
  science/social studies, and little writing feedback. Homa's fit is
  the depth layer: real writing response, meaty projects off-screen,
  and an honest read on whether coin-driven completion equals learning
  (observational evidence vs quiz scores).
- Materials lesson: parent-adjustable gates (work before play) are a
  respected control pattern; Homa's parallel is parent-controlled
  content and pacing settings, never child-facing reward mechanics.
