# Thinkwell

## Snapshot

Thinkwell is video courseware built on a specific bet: that a genuinely
great lecturer on video beats a textbook. Its homeschool line covers math
from 6th grade through Calculus, honors variants, AP-compatible courses
(Calculus AB and BC, Biology, Chemistry, Macro and Microeconomics), and
CLEP prep, all fronted by named academics rather than anonymous
narrators: math is taught by Dr. Edward Burger, an award-winning
mathematician, AP Biology by Emmy-winning teacher George Wolfe, and AP
Chemistry by professors Gordon Yee and Dean Harman. Its center of
gravity among homeschoolers is exactly where parents are weakest:
precalculus, calculus, and AP sciences. Courses are self-paced 12-month
subscriptions with auto-graded everything, and the brand's reputation
runs college-prep serious; it is also used by gifted-and-talented
distance programs. Mindshare is niche but respected: the family that
finds Thinkwell is usually shopping for rigor by name.

## Reputation and rankings

Reviewer verdicts are consistently strong on instruction quality: Smarter
Learning Guide calls the courses comprehensive and approachable with
professional lectures, and aggregator write-ups note that Cathy Duffy and
Common Sense Education both highlight the rigor and clarity, with
Burger's on-camera charisma the single most-cited asset. Long-running
community reviews (homeschoolmath.net user reports, Well-Trained Mind
threads) recommend it for strong, motivated math students, including
gifted students accelerating through the sequence; one veteran reviewer
notes it was used within Johns Hopkins CTY's distance program. The
recurring criticisms: it moves fast and assumes prerequisites, practice
is drill-light compared to Saxon-family programs, struggling students
sink, and there is no physical textbook unless you buy the printed
companions. Trajectory: stable niche; the 2025-26 addition of an
AI-powered help layer (Studdy Buddy) and live tutoring add-ons shows
active investment.

## Pedagogy and method

Lecture-first, understanding-oriented teaching in a traditional course
structure. Burger's style, visible in the sample videos, is explanation
and why-it-works storytelling rather than bare procedure, delivered to
camera with worked examples alongside; courses are organized in
conventional chapter sequences with mastery checkpoints (chapter tests,
midterm, final) rather than spiral review. Assessment is automated:
drill-and-practice exercise sets with step-by-step answer feedback,
quizzes, and exams, all graded and recorded by the platform, with grade
reports and completion certificates available. Each course ships a
day-by-day lesson plan (36 to 38 weeks, roughly 30 to 45 minutes daily)
that families can follow or ignore since the whole thing is self-paced.
The new Studdy Buddy layer adds on-demand step-by-step help inside the
course. There is no religious content; science is standard academic
biology and chemistry.

## Structure and scope

The catalog, per the official store as of Jul 2026: 6th Grade Math, 7th
Grade Math, Prealgebra (8th grade math), Algebra 1, Geometry, Algebra 2,
Intermediate Algebra, College Algebra, Precalculus, and Calculus, plus
honors variants of core courses; AP-compatible Calculus AB, Calculus BC,
Biology, and Chemistry, plus Macroeconomics and Microeconomics; and CLEP
prep courses for College Algebra, Precalculus, and Calculus. Course
scale is substantial: Algebra 1 has 275+ video lessons, Precalculus 350+
(combining college algebra and trigonometry), Calculus 275+ covering
both Calculus I and II college semesters, AP-compatible Biology 380+.
Every course page publishes its full table of contents, and free
placement tests plus a free trial exist on the site. The AP-compatible
label is Thinkwell's own careful phrasing: content-aligned to AP exams,
not College Board audited, which matters for families whose transcript
needs the official AP course designation (see
`../../comparisons/high-school.md` on AP labeling).

## A lesson up close

A day is one lesson from the plan: the student watches a 5-to-20-minute
video of the professor teaching (in the math courses, an overhead camera
shows Burger's hand working problems while printed examples sit beside
the lecture frame), skims the illustrated notes for the subtopic, then
completes the auto-graded exercise set, typically a dozen or so problems
worked on paper with answers entered for instant right/wrong plus
step-by-step feedback. Section quizzes accumulate into chapter tests,
and the year closes with a midterm and final, all machine-graded. When
stuck, the student replays the relevant video segment or asks Studdy
Buddy; families who bought the live office-hours add-on can bring
questions to a human. The parent's job is reading the grade report. A
completed day produces worked paper, recorded scores, and about 30 to 45
minutes of math; reviewers note strong students often move faster than
the plan.

## Materials and visual design

There is no required print. The platform look is clean modern courseware:
lecture video, illustrated notes with key terms, exercise panes, and a
progress dashboard. The videos are professionally shot and personality
forward: Burger lectures direct to camera with humor, a sharp contrast
with the digital-chalkboard genre (Shormann, Khan) and closer to a
filmed college course. Optional printed companion books (two volumes for
Algebra 1 at $49.99 each, answer key $21.95, printed quizzes $32.99) and
printed color course notes for the sciences exist for families who want
paper. The aesthetic signal is university: named professors, tables of
contents, midterms, certificates.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026, from the official store: core math courses $169 per
student (Algebra 1 page), Precalculus $199, Calculus $199, AP-compatible
Biology and Chemistry $199 each. A subscription is 12 months of access
that starts at activation, not purchase, and can be extended for $19.95
per month. Add-ons: live office hours and tutoring support $39, printed
companions as listed above. Everything essential (videos, exercises,
tests, grading, e-book notes, Studdy Buddy) is in the base price. There
is a free trial, and money-back terms are published per course.
Third-party reviews still quote $125 to $189; the store figures above
are current. Price pages: https://www.thinkwellhomeschool.com/collections/all.

## Why families choose it

The teacher, by name: Burger's lectures are the product, and families
buy them the way they would pick a professor. Rigor with independence:
college-prep depth that runs itself, at half the price of live-taught
outsourcing like Mr. D Math (`mr-d-math.md`). The upper end of the
catalog: precalculus, calculus, and AP sciences are exactly the courses
parents refuse to teach, and Thinkwell covers them credibly, including
for accelerated middle schoolers. And the AP and CLEP lanes: a
homeschooler can prep the exams without a co-op or an online academy.

## Why families leave it

Struggling math students: the pace and drill-light practice assume a
capable, motivated learner, and families with a drowning kid step down
to Teaching Textbooks (`../math/teaching-textbooks.md`) or a live
teacher. Repetition-needing kids: some families bolt on worksheets or
switch to spiral programs. Screen and independence limits: like all
courseware, it depends on a teen who will actually watch and work.
Accountability seekers move to Mr. D Math or Derek Owens
(`../math/derek-owens.md`) for a human in the loop. And transcript
purists note AP-compatible is not College Board authorized, pushing
some families to audited providers for the official course label.

## Competitive notes for Homa

Thinkwell marks the top of the self-serve rigor market: what a family
buys when they want college-prep depth without a schedule or a school.
Homa should know its vocabulary (AP-compatible versus authorized, CLEP
prep, honors variants, subscription activation windows) and treat a
Thinkwell family as one that has already outsourced instruction but
kept record-keeping: the gap Homa fills is stitching Thinkwell's grade
reports into the child's whole picture and translating course
completion into transcript lines and credits. Two patterns worth
studying: the named-teacher trust model (families trust a person, not
a platform; Homa's voice should feel like a person), and the
activation-when-ready subscription (respects homeschool calendars;
any Homa time-boxed feature should do the same). The AP-compatible
phrasing is also a preview of a conversation Homa will have often:
what counts as an official AP course, and when the label matters
(answered in `../../comparisons/high-school.md`).
