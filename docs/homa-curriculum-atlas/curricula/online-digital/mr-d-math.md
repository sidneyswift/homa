# Mr. D Math

## Snapshot

Mr. D Math is the personality-brand math outsource for homeschool middle
and high school: live and self-paced online courses from Pre-Algebra
through Calculus, built and fronted by Dennis DiNoia ("Mr. D"), a
Florida teacher in classrooms since 1988 whose pitch is math "in a
language students understand." The program is WASC-accredited as a
supplementary education provider, NCAA-approved for its core live math
sequence, and has expanded into electives (American Sign Language,
economics, SAT/ACT bootcamps, digital music production). Its niche is
the family that hits the wall at algebra: parents who can homeschool
everything except upper math buy Mr. D as the math department, with
live weekly classes, recorded lessons, auto-graded assessments, and
standing help sessions. Since 2025 courses also come in an optional
gamified version with points and badges.

## Reputation and rankings

- Community standing is strong and specific: the recurring praise is
  math-anxious kids recovering confidence, and reviewers score it
  highly (a 2026 homeschoolpicks review rates it 4.7/5 and calls it
  one of the best outsourced options for accountability-needing
  students, [homeschoolpicks](https://homeschoolpicks.com/curriculum-reviews/math-curriculum/mr-d-math-review/)).
- The consistent criticism is price: per course per year it costs
  several times a self-paced family program (CTC Math, Teaching
  Textbooks), which stings for multi-kid families
  ([homeschoolpicks](https://homeschoolpicks.com/curriculum-reviews/math-curriculum/mr-d-math-review/)).
- Personality risk cuts both ways: DiNoia's teaching style is the
  product; kids who click with him thrive, kids who do not have no
  alternate voice inside the program.
- Trajectory: stable and expanding sideways into electives; live class
  registration cycles fill seasonally (2026-27 registration opened
  March 9, 2026 per the homepage).

## Pedagogy and method

Conceptual-explanatory video teaching with human backup. DiNoia's
method emphasizes the why behind procedures and test-taking calm;
courses follow a traditional sequence with computer-graded quizzes,
spiral reviews, tests, and exams built in
([live class catalog](https://mrdmath.edu20.org/visitor_class_catalog/category/309269)).
Two delivery modes ([FAQ](https://mrdmath.com/FAQ)):

- Live: one 55-minute Zoom class weekly (August to May, 34 weeks),
  recorded for replay, homework due before the next session, three
  extra live help sessions per week, daily email support.
- Self-paced: the same embedded video instruction, start anytime,
  15-month access, pace chart provided, two weekly live help sessions.

Assessment is auto-graded inside the platform; parents get weekly
progress emails and do not grade. Instructors beyond DiNoia are
certified in the house methodology via a 2-year training program
([mrdmath.com](https://mrdmath.com/)).

## Structure and scope

- Math ladder: Preparing for Pre-Algebra, Pre-Algebra, Algebra I,
  Geometry, Algebra II, Pre-Calculus with Trigonometry, Calculus I,
  plus Consumer Math (project-based, grades 10-12)
  ([course collection](https://mrdmath.myshopify.com/collections/math-courses),
  [Numa review](https://numaschool.com/learn/glossary/mr-d-math)).
- NCAA approval covers the live Algebra I, Geometry, Algebra II, and
  Pre-Calculus courses ([live catalog](https://mrdmath.edu20.org/visitor_class_catalog/category/309269)).
- Electives: American Sign Language (multiple levels), economics,
  American English grammar, comparative literature, digital music
  production, SAT/ACT bootcamps, College and Career Readiness.
- Each course is a year (34 weeks) of weekly lessons; every course
  ships in gamified and non-gamified variants.
- WASC accreditation applies to Mr. D Math as a supplementary program:
  useful transcript credibility, but the parent still issues the
  transcript in most homeschool arrangements.

## A lesson up close

A self-paced week: the student watches the lesson video (20-30
minutes, DiNoia working problems and talking through reasoning), does
the assignment set, takes the auto-graded quiz, and hits a live Zoom
help session if stuck. A live-class week adds the 55-minute session
where DiNoia or a trained instructor teaches interactively, cold-calls
gently, and works student questions; recordings cover absences.
Work happens on paper beside the screen; grading and record-keeping
happen automatically; the parent's job reduces to reading the weekly
progress email. What a semester produces is a computed grade a parent
can put on a transcript, plus (the fans' claim) a kid who stopped
saying they are bad at math.

## Materials and visual design

No print program: video, PDFs, and the learning platform (an LMS
skin). Production is teacher-at-whiteboard practical rather than
cinematic; the appeal is DiNoia's delivery, not visual polish. The
gamified variant adds points, badges, and level-ups to the same
content. The aesthetic signal is "a real teacher you hired," which is
exactly the purchase.

## Pricing

- As of Jul 2026, retail: self-paced courses $247 (Consumer Math
  $217); live courses are $247 curriculum plus $290 live instruction,
  $537 total, with a $34/mo x 10 plan for the live portion
  ([live catalog](https://mrdmath.edu20.org/visitor_class_catalog/category/309269),
  [course store](https://mrdmath.myshopify.com/collections/math-courses)).
- Charter-school pricing runs slightly lower ($217 self-paced, $507
  live total) through purchase orders
  ([charter terms](https://mrdmath.myshopify.com/pages/charter-school-terms-and-conditions)).
- Self-paced access lasts 15 months; a $25 transfer fee extends an
  expired course one more year ([FAQ](https://mrdmath.com/FAQ)).
- Sibling and returning-student discounts exist on request; help
  sessions, recordings, and grading are included, no add-on fees.
- Note the sourcing spread: third-party reviews quote $197-$497
  depending on year and mode; the figures above are the official
  2026-27 store and catalog prices.

## Why families choose it

- The algebra wall: the parent cannot or will not teach upper math,
  and this is the trusted human-taught outsource.
- Accountability without school: weekly live deadlines, a teacher who
  notices, help sessions with a person.
- Math-anxiety repair: the brand's core testimonial genre.
- Transcript strength for high school: accredited provider, NCAA
  approval, computed grades.

## Why families leave it

- Price per course per year, especially with multiple kids; families
  step down to CTC Math, Teaching Textbooks, or Khan Academy free.
- Teacher-fit mismatch: DiNoia's style is the product; no click, no
  product.
- Kids who need in-person or hands-on teaching rather than any video.
- Scheduling: live classes reintroduce fixed weekly times some
  homeschool families specifically avoid.
- Completion drift in self-paced mode when the 15-month window
  outlasts motivation.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Mr. D is the pattern proof that homeschool parents pay hundreds per
  subject to outsource what they fear teaching, while keeping overall
  control. Homa is not a math teacher, but it should know this
  category cold: when a family says the parent dreads algebra, the
  correct move may be recommending an outsource like this and then
  organizing around it, which builds more trust than pretending Homa
  covers it.
- Stack behavior: a Mr. D family typically runs eclectic everything
  else. Their gap is coherence: math lives in Mr. D's LMS, everything
  else lives elsewhere. Homa's record and progress views should ingest
  outsourced-course evidence (weekly progress emails, grades) so the
  child's picture stays whole.
- Vocabulary to know: live vs self-paced, the $247/$290 split, help
  sessions, pace chart, gamified vs non-gamified, NCAA approval,
  the 15-month access window.
- High school lesson for Homa's later grades: accreditation and
  transcript mechanics start mattering intensely at grade 9. The
  atlas's high school wave (see backlog) should treat Mr. D as the
  template for how single-subject providers win that trust.
