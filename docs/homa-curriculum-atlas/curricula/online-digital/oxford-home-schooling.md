# Oxford Home Schooling

## Snapshot

Oxford Home Schooling is the under-18 arm of the Oxford Open Learning
Trust, a not-for-profit UK distance learning provider founded by Dr Nick
Smith that has served home learners for around 35 years. It sells
per-subject courses for Key Stage 3 (ages 11-14, unexamined), GCSE and
International GCSE, and A level: written course materials with
activities, self-assessment, and assignments, supported by a bank of 200+
PGCE or B.Ed qualified tutors and an exams office that helps families
find private-candidate exam centres. The model recently shifted from
one-off course fees (legacy tables: 325 GBP per KS3 year, from 395 GBP
per GCSE) to a subscription from 14.99 GBP per month per subject with a
six-month minimum, optional printed packs, and unbundled extras (tutor
marking, 40 GBP per hour tuition, mock marking). It recommends about 200
study hours per GCSE over 9-18 months. Against Wolsey Hall Oxford
(`wolsey-hall-oxford.md`, no relation) it is the budget flexible option:
less wrap-around support, materials-first, secondary ages only.

## Reputation and rankings

- Trustpilot 4.7 across 227 reviews as of Jul 2026, with praise for
  tutors and course clarity; the trust's own marketing cites GCSE maths
  pass rates well above the national distance-learning average
  (publisher claim) ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/oxfordhomeschooling.co.uk)).
- Long-standing name recognition in UK home education forums as one of
  the established correspondence-lineage providers, alongside NEC (the
  1963 charity) and Wolsey Hall; no US or Cathy Duffy presence.
- Its honest exam-cost guidance is unusually direct for the sector: board
  entry fees of about 48-54 GBP per subject plus centre admin fees from
  25 to 300 GBP, which the atlas treats as the reference numbers for UK
  private-candidate costs ([GCSE course page](https://existingcustomers.oxfordhomeschooling.co.uk/course/gcse/)).
- Trajectory: stable; the subscription pivot signals price-pressure
  response as cheaper online GCSE providers multiply. A fuller profile
  should verify how tutor marking is packaged in the new model.
