# Euka Future Learning

## Snapshot

Euka Future Learning (formerly Complete Education Australia) is Australia's
largest online homeschool curriculum provider: a secular, all-subjects K-12
program aligned to the Australian Curriculum, written directly to the
student so the parent does not have to teach. It was founded and is led by
Ellen Brown, a mother of five and former teacher who has become the
Australian media's go-to homeschooling commentator (Channel 9, ABC, Sky
News). Euka's growth rides Australia's homeschool boom, and its own
enrolment data is the headline: one in three new Euka students now arrives
because of bullying, up from one in five five years ago (Brown on the
Today Show, 2026-04-21). The product's other defining feature is
compliance-as-a-service: paid add-ons that generate the registration
paperwork Australia's state regimes require. Grades 11-12 offer an
"Assessed/University Pathway" that routes around the ATAR entirely via
university partnerships. Mindshare inside Australia is dominant for
structured secular homeschooling; outside Australia it is nearly zero.

## Reputation and rankings

- No Cathy Duffy or US award presence; its reputation lives in Australian
  channels. ProductReview.com.au shows 4.1 of 5 across 207 reviews, 77%
  positive, with customer service the most-mentioned theme in both
  directions ([ProductReview](https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/euka-future-learning)).
- Fans praise the simplicity: everything planned, "easy to follow layout",
  automated reporting that satisfies state reviewers.
- The critical themes are consistent: no phone support (a recurring anger
  point), repetitive busywork, screen-heavy delivery, technical glitches,
  term access that expires even if the child finishes early, and poor fit
  for some neurodivergent learners. Australian community write-ups report
  "high abandonment rates within the first term" for school-styled kids
  who refuse the format ([Homeschool Start Guide](https://homeschoolstartguide.com/blog/alternatives-to-euka-homeschool-australia)).
- One scathing senior-years review disputes the university pathway's value,
  reporting universities that did not recognise it; treat the pathway as
  real but partnership-specific, not universally recognised.
- Trajectory: rising with the market. NSW registrations doubled 2019-2024
  and Queensland grew fastest of all states, and Euka is the default
  structured option that growth flows into.

## Pedagogy and method

Euka is school-at-home, digitised and de-parented. Lessons are written to
the student in a direct-instruction style with weekly plans laid out per
subject; the parent's designed role is supervisor and marker of record,
not teacher. Marketing emphasises critical and creative thinking over rote
work, but the observed texture in reviews is structured daily modules with
worksheets, and the busywork complaint is the shadow side of the
everything-planned promise. There is no live teaching. Senior years (11-12)
split into two pathways: Assessment Free (self-marked, maximum autonomy)
and Assessed/University (teacher-marked assessments, one per subject per
term, resubmission allowed, feeding an Academic Assessment Transcript).
Mathspace membership is bundled for maths practice. The system is
self-paced within a term-access window: 90 days per term or 365 days per
year of platform access, which is a subscription clock, not a mastery
gate.

## Structure and scope

- Foundation through Grade 12, all core Australian Curriculum subjects,
  with electives added in secondary; Grade 10 includes work-pathway
  connections (apprenticeships, traineeships) alongside academics.
- Enrolment is per grade level per term or year. State-specific course
  pages (NSW, Queensland, and so on) present the same program mapped to
  each state's registration context.
- Compliance add-ons, as of Jul 2026: Government Registration Service (199
  AUD) prepares the state registration application; Premium Reporting (249
  AUD full year, 67 AUD per term) generates the ongoing evidence
  reporting. A 349 AUD bundle covers both
  ([Euka help centre](https://help.euka.edu.au/en/articles/11936661-upgrade-your-single-term-enrolment-to-a-full-year-and-save)).
- Grade 11-12 university pathway: partnerships and pathway arrangements
  with university colleges (Macquarie, UniSA, Adelaide, UWA, Tasmania,
  state TAFEs and others) where students enter a one-year diploma that
  credits as first year of a degree, no ATAR required
  ([pathways article](https://help.euka.edu.au/en/articles/9698536-university-options-with-euka-partnerships-pathways)).

## A lesson up close

The student logs into the Euka platform and opens the current week for a
subject. Lessons are presented as sequenced onscreen instruction written
to the child, with activities and printable worksheets attached; the
child works through the day's modules mostly independently, and the
parent checks completed work. In the primary years this still requires a
nearby adult; by design the secondary years run solo. Assessment depends
on tier: in the standard K-10 program parents observe and keep samples
(or pay for Premium Reporting to formalise them); in the senior assessed
pathway the student submits one assessment per subject per term for
teacher marking with feedback and resubmission. A completed day produces
checked-off modules and worksheet output. The recurring failure mode in
reviews is a child who finds the loop repetitive and disengages, and a
fast child can finish a term's content weeks before the 90-day access
clock runs out, which families discover at upgrade time.

## Materials and visual design

Fully digital: platform lessons plus printable PDF worksheets, no shipped
box. The visual language is bright, contemporary Australian edtech,
friendly rather than institutional. Worksheets are functional print
pages. The aesthetic signal is "modern school, minus the school", which
is exactly the buyer Euka targets: a family leaving a classroom quickly
(bullying, school refusal, anxiety) that wants continuity and legitimacy
rather than a homeschool philosophy. Screen-heaviness is the most common
aesthetic complaint, especially for the primary years.

## Pricing

- Full year paid upfront, as of Jul 2026: 697 AUD for Foundation-Grade 6,
  829 AUD for Grades 7-10 (roughly 460-550 USD). Single term: 279 AUD
  with a 30-day window to upgrade to the year rate
  ([help centre pricing](https://help.euka.edu.au/en/articles/11936661-upgrade-your-single-term-enrolment-to-a-full-year-and-save),
  [Grade 10 page](https://euka.edu.au/all-courses/grade-10-nsw/)).
- Add-ons: Government Registration Service 199 AUD, Premium Reporting 249
  AUD per year or 67 AUD per term, both-services bundle 349 AUD.
- Mathspace membership included (valued by Euka at 120 AUD per year).
- Per child, no meaningful sibling discount reported, so multi-child
  families pay roughly 700-830 AUD each plus add-ons, a recurring cost
  criticism.
- Access expires with the enrolment window; there is nothing to resell.

## Why families choose it

- Crisis exit velocity: a bullied or school-refusing child can leave
  school and be "doing school" at home within days, with the registration
  paperwork handled for 199 AUD.
- The parent does not have to teach, plan, or design anything.
- Secular by default in a market where much homeschool material is
  Christian.
- State-registration confidence: ACARA-aligned scope and auto-generated
  reports that registration reviewers recognise.
- A no-ATAR route to university that lowers the stakes of leaving school.

## Why families leave it

- The child refuses the format: screen-heavy, repetitive daily modules
  that feel like the school they just left. First-term abandonment is the
  community's most-repeated warning.
- Busywork over depth, thin literature strand, inflexible pacing for
  uneven learners.
- Service friction: no phone line, unresponsive support stories, refund
  and access-window disputes.
- Cost per child for multi-kid families versus assembling their own stack.
- Destinations: My Homeschool or Simply Homeschool for offline and gentler
  structure (see `../all-in-one/my-homeschool.md`), state distance
  education schools for teacher-taught legitimacy, or eclectic
  self-assembly.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Euka proves compliance is a purchasable product: families pay 199-349
  AUD per year on top of curriculum for registration paperwork and
  evidence reports. That is Homa's records engine sold as line items, and
  it prices the willingness to pay for exactly the burden Homa automates.
- The bullying-intake statistic is the sharpest crisis-onboarding evidence
  in any market this atlas covers: one in three arrivals, and the product
  wins by being fastest to "school is handled". Homa's crisis onboarding
  should match that speed-to-stability feeling.
- The churn pattern repeats the US courseware story (see
  `../../comparisons/online-digital.md`): relief entry, busywork boredom,
  first-term exit. What the family keeps is nothing; records live inside
  the subscription. A portable, family-owned record is the same unserved
  pain in Australia as in the US.
- Vocabulary for an Australian family: grades are Foundation and Years,
  the reference standard is ACARA (with NSW and other state syllabuses
  distinct), registration is per state with renewal reviews, and "distance
  education" means a government school program, not homeschooling. Homa
  must never conflate the last two; families are touchy about it.
- If Homa expands to Australia, the wedge is not curriculum (Euka and My
  Homeschool own that shelf) but the plan-evidence-renewal loop mapped to
  each state's forms, plus depth supplements for the busywork gap.
