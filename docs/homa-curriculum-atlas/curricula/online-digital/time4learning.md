# Time4Learning

## Snapshot

Time4Learning is the default online all-subjects homeschool courseware in the
US: a secular, subscription-based PreK-12 curriculum that teaches, practices,
quizzes, and grades the child through the browser while the parent watches a
dashboard. John Edelson founded it in 2004; Cambium Learning Group (Lexia,
Learning A-Z, ExploreLearning) acquired it in 2018, and Edelson retired in
early 2023 with Cambium executive Cheryl Dodge succeeding him as president.
It claims hundreds of thousands of student users and is the program most
"can homeschooling even work for us?" parents trial first, because it is
month-to-month, requires no teaching, and costs about what a streaming bundle
does. It is a curriculum provider, not a school: no accreditation, no
transcripts, no diploma; the parent stays teacher of record. For Homa it is
the single most important price anchor: parents comparing subscriptions will
put Homa's monthly price next to Time4Learning's $39.95 (see
[08-segments-curricula](../../../homa-planning/icp-research/08-segments-curricula.md) §4).

## Reputation and rankings

- Cathy Duffy lists Time4Learning among her 102 Top Picks, and reviewers
  note it is one of the few comprehensive online programs designed for
  homeschool families rather than as a school replacement
  ([howtohomeschool.com directory](https://www.howtohomeschool.com/curriculum-directory/online-courses/time4learning),
  [homeschoolpicks review](https://homeschoolpicks.com/curriculum-reviews/time4learning-review/)).
- Other recognitions the publisher promotes: homeschool.com curriculum
  awards (high school category), the National Parenting Center Seal of
  Approval, iParenting Media Award, and IBCCES Certified Autism Resource
  designation ([Modulo 2026 review](https://www.modulo.app/all-resources/time4learning-2026-review),
  [howtohomeschool.com](https://www.howtohomeschool.com/curriculum-directory/online-courses/time4learning)).
- Community sentiment is sharply two-sided. The pro side: it saves the
  marriage-with-homeschooling for working and burned-out parents because it
  runs itself. The critical side, common on Reddit and homeschool forums:
  lessons get called boring, repetitive, and babyish past the early grades.
  One representative forum verbatim: "T4L is VERY babyish- 4th & 6th graders
  were bored to tears in the 1st week" ([Homeschool Base](https://homeschoolbase.com/curriculum/time4learning)).
- Trustpilot carries recurring complaints about refused refunds on annual
  plans and, in 2026, about a rocky platform transition: "This year they
  went AI based and it's absurd," with parents reporting inaccurate reports
  and grading they cannot inspect ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/time4learning.com)).
- Trajectory: stable-to-defensive. It retains its default-option status and
  ESA purchasability, but 2025-2026 price increases and platform churn have
  given loyal customers reasons to shop, and newer competitors (Miacademy)
  are the named destination in several public complaints.

## Pedagogy and method

Time4Learning is instructional-design courseware, not a named pedagogy. Its
lineage is standards-aligned public school scope and sequence delivered as
multimedia: each lesson introduces a concept, practices it with support,
has the student work independently, then assesses with an auto-graded quiz,
with chapter tests rolling up ([Eaton Academic guide](https://eatonacademic.com/guide-to-time4learning-homeschool-curriculum-for-parents)).
The system is self-paced rather than strictly mastery-gated: students can
retake assessments and the lowest score can be dropped from reports, but
nothing forces mastery before advancing ([Cinema Series FAQ](https://www.time4learning.com/pages/lp-cinema-series)).
Grade placement is flexible per subject (a child can do grade 3 math and
grade 4 language arts), which reviewers consistently list as a top strength.
The parent's role is administrator: choose courses, optionally build an
Activity Planner schedule, review the dashboard. No teaching, no grading.
Math draws Common Core style method complaints from parents who dislike
that approach ([Homeschool Base](https://homeschoolbase.com/curriculum/time4learning)).
Assessment philosophy is quiz-heavy and mostly multiple choice, a
recurring criticism for reading comprehension and writing
([Brighterly reviews roundup](https://brighterly.com/blog/time4learning-reviews/)).

## Structure and scope

- Two membership bands, same price: Preschool to 5th grade and 6th to 12th,
  each including up to seven courses per student; middle and high schoolers
  can add extra courses at $5 per course per month
  ([pricing page](https://www.time4learning.com/pages/membership-pricing-discounts),
  [FAQ](https://www.time4learning.com/pages/faq)).
- Core subjects at every level: math, language arts, science, social
  studies. Electives vary by band (art, music, foreign language; middle
  school allows up to 3 electives). Time4MathFacts (fact fluency practice)
  is included for PreK-5. Time4Languages, powered by Rosetta Stone, is an
  add-on ([homeschool.com review](https://www.homeschool.com/productreviews/time4learning/)).
- Lessons are organized as chapters and activities within each course; the
  parent dashboard exposes full lesson plans, answer keys, quizzes, and
  tests for preview ([Cinema Series FAQ](https://www.time4learning.com/pages/lp-cinema-series)).
- The middle and high school curriculum is being replaced by the in-house
  "Cinema Series," film-studio-produced video courses; replaced legacy
  courses get archived, with only their scope and sequence remaining
  accessible ([Cinema Series page](https://www.time4learning.com/pages/lp-cinema-series)).
- High school includes lesson plans, automated grading, reports, and
  curriculum customization, but no accredited credits: parents assemble
  their own transcript from the reports ([Modulo](https://www.modulo.app/all-resources/time4learning-2026-review)).

## A lesson up close

From the child's seat: log in to the student dashboard, open today's list
(either the automated sequence or what the parent staged in the Activity
Planner), click the activity card. An elementary lesson opens as an
animated segment, cartoon characters and songs at the K-2 level, and the
animation cannot be skipped or shut off, a documented irritant for older
kids ([Homeschool Base](https://homeschoolbase.com/curriculum/time4learning)).
Middle and high school lessons in the Cinema Series shift to filmed,
lecture-style instruction with on-screen text and graphics
([Cinema Series page](https://www.time4learning.com/pages/lp-cinema-series),
[The AI Homeschooler review](https://theaihomeschooler.com/time4learning-homeschool-curriculum-review/)).
After instruction comes interactive practice (click, drag, select), then a
short auto-graded quiz, mostly multiple choice. Scores post instantly to
the parent dashboard. Many lessons attach a printable worksheet so some
work can happen on paper; reviewers advise using them to offset screen
fatigue and add handwriting practice. A completed day produces a row of
green checkmarks and updated grades, but no physical artifact unless the
parent prints. Parents preview any lesson and its answer key through
Lesson Planning and Reports. Sessions are meant to be short; families
typically run math and language arts daily, science and social studies a
few times a week ([Modulo](https://www.modulo.app/all-resources/time4learning-2026-review)).
From the parent's seat, the day is checking the dashboard: what got
finished, what the quiz scores were, what to reassign.

## Materials and visual design

There is no box: the product is a website plus printables. The visual
language of the elementary tier is bright, rounded, animated-cartoon
courseware in the early-2010s edtech style; the recurring parent adjectives
are "engaging" from fans and "babyish" and "distracting" from critics on
the same screens ([Homeschool Base](https://homeschoolbase.com/curriculum/time4learning)).
The Cinema Series rebrand is a deliberate aesthetic move upmarket for
grades 6-12: professionally filmed instructors, story-driven course
framing, a more streaming-service look ([Cinema Series page](https://www.time4learning.com/pages/lp-cinema-series)).
Printable worksheets are utilitarian black-and-white PDF practice pages,
not designed artifacts. The parent dashboard reads as an admin tool:
planners, reports, grade tables, printable report PDFs that double as
portfolio evidence for state compliance ([homeschoolpicks](https://homeschoolpicks.com/curriculum-reviews/time4learning-review/)).
What the aesthetic signals: school, digitized. Families seeking a
school-at-home experience read it as legitimacy; Charlotte Mason and
low-screen families read the same signals as everything they left.

## Pricing

- $39.95 per month per student, every grade band PK-12, as of Jul 2026
  ([official pricing page](https://www.time4learning.com/pages/membership-pricing-discounts)).
- Annual plan discount of 30% (about $336 per student per year); family
  discount on additional students (official copy says up to 25%; 2026
  price guides quote $27.97 per additional student, about 30% off)
  ([pricing page](https://www.time4learning.com/pages/membership-pricing-discounts),
  [The AI Homeschooler pricing guide](https://theaihomeschooler.com/time4learning-pricing-2026/)).
- Extra middle/high courses $5 per course per month; Time4Languages is a
  separate monthly add-on ([FAQ](https://www.time4learning.com/pages/faq)).
- 14-day money-back guarantee on monthly billing; Trustpilot complaints
  cluster around annual-plan refund refusals, so the fine print matters
  ([Brighterly cost guide](https://brighterly.com/blog/time4learning-cost/),
  [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/time4learning.com)).
- Price history matters for sentiment: older reviews still quote $24.95
  elementary and $34.95 high school; the move to a flat $39.95 landed as a
  visible increase for loyal customers ([homeschoolpicks](https://homeschoolpicks.com/curriculum-reviews/time4learning-review/),
  [Brighterly](https://brighterly.com/blog/time4learning-cost/)).
- ESA and scholarship funds can cover it; the pricing page markets this
  directly ([pricing page](https://www.time4learning.com/pages/membership-pricing-discounts)).
- Nothing to resell: subscription access ends at cancellation, and access
  cuts off at the end of the billing cycle.

## Why families choose it

- It runs the school day without the parent: lessons taught, graded, and
  logged automatically. The buyer logic is relief, especially for working
  parents, single parents, and crisis-onboarding families leaving school
  mid-year.
- Month-to-month, no contract, cheap enough to trial without ceremony.
- Grade-flexible per subject, so an uneven learner is not locked to one
  grade across the board.
- Automated reports double as compliance evidence in portfolio states.
- Secular by default, which the Christian-dominated print market often
  is not.

## Why families leave it

- Boredom and repetition: the single most repeated churn theme. Kids
  tire of the lesson-quiz loop; parents report the structure gets
  predictable ("boring and repetitive" is the recurring Reddit phrasing
  per [Brighterly's review mining](https://brighterly.com/blog/time4learning-reviews/)).
- Age fit: widely liked for K-2, increasingly called babyish and shallow
  from about grade 3 up; families report older kids "bored to tears"
  ([Homeschool Base](https://homeschoolbase.com/curriculum/time4learning)).
- Screen-time guilt: one blogger's cancellation story ends "Quitting
  Time4Learning was one of the best homeschooling decisions I made"
  after five months of a first grader hating screen school
  ([Queen of the Household](https://www.queenofthehousehold.com/time4learning-review/)).
- Depth doubts: multiple-choice-heavy assessment, thin writing
  instruction, high school less rigorous than textbook programs per
  reviewer consensus ([homeschoolpicks](https://homeschoolpicks.com/curriculum-reviews/time4learning-review/)).
- Platform trust wobbles: the 2026 "went AI based" transition complaints
  (inaccurate reports, opaque grading, parents unable to see answers),
  plus older reports of progress records being lost
  ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/time4learning.com),
  [Brighterly](https://brighterly.com/blog/time4learning-reviews/)).
- Common destinations: Miacademy (named in Trustpilot complaints as the
  switch), Power Homeschool/Acellus for older kids, or off courseware
  entirely into an assembled eclectic stack, the documented
  school-at-home-to-eclectic drift
  ([08-segments-curricula](../../../homa-planning/icp-research/08-segments-curricula.md) §7 card 6).

## Competitive notes for Homa

- The price anchor. Homa at $29-49/mo prices directly against $39.95
  Time4Learning, and the repo's segment research already states the
  consequence: Homa's differentiation must be that it is not courseware
  that teaches the child, it is an assistant that serves the parent's own
  stack ([08-segments-curricula](../../../homa-planning/icp-research/08-segments-curricula.md) §4).
  Expect "why would I pay more than Time4Learning?" verbatim in sales
  conversations; the answer is category difference, not feature depth.
- The churn pattern is Homa's acquisition moment. Families leave T4L at
  the boredom-and-guilt point, usually holding a pile of dashboard data
  and a vague sense the kid is behind on writing. Homa should know how to
  ingest that story: which grade band they used, that reports exist and
  can be exported as PDFs, that writing is the usual gap to shore up.
- Vocabulary to know when a family says they use it: Activity Planner
  (the parent-built schedule), the two membership bands (PK-5, 6-12),
  Cinema Series (new 6-12 courses), Time4MathFacts, chapter tests,
  the archived-courses migration. Levels are plain US grade levels.
- Supplements it typically needs: writing instruction and feedback,
  handwriting, read-aloud and real-book reading culture, hands-on science.
  Homa materials that pair with T4L should be paper-first on purpose, the
  antidote to its screen fatigue, not more screen.
- Materials pattern worth learning: automated reports that double as
  state portfolio evidence are a loved feature; Homa's evidence artifacts
  should be at least that effortless.
- What not to copy: the unskippable animation, the quiz-only assessment
  loop, and the platform-migration trust damage. The 2026 backlash shows
  parents notice when grading logic becomes opaque ("AI-based") and react
  by leaving; Homa's provenance-and-parent-control posture is the direct
  counter.
