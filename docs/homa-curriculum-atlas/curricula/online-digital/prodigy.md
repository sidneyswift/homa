# Prodigy

## Snapshot

Prodigy is a free-to-play fantasy RPG (grades 1-8) where battling
monsters requires answering adaptive math questions; a separate Prodigy
English exists. Prodigy Education reports over 100 million student
registrations, driven by school classroom adoption that spills into home
accounts. The core curriculum-aligned math practice is genuinely free
with no time limits; paid memberships ($9.95-19.95/mo, $58.95-118.95/yr)
sell cosmetic gear, pets, and game areas, not additional teaching. In
homeschool stacks it is a motivation layer and fact-practice treat, not
a curriculum: it reinforces but does not teach new concepts.

## Reputation and rankings

- Engagement reputation is real: kids who fight worksheets will
  voluntarily play Prodigy, and weekly parent report emails show skills
  practiced ([Modern Parent Zone 2026 review](https://themodernparentzone.com/2026/04/05/prodigy-math-game-review-2026-is-the-premium-plan-worth-it-for-your-child/)).
- The standing critique is aggressive monetization aimed at children:
  free players see constant member-perk reminders, Common Sense
  Education scores it 3/5 noting the math problems just interrupt the
  adventure, and a 2021 FTC complaint alleged the home version showed
  up to four times as many ads as math questions
  ([Homeschool Tools guide](https://homeschooltools.net/offering/prodigy-math)).
- Pedagogy critics note gameplay can overshadow the math, with little
  actual practice per session ([Homeschool Tools](https://homeschooltools.net/offering/prodigy-math),
  [Modulo review](https://www.modulo.app/all-resources/prodigymathgame)).
- Homa relevance in one line: Prodigy is the cleanest example of the
  reward-layer trap (game first, learning incidental) that Homa's
  observational, no-points posture defines itself against; treat a
  family's Prodigy time as engagement signal, not learning evidence.
