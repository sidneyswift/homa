# Khan Academy

## Snapshot

Khan Academy is the free juggernaut: a nonprofit platform of short video
lessons, practice exercises, and mastery tracking across K-12 math, science,
humanities, computing, and official SAT prep, serving on the order of 120
million learners a year with 7,000+ lessons and no ads or paywalls
([AnswersQ 2026 review](https://answersq.com/khan-academy-review/)). Sal
Khan founded it in 2008 out of tutoring videos for his cousins. In
homeschool life it functions less as a curriculum brand and more as
infrastructure: the universal free math spine, the backup explainer when
the paid curriculum is not clicking, and the reason "we can't afford to
homeschool" is a weaker objection than it used to be. Two extensions
matter for Homa's market: Khan Academy Kids (a genuinely free, ad-free
early-learning app for ages 2-8) and Khanmigo (the $4/mo GPT-4-based AI
tutor whose public engagement struggles are a load-bearing data point for
parent-facing AI products).

## Reputation and rankings

- Near-universal respect: Common Sense Media Editors' Choice; Cathy Duffy
  praises the K-8 math courses as easy to navigate and ideal for students
  who grasp concepts quickly or need to close gaps fast
  ([Homeschool Tools guide](https://homeschooltools.net/offering/khan-academy)).
- The homeschool-community consensus is "best free resource on the
  internet, use it deliberately": strong as a math spine or supplement,
  weak as a whole education ([OpenEd teacher review](https://opened.co/tools/khan-academy),
  [Homeschool Start Guide](https://homeschoolstartguide.com/blog/khan-academy-homeschool-curriculum)).
- Standing critiques: video-plus-problems monotony, limited cumulative
  review hurting retention, no writing instruction, no accountability
  ("Khan does not call a student out when they're not making progress,"
  [OpenEd](https://opened.co/tools/khan-academy)).
- Khanmigo's reputation is mixed and publicly so: praised for Socratic
  patience and the $4 price, criticized for refusing to just answer and
  for shallowness on hard problems. Sal Khan himself conceded "For a lot
  of students, it was a non-event. They just didn't use it much," and a
  major redesign is slated for the 2026-2027 school year
  ([AI-Learn Insights](https://ailearninsights.substack.com/p/is-khanmigo-a-dud),
  [05-competitor-user-voice](../../../homa-planning/icp-research/05-competitor-user-voice.md) §3).
- Trajectory: stable at massive scale, with the AI layer in active flux
  (a January 2026 Google partnership brings Gemini into its literacy
  tools, [CourseFacts](https://www.coursefacts.com/guides/khan-academy-review-2026)).

## Pedagogy and method

Mastery learning, systematized: each course is a ladder of skills with
practice levels (familiar, proficient, mastered), unit tests, and course
challenges; students advance by demonstrating competence, not by age.
Instruction is a 5-10 minute video (classically Sal's voice over a
digital blackboard), followed by 4-7 problem practice sets with hints and
instant feedback, gamified lightly with energy points and streaks
([Homeschool Tools](https://homeschooltools.net/offering/khan-academy)).
The pedagogy is procedural-conceptual middle ground: clear explanations,
limited discovery work, no manipulatives. The parent's designed role is
"coach": a parent or teacher account gets a dashboard of progress bars,
skill gaps, and assignment tools. In practice most homeschool parents
check it weekly rather than daily. Khanmigo layers a Socratic AI tutor
onto the same content: it guides rather than answers, which is both its
pedagogy and its adoption problem.

## Structure and scope

- Math is the flagship: a coherent PK-through-calculus sequence
  (arithmetic, pre-algebra, algebra, geometry, trig, stats, AP calc),
  organized by grade or by subject, with course challenges for placement.
- Science (biology, chemistry, physics, AP tiers), humanities (US and
  world history, government, art history), economics, computing
  (programming, CS principles), and official SAT/digital-SAT prep via
  College Board partnership ([CourseFacts](https://www.coursefacts.com/guides/khan-academy-review-2026)).
- Khan Academy Kids (separate app, ages 2-8): 5,000+ activities across
  early literacy, math, logic, and social-emotional learning, with
  read-aloud books, an adaptive path, printable worksheets, offline
  mode, and a teacher/classroom mode homeschoolers use to assign work
  ([Good Play Guide](https://www.goodplayguide.com/reviews/khan-academy-kids/),
  [Modern Parent Zone 2026 review](https://themodernparentzone.com/2026/04/05/khan-academy-kids-review-2026-still-the-best-free-learning-app-for-young-children/)).
- No accreditation, transcripts, or diplomas; parents keep records. A
  "course mastery" percentage is the de facto completion evidence.

## A lesson up close

A homeschool Khan session is 15-30 minutes: the student opens their
assigned course, watches a short video (often at 1.5x), then works a
practice set. Wrong answers offer hints that decompose the problem;
persistent misses drop the skill back a level. Math work happens on
scratch paper next to the keyboard; nothing about the screen page is
designed for print. Completing a skill fills a progress cell; unit tests
periodically re-verify. What a day produces is dashboard state, not
artifacts: no workbook page, nothing for the fridge or the portfolio
without screenshots. In Khan Academy Kids the session is shorter and
softer: a guided path of tappable games, animal characters, and
read-alouds, with the parent dashboard summarizing minutes and skills.

## Materials and visual design

Khan's visual identity is utilitarian edtech: clean white screens,
progress bars, the hand-drawn blackboard aesthetic of the classic
videos, newer studio-produced videos in some courses. It signals
seriousness and zero commercial pressure (no ads, no upsells beyond
Khanmigo), which is precisely why parents trust it. Khan Academy Kids
is the opposite register: soft pastel illustration, five animal mascots,
gentle audio design, widely considered among the best-crafted free
children's apps. Printables exist only in the Kids app ecosystem
(worksheet packs on the website); core Khan Academy produces no paper.

## Pricing

- Everything core is free: all courses, practice, dashboards, SAT prep,
  and Khan Academy Kids. Funded by philanthropy and donations.
- Khanmigo Family: $4/mo or $44/yr, up to 10 children per family
  account, chat-history visibility and moderation alerts for parents;
  free for verified US K-12 teachers; some states subsidize statewide
  access ([khanmigo.ai/pricing](https://khanmigo.ai/pricing),
  [05-competitor-user-voice](../../../homa-planning/icp-research/05-competitor-user-voice.md) §1).
- No consumables, no resale economics; the only cost is hardware and
  attention.

## Why families choose it

- Free and rigorous: it removes the cost barrier to a full math
  education, which for tight-budget families is decisive.
- Mastery placement: an uneven learner works at their true level per
  subject, ahead in one, behind in another, without stigma.
- Trustworthy screen time: nonprofit, ad-free, no dark patterns.
- The universal supplement: whatever the primary curriculum, Khan is
  the free second explanation and the gap-filler.
- SAT prep: the official free option, hard to argue against.

## Why families leave it

- Accountability drift: self-paced with no external pressure means
  low-drive kids quietly stall; parents discover weeks of motion
  without progress ([OpenEd](https://opened.co/tools/khan-academy)).
- Retention complaints: limited built-in cumulative review; families
  move to spiral programs (Saxon, CTC Math) when facts do not stick.
- Video fatigue and dryness: kids who need hands-on or human teaching
  disengage; families buy manipulative-based (Math-U-See) or live
  (Mr. D Math, Outschool) options.
- Writing and labs do not exist here, so "Khan as everything" families
  eventually rebuild a stack around it rather than leaving it entirely.
- Khanmigo-specific churn: the kid never opens it. Reviewer guidance
  is to cancel if unopened for 14 days
  ([05-competitor-user-voice](../../../homa-planning/icp-research/05-competitor-user-voice.md) §3).

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Khan is not a budget competitor (free) but a framing competitor: it
  anchors "good online learning costs nothing," which pressures every
  paid subscription including Homa's. Homa should never sell against
  Khan; it should assume Khan is in the family stack and add the layer
  Khan lacks: the parent-facing picture of the child across everything.
- Khanmigo is the cautionary tale for child-facing AI: even at $4 with
  the best brand in edtech, kids did not engage. This validates Homa's
  parent-first architecture and is worth citing in positioning
  ([05-competitor-user-voice](../../../homa-planning/icp-research/05-competitor-user-voice.md) §8).
- Vocabulary to know: mastery levels (familiar, proficient, mastered),
  course challenges (placement), unit tests, energy points, the parent
  vs teacher account distinction, Khan Academy Kids as a separate app,
  Khanmigo. "We do Khan for math" usually means it is the spine and the
  family needs writing, review structure, and evidence artifacts.
- Materials gap Homa fills directly: Khan produces no paper. A family
  living in Khan has no portfolio artifacts; Homa generating printable,
  state-ready evidence and offline practice from the same skill map is
  a concrete complement, not a rip-and-replace.
- The mastery-map idea (skills, progress states, placement by evidence)
  is the same shape as Homa's skill graph; Khan is the market's mental
  model for "progress map," useful when explaining Homa's Progress view.
