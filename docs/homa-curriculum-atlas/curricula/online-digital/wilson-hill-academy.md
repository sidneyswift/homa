# Wilson Hill Academy

## Snapshot

Wilson Hill Academy is the live online classical Christian school:
founded in 2014 by Bob and Nancy Donaldson of Austin, Texas (veterans of
brick-and-mortar classical schooling, including the board of Regents
School of Austin), it teaches discussion-driven, Great Books-centered
classes to thousands of students across all 50 states and more than 20
countries. Unlike Veritas Press, it publishes no curriculum and offers
one delivery format only: synchronous classes of roughly 15 students
meeting live twice a week for 90 minutes, taught by what it calls master
teachers. Accredited live courses run grades 3-12 (trivium-staged as
Grammar School 3-6, School of Logic 7-8, School of Rhetoric 9-12), and
the newer Forma program gives K-2 parents mentor-led homeschool cohorts.
The flagship is The Great Conversation, a six-year humanities sequence
integrating history, literature, and theology, and the school culture
(four student houses, an annual in-person family camp called LINK) is
as much of the product as the academics.

## Reputation and rankings

- Aggregated reviewer sentiment calls Wilson Hill a gold standard for
  live online classical education, with teacher caliber the most-cited
  strength; one veteran quoted moving from Veritas because "the teachers
  were the most excellent part of Veritas" and finding the same warmth
  and rigor at Wilson Hill.
- Student and parent long-form reviews are unusually affectionate: kids
  who call it "their school," inside-joke house culture, and the widely
  shared line that community was "the most precious and unexpected
  gift."
- The standing cautions: significant workload, a fixed twice-weekly
  schedule that constrains traveling and activity-heavy families, and
  per-course prices above most competitors (Veritas, Memoria, Schole).
  Reviewers position it as more demanding than Schole Academy's restful
  pace and more discussion-driven than Memoria's workbook tradition.
- Trajectory: rising within the classical surge; it has become the
  default "live classical school" answer in forums, with the Forma
  launch extending it downward into early elementary.

## Pedagogy and method

Classical, conversational, and confessional. The trivium stages the
program (wonder in grammar school, logical tools in the School of
Logic, persuasive rhetoric in the School of Rhetoric), and the teaching
method is live Socratic discussion of primary texts rather than lecture
or workbook: students are on mic and in the chat, and the school's
pitch quotes Dorothy Sayers on teaching students how to think. Subjects
are taught as an integrated whole under a biblical worldview
(truth, goodness, and beauty "rooted in the triune God of Scripture"),
which in practice means literature classes wrestle with theological
questions and science is framed as studying God's design. Teachers
grade all work and are hired globally for subject mastery; the parent's
role reduces to oversight and logistics. Assessment language is
mastery-flavored ("tested for mastery, not just for comprehension" per
one alumna), with essays, discussion, and exams graded by the teacher
toward a conventional transcript.

## Structure and scope

- Grades and schools: Forma (K-2, parent-mentored cohorts with weekly
  mentor meetings, reading, math, and nature study modules; not part of
  the accredited offering), Grammar School (3-6), School of Logic
  (7-8), School of Rhetoric (9-12).
- The Great Conversation (GC) 1-6: the signature humanities sequence
  from Homer through modernity, integrating history, literature, and
  theology; new upper-school students often enter through GC 1+4, a
  combined on-ramp.
- Full catalog across composition (Fundamentals of Academic Writing
  through Advanced Composition), math (through AP Calculus and AP
  Statistics), lab and AP sciences, Latin (through AP), Greek, Spanish,
  French, logic 1-2, rhetoric 1-2 with senior thesis, and electives
  (AP Art History, government and economics, literature electives).
- Format constants: live, twice weekly, 90 minutes, about 15 students,
  full school year (August to May), recommended courses of study
  published per school, plus AP and summer courses.
- Credentials: accredited by Cognia through SACS CASI, NCAA approved,
  member of ACCS and SCL. Full-time students follow the Graduation
  Track to a Wilson Hill diploma; a la carte enrollment is equally
  supported, with no application required.

## A lesson up close

A Great Conversation week: the student reads the assigned primary text
(say, a book of the Odyssey or a section of Augustine) before class,
then meets the section twice for 90 minutes of guided discussion,
where the teacher poses questions, students respond aloud and in chat,
and the thread of the conversation is the instruction. Written work
(essays, presentations, exams) is submitted and graded by the teacher
with feedback; grades accumulate toward the accredited transcript.
Around class time sits the social layer: house meetings, clubs like
Junior Classical League, and for many families the year points at LINK,
the end-of-year family camp with a formal banquet and commencement. The
parent's day-to-day job is schedule protection: two fixed 90-minute
blocks per course per week is the school rhythm re-entering the
homeschool, and multi-course students effectively keep school-day
hours.

## Materials and visual design

No house curriculum: courses assign real books (Great Books lists,
standard math and science texts) that families purchase, and the
classroom is a video-conference room. The design language of the school
is its brand and community artifacts more than page layouts: house
names, mascots, and colors; LINK; polished parent-facing guides. The
signal to the buyer is "a real private school that happens to meet
online," and the marketing leans on exactly that (the guide's stated
premise is that online is "on purpose," not a last resort).

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026: full-year courses run $750-1,200 each (the admissions
page's published range; individual prices sit in the registration
portal), with payment plans through FACTS and no stated hidden fees. A
five-course upper-school load therefore lands roughly $3,800-6,000 a
year before books, the most expensive single-provider path profiled in
this category, and the school's answer is teacher quality, small
sections, and accreditation. Forma cohorts are priced separately.
Multi-kid families pay per student per course; there is no family cap,
which is the sharpest economic contrast with subscription products
(SchoolhouseTeachers) and publisher self-paced lines (Veritas).

## Why families choose it

- Outsourcing the Great Books: seminar-style humanities most parents
  cannot lead, taught by teachers who visibly love the material.
- The school experience without leaving homeschooling: real classmates,
  houses, traditions, and an accredited diploma path.
- Teacher relationships: the recurring testimonial is teachers who know
  and disciple the student, not just grade them.
- College outcomes and credibility: accreditation, AP courses, NCAA
  approval, alumni at selective universities.

## Why families leave it

- Price: per-course costs 1.5-2x Veritas or Schole equivalents push
  multi-kid families toward cheaper academies or self-paced formats.
- Schedule rigidity: twice-weekly fixed sessions per course conflict
  with travel, sports, and the flexibility families homeschool for.
- Workload: discussion prep plus writing is heavy; students carrying
  jobs or intensive activities step down.
- Fit: kids who will not talk in a seminar get less from the format;
  quieter or younger students sometimes do better with recorded or
  self-paced options (Veritas Self-Paced, Memoria's videos).

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Wilson Hill proves the live cohort model's ceiling: families will pay
  private-school-adjacent money for teachers, peers, and belonging, not
  just instruction. The moat is community, the same lesson as Classical
  Conversations, executed online.
- The schedule is the integration point: a Wilson Hill family's week is
  anchored by fixed 90-minute blocks per course. Homa's planner should
  treat outsourced live classes as immovable anchors and flow
  home-taught subjects around them, including protecting prep time
  before seminars.
- Grades and feedback live in the school's systems and the parent
  mostly relays them; Homa's child record should ingest
  provider-reported grades with provenance rather than re-deriving
  progress for outsourced subjects.
- Vocabulary: Great Conversation (GC 1-6, GC 1+4 for new students),
  Grammar School / School of Logic / School of Rhetoric, Forma,
  houses, LINK, Graduation Track. "She's in GC 3 and Latin 2" places a
  student around 9th grade in a classical sequence with two fixed
  schedule anchors.
