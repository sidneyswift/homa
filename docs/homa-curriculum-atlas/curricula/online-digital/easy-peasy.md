# Easy Peasy All-in-One Homeschool

## Snapshot

Easy Peasy All-in-One Homeschool is the free giant: a complete,
overtly Christian PK-12 curriculum built by homeschool mom and former
teacher Lee Giles, who began posting her own children's assignments
online in 2011 so they could work independently. Every level is 180
days of daily lessons that link out to free resources across the web
(CK-12, Georgia Virtual Learning, McGuffey Readers, YouTube), woven
into a coherent schedule that costs nothing. Reach is massive for a
one-woman project: tens of thousands of families worldwide, over 100
Facebook groups, and a volunteer support corps. High school lives on a
separate site (allinonehighschool.com). It matters to Homa less as a
product to beat than as the strongest proof that free-plus-organized
is a formidable default in this market.

## Reputation and rankings

- Cathy Duffy reviews it warmly ("The entire Easy Peasy website is
  quite impressive") and lists it as one of the only no-cost complete
  curricula, noting the 100+ volunteer-staffed Facebook groups as its
  support system ([Cathy Duffy review](https://cathyduffyreviews.com/homeschool-reviews-core-curricula/unit-studies-all-in-one-programs/all-in-one-programs/easy-peasy-all-in-one-homeschool)).
- Community affection is real and durable: it is the standard first
  recommendation for broke or brand-new homeschoolers, and many
  families credit it with making their first year possible
  ([Homeschool Hideout](https://homeschoolhideout.com/easy-peasy-all-in-one-homeschool/)).
- Standing criticisms: screen-heavy days, lots of link-clicking,
  occasional broken links, uneven quality across sourced materials,
  advanced early reading levels, and weak verification that the child
  actually did the linked work ([homeschoolpicks review](https://homeschoolpicks.com/curriculum-reviews/easy-peasy-all-in-one-review/),
  [howdoihomeschool review mining](https://howdoihomeschool.com/easy-peasy-homeschool-review/)).
- Trajectory: stable institution. It survives on donations and store
  sales, and its constraint (one author curating free web content) is
  also its moat.

## Pedagogy and method

Eclectic-traditional with Charlotte Mason influence: daily scheduled
lessons, McGuffey-style readers, copywork, narration prompts, and short
assignments, but organized as a conventional school-year checklist. The
overview page is explicit about its looseness: no weekly spelling lists,
vocabulary from reading, grammar taught as yearly passes over parts of
speech and sentence structure ([overview](https://allinonehomeschool.com/overview/)).
Lessons are written to the student so children work independently; K-3
needs a parent alongside. Assessment is light: answer keys exist, some
courses have quizzes, but nothing is automated, and parents are advised
to add narration or checks to verify work
([Mama Teaches review](https://mamateaches.com/easy-peasy-homeschool-review/)).
Days are intentionally short to leave time for interests.

## Structure and scope

- Levels, not grades: a child takes one Level (which sets math,
  language arts, reading, computer, and thinking), each independently
  switchable per subject ([overview](https://allinonehomeschool.com/overview/)).
- Themes: all children in a family study the same content-year theme
  across history, science, music, and art. Four rotating themes track
  ancient history, early American history, geography and cultures, and
  modern history, split into Levels 1-4 and 5-8 bands
  ([homepage](https://allinonehomeschool.com/)).
- Coverage: reading, writing, grammar, spelling, vocabulary, math,
  history/geography, science, Bible, Spanish, computer, music, art,
  PE/health, logic ([homepage](https://allinonehomeschool.com/)).
- High school (separate site): four years of core subjects, math
  through calculus, five lab sciences, many courses built on Georgia
  Virtual Learning and CK-12 ([Cathy Duffy](https://cathyduffyreviews.com/homeschool-reviews-core-curricula/unit-studies-all-in-one-programs/all-in-one-programs/easy-peasy-all-in-one-homeschool)).
- My EP accounts (free) give each child a clickable daily assignment
  list; a store sells optional printed workbooks of the same material
  for screen-averse families.

## A lesson up close

The child logs into My EP, opens today's list, and works down it: click
a link, read a passage or watch a video, do the assignment, mark it
done. A day in Level 3 might be a math lesson with linked practice, a
McGuffey reading selection, copywork, a theme-history reading with a
notebook prompt, and a science page. Everything is one click deep into
someone else's website, which is both the genius (zero cost, infinite
variety) and the friction (visual inconsistency, dead links, "so much
clicking"). Elementary days run 3-4 hours including offline work.
What a day produces depends on the parent: notebook pages and written
work if they enforce it; a trail of visited links if they do not,
which is the documented accountability hole families manage with
narration and printed work checks
([howdoihomeschool](https://howdoihomeschool.com/easy-peasy-homeschool-review/)).

## Materials and visual design

The site is deliberately plain: text-dense lesson pages, standard blog
styling, no art direction. The linked-out materials carry every visual
style on the free internet, from vintage readers to modern interactive
sites. The purchasable printables and workbooks are utilitarian. What
the aesthetic signals: thrift, sincerity, and function over polish,
which perfectly matches its buyer and repels design-sensitive families
(the Good and the Beautiful's fine-art softness is the free-market
counterpoint).

## Pricing

- Free, in full, permanently: all levels, all subjects, including high
  school. A suggested donation supports My EP ([homepage](https://allinonehomeschool.com/)).
- Optional purchases: printed Printables workbooks (the worksheets used
  online, so families skip printing) and offline course books for math,
  reading, and language arts, sold through the EP store at commodity
  prices.
- Real cost is paper, ink, devices, and one device per child at
  concurrent work times (a documented logistics complaint,
  [Mama Teaches](https://mamateaches.com/easy-peasy-homeschool-review/)).

## Why families choose it

- Zero cost with real structure: 180 planned days, every subject, no
  decisions required. For tight budgets it is the whole ballgame.
- Open-and-go for the parent and written-to-the-student, so it runs
  with minimal parent time.
- Christian content as a feature for its base (Bible scheduled in,
  worldview throughout).
- The trial-run curriculum: families use a free year to learn what
  they actually want before buying anything
  ([Homeschool Hideout](https://homeschoolhideout.com/easy-peasy-all-in-one-homeschool/)).

## Why families leave it

- Screen-time revolt: "I didn't like my kids sitting in front of the
  computer for 3 hours a day" is the classic exit story
  ([Homeschool Hideout](https://homeschoolhideout.com/easy-peasy-all-in-one-homeschool/)).
- Link fatigue and QA burden: broken links and uneven sourced content
  make the parent the maintenance crew.
- Accountability gap: kids can click through without doing the work;
  trust-but-verify tires parents out.
- Fit ceilings: advanced early reading levels for late bloomers, and
  secular families are outside its audience entirely.
- Graduation upward: once budget exists, families buy the subjects
  that matter most to them (math and writing first) and keep EP for
  the rest, or leave for The Good and the Beautiful (free PDFs,
  print-first, gentler aesthetic).

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Easy Peasy is the strongest evidence for the repo's A22 finding:
  free-plus-organized is the trusted default, and paid products win
  only against a named gap ([05-competitor-user-voice](../../../homa-planning/icp-research/05-competitor-user-voice.md) §6).
  Homa should never argue with free; it should serve the EP family's
  actual gaps: verification of learning, record-building, and per-child
  adjustment.
- The EP mom's unmet needs map cleanly to Homa: she has no automated
  records (compliance evidence is manual), no way to know if clicked
  equals learned (observational evidence gap), and a maintenance tax
  (broken links, level switching decisions). All three are assistant
  jobs, not curriculum jobs.
- Vocabulary to know: Levels vs Themes (the two setup decisions), My
  EP accounts, the separate high school site, Printables workbooks,
  offline course books. "We do EP Level 4 with the modern history
  theme" should parse instantly.
- Worldview handling: EP families chose overt Christianity; Homa's
  worldview-neutral posture serves them only if content controls are
  visible and respectful (the "never surprise her" baseline,
  [08-segments-curricula](../../../homa-planning/icp-research/08-segments-curricula.md) §8).
- Category note: EP could equally sit in all-in-one; it lives here
  because its delivery is entirely online and that is how families
  experience it. Cross-reference when the all-in-one comparison doc
  lands.
