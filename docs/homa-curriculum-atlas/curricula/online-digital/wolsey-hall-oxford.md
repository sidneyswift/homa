# Wolsey Hall Oxford

## Snapshot

Wolsey Hall Oxford is the global default for structured home education on
the British track: an online homeschooling college for ages 4 to 18 whose
students study the Cambridge curriculum at home, at their own pace, with no
live lessons, then sit IGCSE and A level exams as private candidates at
exam centres worldwide. Founded in 1894 as Britain's first correspondence
college (Nelson Mandela studied for his University of London law degree
through Wolsey Hall while imprisoned on Robben Island), it has been owned
since March 2021 by Faria Education Group, the international education
systems company behind ManageBac and Pamoja. The Good Schools Guide counts
about 3,740 pupils (Jan 2026 review); the school says families in over 130
countries, including US expat, military, and rigor-shopping families. It is
a registered Cambridge International School (the first online school
Cambridge approved for Primary) and a Pearson Edexcel approved centre. For
Homa it is the reference model for what "structured homeschooling" means
outside the US: a curriculum, a tutor who marks work, a named human who
tracks progress, and an external exam at the end instead of a transcript.

## Reputation and rankings

- The Good Schools Guide reviewed it (last updated 2026-01-28) and calls it
  "a game changer" for home schooling: exceptionally professional,
  flexible, well supported, and accredited, with 98% of surveyed parents
  willing to recommend it ([GSG review](https://www.goodschoolsguide.co.uk/uk-schools/review/wolsey-hall-oxford)).
- Trustpilot runs strongly positive (4.8 across about 360 reviews as of Jul
  2026): the praised things are Student Progress Managers, course
  structure, and tutor professionalism. The two recurring complaints are
  billing-team communication and tutor feedback that marks answers wrong
  without teaching the fix; one parent wrote "I have decided to use ChatGPT
  to correct my son's assignments instead"
  ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/wolseyhalloxford.org.uk)).
- Exam results, self-reported via parents since all students are private
  candidates: in 2025, 28% of IGCSE grades at 9-7 and 18% A*/A at A level
  (40% A*-B); the school's own 2024 figure is a 72% A*-C pass rate at GCSE
  level ([GSG](https://www.goodschoolsguide.co.uk/uk-schools/review/wolsey-hall-oxford),
  [IGCSE Chemistry page](https://wolseyhalloxford.org.uk/our-courses/gcse-level/gcse-level-courses/chemistry)).
- SEND is a documented strength: the GSG reports around 380 students with
  SEN or SEMH and a sharply rising share of new enrolments, nearly a
  quarter of them autistic; the no-live-lessons model is the draw.
- Trajectory: stable-to-rising. The 130-year heritage story, Faria's
  ownership, and UK school-refusal and SEND demand all feed it; the
  Good Schools Guide notes it is cheaper than most live-lesson online
  schools, which it considers its real competitors.

## Pedagogy and method

Wolsey Hall's model is structured self-directed study, deliberately not a
virtual school. There are no live lessons, no timetables, and no
classrooms; the school markets itself as pupil-led, "no live lessons, set
timetables or crowded classrooms" ([how it works](https://wolseyhalloxford.org.uk/our-school/how-homeschooling-with-us-works)).
Instruction comes from course materials (real textbooks plus multimedia
lessons on Canvas), and the human layer is two named roles. Each subject
has a Tutor, a UK-qualified teacher who holds a welcome call, answers
messages, and marks the course's assignments with written feedback inside
3 to 5 working days. Each student has a Student Progress Manager (SPM),
the form-tutor equivalent, who builds a personalised assignment schedule
around the family's holidays and pace, monitors progress across subjects,
and handles everything non-academic including exam entry guidance
([SPM page](https://wolseyhalloxford.org.uk/our-school/our-team/student-progress-managers),
[FAQs](https://wolseyhalloxford.org.uk/faqs)). Assessment is continuous
tutor-marked assignments (10 to 12 per GCSE-level course), then past
papers in the final module, then the real external exam. The parent's
designed role is monitor, not teacher: an "Observer" account on Canvas
shows progress, grades, and feedback. The intellectual lineage is the
British correspondence-college tradition: the same assignment-and-marking
loop Wolsey Hall ran by post in 1894, moved onto Canvas.

## Structure and scope

- Four phases mapped to the British system: Primary (ages 4-11, Years
  1-6), Lower Secondary (11-14, Years 7-9), GCSE Level (14-16, Cambridge
  IGCSE or Edexcel International GCSE), and AS/A Level (16-18). The US
  page maps these to elementary, middle, and high school for American
  families ([USA page](https://wolseyhalloxford.org.uk/our-school/countries/united-states-of-america)).
- Primary and Lower Secondary courses cover one year each; GCSE and full
  A Level courses cover two years of study (24-month access), with 150
  hours of study per IGCSE plus assignment time. Recommended pace: 18-24
  months per IGCSE cohort, 9-12 months for AS.
- Subject catalogs are wide at every phase: the IGCSE list spans English
  Language and Literature, Maths and Additional Maths, the three separate
  sciences plus Combined and Co-ordinated Science, History, Geography,
  Business, Economics, Computer Science, Psychology, Environmental
  Management, Marine Science, Art and Design, French, German, Spanish,
  Latin, and (from Sep 2026) Sociology ([fees PDF](https://cdn.wolseyhalloxford.org.uk/media/5lfnwyrb/secondary-fees-1-apr-2026-updated.pdf)).
- Families choose any number of subjects; bundles of 1-3, 4, and 5+
  courses price differently, and a set "GCSE study package" mirrors the
  standard UK school load. UK pupils typically sit 7-10 GCSEs; Wolsey Hall
  lets families spread exams across two sittings (May/June and November).
- Students enrol any day of the year. Exams are booked separately at
  Cambridge exam centres in over 150 countries; the school advises on the
  nearest centre but the family contracts with the centre directly.

## A lesson up close

A working day starts inside Canvas, the same LMS US college students
know. The student opens their subject course, which is organised into
modules mirroring the syllabus (each IGCSE course targets a specific
syllabus code, for example Chemistry 0620/0971). A module mixes reading
assignments from the shipped textbook, written lesson pages, embedded
video, self-check quizzes, and revision tools; language courses add
online speaking practice. The unit of accountability is the assignment:
when the student reaches an assignment checkpoint, they complete a
substantial piece of written work and submit it through Canvas, and their
subject Tutor returns it marked with comments, typically within 3 to 5
working days. Between assignments nobody is watching in real time, which
is the model's entire promise and its entire risk. The SPM's assignment
schedule (personalised due dates emailed at enrolment, adjusted on
request) is what keeps a drifting week visible: the SPM checks progress
against it and nudges. A day produces textbook work on paper, quiz
results, and possibly a submitted assignment; a course produces 10 to 12
marked assignments, a mock exam option in the final module (marked for an
extra fee), and readiness for the external paper. The final module of
every GCSE course is past papers, because the terminal artifact of the
whole system is an exam grade, not a portfolio
([course information](https://wolseyhalloxford.org.uk/our-courses/gcse-level/course-information),
[FAQs](https://wolseyhalloxford.org.uk/faqs)).

## Materials and visual design

The product is Canvas courses plus real shipped books. Course fees include
all required textbooks, which are exam-board-endorsed editions from
mainstream UK educational publishers, posted to the family (free UK
shipping, international at cost). The digital layer is functional
courseware in Canvas's plain, institutional visual language: modules,
assignment inboxes, grade views, messaging. Wolsey Hall's own branding is
Oxford-heritage: crest-like logo, the 1894 story, Mandela and "world's
most experienced flexible learning provider" as marketing spine. The
aesthetic signal to the buyer is legitimacy and seriousness, a school in
the institutional sense, not a playful edtech product. There are free
sample courses per subject that expose the first module and an assignment
inside the real Canvas environment, which is the honest way to preview it
([fees FAQ](https://wolseyhalloxford.org.uk/admissions-and-fees/fees)).

## Pricing

Per the April 2026 official fee sheets, paid-in-full prices per course
(instalment plans run about 5% higher, spread over six months):

- IGCSE, most subjects: 900 GBP each for 1-3 courses, 750 GBP in a
  4-course bundle, 700 GBP at 5+ (roughly 950-1,215 USD per course).
  Maths runs slightly higher; French, German, Spanish, and Art and Design
  run 1,250-1,400 GBP; Co-ordinated Sciences counts and prices as two
  courses. Fees cover the full two-year course
  ([secondary fees PDF](https://cdn.wolseyhalloxford.org.uk/media/5lfnwyrb/secondary-fees-1-apr-2026-updated.pdf)).
- Lower Secondary: roughly 500-700 GBP per course per year by subject and
  bundle. Primary: from 475 GBP per course per year in 5+ bundles
  ([bundles page](https://wolseyhalloxford.org.uk/admissions-and-fees/fees/course-bundling)).
- A worked example from the school's own sheet: a four-IGCSE load costs
  about 3,200-3,500 GBP total for the two years, or very roughly
  2,200-2,400 USD per year.
- One-time 95 GBP registration per student, non-refundable. International
  book postage at cost. Exam fees are separate and paid to the exam
  centre, which matters: board fees run about 50 GBP per subject and
  private centres add admin fees that can reach a few hundred GBP per
  subject in some cities.
- 45-day cancellation window with a fee; level and subject swaps allowed
  inside it. Nothing is resellable, but course access covers the full
  1-year or 2-year term even if finished early.
- Context: 57% of GSG-surveyed parents rated it very good value and it is
  cheaper than live-lesson online schools (King's InterHigh's three-A-level
  year lists at 7,585 GBP), while costing far more than US-style
  courseware like Time4Learning.

## Why families choose it

- The credential: Cambridge IGCSEs and A levels are externally examined,
  globally recognised qualifications, so nobody has to trust a parent
  transcript. For UK families this is simply how qualifications work; for
  expat and internationally mobile families it is portability; for some
  US families it is external validation US homeschooling cannot give.
- Complete flexibility with structure underneath: start any date, no
  timetable, athlete and performer friendly, but with named humans, a
  schedule, and marked work preventing drift.
- SEND and school-refusal fit: no camera, no classroom, own pace. This is
  a large and growing share of enrolment.
- The heritage-plus-accreditation trust stack: 1894, Mandela, Cambridge
  registration, Good Schools Guide review. It out-legitimizes nearly every
  competitor on paper.
- Cheaper than live online schools while feeling more like a school than
  buying curriculum boxes.

## Why families leave it

- The self-direction bet fails for some kids: with no live lessons, a
  student who will not open Canvas quietly stalls. Families needing
  real-time teaching move to live online schools (King's InterHigh,
  CambriLearn's live tiers) or back to physical school.
- Tutor feedback depth: the recurring Trustpilot criticism is marking that
  identifies errors without teaching the correction, which pushes
  perfectionist or struggling students toward paid 1:1 sessions or outside
  tutors.
- Cost accumulates per subject: a full multi-subject load lands in
  private-school-lite territory once exam-centre fees are added, and there
  are cheaper UK distance providers (Oxford Home Schooling's subscription
  model, NEC's charity pricing) for families who only need an exam course.
- Billing and admin friction: the accounts team is the one consistently
  criticised function.
- Younger years fit: Primary requires real parent involvement despite the
  self-directed framing, and families wanting a gentler or more hands-on
  early education choose curriculum-in-a-box providers instead.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- This is what "homeschooling" means to much of the world: a structured
  external program, tutor-marked work, and exams that replace transcripts.
  When Homa meets a UK, expat, or international family, the mental model
  is not "mom builds a stack", it is "which provider and which syllabus
  codes". Homa's onboarding vocabulary must include Cambridge, Edexcel,
  IGCSE, AS/A level, syllabus numbers, private candidate, and exam centre.
- The SPM role is the product lesson. Wolsey Hall's most-praised feature
  is not content, it is a named human who builds the schedule, watches
  progress across subjects, and nudges. That is exactly Homa's assistant
  job description, delivered by staff at private-school prices; Homa can
  deliver the SPM behavior pattern (personalised schedule, drift
  detection, gentle check-ins, celebration of wins) in software at
  consumer prices.
- The feedback gap is Homa-shaped: parents already route around thin tutor
  feedback with ChatGPT, verbatim. An assistant that turns "wrong" into a
  taught correction, in the parent's hands, competes directly with the
  weakest part of a 900 GBP course.
- Records insight: in this model the family's record-keeping burden is
  nearly zero until exam season, then becomes exam logistics (centre
  finding, entry deadlines, NEA components). A Homa expansion into the UK
  would sell exam-season project management, not portfolio building; see
  `../../comparisons/international.md`.
- Watch Faria: Wolsey Hall plus Pamoja plus ManageBac is a quiet
  international-education stack with real distribution into schools, and
  its "Wolsey Hall for Schools" line already sells courses into physical
  Cambridge schools.
