# The Potter's School (TPS)

## Snapshot

The Potter's School (TPS) is the grandfather of live online homeschool
academies: a non-profit Christian school founded in 1997, in the first
wave of the movement, that calls itself "the original online school with
synchronous (live) classes" and can plausibly claim to have pioneered
live audio and whiteboard teaching for homeschoolers. It is accredited
as an independent private school (grades 4-12) by the Middle States
Association, serves homeschool and independent students in all 50 states
and 70+ countries, and offers a full secondary curriculum: core
academics, certified AP courses, dual credit through Belhaven
University, and an unusually deep world-language catalog (multi-year
Arabic sits beside the usual Spanish, French, and Latin). Families enroll one course at a time or pursue the
optional Diploma Partnership. The house personality is rigor plus
policy: exacting standards, teacher-graded everything, and famously
precise rules for deadlines, refunds, and enrollment.

## Reputation and rankings

- The writing and English department is the standing legend: Well-Trained
  Mind forum veterans describe strict style rules, one-paper-a-week
  pacing in Advanced Composition, and picky placement tests, mostly as
  praise ("It is a big time investment but worth it!").
- Language breadth is the other repeated strength; families cite strong
  Spanish, Latin, and even multi-year Arabic, and international families
  value sections scheduled for non-US time zones.
- The critical thread is administrative rigidity. A widely read
  Well-Trained Mind thread ("Be careful with The Potter's School")
  recounts a refused teacher change and abrupt handling of a complaint,
  and blames the contractor structure. Long-form reviews also flag the
  deadline regime (late work penalized daily, zero after five days) as
  hard on family life, and the first-semester learning curve as steep.
- No award-circuit presence to speak of; TPS reputation runs on
  longevity, alumni outcomes, and word of mouth in academic homeschool
  circles. Trajectory: stable institution, neither surging nor fading.

## Pedagogy and method

Traditional academics delivered live, with evaluation as the product.
TPS states plainly that its courses use "traditional academic content
and evaluation" as an alternative to declining standards, and its
history page frames the school against homeschool curricula that compete
on being easier and more fun. Classes meet in purpose-built conferencing
(audio, interactive slides, shared whiteboard; cameras not required)
rather than adapted meeting tools. Teachers evaluate all major work with
an objective grade plus individual feedback, usually within one to two
weeks, and the school describes feedback density as its hallmark.
Workload is engineered: high school courses are designed so an average
student working 8-10 hours a week earns about 85%, middle school
courses take 6-8 hours, elementary 4-6, AP 10-12, and a diligent
student is expected to carry 5-6 courses working six days with one
Sabbath day off. Grading design protects the GPA from any single
assignment, lateness draws small daily penalties for up to five days
then a zero, and extended crises are handled by waiving work
(humanitarian leave) rather than extending it.

## Structure and scope

- Grades 4-12, full curriculum in a US scope and sequence: English and
  composition, math, sciences, history and social studies, Bible,
  world languages, arts, and electives. Full-year courses run August to
  May; fall, spring, and summer terms exist.
- Credentials: Middle States accreditation as a school (code VA013W),
  College Board authorization for certified AP courses (CEEB 470144),
  NCAA-approved core courses (code 850857), and CA A-G approval.
- Our Courses Your Way: every course (except AP) can be taken for TPS
  Credit (attendance, assignments, and parent-proctored exams required,
  documented on an accredited transcript) or No TPS Credit (same
  teaching and feedback, compliance optional, family documents it on
  the homeschool transcript).
- Diploma Partnership for full-time students, in Standard and Honors
  tiers, with college application support.
- Weekly rhythm: live class plus assignments due through the week;
  classes are recorded for occasional absence, and AP and dual-credit
  courses may be taken by recording to fit time zones.

## A lesson up close

A TPS high school week pivots on the live session: the student joins by
headset, the teacher teaches on interactive slides and whiteboard, and
participation is expected (regular live attendance is a credit
requirement). Around the session sit the week's readings, problem sets,
or a paper, submitted to deadlines the school treats seriously.
Evaluated work comes back with a grade and individual feedback, and
every Friday the parent receives two automatic emails, an assignment
report and a grade report. The parent teaches nothing and grades
nothing; their defined jobs are supervision, time management support,
and proctoring exams for credit-status courses. What a year produces is
a documented course: description, textbook list, assignment-level
grades, and a cumulative grade the family can put on a homeschool
transcript, or an official accredited TPS transcript on request.
(Oddly specific rule worth knowing: the homeschool transcript version
may not reference TPS by name or logo.)

## Materials and visual design

TPS publishes no curriculum of its own aesthetic; each course lists
required books by ISBN, families source them, and some courses carry a
resource fee for school-licensed eTexts and courseware. The classroom
software and portal are utilitarian, and the school is explicit that
the money goes to teaching, not production polish. What the family is
buying, visually and otherwise, is red ink: dense teacher feedback on
real student work.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026: tuition is listed per course in the catalog and
commonly lands around $498-695 for a full-year course (the range a
homeschool.com directory listing publishes; the catalog is the live
truth). A $75 family registration fee is charged once per year per
family, with no per-student fees. Full-year tuition is due in thirds
(at enrollment, July 15, November 15), each increment non-refundable
once due; there are no late fees but also no auto-pay, and unpaid seats
are dropped. Books and materials are extra, resource fees apply on
some courses, and a donation-funded tuition assistance program plus
acceptance of some state financial-aid programs soften the total. A
five-course full-time year runs roughly $2,500-3,500 before books.

## Why families choose it

- The algebra wall and the essay wall at once: a whole secondary school
  of subjects a parent will not teach, taught and graded by someone who
  demonstrably will.
- Trusted worldview with real rigor, in the same purchase.
- Transcript strength: accreditation, certified AP, NCAA approval, and
  optional official transcripts, without surrendering homeschool status
  (the No Credit option keeps the parent the registrar).
- International practicality: time-zone sections, a long missionary and
  expat heritage, and community events (summer camp, graduation).

## Why families leave it

- Cost stacking: $500-700 per course times several courses times
  several kids reaches private-school money fast.
- The deadline regime: families needing flexibility around health,
  travel, or crisis find the five-day-late-then-zero rules punishing
  despite the humanitarian-leave valve.
- Administrative rigidity: the documented teacher-change refusal
  pattern; families who expect consumer-style service clash with a
  school that runs on policy.
- Workload mismatch: the engineered 8-10 hours per course is real, and
  overcommitted students drown; families step down to self-paced
  providers (Veritas Self-Paced, Thinkwell) or gentler academies.
- Time-zone friction for live attendance where recordings are not
  permitted for the course.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- TPS is the purest evidence that homeschool parents pay a premium for
  evaluation and feedback, not content delivery. Its No TPS Credit
  option (same teaching, compliance stripped) is a segmentation lesson:
  even inside one school, some families want proof and some just want
  teaching.
- The weekly Friday assignment and grade emails are a ready-made
  integration surface: a Homa family with TPS courses has structured
  progress data arriving by email that should flow into the child's
  record without retyping.
- TPS's policy density (readiness approval, wait lists that auto-bill,
  tuition increments, drop deadlines, proctoring rules) is exactly the
  calendar-and-paperwork load Homa can carry for a family running
  multiple providers.
- Vocabulary: TPS Credit vs No Credit, Diploma Partnership (Standard
  and Honors), readiness approval, resource fee, Friday reports.
  A family "doing TPS for English and Arabic" is running a rigor-first,
  worldview-anchored stack and almost certainly juggling other
  providers around fixed class times.
