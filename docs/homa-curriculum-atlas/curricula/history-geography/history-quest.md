# History Quest

## Snapshot

History Quest is Pandia Press's secular narrative history for elementary
grades: a story-style chapter book plus a heavyweight study guide that
together make a full-year curriculum, deliberately built as the secular
alternative to The Story of the World. Pandia Press is the respected secular
publisher behind REAL Science Odyssey (the same brand promise, "secular and
facts-based", spans both lines) and the older History Odyssey guides for upper
grades, which History Quest gradually replaced at elementary level between
2019 and 2021. Three volumes exist (Early Times, Middle Times, United States);
the world sequence is a three-year cycle with a stopgap Modern History Bridge
guide until History Quest: Modern Times ships. Its signature flourish is the
History Hop, an imaginative time-travel vignette closing every chapter that
drops the reader into the era just studied.

## Reputation and rankings

Cathy Duffy reviews the series positively as a well-constructed secular
option, and it is one of the two default answers (with Curiosity Chronicles)
whenever r/secularhomeschool or secular curriculum roundups are asked for
elementary history. Reviewers, including a certified teacher writing at The
Homeschool Graduate, praise the narrative voice, the affordability of the
chapter book alone, and the production quality; Modulo's reviewer calls Pandia
Press the gold standard for secular project-based materials. Critical threads:
the study guide's price has climbed (now $64.99, nearly twice the chapter
book); the course requires outside resources (the Usborne Encyclopedia of
World History plus library literature) to run as written; the world cycle is
incomplete past the early 17th century, with the Bridge guide leaning on other
publishers' spines (including, ironically, SOTW Volumes 3 and 4); and it is
younger-skewing, so families need History Odyssey or another program by middle
school. Trajectory: stable and well-regarded in its niche, with the missing
modern volume the standing complaint.

## Pedagogy and method

History Quest is read-aloud narrative history with a unit-study wrapper. The
chapter book carries the content in storytelling register (designed as a
read-aloud for grades 1-4 and an independent read for grades 5 and up), and
the study guide turns each chapter into a five-day arc: Day 1 Discover (read
the chapter), Day 2 Explore (read the History Hop, complete the Travel Log),
Day 3 Create (choose a hands-on project), Day 4 Demonstrate (narration, key
terms, short answers), Day 5 Enrich (websites, extra books). The Travel Log is
the program's memory spine: a student-built binder where kids log each week's
journey (when they went, where, who they met), functioning as timeline,
geography record, and notebook at once. Four Hygge History weeks per year swap
all assignments for cozy shared reading of myth and folklore from four
cultures (Gilgamesh, the Ramayana, Greek and Chinese stories in Early Times),
an explicitly relationship-first design move. Religion is taught about, never
taught as fact.

## Structure and scope

Three published volumes, each one school year, as of Jul 2026:

- Early Times (grades 1-6): Paleolithic through the eighth century CE,
  including the early Islamic empires. Chapter book plus a 270-page study
  guide with 31 weekly units (including the four Hygge weeks) and 13 blackline
  maps.
- Middle Times (grades 1-6): fall of Rome through the early 17th century; 34
  chapters spanning Byzantium, the Mongols, medieval Japan, the Crusades,
  exploration, and the Renaissance.
- United States (grades 3-6): 1500s to the early 2000s including September 11,
  with civics coverage; 34 weekly units; notably willing to cover
  uncomfortable episodes.
- Modern History Bridge (grades 3-7, $43.99 ebook): a compiled study guide
  covering colonization to the modern era until History Quest: Modern Times is
  released (Pandia announces no dates until a month out).

Components per volume: chapter book ($36.99), study guide ($64.99,
reproducible within a family), audiobook ($33.99), plus required outside
resources (the Usborne encyclopedia for world volumes, literature titles
listed per guide). After the elementary cycle, Pandia routes families to
History Odyssey levels 2 and 3 for grades 6 and up.

## A lesson up close

A week with Early Times: Monday, the parent reads the week's chapter aloud, 15
to 25 minutes; the writing is warm narrative with hand-drawn illustrations and
maps, and it ends with the History Hop, a second-person time-travel scene
(read this on Tuesday in the official plan). Tuesday, the child completes the
week's Travel Log entry in their binder: dating the stop on their timeline
strip, marking the geography, noting who they met, sometimes with a drawing.
Wednesday is Create day: the guide offers several options per unit ranging
from cooking to crafts to art projects (bas-relief sculpture and a
water-spitting gargoyle are real examples from the guide's menu), with supply
lists gathered in an appendix. Thursday, Demonstrate: the child works through
the key terms and concepts, gives a narration, and answers short comprehension
questions from the guide; the parent checks against included answers. Friday,
Enrich: optional deeper reading from the literature list or linked websites,
or the family banks the day. Map work uses the guide's blackline masters. A
completed week leaves a fatter Travel Log, one project, and a page or two of
written work; there are no tests anywhere in the program.

## Materials and visual design

The chapter books are handsome trade paperbacks with hand-drawn
black-and-white illustrations and maps, storybook-feeling, visually calmer
than Curiosity Chronicles' full color and warmer than SOTW's older line art.
Study guides are thick, utilitarian, spiral-friendly workhorses: schedules,
lesson plans, student pages, and appendices, perforated and reproducible for
family use. The audiobooks are professionally narrated singles per volume. The
overall aesthetic signals thoughtful secular craft: no cartoon mascots, no
devotional warmth, a slightly Montessori-adjacent earnestness consistent with
Pandia's science line.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026, direct from pandiapress.com: chapter book $36.99, study guide
$64.99, audiobook $33.99; the core pair is about $102 per year, with retailers
like Rainbow Resource discounting guides toward $52. Add the Usborne
encyclopedia (about $25 street) and library literature. The chapter book alone
is a viable budget spine (a common practice cited in reviews), and Pandia runs
well-known seasonal sales; guides are reproducible across siblings, keeping
multi-kid cost flat. Used copies circulate but PDFs are licensed per family.

## Why families choose it

It is the SOTW experience without the worldview argument: same read-aloud
narrative pleasure, same era sweep, but rigorously secular and more globally
balanced. The five-day structure and Travel Log give parents an obvious weekly
rhythm with zero planning. Hygge History legitimizes the snuggle-and-read days
families want anyway. The publisher's science line (REAL Science Odyssey)
means many families already trust the brand, and buying history and science
from one secular house simplifies life.

## Why families leave it

The world cycle dead-ends after Middle Times: families hit the modern gap and
either use the Bridge (which requires buying other publishers' books,
including SOTW, defeating the point for some) or jump programs. Kids age out
around grade 6 and move to History Odyssey, Curiosity Chronicles Modern, or
textbook programs. The guide price plus required extras makes the full
experience cost real money next to a $37 book. And families wanting color-rich
pages or a livelier voice pick Curiosity Chronicles instead.

## Competitive notes for Homa

History Quest's five-day verb structure (Discover, Explore, Create,
Demonstrate, Enrich) is a clean, teachable weekly grammar that Homa's planner
could adopt for any content subject. The Travel Log is the best low-tech
progress artifact in the category: a child-owned record that doubles as
timeline and geography log, and a natural model for how Homa could render
history progress to kids rather than parents. Vocabulary to know: volume names
(Early Times, Middle Times, United States), History Hops, Travel Log, Hygge
History weeks, and the Bridge. When a family says History Quest, Homa should
assume strict secularity, grades 1-6, no tests, and a coming modern-history
gap; generating Bridge-equivalent modern units and grade 6-plus continuation
material is a concrete opening. Also note the sibling pattern: these families
likely run REAL Science Odyssey, so science and history tone should match.
