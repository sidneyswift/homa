# Curiosity Chronicles

## Snapshot

Curiosity Chronicles is a secular, deliberately global world history
curriculum whose core texts (the Snapshots of History series) are written
entirely as dialogue between two kid characters, Mona and Ted: Mona carries
the history, Ted asks the questions and cracks the jokes. Created by
homeschool mother Rachel Meyers (master's in comparative studies with a
Classics emphasis) after she found nothing both rigorous and truly secular for
her own kids, it has become the darling of the secular homeschool community
for elementary and middle grades. The series follows the classical four-period
rotation (ancient, medieval, early modern, modern) while explicitly rejecting
the Eurocentric and providential framings of the older programs, covering the
Kongo Empire, Qing China, and the Jamaican Maroons with the same seriousness
as Tudor England.

## Reputation and rankings

There is no Cathy Duffy review; the program's standing was built almost
entirely in secular homeschool spaces: r/secularhomeschool threads, secular
curriculum roundups, and Facebook groups, where it is routinely named the most
engaging genuinely secular history option. Aggregators and review sites
(Smarter Learning Guide, Modulo) praise the dialogue format, the global
coverage, and the professional voice-actor audiobooks. The publisher showcases
parent testimonials calling it the answer to whitewashed history. Critical
threads are mild but real: the constant back-and-forth banter grates on some
parents and older kids; the humor is divisive; it is a small self-published
operation, so availability, editions, and support are cottage-scale; and the
series is unfinished at the modern end (Modern Volume 1 stops at 1914; Volumes
2 and 3 are still being written as of Jul 2026), so there is no complete path
through the twentieth century yet. Trajectory: rising within its niche.

## Pedagogy and method

The dialogue format is the pedagogy: presenting history as a conversation lets
the text hold competing interpretations, flag disputed facts, ask "how do we
know?", and model critical thinking rather than delivering one settled
narrative, which is precisely the epistemic posture secular families say they
miss in narrative programs. Chapters are short and designed for read-aloud
with role-play (parent and child each take a character) or independent reading
for older kids. Reinforcement comes from the activity guides: comprehension
questions, discussion prompts, map work, timelines, coloring, puzzles, crafts,
recipes, and (a signature quirk) Minecraft build tie-ins per chapter in the
instructor guide. There is no testing apparatus; assessment is conversation
and the student guide's written work. Interdisciplinary culture coverage (art,
science, philosophy) is woven in, including Culture Corner interludes hosted
by two more characters, Arthur and Lily, in later volumes.

## Structure and scope

Five published levels as of Jul 2026, each one school year (the two-volume
levels can be one year at two chapters per week or two years at a gentler
pace):

- Snapshots of Ancient History (grades 1-6): beginnings to 550 CE.
- Snapshots of Medieval History (grades 2-7): 550-1550.
- Snapshots of Early Modern History Vol. 1 (grades 3-7): 1550-1765; Vol. 2
  (grades 4-8): 1765-1850.
- Snapshots of Modern History Vol. 1 (grades 5-10): 1850-1914. Volumes 2 (1914
  to the 1950s) and 3 (1950s to the 2000s) are in progress with no announced
  dates.

Every Snapshots text is 36 chapters organized as 18 sections of paired
chapters, 250-plus full-color pages. Per level the ecosystem is: the Snapshots
text (hardcover, softcover, or PDF), a digital audiobook voiced by
professional actors, an Instructor's Activity Guide (answers, activities, book
recommendations), a Student's Activity Guide (matching, short answer,
discussion questions, maps, coloring, a puzzle per lesson), Ted's Timeline
Kit, and optional lapbook and interactive notebook packages. Bundles combine
these in print, mixed, or digital formats.

## A lesson up close

A typical week covers one section (two related chapters). Day one: parent and
child read a chapter together, often splitting the Mona and Ted parts like a
script, 15 to 25 minutes; the full-color pages mix the dialogue with photos,
art, and maps, and the tone stays light even through heavy material. The
parent then pulls the matching pages from the Student's Activity Guide: a
matching or short-answer exercise, a discussion question that intentionally
lacks one right answer, and map work locating the chapter's places. Day two
repeats with the paired chapter, plus a chosen extra from the instructor
guide: a craft, a recipe from the culture studied, a coloring page, the
timeline entry, or the Minecraft build for kids who live there. Families using
the audiobook play the chapter's voice-acted track in the car and do only the
paper work at home. A completed week produces a few student-guide pages, an
updated timeline, and a marked map; there are no tests to file.

## Materials and visual design

The Snapshots books are bright, full-color, photo-and-illustration-rich, with
the two cartoon hosts recurring as visual anchors; the energy is closer to a
good DK book crossed with a graphic-novel sensibility than to a textbook, and
it reads as friendly and modern next to SOTW's black-and-white austerity.
Activity guides are clean printable PDFs or softcovers, functional rather than
beautiful. The audiobooks are genuinely produced (multiple voice actors
playing the characters), which matters in a category where audio is how many
families actually consume history. As a self-published line, print quality and
design polish are strong for the niche but a step below big-publisher
production.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026, direct from curiositychronicles.com: complete one-volume
curriculum packages run about $73 (digital) to $95 (print); the two-volume
Early Modern level bundles at roughly $145 digital to $180 print; the
audiobook adds $10 to any bundle; lapbook and notebook packages are $10; Ted's
Timeline Kit is $0.99. Texts are reusable; student guides are per-child
printables. No meaningful resale market yet given PDF licensing and the small
print runs; most families buy direct during the publisher's sales.

## Why families choose it

They want actually secular, not merely neutral: religion treated as history,
evolution uncontested, and no biblical narrative weighting. They want the
world outside Europe treated as history rather than garnish. The dialogue
format wins over kids who tune out lecture-voice narration, and
neurodivergent-friendly design is part of the origin story. The voice-acted
audiobook and Minecraft hooks meet 2020s kids where they are. And the
whole-year bundle stays reasonably priced.

## Why families leave it

The banter wears thin for some families, especially by upper elementary; they
move to History Quest's calmer narrative or straight to secular middle school
texts. The unfinished modern sequence forces a patch year (many borrow Pandia
Press materials or unit-study the twentieth century). Families needing
rigorous output, testing, or transcript support outgrow the activity guides.
And classical families who want the traditional canon-and-chronology voice go
back to SOTW or its descendants.

## Competitive notes for Homa

Curiosity Chronicles is proof of demand for a history product built on
epistemic honesty (competing views, how-we-know framing) and global scope, the
exact values a Homa-generated history layer should default to for secular
families. Its component pattern (text plus per-child activity guide plus
produced audio plus timeline) is the modern minimum viable history stack.
Vocabulary to know: Snapshots levels by era name (not grade), Mona and Ted,
sections as chapter pairs, one-year versus two-year pacing of the double
volumes. When a family uses it, Homa should mirror the dialogic,
multiple-perspective tone in generated materials, respect the strictly secular
stance, and expect the family to hit the modern-history gap after Early
Modern: a concrete, dated opening where Homa can supply the missing
1914-to-present materials before the publisher does.
