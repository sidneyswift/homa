# The Mystery of History

## Snapshot

The Mystery of History (MOH) is a four-volume chronological world history by
Linda Lacour Hobar, published by Bright Ideas Press, and it is the Christian
counterpart to The Story of the World: the standard answer when a family loves
the chronological cycle but wants Bible history woven into world history as
one integrated timeline. Volume I starts at Creation (dated to about 4004 BC
via Ussher) and ends at the Resurrection; the series runs to 2014. Its
signature move is teaching every age at once: the whole family hears the same
lesson, then a Companion Guide assigns different work to Younger (K-3), Middle
(4-8), and Older (9-12) students. Hobar, a homeschooling mother who writes
in a direct personal voice, narrates the audiobook herself. MOH holds a
large, durable share of the conservative Christian homeschool market.

## Reputation and rankings

Cathy Duffy's review praises MOH as a way for "even inexperienced parents" to
break free from textbooks, highlighting the multi-sensory, K-12 adaptability
and the strong biblical base. Review sites consistently rank it the leading
Christian chronological history program, and comparison articles (for example
homeschoolpicks.com) recommend it over SOTW for Christian families teaching
many ages together. Community sentiment is warm: moms report the open-and-go
quality and the geography integration as standouts. The critical threads are
consistent too: secular reviewers find the providential framing disqualifying;
the reading level and emotional intensity jump sharply by Volume III and IV
(one detailed review stopped using the audiobooks in the car over Volume III's
coverage of historical figures' sexual conduct and disease, calling it
unnecessary "gossip"); and some older students find the author-read audio and
conversational tone young for them. Trajectory is stable: the third editions
(full-color hardback readers, improved digital companion guides) are rolling
through the series, and the brand remains a fixture of Christian curriculum
fairs.

## Pedagogy and method

MOH is classical in structure and providential in outlook. The trivium mapping
is explicit: Younger students (grammar stage) listen and do hands-on
activities, Middle students (logic stage) are the target audience for the full
program, Older students (rhetoric stage) add research, essays, and can earn a
high school credit per volume. The pedagogy is story-driven direct instruction
in a conversational authorial voice, followed by multi-modal reinforcement:
pretests to prime attention ("What do you know?"), oral or written
narration-style review, memory cards as a growing review deck, timeline
building, and mapping exercises. The distinctive doctrine is one unified
chronology: biblical figures and events are placed on the same timeline as
secular history (who was alive when the Trojan Horse was built), taught as
fact from an evangelical Protestant, creationist stance. Weekly rhythm is
three reading lessons plus an activity or review day. The parent reads aloud
with younger kids; middle and older students can read independently, making
parent involvement genuinely medium for the core use case.

## Structure and scope

Four volumes, each one school year:

- Volume I: Creation to the Resurrection (c. 4004 BC to AD 33). Third edition,
  full-color hardback.
- Volume II: The Early Church and the Middle Ages (AD 33 to 1456).
- Volume III: The Renaissance, Reformation, and Growth of Nations (1455-1707).
- Volume IV: Wars of Independence to Modern Times (1708-2014). 84 lessons
  across 28 weeks, three lessons per week; written at a rigorous
  middle-to-high-school level and recommended for about grade 6 and up.

Components per volume: the hardback Student Reader (each copy includes a
download code for the Companion Guide PDF); the Companion Guide itself, 500 to
600 pages of pretests, quizzes, tests, mapping, timeline directions, and
activity menus split by Younger, Middle, and Older; author-narrated audiobook
MP3s sold by quarters, with or without background music; and a Super
Supplemental Collection (challenge cards, coloring pages, notebooking pages,
and quarterly Folderbook lapbook kits, $32 each alone or $59.95 and up
bundled, as of Jul 2026). Print companion guides exist for Volumes I-III;
Volume IV's guide is digital only. An appendix lists supplemental books and
videos lesson by lesson, broken down by age level. World history coverage is
intentionally interleaved with American history in Volume IV rather than
treated as a separate course.

## A lesson up close

A week starts with the pretest, a short oral quiz that is expected to be
mostly missed; it primes curiosity rather than grades. The parent (or the
audiobook, or an older student solo) then reads that day's lesson, typically
two to four pages of conversational narrative on one person or event, often
connecting a biblical figure or theme to the secular history in view. After
the lesson, the family turns to the Companion Guide's activity menu for that
lesson: a Younger child might act out charades, do a coloring page, or find
the location on a globe; a Middle student plays a review game, adds a figure
to the timeline, or does the mapping exercise; an Older student takes a
research or essay assignment. The guide's mapping exercises come with answer
keys, and quarterly reviews and semester tests provide formal assessment for
older students. A completed week produces timeline entries, one or two maps, a
quiz or exercise page, and whatever craft or notebook output was chosen;
Folderbooks accumulate quarter by quarter into a keepsake. Sessions run 20 to
45 minutes depending on how many activities the family takes. Student pages
are reproducible within a family, and the third-edition Volume I integrates
write-in activity pages directly.

## Materials and visual design

The current-generation readers are full-color, Smyth-bound hardbacks with
photographs and illustrations, a deliberate step up from the paperback second
editions and a direct answer to SOTW's black-and-white austerity; the shelf
presence is closer to a gift book than a textbook. Companion Guides are dense
black-and-white PDF or print volumes, workmanlike, organized lesson by lesson.
The audiobook is the author's own voice over light music beds, warm and
devotional in tone, which fans find personal and some older kids find young.
The supplemental ecosystem (challenge cards, coloring pages, folderbooks,
notebooking pages) is printable cardstock-and-scissors material in a homespun
aesthetic. The overall signal to the buyer is trusted homeschool mom, not
academic publisher: approachable, family-scaled, explicitly devout.

The reader is reusable; the Companion Guide PDF prints per child, so multi-kid
economics are strong (buy once, reuse for every student and every pass through
the cycle). Used second-edition paperbacks circulate cheaply, though the
publisher pitches the third edition as a meaningful upgrade.

## Why families choose it

They want the four-year chronological sweep with the Bible inside it rather
than beside it: one timeline where Old Testament events, church history, and
world history interlock. The three-level Companion Guide lets one lesson serve
a kindergartner, a fifth grader, and a tenth grader at once, the strongest
multi-age design in the category. It is open-and-go for the parent, the
geography integration is unusually good, and the author-narrated audiobook
covers car days. For families leaving SOTW over worldview, MOH is the named
destination.

## Why families leave it

Secular and religiously moderate families never start, or leave once the
providential framing intensifies. The difficulty ramp is real: Volumes III and
IV outpace younger siblings, forcing families back into split-age teaching
just when the multi-age promise mattered. Some parents object to mature
content in later volumes (conduct and disease details they consider gossip),
especially on audio where skipping is awkward. Sourcing the recommended
supplemental books takes work. And students who want a more academic, less
devotional voice in high school move to Notgrass, Sonlight levels, or dual
enrollment.

## Competitive notes for Homa

MOH's three-tier activity system (same lesson, Younger, Middle, Older outputs)
is the single best pattern in this category for Homa's materials generation:
age-differentiated artifacts from one shared content moment is exactly what an
AI assistant can do dynamically and per-child rather than from a static menu.
Vocabulary to know: volume numbers with era nicknames (MOH 1 equals ancients
plus Bible), quarters (audio and folderbooks ship by quarter), pretests,
memory cards, and the Younger/Middle/Older labels. When a family says they use
MOH, Homa should assume a Christian worldview posture in history materials
(creation-era dating, Bible-as-history framing) unless told otherwise, and
should never generate content that contradicts the family's curriculum
theology unprompted. The gap MOH leaves: assessment and writing support for
the high-credit path is thin, the modern volume ends at 2014, and parents
juggling the giant Companion Guide PDF would benefit from Homa picking that
week's activities for each child automatically.
