# TruthQuest History

## Snapshot

TruthQuest History, by Michelle Miller, is not a curriculum so much as eleven
guidebooks for building your own literature-based history from a Christian
worldview: each guide is chronological commentary (lively, informal, aimed at
the student) stitched between exhaustive annotated living-book lists, with
ThinkWrite essay prompts scattered through. Three American History for Young
Students guides serve grades 1-5; eight core guides (Beginnings, Ancient
Greece, Ancient Rome, Middle Ages, Renaissance and Reformation, and Age of
Revolution I-III) serve grades 5-12. Families source the books themselves,
typically from the library, and often read a spine like Story of the World or
A Child's History of the World alongside. The providential framing is the
whole point: history is presented first as what God does, then as human
response.

## Reputation and rankings

Cathy Duffy reviews it warmly as the solution for parents who want a
real-books history but fear going guideless, and Charlotte Mason and classical
communities have recommended it for two decades. Fans praise the commentary's
connective tissue and the sheer depth of the book lists (with reading levels
and even page ranges noted per title). Critics, including otherwise
sympathetic reviewers, find the exclamation-heavy conversational voice grating
or condescending, note that the guides are unscheduled (all assembly required:
no lesson plans, no daily rhythm), and observe that the burden of sourcing
dozens of out-of-print titles falls on the parent. It is a small, stable,
single-author operation with a devoted but niche following; optional lapbook,
notebook, and map/timeline companions come from A Journey Through Learning.
