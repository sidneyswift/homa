# Notgrass History

## Snapshot

Notgrass History is a family-run publisher (Ray, Charlene, and the Notgrass
family) whose full-color narrative history courses dominate the
Christian homeschool market for middle and high school. Its signature is the
integrated credit stack: a high school course like Exploring America, used as
written, earns one year each of history, English, and Bible from a single box.
Courses are one-year, era-complete surveys rather than a multi-year
chronological cycle, which is why families who spent elementary in Story of
the World or Mystery of History often land here when transcripts start to
matter. The house style is warm, devotional, and open-and-go.

## Reputation and rankings

Cathy Duffy reviews the major courses at length, frames them as effectively
unit studies, and notes they suit families wanting a "biblical Christian
(Protestant) worldview and a conservative, limited-government philosophy".
Forum and blog sentiment is strongly positive for grades 5-12: the easiest
rigorous, transcript-ready history year with no planning. Critical threads:
the worldview is pervasive (a dealbreaker for secular families), the reading
and writing load is heavy for reluctant readers, and the literature picks skew
older and gentle. Trajectory is stable to rising; editions keep refreshing
(Exploring America's 5th edition shipped in 2026).

## Pedagogy and method

Traditional textbook pedagogy wrapped in unit-study integration. Each course
is 30 units of five lessons (three in elementary); a lesson is a few pages of
narrative followed by an assignment menu. From Adam to Us rotates lesson types
across biographies, landmarks, daily life, natural wonders, and key events.
Workbooks or lesson review books carry comprehension; writing assignments,
Bible study, and assigned literature carry the integration. High school
students self-run: read the lesson and primary sources, complete weekly
writing, take quizzes and exams every five units. The parent schedules and
grades rather than teaches, making Notgrass one of the lowest-lift options for
upper grades. No placement machinery: pick the course for the grade band and
go.

## Structure and scope

The lineup as of Jul 2026, one year each:

- Elementary (grades 1-4): Our Star-Spangled Story (US history, 90 lessons,
  $95 package), plus Our 50 States and Our Great Big World.
- Middle (grades 5-8): America the Beautiful (US history, 2nd edition, $125
  package, $221 bundle) and From Adam to Us (world history, $125 package, $246
  bundle with its ten literature titles).
- High school (grades 9-12): Exploring America (5th edition 2026, $125
  package, $275 bundle), Exploring World History, and further Exploring
  courses (government, economics, world geography).

A curriculum package is typically two hardcover text volumes, a primary-source
reader (We the People, American Voices, Our Creative World), map and timeline
books, and an answer key; student workbooks (younger), lesson review books
(older), and literature packages are add-ons. The publisher posts full sample
units for every course.

## A lesson up close

A middle school day with America the Beautiful: the student (or parent aloud)
reads a richly illustrated lesson of about 15 minutes on one story, place, or
person. The lesson ends with a short assignment block: a Thinking Biblically
prompt, vocabulary, a map exercise in the Maps book, a timeline entry to copy
in, sometimes a literature reading, and an optional hands-on family activity.
A workbook page (grades 5-6) or lesson review questions (grades 7-8) close the
loop, with unit tests available. The high school rhythm swaps crafts for
primary-source readings, weekly essays or projects, and exams every fifth
unit; the text tells students exactly which assignments earn which of the
three credits. A completed week yields marked maps, timeline entries, written
answers, and progress through a real novel.

## Materials and visual design

Full-color hardcovers dense with photographs, paintings, and maps, closer to a
coffee-table survey than a school textbook; the design language is
patriotic-pastoral (quilts, landscapes, monuments) and signals wholesome
traditionalism. Consumables are functional softcovers. Audio support exists
for some courses (MP3 lesson readings for From Adam to Us, song recordings for
the elementary course). Everything ships complete: answer keys, schedules, and
credit accounting included, and the full bundle is a heavy shelf of books.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026, direct from shop.notgrass.com: curriculum packages $95
(elementary) to $125 (middle and high school); student review packs about
$20-24; literature packages $70-130; complete bundles $221-275. Texts are
reusable for siblings; only workbooks are consumable. New editions
periodically break compatibility (5th edition Exploring America components do
not work with earlier editions), which irritates the resale market but keeps
content current.

## Why families choose it

One box, three credits, no planning: the strongest transcript story in
homeschool history. The narrative voice keeps middle schoolers reading without
a parent performing. Bible integration is genuine rather than decorative. Free
full-unit samples and a money-back guarantee lower the risk. For families
exiting elementary cycle programs, it is the obvious next step that still
feels homeschool-native.

## Why families leave it

Secular and non-Protestant families exclude it on worldview. Reading-heavy
courses grind down struggling readers, and the high school writing load pushes
some to cut components, losing the credit integration that justified the
price. Families wanting the four-year world chronology go to BiblioPlan,
Tapestry of Grace, or MOH volumes. Students bound for AP or dual enrollment
sometimes want a more secular academic register late in high school.

## Competitive notes for Homa

Notgrass owns the upper-grades pain point Homa will hit with every
long-horizon family: credits, transcripts, and proof of work. Homa should know
that a family saying "she's doing Exploring America" implies English and Bible
coverage on the transcript too. The credit accounting pattern (this assignment
feeds this credit) is worth copying in Homa's progress surfaces for older
kids. Gaps Homa can fill: grading and feedback on the many written assignments
(the real parent burden), pacing recovery when a heavy unit slips, support for
the reluctant readers the courses lose, and a secular equivalent of this
structured path, which barely exists at this polish level.
