# Lovevery Play Kits

## Snapshot

Lovevery is not a curriculum, but it functions as tot-school
infrastructure in enough homeschool homes to belong in this category's
map. Founded in 2015 by Jessica Rolph and Roderick Morris in Boise,
Idaho (a certified B Corp), it sells Montessori-inspired, stage-based
play kit subscriptions: $80 kits every two months for ages 0-12
months, $120 kits every three months from age 1, with a 2026 series
(The Connector, The Examiner, The Persister, The Planner) extending
coverage to ages 4-5 around executive function skills. Each kit pairs
heirloom-grade wooden toys with a parent play guide explaining the
developmental window and suggesting activities, plus a companion app.
CNBC reported $226 million in 2023 revenue, and the kits hold strong
resale value in parent marketplaces. Homeschool mothers of toddlers
treat the guides as a done-for-you developmental scope and sequence:
the kit tells them what this age needs, which is precisely the
reassurance job preschool curricula sell.

## Reputation and rankings

Mainstream press and parenting media review it as the premium standard
for developmental toys; homeschool bloggers describe using kits as the
backbone of tot school alongside read-alouds. The consistent criticisms
are price (a luxury against ordinary toys), all-or-nothing kits (no
per-toy choice), and the honest observation that household items build
the same skills for free, an argument its own fans concede while
renewing anyway. In homeschool terms it is adjacent product, not
curriculum: no academics, no lesson plans, no letters or counting
sequence, and families layering academics add a real program (Playing
Preschool and Before Five in a Row are the common pairings).
Trajectory: rising, with the 2026 age-4-5 expansion moving it directly
into preschool-curriculum shopping conversations.
