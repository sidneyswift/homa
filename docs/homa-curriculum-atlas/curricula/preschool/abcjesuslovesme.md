# ABCJesusLovesMe

## Snapshot

ABCJesusLovesMe (ABCJLM) is the free Christian preschool curriculum: a
five-level program for ages 1 through 5 (levels named 1 Year through 5
Year), each level giving 36 weekly lesson plans that braid Bible
stories, academics, and developmental skills into play and daily life.
Heidi Franz, a former classroom teacher, built it to prepare her son,
who had open heart surgery at six days old and years of developmental
therapies, for kindergarten, and published it free online on April 26,
2008. It has since grown into a ministry shipping to over 80 countries
(publisher claim), with printed curriculum guides and workbooks, a
Bible curriculum for churches, and a paid third edition, while the
original spirit survives as the free Basic Curriculum. In budget-
conscious Christian homes it is the standing answer to "what can I do
with my 3-year-old that costs nothing," and its 4 Year level doubles
as many families' kindergarten-prep year.

## Reputation and rankings

Cathy Duffy reviews it favorably as a comprehensive, research-based,
standards-aligned program equally at home in family rooms, church
preschools, and daycares, noting that the classroom-capable design
means more preparation and resource-gathering than homeschool-only
programs. Community sentiment is warm and ministry-flavored: families
praise that it is genuinely free, that Jesus is the focus rather than
a veneer, that supplies are household items and library books, and
that weekly objectives tell an unsure first-time parent what to teach
when. The recurring criticisms: the website's volume of links and
ideas overwhelms newcomers, the free lessons require real planning
(printing, supply gathering, library holds, looking ahead), the
recommended extras (Through the Bible in Felt flannel-board sets,
specific books and CDs) add cost to a "free" program, and the design
aesthetic reads dated next to Instagram-era competitors. The third
edition (a full rewrite Franz shipped level by level) modernized the
paid product; the free version remains a limited first-edition
snapshot. Trajectory: stable ministry with a loyal base, invisible in
secular circles by design.

## Pedagogy and method

Intentional, gently structured, Bible-first early learning. Each
level's 36 weeks follow a school-year arc (starting late August so
Bible themes line up with holidays, though any start date works) and
can run one to five days per week. Learning is play-based and woven
into daily routines: songs in the car, counting at the grocery store,
motor skills in the yard. The academic thread is conventional
readiness content: letters (upper and lowercase) and sounds, numbers,
shapes, colors, sorting, patterns, fine and gross motor work, with
writing arriving in the 4 Year level (name, letters, numbers, plus
life skills like address, phone number, and shoe-tying) and the
5 Year level functioning as pre-K or kindergarten with supplements.
The Bible thread is chronological stories (Genesis onward in 3 Year),
weekly memory verses, Bible songs, and character material (4 Year
centers on the Fruit of the Spirit). Assessment is informal; the
ministry's stated goal is equipping adults to be intentional, and its
planner product (Be Intentional Planner) sells that verb.

## Structure and scope

- Five levels by age: 1 Year (12-24 months) through 5 Year, each 36
  weekly lesson plans spanning 9-12 months of use.
- Free Basic Curriculum: first-edition weekly plans hosted online for
  immediate-family use, with links to printable activity sheets, site
  pages, and outside resources (some of which are purchases).
- Paid 3rd edition Complete Curriculum per level: a printed curriculum
  guide ($55) and consumable workbook ($30), bundled at $95 with
  digital downloads (Bible story visuals, Bible song cards, poem
  cards); adds themes, scope and sequence, daily schedules, weekly
  supply lists, and teaching tips the free version lacks.
- Historical rollout, per the publisher: 3 and 4 Year first (2008),
  printed books 2010, workbooks and 2 Year 2011, 5 Year 2012, 1 Year
  2016, Bible Curriculum for churches 2018, 3rd editions from 2019
  onward.
- Supporting products: handwriting curriculum ($40), visual perception
  workbook ($30), unit studies ($3-10), and annual licenses for
  preschools, churches, and daycares (from $156 first year for up to
  9 children, renewals discounted).
- Supplies are deliberately ordinary (magnetic letters, sidewalk
  chalk, craft sticks, cotton balls); weekly supply lists sit at the
  end of each lesson plan, and the publisher tells families to
  substitute freely.

## A lesson up close

A week, not a day, is the unit. The parent reads the weekly plan (a
Bible story and verse, a letter, a number, a color or shape, songs and
rhymes, a book of the week, plus a menu of fine motor, gross motor,
art, and life-skill activities), gathers what she will actually use,
and spreads it across however many mornings the family does school. A
day might be ten minutes of Bible story and song, a letter craft, a
counting game, and reading the book of the week before nap. Paid-
edition users work from the printed guide and have the child complete
a few workbook pages across the week; free users click through lesson
links and print what they want. Output is crafts, workbook pages at
the paid tier, and memory verses recited; nothing is graded. Veterans'
advice in reviews is consistent: treat it as a menu, prep in weekly
15-minute sittings, and do not attempt everything.

## Materials and visual design

Functional ministry design rather than brand aesthetics: the site is
dense with text links, the printed guides are workmanlike, and the
workbooks are simple consumables. The 3rd edition added colorful poem,
shape, color, and song posters and refreshed visuals, but the overall
signal remains church-basement practical: this is a program bought
for content and conviction, not for how it photographs. That
practicality is part of the trust: nothing about it feels like it is
selling a lifestyle.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026: the Basic Curriculum is free online for family use.
The 3rd edition Complete Curriculum bundle is $95 per level ($55
guide, $30 workbook, $5-10 digital downloads a la carte); Bible
Curriculum bundle $100; handwriting $40. Non-family use requires
annual licenses: $156 first year for up to 9 children per product,
scaling by headcount, renewals roughly $47 and up. Workbooks are the
only consumable; guides reuse across siblings. The free tier plus a
library card remains a genuinely $0 preschool, which is the program's
load-bearing fact.

## Why families choose it

- Free, and free without a catch for home use: the default when the
  budget is zero and the family wants structure anyway.
- Bible-first: parents who want faith formation as the point, not a
  supplement, get exactly that.
- Confidence scaffolding: weekly objectives, age-banded levels, and
  supply lists tell an anxious first-timer what "enough" looks like.
- Scales down gracefully: one to five days per week, activities
  swappable for what is already in the house.

## Why families leave it

- Planning fatigue: the free version's click-print-gather loop wears
  on parents, who move to open-and-go products (TGTB Preschool, boxed
  kits) once budget allows.
- Overwhelm: the sheer volume of ideas and links makes some families
  bounce in week one.
- Aesthetic and tone mismatch: Instagram-era parents often find the
  materials dated and migrate to The Peaceful Preschool or Playing
  Preschool, keeping only the Bible thread.
- Secular disqualification: the Bible integration is total, so it is
  never a candidate for secular families (its free-tier competitor
  there is Reading the Alphabet plus library lists).

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: ABCJLM (the community says the acronym), levels
  named by age (2 Year, 3 Year), Basic vs Complete (free vs paid
  editions), Be Intentional Planner, Through the Bible in Felt. A
  family "doing 3 Year" is running a structured-but-gentle Christian
  preschool on a near-zero budget.
- The free tier proves the price floor of this category is $0, and
  that a large Christian segment will trade prep labor for money.
  Homa cannot win this family on content price; it wins on removing
  the click-print-gather loop the free tier imposes.
- The weekly-objective format (one letter, one number, one verse, one
  book, activity menu) is a proven low-anxiety planning shape for
  ages 2-5. Homa's preschool planning surface should be able to emit
  exactly this shape, with the family's own faith content slotted in.
- Homa must handle Bible-thread requests gracefully for this family
  (memory verse copywork, Bible story coloring pages) while keeping
  its own generated academics worldview-neutral, the same posture the
  atlas records for neutral-math publishers.
