# Playing Preschool

## Snapshot

Playing Preschool is the dominant secular homeschool preschool program:
a two-year, 190-days-per-year, play-based curriculum for ages 2.5 to 5,
sold as a PDF ebook for $39.90 per year. Susie Allison, a former
kindergarten and first grade teacher with a Master's in Early Childhood
Education who also worked as a math and reading curriculum specialist
for a district outside Seattle, wrote Year 1 for her own family and
launched it in 2017, with Year 2 following in 2018. Her Busy Toddler
platform (over 3 million social media followers) made her the most
visible voice of the "play is enough" school of early childhood, and
the program is the direct product of that stance: no worksheets, no
desk time, hands-on activities built from household items, anchored in
two-week themed units. It sits at or near the top of nearly every
current preschool curriculum roundup, secular and Christian alike, and
is a Cathy Duffy Top Pick.

## Reputation and rankings

- Cathy Duffy reviews it warmly and includes it in her 103 Top Picks,
  crediting it with building "listening, thinking, and speaking skills"
  ahead of formal academics.
- Roundup dominance: it is the "best overall" pick in Such a Little
  While's 2026 preschool list, first in multiple expert roundups, and
  present in essentially every "homeschool preschool" listicle in both
  worldview universes. Its named competitors in those roundups are
  Blossom and Root Early Years, The Peaceful Preschool, and The Good
  and the Beautiful Preschool.
- Community sentiment is unusually loyal: parents credit it with
  making at-home preschool feel doable, and grandparents and working
  parents appear often in testimonials. The publisher's claim that it
  is among the most popular home-learning curricula is self-reported
  but consistent with its roundup footprint.
- The standing criticisms, from long-form reviews: activities run too
  easy for older or advanced fours and fives (the fix families use is
  skipping and condensing); the suggested "picture walk" pre-reading
  ritual bores some kids; two weeks on one theme feels redundant to the
  adult by day seven; and while prep is light, it is not zero, so
  parents expecting pure open-and-go are surprised by the gathering
  and library-hold routine.
- Trajectory: stable-to-rising. The recent launch of Offshoots (16
  units as of May 2026; see Structure) extended the brand into
  elementary, which keeps Playing Preschool alumni in the ecosystem.

## Pedagogy and method

Play-based early learning by a credentialed insider. Allison's public
position, repeated across Busy Toddler, is that preschoolers learn
through play and daily life, not worksheets, and the program is
explicitly marketed as having none. Learning is activity-based
(sorting, hunting, measuring, experimenting, games), language-heavy
(read-alouds with discussion, songs, poems, guiding questions), and
sequenced: the publisher stresses that units build in complexity, so
Year 1 Unit 1 math works numbers 1 to 5 while Unit 19 works 1 to 12
and joining groups. Phonemic awareness is woven through both years,
and Year 1 was designed backward from what a kindergarten teacher
hoped incoming students would have. The program deliberately does not
teach reading, writing, or handwriting; letters are taught as
meaningful tools (letter recognition, sounds, an alphabet portfolio
art project per letter) rather than drilled. Lessons are not scripted,
but plans include leading questions, and parents are told to adapt
freely. A day takes about 45 to 60 minutes and can be split into
chunks. There is no assessment anywhere in the program.

## Structure and scope

- Each year: 19 thematic units, two weeks each, five days per week,
  190 daily lessons, delivered as a 300+ page PDF.
- Year 1 units, in order: Apples, Colors, Nursery Rhymes, Clothing,
  Food, Five Senses, Teddy Bears, Things That Go Together, Community
  Helpers, Transportation, On the Farm, Weather, Eggs, Plants,
  Construction, Pets, Opposites, Fairy Tales and Folk Tales, Water.
- Year 2 units, in order: All About Me, Animals, Alphabet, Blocks,
  Environment, Numbers, Systems, Shapes, Solids and Liquids, Maps and
  Directions, Balls, Size, Patterns, Magnets, Texture, Science
  Experiments, Health, Rhyming Words, Rocks.
- Each unit is linked to one or two alphabet letters, sometimes
  matching the theme (A with apples, F with farm) and sometimes not
  (I taught during the water unit).
- Year 1 skills: letter names and sounds, story recall and retelling,
  predicting, sorting and classifying, shapes, counting and number
  recognition, object graphing, measurement, plus a daily calendar
  routine (days, months, weather, with songs).
- Year 2 skills: letter-sound review via a second alphabet portfolio,
  reading comprehension (sequence, cause and effect, compare and
  contrast, story elements, prediction, vocabulary), counting to 20,
  one-to-one correspondence, more/less/equal, beginning addition and
  subtraction, patterns, ordering numbers, and school skills (cutting,
  gluing, folding, tracing, pre-writing). Year 2 assumes Year 1 is
  complete, and the publisher tells families not to skip Year 1 even
  with older children.
- Components per year: the coursebook, a printable alphabet-portfolio
  file (the closest thing to worksheets), weekly overviews, bi-weekly
  supply and book lists, and a 20+ page Start-Up Guide (Year 1) or
  Introduction to Learning (Year 2).
- Books: six suggested library titles per unit, nearly all
  substitutable with any book on the theme; only a few activities
  depend on a specific title.
- Brand extension: Offshoots (offshootslearning.com), Allison's
  elementary unit-study program for ages 6 to 12, launched with 16
  four-week units as of May 2026 at $14.95 per unit with bundle
  discounts. It is the designated next step after Playing Preschool
  and belongs to the atlas's elementary categories, not this profile;
  it matters here as proof the brand retains families past preschool.

## A lesson up close

The parent works from a weekly chart plus a one-page daily plan. Each
unit opens with an overview, supply list, six-book reading list, and
the unit's poem and song; each week's chart carries unit goals and
guiding questions at the top. A day has a fixed rhythm: the calendar
routine, the unit song and poem, a read-aloud from the theme list,
"things to talk about" (discussion connecting the story to the theme
and the day's activity, introducing vocabulary), one learning activity
(the core skill work: a sort, a hunt, a measuring task, a game, an
alphabet portfolio page), and one easy activity (a lighter, often
open-ended reinforcement, like the famous egg-off-the-wall Humpty
Dumpty experiment Cathy Duffy describes). Weekly columns rotate skill
emphases; in Year 1, Monday is predicting and sorting, Tuesday letter
names and numbers, Wednesday predicting and measuring, Thursday
letters and numbers again, Friday recall-retell and shapes. Regular
supplies are deliberately mundane: construction paper, sticky notes,
tempera paint, cotton balls, dry rice, dice, butcher paper, and a
28-quart storage bin as the sensory table. A completed day produces
conversation and played-with stuff, not paper; over a year the only
artifact is the alphabet portfolio. Reviewers consistently report the
day runs 25 to 60 minutes depending on the child's appetite.

## Materials and visual design

A digital-first ebook designed to be read from a tablet or phone, not
printed (the publisher says only the alphabet pages need printing).
Layout is clean, friendly, and text-forward with photography in the
Busy Toddler Instagram style: real kitchen tables, taped paper, dot
stickers, rainbow manipulatives. The aesthetic signals "a teacher mom
you follow online," approachable and unfussy, the visual opposite of
both boxed-kit polish and Charlotte Mason botanical softness. The free
Unit 1 sample on the publisher site shows the exact page anatomy
(supply list, overview, poem and song page, daily plan pages).

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026: Year 1 is $39.90, Year 2 is $39.90, and the two-year
bundle is $69.80, all non-refundable PDFs sold directly at
playingpreschool.com / shop.busytoddler.com. Marketing leans on the
"less than $1 per week" frame. There is nothing else to buy: supplies
are household items, and books come from the library. The PDF license
covers the family, so siblings reuse it free, and the program is
"evergreen" (start any date). Resale is not a factor (digital,
non-transferable); the low price does the same job.

## Why families choose it

- Price: a full preschool year for under $40 makes it the lowest-risk
  formal purchase in homeschooling.
- Credibility: written by a former kindergarten teacher with a
  curriculum-specialist background, which lets parents trust that play
  is covering the right skills.
- Philosophy fit: it validates the anti-pressure instinct. Parents who
  feel guilty about worksheets get a credentialed permission slip.
- Kindergarten readiness without academics: the program promises the
  skills K teachers actually want (attention, language, counting,
  letter familiarity) rather than early reading.
- Household-item supply list and library books: no kit to wait for,
  nothing expensive to store.

## Why families leave it

- The child ages out or runs ahead: bright fours find Year 1 too easy,
  and families either condense units or jump to kindergarten programs
  (The Good and the Beautiful K, Math with Confidence K, 100 Easy
  Lessons for reading).
- The no-writing stance: families wanting handwriting or phonics add a
  supplement mid-stream (reviewers commonly pair Year 2 with a pre-K
  handwriting workbook) and sometimes just switch to a skills-forward
  program like TGTB Preschool.
- Repetition fatigue: two weeks per theme wears on parents even when
  it works for the child.
- Prep friction: light but real (library holds, supply gathering).
  Parents who want a boxed, zero-decision day move to kits.
- Structure seekers: parents who need visible output (pages completed,
  boxes checked) never feel "done" and drift to workbook programs.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: Busy Toddler, Year 1 and Year 2, units (two-week
  themes), the alphabet portfolio, "picture walk," the calendar
  routine, Offshoots (the elementary follow-on). A family saying "we
  do Playing Preschool" is almost certainly Homa's youngest cohort:
  child 2.5 to 5, parent Instagram-native, allergic to drill, price
  sensitive, and pre-committed to play-based values.
- The emotional register is the lesson: this segment buys reassurance
  that play counts. Homa's preschool-age materials must never look
  like worksheets by default; activity cards, scavenger hunts, and
  sensory-bin recipes from household items are the native genre.
  Homa's activity generation is a direct complement here, and a direct
  competitor to the $39.90 one-time purchase, so Homa's version must
  win on personalization (this child, this week, these interests).
- The sequencing claim is the moat Allison built against free
  Pinterest ideas: parents pay for the guarantee that activities build
  toward readiness. Homa should make the same guarantee legible when
  it generates activities (name the skill, name what comes next).
- The evidence gap: the program produces no record of the child at
  all. Homa can quietly turn the same played days into a readiness
  narrative (what she counted, what letters she knows) without ever
  testing the child, which is exactly the reassurance this buyer
  wants at kindergarten-decision time.
- The brand's arc (preschool ebook, then Offshoots for elementary)
  shows this audience stays loyal to trusted early-years voices as
  kids age into Homa's core 2-8 band. Meeting a Playing Preschool
  family well at 3 positions Homa for the whole run.
