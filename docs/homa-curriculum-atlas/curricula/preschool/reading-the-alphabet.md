# Reading the Alphabet

## Snapshot

Reading the Alphabet is the free pre-reading curriculum of the
printable-blog economy: a letter-a-week program from Becky Spence of
This Reading Mama, built for preschoolers who already know most letter
names and sounds but are not ready for a full kindergarten phonics
program. Each weekly lesson (letters run in a custom order starting
with T, not ABC order) packs a five-day plan around seven strands:
applying letter sounds, one beginning sight word per letter, phonological
and phonemic awareness, book and print awareness, fine motor work,
numbers 1-20 and patterns, and CVC word-family reading once short a
arrives. Every week includes a printable emergent reader plus picture
cards, games, and cut-and-paste activities; an early reviewer counted
roughly 1,500 printed pages across the program. Spence, a homeschool
mother of four who tutored struggling readers and became certified in
dyslexia and language-based learning differences in 2019, has blogged
free literacy materials since 2011; her free follow-on, Phonics by The
Book, continues into beginning readers.

## Reputation and rankings

A quiet workhorse rather than a brand: it spreads through Pinterest,
free-curriculum roundups, and special-needs communities, where its
slower pace and multisensory activities are recommended for kids who
need more repetition than kindergarten programs allow. Fans praise
that it is genuinely free, systematic, and written by someone with
reading-intervention credibility; the friction is the printing (each
week is a download-print-cut session, laminating optional but common)
and the DIY assembly of 26+ separate lesson posts, which is exactly
what the $35 updated-and-expanded bundle sells its way out of (single
download, bonus readers, handwriting pages, sight word songs, games).
Trajectory: stable evergreen freebie; the blog model has faded from
fashion, but the links keep circulating because free plus credible
never stops being recommended.
