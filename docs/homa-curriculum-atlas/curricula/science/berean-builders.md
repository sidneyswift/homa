# Berean Builders (Dr. Jay Wile science)

## Snapshot

Berean Builders is the current publishing home of Dr. Jay Wile, the nuclear chemist who founded Apologia and wrote its original Exploring Creation junior high and high school courses. Wile sold Apologia in 2008, resigned in 2010 over its new direction, and now writes and sells his ongoing work here, which makes Berean Builders the "author's cut" brand for a meaningful slice of the Christian homeschool market. Its elementary flagship is the Science in History series (K-6), which teaches science chronologically through the history of scientific discovery, an organizing idea no major competitor uses. Junior high and high school run through the newer Discovering Design series plus Wile's advanced courses. Everything is young-earth creationist, hardback, deliberately budget-priced, and built around an experiment or activity in every single lesson. Mindshare is smaller than Apologia's but the audience is devoted, and cross-shopping between the two is a standing ritual in Christian homeschool groups.

## Reputation and rankings

- Practical Homeschooling Reader Awards: Wile's courses have won their categories for many years; the elementary series took first place in Elementary Science in the 2017 awards (per Wile's announcement), and his junior and senior high courses were perennial winners in the Apologia era.
- Cathy Duffy reviews the Science in History series in depth and treats it as a top-tier option for Christian families.
- Community sentiment: strongly positive among users; the recurring praises are "an experiment every lesson with things I actually have" and strong retention. The recurring hesitations are the history-based ordering (some parents want topic-organized science) and the plain, text-forward look.
- The defining reputation fact is the Wile-vs-Apologia split: everyhomeschool and Wile's own blog frame Berean Builders as the founder's current thinking versus Apologia's post-sale editions. Wile publishes direct comparisons of his courses against Apologia's on his blog.
- Trajectory: stable, slowly growing as the Discovering Design line fills out the high school ladder.

## Pedagogy and method

- Elementary: activity-first lessons. Each lesson opens with a hands-on experiment or demonstration using household items, then explains the science behind what the child just saw, then closes with discussion and review. Wile argues the activity creates the question the text answers.
- Multi-age by design: every elementary lesson ends with three levels of review (youngest, older, oldest students), so one book teaches a K-6 spread at once.
- History as the spine: science concepts are introduced in the order humans discovered them, from creation week (Science in the Beginning) through the atomic age. Fans credit this with making science feel like a story; it also means any given year mixes physics, biology, chemistry, and astronomy rather than covering one discipline.
- Upper levels: traditional rigorous textbook self-study in the Wile voice, with labs, tests, and a strong college-prep orientation; students are expected to work independently.
- Worldview: young-earth creationist throughout, but Wile's signature is engaging counterarguments; his texts discuss old-earth and evolutionary positions in order to argue against them, which some families prefer to silence.

## Structure and scope

- Elementary, Science in History series (K-6, one book per year, in order): Science in the Beginning (organized by the six days of creation), Science in the Ancient World, Science in the Scientific Revolution, Science in the Age of Reason, Science in the Industrial Age. Each course: hardcover textbook plus a thin softcover Helps and Hints book (answers, hints, optional tests). Six units of 15 lessons each (90 lessons, 12 of them optional challenge lessons); about 90 hours of instruction, 35 hands-on. Students write in a separate blank notebook; free printable notebooking pages exist from retailers.
- Middle grades: Science in the Atomic Age (grades 7-8 territory), bridging to high school.
- High school, Discovering Design series: Earth Science (grade 8), Biology, Chemistry, Physics, sequenced against the student's math level (biology with algebra 1), plus advanced courses (for example Advanced Chemistry in Creation) that together with the first-year course cover a university-level survey and point at AP or CLEP exams. Discovering Design with Biology: 180 hours of instruction, 36 hands-on, including four dissections and microscope work; lab kits sold by third parties.
- Recorded video classes ("audio/visual supplements") are available for several courses.

## A lesson up close

Elementary, from the parent's seat: read the short supply note, gather two or three kitchen items, run the experiment with the kids first (the book is written for the parent to read aloud), then read the explanation, which names what happened and connects it to the lesson's historical discoverer or creation-day theme. The lesson ends with narration-style discussion and the three-tier review: the youngest child answers a recall question aloud, the middle child writes a sentence or two, the oldest writes more and may take on the optional challenge lesson. A completed day produces a notebook entry per child (their own words plus a drawing), not a filled worksheet, because there is no consumable workbook. Lessons run about 15-30 minutes plus cleanup; two lessons a week finishes a book in a school year, three a week if the challenge lessons are included. High school lessons look like Apologia's pattern (independent reading, experiments, review questions, tests from the Helps and Hints or answer key) with a slightly drier, more precise voice.

## Materials and visual design

Hardcover textbooks with full-color photos and diagrams, deliberately durable so one copy serves many children; the look is clean, practical, and unflashy, closer to a trade science book than a glossy school text. Helps and Hints volumes are thin softcovers. There are no branded journals, kits, or boxed extras; the publisher leans into "budget friendly" as identity, and the visual message matches: substance over styling. The absence of a consumable layer is a real difference from Apologia's journal-heavy shelf.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026 (catalog and product pages):

- Elementary: textbook $43, Helps and Hints $6, set $49. One set covers all children in the family for the year; add only blank notebooks.
- High school: Discovering Design with Biology book $69, answer key and tests $10; lab kits priced separately by third-party vendors (Home Science Tools and others); audiobook USB $21.
- Price pages: https://bereanbuilders.com/ecomm/product/science-in-the-beginning/ and the current catalog PDF.
- Multi-kid economics are the best in the Christian science market: hardbacks, no per-child consumables, sub-$50 years at elementary.

## Why families choose it

- Wile's name and voice: trust built over two decades of Apologia-era courses, plus the sense of buying the author's current, corrected thinking.
- An experiment in every lesson with household stuff: the promise that science will actually happen.
- One book teaches all the kids: the three-level review is genuine multi-age engineering.
- Price: roughly half an Apologia year at elementary.
- Worldview with arguments: young-earth families who want their teens to hear and answer opposing views prefer Wile's engagement to sanitized silence.

## Why families leave it

- The history ordering frustrates families who want "a year of astronomy" or need topic alignment with a co-op; they move to Apologia's immersion books or topical unit programs.
- Text-plain presentation: kids hooked on color-saturated or video-led materials drift to The Good and the Beautiful or video options.
- No consumable journal means parents must run the notebook habit themselves; families who want structured output buy Apologia journals or TGTB units instead.
- Secular, old-earth, and neutral-preferring families are out of market entirely.
- At high school, families wanting live instruction or auto-grading pick Apologia Self-Paced or online academies.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary: "Science in the Beginning" (often abbreviated SITB), "Helps and Hints", "challenge lessons", "Discovering Design". A family "in unit 4 of Ancient World" is mid-year in the second elementary book.
- The three-level review is the cleanest multi-age differentiation pattern in the category: one stimulus, three graded response tiers. Homa's generated materials for multi-kid families should copy this shape.
- The activity-first lesson arc (do, then explain) is a strong template for Homa-generated science lessons; it also means supply lists are per-lesson and tiny, a pattern Homa can surface ("tomorrow you need a candle and a jar").
- Gap Homa fills: the missing consumable layer. Berean families keep blank notebooks; Homa can generate notebooking pages tuned to each lesson and each child's tier, which the publisher deliberately does not sell.
- When a family says "we do Jay Wile" or "Berean", assume young-earth settings, multi-age teaching, low budget tolerance for extras, and comfort with discussion-based assessment.
