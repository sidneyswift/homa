# The Good and the Beautiful science units

## Snapshot

The Good and the Beautiful (TGTB) sells science as a set of 19 family-style topical units for grades 3-8 (Arthropods through Wonders of Energy), designed to be taught to all the children in a family at once, in any order, at 12-14 lessons per unit with 3-5 units recommended per year. It is the science arm of Jenny Phillips' juggernaut all-in-one brand (profiled at `../all-in-one/the-good-and-the-beautiful.md`), and it inherits the brand's signature traits: startlingly low prices, fine-art visual polish, open-and-go parent scripts, and a general-Christian voice that is deliberately neutral on the age of the earth. Science is widely considered the lightest of TGTB's subject lines, a beloved add-on for families already inside the ecosystem rather than a destination science program. A high school Biology course (120 lessons) extends the line upward, and the Marine Biology unit is a free download used as the brand's science sampler.

## Reputation and rankings

- Cathy Duffy reviews the units positively as "comprehensive enough to serve as your complete science curriculum," noting the very basic Christian point of view, occasional King James Version quotes, and the explicit no-position stance on the age of the earth.
- Community sentiment tracks the brand: enormous goodwill for beauty, price, and ease; recurring critiques that science is TGTB's thinnest subject (light on depth and real experiment rigor, strongest in life science topics). Secular homeschoolers exclude it (faith content is integral); some evangelical families exclude the brand over its LDS founder (a brand-level issue covered in the all-in-one profile).
- Trajectory: stable and large on the back of the brand's growth; the science line was reformatted recently (course book plus per-child student journals replacing the older PDF-included model), which signals continued investment.

## Pedagogy and method

- Family-style unit study: one parent-led lesson serves grades 3-8 at once; younger siblings tag along informally. The course book is a full script: read this, ask this, play the video now, hand out the journal activity.
- Open-and-go is the core promise: no prep beyond gathering simple supplies; embedded videos carry anything hard to demonstrate at home.
- Charlotte Mason-adjacent softness (beautiful images, wonder, gratitude) without CM machinery; no narration requirements, no notebooking discipline. Assessment is informal; the high school biology course adds quizzes, tests, and lab reports for transcripts.
- Worldview method: God is credited as creator throughout, but datable claims are avoided entirely; the publisher explicitly leaves age-of-earth and evolution teaching to parents and churches.

## Structure and scope

- 19 units, grades 3-8, any order, 12-14 lessons each: Arthropods, Birds, Botany, Chemistry, Ecosystems, Geology, Health and the Physical Body, Health and the Human Mind, Kingdoms and Classification, Mammals, Marine Biology, Maturation and Sexual Reproduction, Motion and Simple Machines, Paleontology, Reptiles Amphibians and Fish, Safety, Space Science, Weather and Water, Wonders of Energy.
- Components per unit: one full-color Course Book per family ($19.99) containing the scripted lessons, activities, mini-books, and vocabulary; one Student Journal per child ($9.99), grade-banded (3-6 and 7-8) with activities, games, and worksheets; the 7-8 journals add student-directed Lesson Extensions for depth. Some units (Chemistry) are written for grades 5-8 only.
- Videos stream free from the publisher's site and are cued from the course book.
- Younger-family and high school options bracket the core: simpler offerings for little kids and a 120-lesson high school Biology course with labs on video, workbook note-taking, and unit quizzes and tests.
- Recommended dose: 3-5 units per year, which most families schedule as one unit per term.

## A lesson up close

From the parent's seat: open the course book to the next lesson and read straight through. The spread interleaves teacher text (read-aloud paragraphs in a serif face), boxed discussion questions, vocabulary callouts, instructions like "watch Video 4.2 at goodandbeautiful.com/science", and pointers such as "Grades 3-6: complete Journal page 12; Grades 7-8: complete pages 14-15 and Lesson Extension." Hands-on activities appear most lessons: simple demonstrations and crafts from household supplies, with any cut-out pieces printed in the course book (reusable) or the journals (consumable). The child's journal page is TGTB's signature artifact: watercolor-soft illustration, one or two activities per page (matching, labeling, short writing, a game board), generous white space, nothing drill-like. A lesson runs roughly 30-45 minutes for the whole family and produces a few completed journal pages per child. There are no tests in the units; retention rides on discussion and the journals.

## Materials and visual design

The most visually beautiful science materials in the homeschool market: fine-art and watercolor illustration, muted palette, elegant serif typography, heavy matte paper, bound course books that look like gift books. The design signals gentleness and taste, and it is a primary purchase driver (moms photograph these for Instagram). The trade-off critics name: the aesthetic can outclass the science; diagrams favor loveliness over information density, and the hands-on layer is craft-adjacent more than lab-adjacent.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026 (price hub: https://www.goodandbeautiful.com/pages/choose-science):

- Course Book $19.99 per unit, one per family, reusable. Student Journal $9.99 per child per unit, consumable. PDF versions sold at the same price points.
- A 4-unit year: about $80 in course books plus $40 per child in journals; first student about $120, each sibling about $40. The free Marine Biology download drops year-one cost further.
- High school Biology is priced separately (course book plus per-student workbook, similar order of magnitude per year).
- No kits: supplies are household-level; videos are free.

## Why families choose it

- Already in the ecosystem: the path of least resistance for TGTB language arts and math families.
- One lesson, all the kids: family-style teaching collapses the multi-kid science problem.
- Price: among the cheapest print science options per family in the market.
- The look: beauty as a feature, and kids treat the journals as keepsakes.
- Worldview safety across the Christian spectrum: God-honoring but datable-claims-free, so young-earth and old-earth parents both stay comfortable.

## Why families leave it

- Depth ceiling: science-hungry kids and STEM-ambitious parents outgrow the gentle treatment, moving to Apologia or Berean Builders (Christian) or secular spines for rigor.
- The neutrality itself: convinced young-earth families sometimes want creation taught explicitly (they go to Apologia or Berean Builders); secular families were never in market.
- Brand-level worldview friction: evangelical families who learn of the LDS founder sometimes exit the whole brand (see the all-in-one profile).
- Middle schoolers plateau: by grade 7-8 the family-style format thins out, and families want a real lab-and-test course before high school.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary: "unit" (not course or module), "Course Book" vs "Student Journal", "Lesson Extensions" (the 7-8 depth layer), "family style". A family "doing Space Science and Botany this year" is running about 26 lessons of science total.
- Patterns worth learning: the scripted family-style lesson (one parent script serving two grade bands with explicit per-band handoffs) is the best-executed multi-age script in the category; the free flagship-unit sampler (Marine Biology) is a conversion pattern worth noting for Homa's own funnel thinking.
- Gap Homa fills: depth on demand. TGTB families frequently want "more about volcanoes for my 12-year-old" without leaving the unit; Homa can generate extension material in TGTB's gentle register, plus the record-keeping the units lack (no tests, no tracking).
- When a family says "we use TGTB for science", assume: Christian-general settings with age-of-earth neutrality (do not inject either young-earth or deep-time framing unrequested), grades 3-8 kids taught together, roughly 30-minute lessons, high sensitivity to visual quality in any generated materials. Cross-reference the family's other TGTB subjects via `../all-in-one/the-good-and-the-beautiful.md`.
- Materials bar: any Homa-generated page set next to a TGTB journal will be judged on aesthetics first. This is the design-quality benchmark for the gentle-Christian segment.
