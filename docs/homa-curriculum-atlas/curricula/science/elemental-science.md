# Elemental Science (Classic Series)

## Snapshot

Elemental Science is the classical-education science curriculum: author Paige Hudson (a biochemistry graduate and homeschool mom) built the Classic Series to follow The Well-Trained Mind's trivium stages, cycling biology, earth science and astronomy, chemistry, and physics through the grammar stage (elementary), logic stage (middle), and high school (see the classical method background in `docs/homa-planning/icp-research/10-homeschool-methods-glossary.md`). Rather than writing textbooks, Hudson writes the plans: a scripted teacher guide and a student notebooking workbook wrapped around off-the-shelf spine encyclopedias from Usborne, DK, Kingfisher, and Basher. The method is her "three keys": read about science, do science (weekly scripted demonstrations), and write about science (notebooking). The publisher claims over 10,000 users, a Well-Trained Mind top recommendation, a Cathy Duffy Top Pick, and multiple Practical Homeschooling reader awards (publisher claims; the Cathy Duffy review and WTM association are independently visible). Elemental also publishes the story-based Sassafras Science line (see `sassafras-science.md`).

## Reputation and rankings

- Cathy Duffy reviews the Classic Series and lists it among her Top Picks (per the review and multiple secondary confirmations); she describes the presentation as secular while noting the spine books' evolutionary assumptions.
- The Well-Trained Mind community treats it as the default done-for-you implementation of Susan Wise Bauer's science recommendations, which is its main discovery channel.
- Practical Homeschooling reader awards: the publisher claims category favorites "several years in a row" (publisher claim).
- Community sentiment: praised for flexible 2-day or 5-day schedules, low prep, and the notebooking output; recurring critiques are the dependence on exact spine editions (books go out of print and versions must match), black-and-white workbook plainness, and demonstrations that skew simple.
- Trajectory: stable niche leader inside classical homeschooling; 3rd editions of the grammar-stage programs show maintenance investment.

## Pedagogy and method

- Trivium staging: grammar stage (roughly K-4 across the four subjects) is facts-and-wonder survey work; logic stage (5-8) re-cycles the subjects with more writing, experiments with variables, and optional origins content; high school schedules CK-12 textbooks with labs.
- The three keys per week: read (spine encyclopedia spreads), do (a scripted demonstration or experiment), write (notebooking pages, narration-style summaries, lab reports, vocabulary).
- Scripted but flexible: the teacher guide lays out either a 2-day or 5-day weekly plan; the parent reads the script, runs the demo, and assigns the pages; sessions run about 10-30 minutes at grammar stage.
- Neutral by construction: the guides themselves avoid origins claims; contested content lives in the optional pages of the (secular) spines, which lets each family lean either way.

## Structure and scope

- Grammar stage programs (36 weeks each, ages 5-10 band): Intro to Science (pre-level), Biology, Earth Science and Astronomy, Chemistry, Physics, each "for the Grammar Stage" in 3rd editions. Biology for the Grammar Stage covers the living world, animals, the human body, and plants across four units.
- Logic stage mirrors the cycle for middle school; high school programs schedule free CK-12 texts with labs and are the thinnest tier.
- Components per program: Teacher's Guide (about 220 pages, black-and-white, weekly plans and scripts), Student Workbook (about 160 pages of notebooking, lab report, and glossary pages), plus 2-4 required commercial spine books (for Biology 3rd edition: a Kingfisher animal encyclopedia, a DK human body encyclopedia, and a Basher Science paperback). Optional: experiment kits, lapbooking templates, coloring pages, extra student workbooks.
- Everything is available in print or ebook; kits ship the demonstration supplies.

## A lesson up close

A grammar-stage week from the parent's seat, on the 2-day plan: day one, read the assigned encyclopedia spread aloud (bright, image-dense commercial pages, which do the visual heavy lifting the black-and-white workbook does not), then the child narrates back while the parent writes or the child copies a summary onto the notebooking page, which is a titled page with lines and a drawing frame. Day two, run the scripted demonstration from the teacher guide (materials list, numbered steps, expected outcome, discussion questions), then complete the experiment sheet: a junior lab-report page with prediction, what-we-did, and what-happened blocks. Sessions run 20-30 minutes on the 2-day rhythm or 10-15 on the 5-day. A completed week produces two to three workbook pages; a completed year produces a bound portfolio of the child's own science writing, which is the program's real deliverable and the thing families photograph at year end.

## Materials and visual design

The Elemental-authored pieces are utilitarian: 8-by-10 perfect-bound black-and-white teacher guides and workbooks with clean, unadorned layouts and generous writing space. All color and character comes from the commercial spines (Usborne and DK saturation, Basher's cartoon-mascot science). The aesthetic message is exactly classical-homeschool: the child's own written pages, not the publisher's decoration, are the point. Kits are plain-packed supply boxes. Shelf footprint per year is light: two slim softcovers plus two or three trade books.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026 (price pages: https://elementalscience.com/collections/grammar-stage-science):

- Printed combo (teacher guide plus workbook): about $35-48 per program year (sale vs list). Ebook guide $22.50.
- Book package (combo plus the required spines): about $67-99.
- Bundle with experiment kit: about $85-135.
- Extra student workbooks about $13-16 each, which is the whole per-sibling cost; guides and spines are reusable.
- Library users can run a year for under $40 by borrowing the spines, a documented budget path.

## Why families choose it

- It operationalizes The Well-Trained Mind: WTM-convinced parents get the recommended rotation without building it themselves.
- Neutral posture: usable in mixed co-ops and by both secular-leaning and faith-based families willing to steer origins content their own way.
- Notebooking output: a year of child-authored science pages satisfies portfolio states and classical sensibilities.
- Cheap and reusable, with a genuine budget path through the library.

## Why families leave it

- Spine-edition fatigue: hunting exact encyclopedia editions (and replacing out-of-print ones) wears parents down; boxed or self-contained programs (Noeo, RS4K, TGTB) are the destinations.
- Plainness: kids who need color, story, or video in the core materials drift to Sassafras (same publisher), Mystery Science, or TGTB.
- Secular purists object to optional-ized evolution and leave for BFSU or REAL Science Odyssey; convinced creationist families conversely want explicit creation content and go to Apologia or Berean Builders.
- The high school tier is thin (scheduled CK-12), so families exit upward to Apologia, Berean Builders, or outsourced classes.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary: "grammar stage" and "logic stage" (trivium levels), "three keys", "spines" (the commercial encyclopedias), "Classic Series" vs "Sassafras". A family "doing Chemistry for the Grammar Stage, week 20" has young elementary kids about two-thirds through their chemistry year.
- Pattern worth learning: the plans-around-commercial-spines model proves families will buy structure separately from content; Homa's materials generation is structurally similar (Homa generates the wrapper around whatever the family already reads).
- Gap Homa fills: spine substitution (suggesting in-print or on-shelf equivalents when an edition is unavailable is a real, recurring pain), plus enrichment depth for kids who outpace the scripts.
- When a family says "we use Elemental", assume classical vocabulary, weekly demo-plus-notebooking rhythm, neutral origins settings by default, and an existing portfolio habit Homa-generated pages should match (titled page, lines, drawing frame, lab-report blocks).
