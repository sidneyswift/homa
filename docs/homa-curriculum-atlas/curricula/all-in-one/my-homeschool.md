# My Homeschool

## Snapshot

My Homeschool is Australia's Charlotte Mason answer to Euka: complete
Foundation-to-Year-10 programs built on Mason's gentle methods (living
books, narration, short lessons, picture study, nature study) and mapped,
year by year, to the Australian Curriculum and state syllabuses so
families can pass registration reviews without abandoning the philosophy.
Michelle Morrow, who began homeschooling four children in 2001 and ran the
long-running Homeschooling Downunder blog, founded it in 2016; her two
homeschool-graduate daughters (one a qualified teacher, one running
business and design) now help lead it. The registration-support layer is
the strategic moat: every course ships an editable Education Plan, an
ACARA and NESA aligned scope and sequence, report templates, and a "State
Rego and Planning Course" that walks parents through their application.
Cathy Duffy reviews it internationally, and it markets to New Zealand,
Singapore, and US families as well. Within Australian home education it is
the established books-first alternative to screen-first programs.

## Reputation and rankings

- Reviewed by Cathy Duffy, which is rare for a non-US program: she notes
  complete programs except math for Years 3-10, the family-reuse policy,
  and the legal-documentation article series covering Australia, the US,
  NZ, and Singapore ([Cathy Duffy review](https://cathyduffyreviews.com/homeschool-reviews-core-curricula/grade-level-packages-and-courses/grade-level-packages-and-courses/my-homeschool)).
- US and Australian blog reviews are warm about the gentleness: short
  lessons a few days per week, poetry and picture study kids enjoy, "a
  beautiful supplement" in one US eclectic family's use
  ([This Homeschool House](https://thishomeschoolhouse.com/my-homeschool/)).
- Community guides treat it as the leading offline-leaning alternative for
  families whose children rejected Euka's screen modules; the honest
  trade-off named is that parents do more of the work, including
  translating content into state evidence language themselves
  ([Homeschool Start Guide comparison](https://homeschoolstartguide.com/blog/euka-vs-simply-homeschool)).
- The company claims a 100% registration success record among its
  families; unverified, publisher claim.
- Trajectory: stable and rising gently with the Australian market and the
  global Charlotte Mason revival.

## Pedagogy and method

Straight Charlotte Mason, localised: living books over textbooks,
narration as the primary assessment, short varied lessons, habit
formation, nature study, artist and composer study, and a knowledge-rich
sequence the site now foregrounds ("knowledge rich" is doing modern
marketing work for a 19th-century method). Lessons are parent-led in the
early years and progressively more independent. From Year 5, optional
Charlotte Mason style quarterly exams (narration-based prompts, not
multiple choice) give families evidence artifacts. The Christian layer is
present but gentle; the founders describe a Christian worldview while
noting many families of other faiths adapt around it. Math is
deliberately outsourced for Years 3-10: the program includes math only
for Foundation-Year 2 and hands families partner discounts (CTC Math for
the US, Math Online for Australia, Math Buddy for NZ) rather than writing
its own.

## Structure and scope

- One program per year level, Foundation through Year 10, each covering
  English, history, geography, science, art, music, and health-adjacent
  content through scheduled living books, with math separate from Year 3.
- Each course includes an introduction, a resource checklist, a
  Registration Education Plan in editable form, an ACARA and NESA aligned
  scope and sequence, a printable and online weekly planner with links to
  all online resources, end-of-term report templates on a nine-week
  cycle, and access to the State Rego and Planning Course
  ([Year 5 page](https://myhomeschool.com/homeschooling/programs/year-five-curriculum/)).
- Books: the schedules lean on real books, a mix of purchasable titles,
  My Homeschool's own published editions, and free digital texts; the
  resource checklist is the shopping list.
- Reuse policy: a purchased year can be reused with younger siblings at
  no charge while the parent remains an active member.

## A lesson up close

A My Homeschool morning is a Charlotte Mason morning with an Australian
accent. The parent opens the online weekly planner, which lays out the
week as short subject blocks: a reading from a scheduled living book
followed by the child narrating it back, a copywork or dictation passage,
a math lesson from the family's separate program, then one of the rotating
riches (picture study of a scheduled artist, a poem, a composer, nature
journaling outside). Lessons stay short on principle, roughly 10 to 20
minutes each in the early years, and formal work runs a few focused hours
with afternoons free. The parent teaches and reads aloud in the younger
years; older students work from their own schedule with narration shifting
to written form. What a day produces is narrations, copywork, a nature
journal entry, and ticked planner boxes; what a term produces is the
report template filled from those artifacts; what registration season
produces is the Education Plan, adapted per child, submitted to the state
reviewer. The program's design bet is that Mason's artifacts, narrations
and journals and exams, double as compliance evidence.

## Materials and visual design

The aesthetic is soft, literary, and unhurried: real books, watercolour
warmth in the branding, planners designed to be printed and written on.
It reads closer to The Good and the Beautiful's fine-art softness than to
any courseware, though with a plainer, more practical Australian
sensibility and no attempt at TGTB's production spectacle. The online
layer (course pages, planner, links) is functional membership-site
design, not an app. The signal to the buyer: this is a books-and-kitchen-
table education that will still satisfy the department reviewer.

## Pricing

- Year-level programs list at about 660 AUD with sale pricing at 560 AUD
  on current pages (roughly 370-435 USD) as of Jul 2026; guides report a
  wider 330-880 AUD spread across levels and formats
  ([Year 5 page](https://myhomeschool.com/homeschooling/programs/year-five-curriculum/),
  [Homeschool Start Guide](https://homeschoolstartguide.com/blog/my-homeschool-nt-registration-support)).
- Math for Years 3-10 is a separate purchase; partner discounts of about
  50% soften it.
- Books on the resource checklist are extra, mitigated by free digital
  texts and library use.
- Family reuse across siblings while membership is active makes the
  effective per-child price fall for larger families, the reverse of
  Euka's per-child economics.

## Why families choose it

- Philosophy with paperwork: the only mainstream way in Australia to run
  a genuine Charlotte Mason education and still hand a state reviewer a
  recognisable, curriculum-mapped plan.
- Low screen, high books: the direct antidote to courseware fatigue, and
  the common landing spot for Euka refugees whose kids refused modules.
- Gentle Christian framing that neither secular-adjacent nor devout
  families find heavy-handed.
- Multi-kid economics via family reuse.
- A guided path for overwhelmed beginners: the Rego course plus a planned
  year removes the research burden that stalls new home educators.

## Why families leave it

- The parent is the engine: read-alouds, narration, and planning take
  real daily time, and burnout pushes families toward self-running
  programs (Euka, distance education).
- Registration work is guided, not done: parents must still individualise
  the plan and translate learning into ACARA-referenced evidence, which
  families in strict states find heavier than Euka's auto-reports.
- Math outsourcing means one more decision and one more subscription.
- Mason's methods do not suit every child; kids who want workbooks,
  checklists, or screens push back on narration-heavy days.
- No credentialed senior years: it ends at Year 10, so families must plan
  their own Years 11-12 route (state distance education, Euka's pathway,
  or adult-entry options).

## Competitive notes for Homa

- My Homeschool is the clearest non-US proof of the atlas's central
  pattern: philosophy programs win by translating their own artifacts
  into the state's language. Its entire moat is a mapping layer (Mason
  practice to ACARA outcome codes) plus templates. That is a data
  problem, and it is exactly the shape of work Homa's records engine
  automates; an assistant that turns narrations and nature journals into
  outcome-coded evidence would compete with the moat, not the books.
- Vocabulary when a family says they use it: year levels Foundation to
  Year 10, terms on a nine-week cycle, narration as assessment, the
  Education Plan and TLAP as registration artifacts, NESA for NSW
  families, and a separate math program (usually CTC Math or Math
  Online).
- Supplements it typically needs: math (by design), and often extra
  writing structure in the upper years, the same gap US CM programs show.
- The reuse-while-member policy is a retention design worth studying:
  membership, not content, is the thing that expires.
- For US families: it is a live example that CM-plus-compliance packaging
  travels across jurisdictions. The Homa lesson is that the philosophy
  layer stays constant while the compliance layer must be swapped per
  jurisdiction; see `../../comparisons/international.md`.
