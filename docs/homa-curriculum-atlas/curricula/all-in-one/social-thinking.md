# Social Thinking

## Snapshot

Social Thinking is the dominant commercial methodology for teaching social
cognition explicitly: how to observe, interpret, and respond in social
situations, taught as curriculum rather than absorbed. Founded by
speech-language pathologist Michelle Garcia Winner, who coined the term in
the mid-1990s, Think Social Publishing sells storybook-plus-lesson
curricula used by schools, clinics, and families for learners roughly ages
4 to 18-plus with solid language skills (autism without intellectual
disability, ADHD, and social learning differences broadly). The flagship
homeschool-relevant products: We Thinkers! (ages 4-7, five storybooks
plus unit lessons, $136.99 deluxe), the superhero-framed Superflex series
for flexible thinking and self-regulation (ages 7-10+, 2nd edition kit
$81.99, updated in 2024 with neurodiversity-friendlier language), You Are
a Social Detective, and the closely associated Zones of Regulation. The
company maintains a dedicated homeschool resources page and free article
library; its own materials-use rules are notably strict about copying.

## Reputation and rankings

- Enormous institutional mindshare: the publisher reports its work has
  reached over 2.5 million educators, clinicians, and families, and
  Superflex's Thinkable and UnthinkaBot characters are recognizable
  vocabulary in many schools. Products carry education-industry awards,
  and homeschool special-needs lists routinely include We Thinkers! and
  Superflex as the social-skills slot.
- The serious countercurrent: autistic self-advocates and
  neurodiversity-affirming clinicians argue social skills training of
  this kind teaches masking (performing neurotypical behavior at cost to
  mental health) and pathologizes autistic communication, and note the
  methodology's independent evidence base is thin relative to its reach.
  The company has responded publicly (a "respecting neurodiversity"
  position piece and the 2024 Superflex revision). Families in 2026
  encounter both the products and the debate; a growing minority chooses
  connection-first alternatives instead.
- Trajectory: stable commercially, contested culturally.
