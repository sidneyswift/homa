# Torchlight

## Snapshot

Torchlight is a secular, literature-based PDF curriculum for roughly ages
4 to 13 (Pre-K through Level 4-5, with levels released about one per
year), built around Socratic-inspired discussion and explicitly Humanist
values: the Golden Rule, questioning, equality, empathy, and scientific
thinking. It schedules diverse, modern trade books into 36-week plans
(32 for Pre-K), layers in "gameschooling," media, car-schooling, and
bedtime-story recommendations, and deliberately excludes math and reading
instruction so families can fit those to each child. In the secular
eclectic world it is the intellectual, discussion-heavy option next to
Build Your Library's Charlotte Mason warmth and Blossom and Root's nature
focus. Homa's segment research records it at $45-70 per level plus books
(`docs/homa-planning/icp-research/08-segments-curricula.md` §4).

## Reputation and rankings

No Cathy Duffy review was found as of this writing; coverage lives in
secular homeschool blogs, TheHomeSchoolMom's listing, and community
threads. A certified teacher's long-form review praises its genuinely
secular content (climate change taught at Level 1, books from many
cultures) and its critical-thinking focus. Community reputation in SEA and
r/secularhomeschool circles: rigorous and thoughtful, "known for being on
the advanced side of academics" (the publisher's own FAQ acknowledges
many families fit older kids into lower levels), with recurring caveats
about the reading load, the parent-intensity of Socratic discussion, and
levels arriving slowly. The Level 2 integration with a separately
purchased science program (Real Science Odyssey) is a noted friction.
Trajectory: stable and respected in its niche; growth is capped by the
one-level-per-year release pace and the sub-middle-school ceiling.

## Pedagogy and method

The signature is Socratic-inspired questioning instead of comprehension
questions. The publisher distinguishes it explicitly from Charlotte Mason
narration: narration aims at comprehension through the child's own lens,
while Socratic discussion aims to question that lens, testing what you
know and how you know it. Guides provide "Questioning Trees" (open-ended
question scaffolds) and learning-partner notes on the philosophy,
psychology, logic, and moral threads in each reading. The adult is styled
a "learning partner," not a teacher; the stance is "I don't know, let's
look it up." Each level adds a distinctive extra subject: emotional
intelligence in Pre-K, world mythology in Level 1, logic in Level 2,
innovation in Level 3, moral philosophy in Level 4. Scheduled literature
is meant to be read aloud, even with fluent readers, because the
discussion is the point. Time expectation from the FAQ: about 15 minutes
per year of the child's age per day. No tests or assessments exist by
design.

## Structure and scope

Levels and themes (each is a year, 36 weeks except Pre-K's 32):

- Pre-K, Curiosity and Character (ages 4-5): the only level including all
  subjects, with pre-literacy and math.
- Level K, Worldly Wisdom (5-7): world cultures, cooking, character.
- Level 1, Myth and Magic (6-8) and Level 2, Logic and Legend (7-9):
  history, mythology, science, art, music; chronological history begins.
- Level 3, Innovation and Inquiry (8-12) and Level 4, Ideas and Identity
  (9-13): history, science, writing, philosophy, game logic.

Each level's PDF bundle includes daily and weekly lesson plans with prep
pages, workbooks and appendices, extension lists per week, alternate and
additional book lists for older learners, and a year-long project. Math
and reading are excluded above Pre-K on purpose; the guides recommend
"top picks" (Logic of English is the flagged reading recommendation).
Books are sourced by the family; the publisher marks which titles are
"spines" (used weekly) versus single-read titles, maintains Bookshop.org
lists per level, and coaches buyers on library workflows (Library
Extension browser add-on, interlibrary loan). Levels roughly track grade
numbers but are designed for multi-age use.

## A lesson up close

The parent's document is the level guide PDF, designed for double-sided
black-and-white home printing. A week presents scheduled read-alouds with
learning-partner notes, a Questioning Tree for discussion, vocabulary and
(from Level 2) grammar work in the included student workbook pages,
hands-on projects or engineering challenges, art and music exposure, and
an extensions list for rabbit trails. A legend of icons flags things like
incidental religious mentions in a day's reading so the parent can decide
how to frame them. The child's day centers on being read to and talking:
the publisher recommends letting wiggly kids mold clay or doodle during
read-alouds. Output is discussion, occasional open-ended writing (with
expectations set by the parent, from dictated partner writing up to
independent work), workbook vocabulary pages, and progress on the
year-long project. Families can run it to the letter from the optional
weekly schedule or treat it as a checklist.

## Materials and visual design

Digital-only PDFs with a 60-day download window; copy-protected but
printable for household use (co-ops need licenses). The main guide prints
clean in black and white; some student books and appendices are designed
single-sided for cutting. The aesthetic is functional-modern rather than
precious; the visual richness comes from the trade books themselves,
which skew contemporary, diverse, and secular. Resale of printed copies
is allowed but tightly controlled (only to buyers who own the PDF, via
the official buy-sell-trade group), a notable example of PDF-economy
license policing.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026: levels list between about $30 and $100, rising with
level (third-party trackers record $30-74; the repo's segment research
recorded $45-70 per level). Books are extra: heavy library use is the
designed default, with Bookshop and Amazon lists for purchase. Recommended
companion programs are separate purchases (Real Science Odyssey for Level
2 science, with a publisher-provided discount code; Logic of English for
reading; any math program). Sales run 10-15% each late June and late
November. Multi-kid economics are excellent: one PDF covers the household,
and printing is the only per-child cost.

## Why families choose it

- The most explicitly secular-and-Humanist option in the category:
  religion handled academically with per-day content flags, climate
  change and standard science taught.
- Discussion depth: families who want a thinking curriculum, gifted-ish
  kids, and parents who enjoy ideas choose it over gentler options.
- Diverse modern book lists that do not need vetting.
- Cheap core plus library books; flexible across sibling ages.

## Why families leave it

- Parent intensity: Socratic discussion cannot be delegated or automated;
  tired parents drift to open-and-go or workbook programs.
- Reading load and advanced pitch: kids who dislike being read to at
  length, or who need more visual and kinesthetic variety, bounce.
- The assembly tax: no math, no reading, science partly external; the
  family is still running a multi-vendor stack.
- The ceiling: nothing above roughly age 13 yet, so families graduate
  into Build Your Library's upper levels or self-assembled middle and
  high school plans.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: learning partner, Questioning Tree, spines, level
  themes (Myth and Magic, Logic and Legend, and so on), extensions,
  year-long project, gameschooling. "We use Torchlight Level 2" implies:
  read-aloud-centered days, a separate math and reading program (ask
  which), Real Science Odyssey likely present, and a parent who values
  open-ended questioning over right answers.
- Materials lesson: the Questioning Tree is a strong pattern, a scaffold
  of branching open-ended prompts attached to a text. Homa's discussion
  and comprehension supports for literature-based families should offer
  this shape, not quiz-style questions, for this segment.
- Gap Homa fills: the discussion prep is the product, and it is also the
  burnout point. Homa can lighten the load (per-book discussion support,
  scheduling across a multi-vendor stack, tracking what the child's
  conversations demonstrate) without replacing the parent-led ethos.
  Compliance records are absent by design; Homa's evidence log serves the
  same family.
- Copyright posture note: Torchlight polices digital sharing (no public
  Trello boards); Homa integrations should never ingest or reproduce
  guide content, only the family's own schedule and observations.
