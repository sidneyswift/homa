# BookShark

## Snapshot

BookShark is the flagship secular-market literature-based box: a full-year,
all-subject curriculum built on real trade books instead of textbooks, sold
as packaged levels with a day-by-day instructor's guide. It was spun off
from Sonlight in 2014 as a separate sister company so families could buy a
"Sonlight education" without the Bible, missionary biographies, or Christian
framing. Levels run from Pre-K through Level J (roughly 10th grade), labeled
by letter and age band rather than grade. Every program is planned for 36
weeks at 4 days per week. In the secular and eclectic world it is the
default answer to "I want the box experience without the worldview," which
also makes it the most scrutinized curriculum in secular-vetting debates.
Homa's segment research places it as the anchor of the secular box universe
at $500 to $1,045 per level (see
`docs/homa-planning/icp-research/08-segments-curricula.md` §4).

## Reputation and rankings

Cathy Duffy carries a full, favorable review. She describes it as a
spin-off of Sonlight whose content has gradually diverged while the
educational approach stays identical, and classifies its approach as
eclectic and Charlotte Mason influenced. Her review notes the levels-not-
grades design and the 4-day schedule as distinctive strengths.

Community sentiment splits three ways:

- Fans praise the instructor's guides ("you just open your binder and start
  teaching" is the publisher's pitch, and reviewers largely agree), the
  book quality, and the one-binder, whole-year-at-a-glance organization
  (Texas Home School Coalition review).
- Critics describe the load as intense. A representative long-form review
  of Level B found the daily stack exhausting with multiple young kids,
  the subjects disjointed, and the real time cost above the publisher's
  claimed 1.5 to 2 hours for young levels.
- The secular-vetting fight is the defining thread. BookShark's own FAQ
  says the curriculum is "faith-neutral" and that science and history
  teach neither evolution nor creationism. Strict secular reviewers call
  this disqualifying: a 2018 secular-homeschool blog post ("Not a Secular
  Curriculum: Bookshark") and a 2025 Rabbit Hole Learning review both
  argue the history spines retain Christian-influenced framing, note the
  brand's drift from "secular" to "faith neutral" language, and flag the
  health and puberty titles as not LGBTQ-inclusive. Reviewers also note
  the oddity of marketing NGSS-aligned science as "faith neutral."

Trajectory: stable. It owns the "secular Sonlight" position, but the
faith-neutral repositioning trades trust in strict secular circles (SEA
buyers increasingly route to Build Your Library, Torchlight, or Blossom and
Root) for reach into relaxed religious families. A comparison site
documents the mirror-image disclaimers: BookShark warns parents when a book
touches religious content, Sonlight warns when one leans secular.

## Pedagogy and method

The method is Sonlight's: literature carries the content subjects. History,
geography, and culture come from a spine book plus historical fiction
read-alouds and readers, scheduled page-by-page on a daily grid. The parent
reads aloud daily, the child reads their own readers, and comprehension is
assessed through guided discussion questions, not tests or worksheets. This
is Charlotte Mason adjacent (living books, conversation as narration) but
scheduled with school-at-home precision. Skill subjects are deliberately
outsourced: language arts is tied to the readers, and math is a paired
third-party program chosen at purchase (Math-U-See, Saxon, Singapore, or
RightStart for K-8). The 4-day week is a design commitment; day five is
reserved for co-ops, field trips, or catch-up. Parent involvement is high
by design: the parent is the reader and discussion leader for most of the
elementary years.

## Structure and scope

Levels: Pre-K plus A through J, each pitched at an age range of about three
years (Level A is ages 5-7, grades K-2; Level B ages 6-8, grades 1-3), one
level short of a program per grade through 10th. The age-band design lets
families combine siblings into one content level.

An All-Subject Package contains:

- Reading with History: the instructor's guide, history spine, readers, and
  read-alouds for the level.
- Language arts: keyed to the readers. In Levels A-C the reader level is
  selectable; from Level D up, readers and LA tie directly into the history
  content and are not standalone. Handwriting Without Tears is bundled in
  Levels A-D; Spelling You See in the middle levels (publisher pages vary
  between D-G and D-H).
- Science: BookShark's own program with scheduled books, activity sheets,
  and a supplies kit for experiments.
- Math: the buyer's choice of Math-U-See, Saxon, Singapore, or RightStart
  (K-8); Math-U-See, Singapore, or Saxon at high school.
- Required resources: The Timeline Book, a markable laminated map with
  markers, and a 3-inch binder with tabs for the guides.

Everything is scheduled for 36 weeks, 4 days per week. Level themes move
through world cultures and multi-year world history and American history
sequences, paralleling Sonlight's rotation; the publisher publishes scope
and sequence charts per level rather than aligning to any single state's
standards. BookShark is
an approved vendor with many homeschool charter schools and markets ESA
eligibility state by state.

## A lesson up close

The parent's artifact is the instructor's guide binder. Each week opens
with a grid page: four day-columns crossed with subject rows, each cell
holding page numbers of the day's reading. Behind the grid sit the daily
notes: discussion questions with answers, background and context notes for
the parent, map assignments keyed to the markable map, and timeline
entries. The publisher's stated goal is zero to minimal weekend prep, and
the weekly shopping list for upcoming science supplies is printed in the
guide.

A Level B day from the child's seat: listen to the read-aloud chapter
(roughly 20 to 30 minutes), read a short passage from their own reader,
answer the parent's discussion questions from the guide, do one language
arts activity tied to the reading (copywork or dictation at this level), a
handwriting page, then science: a scheduled reading plus a hands-on
activity from the kit with its activity sheet. Math runs in parallel from
whichever program was chosen. A completed day produces conversation, one
or two consumable pages (LA, handwriting, science sheet), and occasional
map or timeline marks: deliberately thin on paper output, because
discussion is the assessment. Publisher time estimate for young levels is
1.5 to 2 hours; community reviews report the full stack runs longer,
especially with siblings on different levels.

There is no built-in testing in the core; the optional BookShark Virtual
platform (below) adds auto-graded assessments for families who want them.

## Materials and visual design

The box is mostly trade books: dozens of paperbacks and hardcovers per
level, the same editions a library would carry. The aesthetic signal is "a
library, not a classroom": no textbook look anywhere in the content
subjects. The instructor's guide is utilitarian black-and-white grid pages
punched for the included binder; families assemble it themselves (the
publisher posts an assembly video). Consumables are the LA and science
activity sheets and whatever math workbooks the chosen program uses. The
science kit is a supply box keyed to the year's experiments. The markable
map and timeline book are the two persistent artifacts a family keeps
marking all year. Box day, the unboxing ritual BookShark inherited from
Sonlight culture, is a real onboarding emotion the brand leans into.

BookShark Virtual, the optional online layer, runs on the Buzz LMS: reading
schedules, auto-graded assessments, parent dashboards. Math-U-See is fully
integrated; Saxon, Singapore, and RightStart get schedules and upload
dropboxes. Each seat covers one student plus one teacher account, and the
first three weeks of any course are a free trial.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026, from the publisher's product pages:

- Level A All-Subject Package: $915.44 (itemized retail $1,144.30), 20%
  package discount. https://www.bookshark.com/product/level-a-all-subject-package
- Level B All-Subject Package: $871.10 (retail $1,088.87).
  https://www.bookshark.com/product/level-b-all-subject-package
- Other levels sit in a similar band (roughly $850 to $1,050 on current
  product pages); high school packages discount 10% instead of 20%.

Cheaper entry points: Reading with History cores alone (15% package
discount), individual subjects, and used sets. Books and instructor's
guides are reusable across children; the consumable tax is LA and science
activity sheets plus math workbooks per child. Resale is strong and
organized (dedicated Sonlight and BookShark buy-sell-trade groups; the
repo's segment research documents 40 to 60 percent recovery on clean
non-consumable sets). Charter and ESA funds are a material purchase path:
BookShark actively markets itself as a charter vendor and ESA-eligible
purchase where state rules allow.

## Why families choose it

- The planning is done: a full year of all subjects arrives scheduled, and
  the guide really is open-and-go for most families.
- Real books instead of textbooks, and kids who get read to daily.
- The only full box sold without explicit Christian content: the escape
  hatch for families leaving Abeka, Sonlight, or church-adjacent co-op
  culture who still want structure.
- Levels combine siblings; the 4-day week leaves margin.
- Charter and ESA funds often cover it, which matters at its price.

## Why families leave it

- Cost: it is priced like Sonlight, and strict secular buyers resent paying
  box prices for a curriculum they still have to vet book by book.
- Read-aloud burnout: the daily reading load is the single most cited
  strain, especially for parents of multiple young kids.
- The secular-vetting verdict: families who wanted truly secular content
  discover the neutral science (no evolution) and Christian-influenced
  history framing and move to Build Your Library, Torchlight, or Blossom
  and Root, or assemble their own stack.
- Disjointedness: it is parallel curricula in one box, not integrated unit
  study; families wanting integration move to Moving Beyond the Page or
  unit-study systems.
- Checkbox guilt: the daily grid turns into a compliance document for some
  families; skipping days visibly "breaks" the guide.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: Levels (letters, age bands, not grades), Reading with
  History, IG (instructor's guide), All-Subject Package, box day, 4-day
  schedule, Virtual. When a mom says "we're in BookShark D," Homa should
  hear: age 8-11 band, American-history-era content, LA tied to the
  readers, and an unknown math brand. Always ask which math; BookShark
  families by construction run a separate math program.
- The weekly grid is a materials pattern worth learning: parents love one
  page that shows the whole week with checkable cells. Homa's planner and
  weekly digest should be able to mirror that shape.
- The gap Homa fills: pacing relief and discussion support when the
  read-aloud load overwhelms; skill practice the literature model
  deliberately omits; honest per-book content answers ("does this title
  have religious framing?") for the secular-vetting anxiety BookShark
  itself triggers.
- BookShark Virtual overlaps Homa's planner and progress surfaces
  (schedules, assessments, parent dashboard), but it is single-curriculum
  and single-vendor. Homa's advantage is serving the family's whole mixed
  stack, which is how BookShark is actually used once families start
  swapping components.
- Sonlight is profiled separately in this folder; the two share method,
  and much community advice transfers across them.
