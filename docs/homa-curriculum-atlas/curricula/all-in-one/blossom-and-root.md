# Blossom and Root

## Snapshot

Blossom and Root is a secular, nature-based, PDF-delivered curriculum for
pre-K through grade 6, written by Kristina Garner, a former kindergarten
teacher and homeschool mom, and sold since 2015. It blends Charlotte Mason
and Waldorf influences (living books, narration, nature study, picture and
composer study, watercolor, handwork) with a STEAM science strand, and it
is one of the three names that come up in nearly every secular curriculum
roundup alongside Build Your Library and Torchlight. It is a creation
shop, not a curator: Garner writes the guides, and families print them,
pull books from the library, and assemble the rest. In Homa's segment
research it anchors the $0-200 assemble-it-yourself universe
(`docs/homa-planning/icp-research/08-segments-curricula.md` §4).

## Reputation and rankings

Cathy Duffy reviews it positively as a relaxed, flexible, interactive
program with visible Waldorf design and Charlotte Mason mechanics, and
flags its secularism as strong enough to exclude conservatively religious
buyers. Community sentiment (Reddit secular homeschool threads, review
aggregations) is overwhelmingly warm: "fully secular, a rare find for
science and literature" is the recurring compliment, along with the
diverse, global book and artist selections and the low guide prices. The
recurring criticisms: it is not open-and-go (printing, binding, prep, and
library sourcing fall on the parent), the preschool level reads
"loosey-goosey" to structure-seeking families, it currently ends around
level 6 (the full Level 6 language arts, Creativity and Community, shipped
in 2026), and there is no full math program after kindergarten.
Trajectory: rising within its niche; it keeps extending upward one level
at a time.

## Pedagogy and method

Gentle, arts-forward, and nature-centered. Charlotte Mason supplies the
mechanics (living books read aloud, narration instead of comprehension
quizzes, copywork, nature journaling, picture study); Waldorf supplies the
aesthetic and rhythm (seasonal pacing, watercolor, handwork, storytelling).
Science is the distinctive strand: hands-on STEAM units built around
observation and experiment rather than a textbook. Sessions are short (a
reported 30 to 60 minutes for the core in early years), and the design
assumes the parent teaches and adapts. Learning is demonstrated through
narration, journals, and made things, not worksheets. Multiple options per
subject and budget-conscious "use the library" paths are design features;
the author explicitly frames curriculum as supporting the parent educator
rather than scripting them.

## Structure and scope

- Early Years Vol. 1 and Vol. 2 (preschool, roughly ages 3-5): literature,
  kindness prompts, gentle math and science foundations, nature study,
  picture and composer study, art; Vol. 2 adds reading and writing
  readiness and STEM.
- Level 0 (kindergarten) through Level 6: per-level guides for language
  arts, science (STEAM), and nature study, purchasable as a full-grade
  bundle or by subject. History courses have been added progressively.
  Math is included only through the kindergarten foundations; families add
  their own math program from level 1 up. Level 6 language arts is built
  from novel studies.
- Everything is a digital PDF from the publisher's site or Gumroad store;
  no physical product exists. Book lists lean on the public library.
- No records, reporting, or accreditation; the publisher states plainly
  that compliance is the parent's job.

## A lesson up close

A typical week at the elementary levels is organized as a rhythm rather
than a daily grid: a read-aloud block with narration or discussion, a
nature study outing with journaling, a hands-on science activity from the
STEAM guide, an art or picture study block, and short skill work
(copywork, journaling prompts). The parent works from the PDF guide,
which lays out the week's readings, activity instructions, and supply
lists; the child's output is a nature journal entry, artwork, a
narration, or a small project, not a completed worksheet packet. Families
report rearranging weeks and rotating activities freely, and the guides
are written to permit that. The trade-off is visible in reviews: parents
who love planning, printing, and library trips thrive; parents who want
to open a box and point at today's page do not.

## Materials and visual design

PDF pages designed for home printing, with a soft, botanical, earthy
visual identity (the brand aesthetic matches the Wild + Free gentle
segment). Families typically print and bind guides themselves or use a
curriculum printing service; student notebooks and journals are assembled
per family. The real "materials" are library books, watercolor supplies,
and the outdoors. This is the PDF-plus-library-books economy in its purest
form: the publisher sells the plan, and the family assembles the object.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026: individual subject guides start around $24; full-grade
bundles run about $79 (kindergarten) to $139-162 (grades 3-4). Books are
extra and mostly library-sourceable. Seasonal sales (spring and holiday)
discount 20 to 30 percent, and the publisher runs a Gumroad store with
occasional free resources. There are no consumable repurchases; the PDF
license covers the buyer's own children, and per-child cost is printing.
Compare `docs/homa-planning/icp-research/08-segments-curricula.md` §4,
which recorded $40-162 per year against the boxed universe's $500-1,900.

## Why families choose it

- Genuinely secular science and literature, vetted as such in SEA circles.
- Cheap to try and cheap to run: a full year of guides costs less than one
  month of most boxes.
- Gentle and beautiful without a worldview: the Waldorf-adjacent aesthetic
  for families who do not want Oak Meadow's price or institutional shape.
- Flexibility for neurodiverse kids and ADHD parents (repeatedly cited in
  reviews): nothing breaks if you rearrange the week.

## Why families leave it

- Prep burden: printing, binding, sourcing books, and gathering supplies
  is real labor; parents wanting open-and-go move to boxed programs or
  workbook-centered curricula.
- The math hole: after kindergarten every family must run a separate math
  program, and structure-seeking families often add a formal LA program
  too.
- Ceiling: it ends around grade 6, so families age out and move to
  Build Your Library, Torchlight upper levels, or assembled middle school
  stacks.
- Kids who crave predictable checklists sometimes bounce off the loose
  rhythm.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: Early Years volumes, Level 0-6, STEAM guide, nature
  study, "book seed" budget paths, Gumroad. A family saying "we do Blossom
  and Root level 2 plus Math with Confidence" is the archetypal Homa
  beachhead family: per-child stack, library-based, secular-vetted.
- The gap it leaves is exactly Homa's shape: no math, no assessment, no
  planning surface, no record-keeping, heavy printing and assembly labor.
  Homa can generate the skill practice it omits, track progress it never
  measures, and absorb the compliance paperwork its publisher explicitly
  disclaims.
- Materials lesson: its buyers actively prefer prompts, journals, and
  observation logs over drill sheets. Homa printables for this family
  should look like nature logs and narration cards, matching the twaddle
  correction in the segment research.
- Aging-out moment (end of level 6) is a natural switching window Homa
  should recognize and support.
