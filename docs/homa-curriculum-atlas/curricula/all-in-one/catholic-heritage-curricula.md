# Catholic Heritage Curricula (CHC)

## Snapshot

Catholic Heritage Curricula is the gentle option in Catholic
homeschooling: a family-owned publisher (not a school) founded in 1993 by
Theresa Johnson, launched on Nancy Nicholson's phonics program Little
Stories for Little Folks, and directed since 2016 by Johnson's daughter
RoseMary Johnson, who was herself CHC-educated from preschool through high
school. CHC sells workbooks, readers, and daily lesson plans for PreK-8
plus a design-your-own high school guidebook, with no tuition, no
enrollment, no grading services, and a stated philosophy that short,
beautiful, faith-suffused lessons prevent the burnout that heavier
programs cause. In the Catholic market's mental map it is the
counterweight to Seton: where Seton sells structure and accountability,
CHC sells peace, and switching stories between the two are the segment's
most common curriculum biography.

## Reputation and rankings

- Cathy Duffy reviews CHC's complete grade-level programs positively,
  noting the blend of traditional, classical, Charlotte Mason, and
  Montessori influences (Montessori mostly at preschool), the current
  rather than reprinted resources, and the surprisingly low cost.
- Reviewer and community sentiment is consistent: praised as easy on the
  parent, warm in tone, and genuinely Catholic without polemics;
  described as the Catholic cousin of Charlotte Mason gentleness.
- The standing criticism, in Well-Trained Mind threads and review
  roundups, is rigor: some families judge the pace below grade level in
  spots, and the do-it-yourself high school (a guidebook, not courses) is
  thin next to Seton, Kolbe, or MODG for college-bound teens; families
  often supplement math and upper-grade science.
- Trajectory: stable. CHC occupies a defended niche (gentle Catholic
  print) that the enrollment schools do not serve, and its anti-burnout,
  anti-accreditation messaging has aged well as the market grows.

## Pedagogy and method

CHC's method is gentleness with a doctrinal core. Lessons are short (15
to 20 minutes of one-on-one time per subject for young children, two to
four hours per day in total), workbook-based, and designed open-and-go
for mothers of many children. The faith is woven into academic content
naturally rather than bolted on: grammar sentences and readers carry
Catholic family life, saints, and doctrine inside the exercises. CHC
answers the "there is no such thing as Catholic grammar" objection
directly in its FAQ: the faith should be as present on the page as it is
in the home. Religion itself pairs a catechism (CHC recommends and
schedules Ignatius Press's Faith and Life series) with CHC's own
Hands-On Religion strand, activity-and-story courses meant to move
doctrine from the head to the heart. Sacramental preparation is treated
as a family milestone: Faith and Life 2 carries First Reconciliation and
First Communion prep, supported by CHC's Preparing to Receive Jesus
activity supplement. Assessment is deliberately absent before high
school: CHC tells parents that daily correction beats letter grades, and
that parent-made transcripts are legally sufficient (it researched top
colleges' admissions policies in 2023 to argue accreditation is
unnecessary).

## Structure and scope

- Levels: PreK through 8 as full programs; high school via the High
  School of Your Dreams guidebook, which walks parents through designing
  a custom program on a College, Community College/Vocational, or
  Apprenticeship/School-to-Work track, with transcript templates.
- The spine artifact is the CHC Daily Lesson Plans book per grade:
  loose-leaf, three-hole punched pages in a two-page-per-week spread,
  weekly checklist goals beside daily plans, core subjects shaded for
  priority, space for notes and read-alouds. PreK-4 plans schedule four
  days per week, leaving a flex day; kindergarten runs 30 weeks.
- Each grade has a Core Kit (CHC-published materials) plus an Also
  Required list, most notably math, which CHC does not publish; it links
  buyers to discount retailers for the scheduled program (Saxon among
  the programs CHC supports with keys; see `../math/saxon-math.md`).
- House product lines: Little Stories for Little Folks (Catholic phonics
  readers), Language of God grammar and composition (levels A-G, with
  E-G building formal essay and research skills), My Catholic Speller,
  Behold and See science (up-to-date science in a Catholic frame, with
  hands-on activities; its Life Science course recommends a compound
  microscope),
  Hands-On Religion, and newer literature and art history lines (The
  Treasure Trove of Literature, Ever Ancient Ever New).
- Free academic counseling by contact form; no grading, records, or
  transcripts by design (families wanting accreditation with CHC
  materials are pointed to umbrella options like Clonlara).

## A lesson up close

The parent opens the binder to the week's two-page spread: each subject
row holds four short daily cells plus a weekly goal checklist, with core
subjects shaded so a hard week can shrink to essentials without guilt. A
first grade morning runs a Little Stories phonics story and its
mini-book, a Language of God page (a handful of grammar items wrapped in
a story about siblings helping each other), a speller list, a math
lesson from the separately purchased program, and a Hands-On Religion
activity (coloring, cutting, a story about a saint) or a Faith and Life
lesson read on the parent's lap. Pages are colorful, lightly loaded, and
meant to be finished while the toddler naps; the fifth day is for Mass,
field trips, or catching up. What a completed week produces is a stack
of short workbook pages and a checked spread in the binder; nothing is
mailed anywhere, and nothing is graded unless the parent chooses.

## Materials and visual design

CHC materials are print-first, full color, on good paper, with classic
and devotional illustration (fine art, warm drawings, liturgical
imagery) rather than cartoons; the look is closer to The Good and the
Beautiful's softness than to Seton's density (see
`the-good-and-the-beautiful.md` for the design benchmark comparison, and
note CHC did it first in the Catholic lane). Lesson plans are utilitarian
binder pages. Workbooks are consumable, one per child, and CHC's FAQ
asks families to honor the copyright rather than photocopy. The signal
to the buyer is tenderness and order: school that will not swallow the
home.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026 (grade pages at chcweb.com are the live truth):

- CHC Daily Lesson Plans: $34.95 per grade.
- Full grade Core Kits: roughly $250-650 depending on grade, sold
  without bundle discounts so items can be swapped freely.
- Math and a few literature items are bought separately at discount
  retailers; CHC provides links and ISBNs.
- No tuition, enrollment, or service fees; CHC's own FAQ contrasts this
  with the $400-1,000 per child cost of accredited home study programs.
- Consumables (workbooks, spellers, activity books) re-buy per child;
  readers and teacher materials reuse.

## Why families choose it

- Gentleness with orthodoxy: short lessons, warm materials, and a fully
  Catholic voice without the enrollment-school workload.
- Cost and freedom: a complete grade for a few hundred dollars, no
  contracts, swap anything.
- Big-family logistics: open-and-go plans, four-day weeks, and free
  counseling fit mothers teaching several children.
- Recovery positioning: it is the standing recommendation for families
  burned out by Seton-style programs who want to stay Catholic and
  structured but breathe.

## Why families leave it

- Rigor anxiety in the upper grades: families wanting external
  accountability, honors tracks, or taught courses move to Seton, Kolbe,
  MODG, or Homeschool Connections as high school approaches.
- No records: compliance-heavy states and transcript-minded parents
  outgrow the no-grading stance.
- The gentle pace itself: quick studies need supplementation, and
  parents who equate rigor with volume read CHC as light.
- Subject gaps: math was never CHC's, and upper science usually gets
  replaced or outsourced.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: Core Kit, Also Required, Daily Lesson Plans, flex
  day, Little Stories for Little Folks, Language of God levels, Behold
  and See, Hands-On Religion, High School of Your Dreams. "We use CHC"
  usually means PreK-8, a separate math program (ask which), and a
  parent allergic to overload.
- CHC proves the gentle-Catholic segment exists and prices it: the same
  reassure-first psychology Homa's own voice rules encode. Assistance
  for a CHC family should protect the short-lesson rhythm, never pile
  on: suggest less, not more, when the week slips.
- The records gap is explicit and defended: CHC tells parents to keep
  their own transcripts and offers templates. Homa's evidence log and
  portfolio output serve exactly the family CHC leaves without paper,
  without pushing them toward accreditation they rejected.
- Materials fit: warm, full-color, lightly loaded pages with Catholic
  content woven in naturally; practice generated for a CHC family should
  match that register (and respect that religion content must be
  doctrinally safe; source from the family's own catechism rather than
  generating doctrine).
- The high school guidebook model (parent-designed, track-based) is a
  planning artifact Homa can operationalize: turning a High School of
  Your Dreams plan into a tracked four-year schedule with credits is
  clerical work the guidebook leaves to the parent.
