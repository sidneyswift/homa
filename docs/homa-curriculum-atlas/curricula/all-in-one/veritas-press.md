# Veritas Press

## Snapshot

Veritas Press is the technology-forward wing of classical Christian
homeschooling: a Lancaster, Pennsylvania publisher founded in 1996 by
Marlin and Laurie Detweiler (also founders of two brick-and-mortar
classical schools), built from a first product that still defines it, a
set of 32 chronological history flashcards. Today it spans a K-6
history-and-Bible core (cards, memory songs, teacher manuals), the Omnibus
Great Books humanities sequence for grades 7-12, the Phonics Museum
reading program, and three delivery formats for most of it: You Teach
kits, gamified Self-Paced online courses (first launched 2008), and live
online classes through Veritas Scholars Academy (VSA), an accredited
online school the founder describes as serving more than 10,000 students
(self-reported). Its cultural footprint is the "omnibus culture" of
classical homeschooling: teens reading Homer, Augustine, and Calvin in
three-credit integrated humanities courses.

## Reputation and rankings

- Cathy Duffy reviews both pillars warmly: the history courses as a
  flexible chronological program with "lovely" memory songs (noting
  sometimes awkward phrasing), and Omnibus as "high-caliber" humanities
  that a motivated parent without classical background can still teach,
  with the caveat that it was written for group discussion.
- The Self-Paced history courses are the segment's best-known example of
  screen-taught grammar-stage content done acceptably: community reviews
  and the publisher's own FAQ position them as the fix for the
  many-children-one-parent problem. Skeptical CM and purist-classical
  parents still object to daily screen lessons for young kids.
- Omnibus's reputation is rigor: hardcover 500-700 page full-color texts,
  37-week years, heavy reading loads. Common critical notes in community
  threads are workload and the strongly Reformed theological voice.
- Trajectory: stable-to-rising, with VSA's live-class enrollment as the
  growth engine and self-paced sales pushed hard (frequent $100-off
  promotions).

## Pedagogy and method

Trivium-shaped and unapologetically memory-first in the grammar years:
each history year is 32 events, one per week, memorized in chronological
order via a 10-15 minute memory song, then studied through readings,
worksheets, projects, and a test per event. Bible history and secular
history are integrated as one chronology (a Reformed classical
distinctive). At the dialectic/rhetoric stage, Omnibus flips to Great
Books seminar method: Primary courses read the era's primary sources,
Secondary courses carry lighter literature and theology, and both press
worldview analysis ("think critically... from a Christian worldview").
Delivery choice is itself pedagogy: You Teach (parent-led), Self-Paced
(video characters, interactive exercises, built-in quizzes, no homework,
no parent grading), or VSA live seminars with professional teachers.

## Structure and scope

- K-1: Bede's History of Me / of US (gentle intro years).
- Grades 2-6, one era per year: Old Testament and Ancient Egypt; New
  Testament, Greece and Rome; Middle Ages, Renaissance and Reformation;
  Explorers to 1815; 1815 to Present. Each year: 32 cards (art on front,
  date, summary, and resource list on back, color-coded across series),
  memory song, teacher manual with a worksheet, project, and test per
  card, plus a literature list (roughly 19 supplemental books in a You
  Teach kit).
- Self-Paced history/Bible (grades 2-6): about 160 lessons per course,
  30-45 minutes daily, one event per week across 32 weeks, self-contained.
- Omnibus I-VI (grades 7-12): each year has Primary and Secondary
  courses; both together earn three credits (history, theology,
  literature). I-III cover ancients, medieval, and modern before 10th
  grade; IV-VI repeat the sweep at greater depth. Self-Paced exists for
  I-III ($349 list per course, $85-190 book kits); all six run at VSA
  live.
- VSA: full accredited diploma school (Middle States accreditation),
  live sections, transcripts, an annual in-person year-end gathering.

## A lesson up close

Grammar-stage, Self-Paced version: the child logs in alone, launches the
day's 30-45 minute lesson, and is walked through the week's historical
event by recurring on-screen characters, with animated teaching segments,
interactive games, and auto-graded quizzes woven in. No homework exists by
design; the optional paper layer (flashcards, project workbook, teacher
manual, historical fiction) is explicitly not required. The parent's job
is enrollment and encouragement, a genuinely low-involvement posture rare
in classical education. You Teach version of the same week: sing the
memory song daily, read the card and its listed resources, complete the
manual's worksheet, maybe the project, test at week's end. Omnibus
version of a week: long primary-source reading assignments at home,
then either self-paced video sessions or a live seminar discussing the
text through worldview questions; output is discussion, essays, and
exams. The Omnibus texts themselves carry the reading schedule and
session questions, so the parent can run it as a book-club-with-grades.

## Materials and visual design

The history cards are the signature artifact: sturdy card stock, fine art
or photographic front, dense but tidy back matter, built to be handled
and re-sequenced for timeline drills. Omnibus volumes are heavy hardcover
textbooks with full-color art throughout, deliberately signaling
"serious school" on the shelf. Self-Paced courses have a polished
edu-game look (costumed characters, period sets) that is beloved by kids
and slightly embarrassing to purists. Print teacher manuals are
utilitarian. The overall signal is upmarket classical: production
values closer to a private school vendor than a cottage publisher.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026: Self-Paced courses list at $349 each (12-month access)
with frequent sale pricing near $249 and a 14-day free trial; course book
kits add roughly $85-190. Omnibus Primary plus Secondary in one year at
list is about $700 in courses plus kits, or $899 bundled. You Teach
history kits (cards, songs, manual, book stack) come in below the online
courses; individual card sets and manuals are purchasable a la carte.
VSA live classes are priced per course above Self-Paced (varies by
course; see the VSA catalog). Cards, manuals, and Omnibus texts are
reusable and resell well; the Self-Paced access is per student and
expires, the one non-recoverable spend.

## Why families choose it

- The one-parent-many-kids problem: Self-Paced history offloads a whole
  subject to a screen without leaving the classical worldview.
- Chronological history done for you, with memory hooks (songs, cards)
  that demonstrably stick.
- Omnibus as a credible, credit-bearing Great Books high school without
  hiring a co-op: three credits per year, texts that teach the teacher.
- VSA as the full-service option: accreditation, live teachers,
  transcripts, community.

## Why families leave it

- Screen-time conviction: families uncomfortable with daily video
  lessons for 7-year-olds go back to parent-taught history (Story of the
  World, SCM, Memoria).
- Workload and theological voice in Omnibus: some swap to Tapestry of
  Grace (more flexible), WTM-style self-built Great Books plans, or
  lighter literature programs.
- Cost stacking: $349 per subject per child per year plus kits
  approaches VSA or co-op pricing, and expiring access removes resale.
- Memory-song fatigue mirrors CC's: chanting chronology loses some kids;
  narrative-first families prefer Story of the World's storytelling.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary: the five history eras (families say "we're in Explorers
  year"), history cards, memory song, You Teach vs Self-Paced vs VSA,
  Omnibus I-VI, Primary vs Secondary, the three-credit structure. A mom
  saying "Omnibus II Secondary" has named era (medieval-Reformation),
  workload tier, and format ambiguity Homa should clarify (self-paced or
  VSA live).
- Veritas proves classical families accept self-running digital
  instruction when the worldview is trusted and the parent stays
  informed. The permission structure ("it is difficult for a parent to
  teach every child in every subject") is a framing Homa can reuse for
  its own parent-relief features.
- Gap: Veritas courses are sealed boxes; nothing adapts, and the parent
  gets little visibility into what the child actually retained beyond
  quiz scores. Weekly digest of what the child covered, plus practice
  aligned to the era week, is complementary work Homa can generate
  without reproducing card text or song lyrics.
- The 32-events-per-year skeleton is a clean, parseable scope: era plus
  week number resolves to one historical event, useful for grounding
  Homa-generated materials for these families (describe the event in
  Homa's own words; never copy card text).
