# Simply Classical

## Snapshot

Simply Classical is Memoria Press's complete curriculum line for children
with special needs, and it has essentially no competitor in its niche:
classical Christian education, adapted. Author Cheryl Swope holds a
master's degree in special education with lifetime K-12 certifications in
learning disabilities and behavior disorders, and she and her husband
homeschooled adopted twins with autism, learning disabilities, and
schizophrenia through high school; her book Simply Classical: A Beautiful
Education for Any Child is the line's manifesto. The curriculum runs from
readiness levels for skill ages 2 to 5 up through high school, pacing
Memoria's Classical Core content slower, more incrementally, and more
multisensorially, with readiness assessments replacing grade labels. The
pitch to parents is dignity: a child with Down syndrome or autism gets
Aesop, myths, Latin, and beautiful literature, not a lifetime of remedial
worksheets. See the [Memoria Press profile](memoria-press.md) for the
parent publisher's full anatomy.

## Reputation and rankings

- A Cathy Duffy Top Pick in its own right, separate from Memoria's main
  line; her review praises the step-by-step lesson plans, the playful
  multisensory techniques at lower levels, and the skill-area teaching
  tips, noting it also suits neurotypical children who want gentler
  pacing.
- Wave 1 research (see the Memoria profile) recorded that Simply
  Classical "has no real competitor in the classical special-needs
  niche," and community sentiment matches: it is the standing answer in
  classical and Charlotte Mason forums whenever a special-needs question
  appears.
- The line publishes a free Simply Classical Journal and runs an active
  online forum and Facebook community; Swope's personal visibility (con
  talks, podcast appearances) anchors trust.
- Critiques are the parent line's, inherited: heavy daily load for the
  parent, plain black-and-white materials, explicitly Christian content
  that secular families must edit around, and the underlying resources
  (Rod and Staff math, First Start Reading) are traditional and
  repetition-driven, which suits some profiles and wearies others.
- Trajectory: stable and quietly growing with Memoria's classical surge.

## Pedagogy and method

The trivium slowed down and made concrete. The method keeps Memoria's
pillars (recitation, copybook, phonics-first reading, real literature,
Latin eventually, mastery through repetition) and adapts delivery:
smaller increments, more oral work, more movement and manipulatives, two
readings where one was assumed, and explicit accommodation notes in the
teacher materials. Placement is by readiness assessment, not age: free
checklists per level cover language, cognitive ability, social-emotional
development, fine motor, and gross motor skills, and the manuals follow
each checklist with "skills to develop" teaching tips for weak areas.
Lesson plans run four days per week with an optional fifth day for review
or enrichment ("Delve Deeply"), and the lower levels include an
"enrichment or therapies" slot where a family schedules the child's own
therapy homework inside the school day, a genuinely unusual design
choice. Levels 5&6 and above ship in one-year (accelerated) or two-year
(standard) pacing so the same content can stretch to the student. The
parent teaches everything from a scripted-adjacent manual, as in the
parent line.

## Structure and scope

Named levels rather than grades, each with a themed title:

- My First Words (early language), then readiness Levels A (skill ages
  2-3), B (3-4), and C (4-5): motor skills, ear training, letters,
  numbers, manners, Bible stories, poetry set to music.
- Level 1, Sentences, Sums, and Stories (skill ages 5-6): beginning
  phonics with First Start Reading, Rod and Staff Arithmetic 1, copywork
  and recitation, and a Wonder, Beauty, and Imagination strand bundling
  literature, science, art, and music.
- Level 2, Comprehension, Calculations, and Character (6-8) and Level 3,
  Literature, Latin, and Liberty (7-9): phonics continues, Simply
  Classical adaptations of Memoria's StoryTime Treasures literature
  guides, step-by-step writing and oral spelling series, cursive, and
  Latin beginning (Prima Latina at Level 3).
- Level 4 (8-10) and combined Levels 5&6 (9-12), 7&8 (11-14), 9&10
  (13-16), 11&12 (15-18): progressively fuller Memoria Classical Core
  content (Famous Men histories, Christian Studies, Classical
  Composition, First Form Latin) at reduced pace with adapted guides.

Packages include the curriculum manual (about $30 alone, with the plans,
assessments, and notes), all student books and consumables, and teacher
keys; New User add-on sets cover items carried over from prior levels,
and optional read-aloud, supply, and enrichment sets orbit each level.
Simply Classical online classes through Memoria Academy offer small,
slower-paced live sections for single subjects.

## A lesson up close

The operating system is the same week-per-spread checkbox grid described
in the [Memoria Press profile](memoria-press.md), with more cells given
to review and readiness work. A Level 2 day, per the publisher's posted
sample plans: recitation first (prayers, poem, phonics sounds, counting,
personal information), then short blocks of intensive phonics, oral
spelling, a Rod and Staff arithmetic lesson enhanced with a living-book
counting story, copybook in manuscript or beginning cursive, and a
literature block where the parent reads Little Bear or Blueberries for
Sal and works the adapted StoryTime Treasures guide orally before any
pencil work. Teacher notes coach the accommodations (say or sing along
with the child until mastery, shorten copywork selections, accept oral
answers). The child's paper output is smaller than mainline Memoria on
purpose; the day still produces checked boxes, a copybook line or two,
and a workbook page, keeping the portfolio trail parents of IEP-refugee
kids badly want. Daily time reported by users runs one to three hours
depending on level.

## Materials and visual design

Memoria's restrained house style carried down: clean serif typography,
black-and-white consumable workbooks, fine-art plates, spiral manuals
that lie flat. Simply Classical's own additions (copybooks with shorter
selections, oral spelling cards, adapted literature guides) match the
parent aesthetic so the child's shelf looks like school, not therapy.
Lower levels lean on real objects the family gathers (sandpaper letters,
playdough, Wikki Stix, Candy Land) rather than branded plastic. The
signal to the buyer: your child gets the same beautiful, serious
education as everyone else's, slower.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026: curriculum manuals about $30; core packages roughly $270
to $400 per level at retail (Rainbow Resource listed the Level 2 core
package at $269.30 full price, with Level 3 sets around $313 to $393),
before optional sets: read-aloud packages (the Level 2 literature,
science, history, and geography set is $298.58 direct), supply packages,
and enrichment. Memoria's own package builder allows dropping strands a
family already owns. Manuals, readers, and teacher keys are reusable for
siblings; workbooks are consumable. Two-year pacing on the combined
upper levels effectively halves the annual cost of a package. Simply
Classical online classes price like Memoria Academy courses (hundreds of
dollars per course per year). Live prices: the
[Simply Classical catalog pages](https://www.memoriapress.com/curriculum/special-needs/).

## Why families choose it

- It is the only complete classical Christian program that plans every
  day around a special-needs child; the alternative is assembling
  therapy-informed pieces alone.
- The readiness assessments give a shame-free placement ritual and a
  vocabulary ("Level C," "skill level, not grade level") that spares the
  child comparisons.
- Swope's credibility: a specialist credentialed author who lived this
  exact life with her own children.
- The therapies slot legitimizes the real shape of these families' days
  instead of pretending school and therapy are separate worlds.
- Beauty as dignity: myths, art cards, and classical music instead of
  remedial drill aesthetics.

## Why families leave it

- Load and dryness, same as mainline Memoria: the parent-teacher burns
  out, or the child needs more color and movement than traditional
  materials provide; leavers drift to gentler Charlotte Mason blends or
  unit studies.
- The Christian content is structural (Bible story editions, Christian
  studies), so secular families tend to cherry-pick resources rather
  than buy packages.
- Children who close the gap outgrow the adaptation and merge into
  Memoria's standard or accelerated cores (an exit the publisher
  designed for and celebrates).
- Families needing diagnosis-specific intervention (OG reading therapy,
  dyscalculia work) discover Simply Classical paces content but is not
  itself a clinical intervention, and they bolt on Barton, AAR, or math
  intervention alongside or instead.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: level letters and numbers with their themed names,
  readiness assessment, skills to develop, Wonder Beauty and Imagination
  strand, the therapies slot, one-year vs two-year pace. "She is in
  Simply Classical Level 2" states a skill band (roughly ages 6-8
  work), never an age.
- The readiness assessment pattern (five checklists, then teaching tips
  keyed to each weak area) is the best model in the atlas for
  strengths-aware placement without grade shaming; Homa's onboarding and
  map placement for special-needs kids should study it.
- The therapies slot is a scheduling insight Homa can generalize: these
  families' planners must hold speech, OT, and vision appointments as
  first-class citizens beside phonics, or the plan is fiction.
- Materials fit: plain, serious, short-selection pages; regeneration for
  a Simply Classical family should shorten and slow, never cartoonify.
- Two-year pacing of one level is exactly the kind of stretch-without-
  shame Homa's planner can offer any family on any curriculum, and this
  line proves the demand exists.
