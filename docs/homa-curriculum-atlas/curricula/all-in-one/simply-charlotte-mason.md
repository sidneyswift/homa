# Simply Charlotte Mason

## Snapshot

Simply Charlotte Mason (SCM) is the commercial, open-and-go implementation
of the Charlotte Mason method: where AmblesideOnline gives a purist book
list and leaves execution to the parent, SCM sells finished daily lesson
plans. Founded in 2003 by Sonya Shafer, the company publishes subject
guides (history, Bible, geography, science, language arts) plus Enrichment
Studies volumes bundling the "beauty subjects," organized around
family-style teaching: the whole family studies the same history period
together, with readings leveled by age. Shafer's 500+ episode podcast, the
free CM Organizer planning tool, and the site's method articles make SCM
the de facto teaching hospital of the CM world; its definition of
"twaddle" is the one the repo's segment research quotes. Reviewers place
it as the most practical of the major CM curricula: more polished and less
overwhelming than AO, more traditional-feeling than purists prefer.

## Reputation and rankings

- Reviewer verdict (representative): "the most open-and-go of all the major
  Charlotte Mason curricula," recommended for multi-age Christian families
  who want structure without overwhelm; the same reviews note it is "not as
  deep as Ambleside Online" and can feel more "curriculum-y."
- Community position: SCM is the standard "AO was too much" landing spot,
  and the reverse migration (SCM to AO for more literary depth) is also
  common. Its store reviews and community are large and warm; no
  significant controversies surfaced in research.
- Trajectory: stable. SCM predates and outlasted most CM startups, and its
  free tools (Organizer, Bookfinder, printable Book of Centuries) keep it
  in every CM getting-started conversation.

## Pedagogy and method

Mason's method operationalized with maximum practicality: living books,
short lessons (SCM's own sample schedule blocks run 5-20 minutes),
narration in place of quizzes, habit training as a scheduled subject
(the Laying Down the Rails series), scripture memory, picture and music
study, nature study, and end-of-term exams in narration style. The house
distinctives are family-style teaching (one history spine aloud for all
ages, per-child narration expectations) and decision-removal: every guide
ships a complete book list and a daily itinerary so the parent opens the
book and teaches. SCM is explicitly Biblical: the history rotation weaves
Bible history through world history as one strand.

## Structure and scope

- History/Bible/geography: a six-year rotation from Genesis and Ancient
  Egypt through modern times, completed twice across K-12, taught
  family-style with age-banded assigned books and a Book of Centuries
  timeline notebook (free printable version; a nicer bound one for
  purchase).
- Enrichment Studies: volumes bundling picture study, music study, poetry,
  Shakespeare, hymns, scripture memory, handicrafts, foreign language, and
  habits into about one hour per day of family lessons, with matching
  resource bundles (Picture Study Portfolios, Music Study with the
  Masters, Shakespeare in Three Steps).
- Individual Studies: per-grade schedules for language arts, math, and
  science at each child's own level (SCM points to third-party math).
- Planning layer: Planning Your Charlotte Mason Education (book plus
  video seminar, a five-step planning process) and the CM Organizer, a
  web planner with a books-as-resources model, division-based scheduling,
  and a "do what's next" rolling schedule instead of dated deadlines.
  Basic accounts are free; full features are subscription.

## A lesson up close

The parent opens the guide to today's page: a short itinerary telling her
which book, which pages, and what to do after (usually "ask for a
narration"). A family morning per SCM's own sample: poem recitation (10
minutes), history read-aloud from the period spine (20 minutes), each
child narrates at their level, hymn singing (5 minutes), then individual
studies (math, language arts) per child. Enrichment lessons rotate through
the week (picture study Monday, Shakespeare Friday). The child's output is
oral narration, copywork, a Book of Centuries entry, a nature journal
page; there are no worksheets in the content subjects by design. Completed
guides read like a checked-off itinerary, which is the record.

## Materials and visual design

Softcover guides and spiral planners with clean, calm layouts: serif
typography, cream space, watercolor-adjacent covers, fine-art plates in
the Picture Study Portfolios (large loose prints in a folder). The
aesthetic is gentle and uncluttered, signaling "peaceful homeschool" in
contrast to both Abeka's schoolroom look and AO's no-design website. The
guides are teacher-facing; child-facing print is mostly notebooks
(copywork books, Book of Centuries) rather than workbooks.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026: individual subject guides about $30-60 digital, $40-80
print; full-year multi-subject bundles roughly $200-300; living books add
$50-150 depending on library use. One aggregator prices a full year of
new materials near $380 before shipping; a three-child family-style year
lands around $250-450 total because most purchases are shared. Guides are
reusable across children; consumables are just notebooks. Free layer:
Bookfinder, basic CM Organizer, printable Book of Centuries, planning
articles.

## Why families choose it

- The CM method with the planning already done: open-and-go daily plans.
- Family-style economics: one purchase teaches all the kids together.
- Trustworthy on-ramp: Shafer's podcast and articles teach the method,
  not just the product.
- Affordable relative to boxed programs, with a strong free tier.

## Why families leave it

- Purists outgrow it toward AO (richer, harder books, free).
- Families wanting secular content leave for Build Your Library or
  BookShark; the Bible-woven history is not separable.
- Families wanting more academic rigor or a classical arc move to Memoria
  Press or WTM-style plans.
- The family-style model strains when children's needs diverge sharply,
  pushing families to per-child programs.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary: family-style, the six-year rotation, Enrichment Studies,
  Picture Study Portfolio, Laying Down the Rails (habits), Book of
  Centuries, CM Organizer, "do what's next" scheduling.
- SCM's CM Organizer is the closest existing product to a Homa planning
  surface for CM families: books as resources, divisions scheduled, no
  dated deadlines, progress by what actually happened. Its "rolling
  schedule" is a proven pattern for this segment and worth matching.
- Gap: the Organizer plans books but does not generate materials, adapt
  when a child stalls on a book, or turn narrations into records; Homa
  can do all three while respecting the same no-deadlines posture.
- Materials rules: identical to the AO card (narration prompts and
  notebook pages yes, drill sheets no), but SCM families are slightly more
  tolerant of structure since they chose the pre-planned version. See
  `docs/homa-planning/icp-research/08-segments-curricula.md` §7 card 2.
