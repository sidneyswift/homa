# The Good and the Beautiful

Note for atlas readers: the math, language arts, and science category
profiles cross-reference this file for publisher-level facts (the free PDF
model, worldview posture, design language, edition churn). Keep those facts
here, not duplicated.

## Snapshot

The Good and the Beautiful (TGTB, community shorthand TGATB) is the fastest
riser in the homeschool market: a Christian, Charlotte Mason-influenced,
open-and-go curriculum founded in 2015 by Jenny Phillips, a homeschool
mom based in Lehi, Utah. Its disruptive move is the price model:
complete language arts and math courses for levels K-8 are free full-course
PDF downloads, with beautifully printed course sets sold at low prices. The
flagship is the integrated Language Arts line (reading, writing, grammar,
spelling, literature, geography, and art in one course); math, unit-based
science, family-style history, and early high school courses have followed.
Reviewers repeatedly call it one of the fastest-growing curricula in the
country, and it dominates the gentle-Christian-eclectic aesthetic that the
2-8 age band's Instagram and Facebook culture rewards. It is also the
market's most argued-about curriculum, because the founder is a member of
The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints while the content is
marketed as broadly Christian.

## Reputation and rankings

- Cathy Duffy reviews the language arts courses as challenging and
  substantive, noting "the overall quality is surprisingly good" for a
  free-or-cheap curriculum, with the trade-off that it leans on older
  public-domain literature and art. She stresses using the free placement
  tests because course levels run slightly above public school grade
  levels.
- Growth reputation: review sites from Homeschool Fox to Homeschool Picks
  describe TGTB as among the fastest-growing homeschool publishers in the
  US; the repo's segment research (Jul 2026) reached the same finding
  ([08-segments-curricula.md](../../../homa-planning/icp-research/08-segments-curricula.md) §3).
  The publisher markets its language arts as award-winning (publisher
  claim, specific awards not independently verified here).
- The controversy is a permanent feature of its community presence: some
  evangelical reviewers argue the broadly-Christian framing obscures the
  founder's LDS faith or that the content is morality-forward rather than
  gospel-forward, and a few (like Foundation Worldview) recommend against
  it; many other Protestant and Catholic reviewers report using it happily
  and finding no denominational doctrine in the materials. Both positions
  show up in nearly every Facebook or Reddit thread about it.
- Critical threads beyond worldview: phonics is not an Orton-Gillingham
  structured-literacy program (reviewers steer dyslexic readers to All
  About Reading or Logic of English instead), the math line is newer and
  read by some as gentle, and the publisher revises and discontinues
  editions at a pace that frustrates multi-year planners.
- Trajectory: rising, clearly. It is the program legacy publishers now get
  compared against on price, beauty, and prep time.

## Pedagogy and method

- Charlotte Mason-influenced but productized: real literature, poetry,
  picture study, nature themes, short varied lesson segments, and habit
  and character emphasis, packaged into a scripted, open-and-go course
  book rather than a DIY book list.
- Open-and-go is the operating promise: no daily prep, teacher text and
  student work in one course book, with blue text for the parent and black
  text read to the child in early levels.
- Language arts integrates subjects deliberately: one daily lesson covers
  some mix of reading, grammar, spelling, writing, literature, geography,
  and art, which is the time-saving pitch to moms.
- Parent role shifts by level: LA levels K-3 are parent-led one-on-one;
  Level 4 and up are written to the student and primarily self-directed,
  with the parent checking work daily. Math is parent-led through the
  early levels with video lessons carrying more of the load in Math 4-8.
- Math (Simply Good and Beautiful Math) is spiral and eclectic: concept
  instruction plus games, manipulatives (Math Box), mental math, and a
  review section in most lessons.
- Assessment is light-touch: unit-end assessments rather than heavy
  quiz-and-test apparatus; the culture of the curriculum is anti-drill,
  pro-delight.
- Worldview method: God, moral character, and wholesome family life are
  woven through content; no denomination's doctrine is taught. Secular
  families read it as clearly religious.

## Structure and scope

- Language arts: Preschool, Kindergarten Prep, Levels K-8 (Level 8 as book
  studies), plus High School 1-3. Most levels are 120 lessons across 6
  units; upper levels split into two course books, with unit project
  lessons and assessments. Levels 4-8 use the free online Simply Spelling
  program alongside the books. Level 7 is being replaced by a new edition
  in Jul 2026 (edition churn in action).
- Math: Simply Good and Beautiful Math K through Math 8 (pre-algebra), one
  course book per level (more volumes in 6-7), Math Box manipulative kits
  for K-3, free video lessons for Math 4-8 via the site and app. The
  publisher states no high school math will be offered; Math 8 is the
  ceiling.
- Science: unit-based courses designed for multiple ages together, plus a
  new High School Biology (2025), with high school earth and space science
  announced for 2027.
- History: family-style 4-year rotation for grades 3-8 (Year 1 released
  2025, Year 4 expected Feb 2027). One Course Book teaches the family; each
  child has a grade-banded Student Explorer (3-6 or 7-8), a Time Line Book,
  and shared Time Tunnel review cards; two 20-30 minute lessons per week.
- High school: intentionally incomplete; individual subjects release as
  finished (language arts, biology). No accreditation, no records service,
  no full diploma path.
- Delivery: free PDFs, printed sets, the Good and Beautiful Homeschool
  app (video lessons, audio dramatizations, and course companions), the
  courses site for online components, and free placement tests for LA and
  math.

## A lesson up close

Language arts, around Level 1 (parent-led):

- The parent opens the course book to the day's lesson; everything needed
  is on the page or in the kit (reading booster cards, phonics cards,
  whiteboard). Blue text tells the parent what to do; black text is read
  aloud to the child.
- A lesson runs through several short segments: a phonics or reading skill
  taught explicitly, shared reading practice, a few minutes of spelling or
  handwriting, and often a literature passage, poem, art study moment, or
  geography touch. Segments are short and varied on purpose.
- The lesson ends with a marked independent practice section the child
  completes alone, which is also the handoff moment for a mom teaching
  the next sibling.
- At Level 4 and above the course book speaks directly to the student
  ("complete the next section, then go to the online activity"), lessons
  target under 40 minutes, and the parent's job is a daily check of
  completed pages against the answer key.

Page anatomy: full-color pages built around fine art and nature
illustration, generous white space by workbook standards, mixed item types
(short writes, matching, diagram labeling, copywork lines, discussion
prompts) rather than banks of repeated drill items. A completed day
produces a few beautiful filled pages, not a stack of worksheets. Free PDFs
mean a home printer prints these full-color pages, which users note gets
expensive; that friction is what the cheap printed sets monetize.

## Materials and visual design

- The visual language is the brand: vintage fine-art paintings, botanical
  illustration, soft color palettes, serif typography, thick perfect-bound
  course books. It signals wholesomeness, calm, and taste, the opposite
  pole from Abeka's bright classroom Americana.
- Course books are consumable and full color throughout. Math Boxes hold
  chunky color manipulatives; reading booster cards and read-together book
  sets support early levels.
- The aesthetic is a primary purchase driver, is heavily shown off in
  unboxing and flip-through content, and defined the "beautiful
  curriculum" category that competitors now chase.

## Pricing

All prices as of Jul 2026 from the publisher's pages.

- Free: complete LA K-8 and Math K-8 course PDFs
  ([LA downloads](https://www.goodandbeautiful.com/pages/free-homeschool-language-arts),
  [math downloads](https://www.goodandbeautiful.com/pages/free-homeschool-math-curriculum)).
  The publisher's stated rationale: "the price is a reflection of our
  values and purpose."
- Printed course sets ([LA course chooser](https://www.goodandbeautiful.com/pages/choose-language-arts)):
  LA sets run about $49-90 per level (for example Level K $69.97, Level 4
  $79.94, Level 6 $55.96); High School LA sets about $118-126. Math 2
  course set $58.98 (course book $29.99, Math Box $28.99); optional answer
  key $11.99.
- History: per family, a Family Set plus one Student Explorer per child,
  with one-time Time Line Book and review card purchases reused across
  all four years.
- Consumables economics: course books are consumable per child, but the
  free PDF right means a second child can be run off the printer at toner
  cost; math boxes, read-alouds, and family sets are shared. This is the
  cheapest complete-feeling stack in the Christian market, and the segment
  research's price map places it at the center of the $0-200 assemble-it-
  yourself universe.

## Why families choose it

- Price: a real, complete curriculum for free removes the biggest
  first-year risk. Trying it costs nothing but printer ink.
- Beauty: it is the market's best-looking product, and moms say so before
  they say anything about pedagogy.
- Open-and-go: minimal prep, one integrated LA course instead of five
  separate programs, family-style science and history for multi-kid
  efficiency.
- Values without denomination: broadly Christian, morally wholesome
  content that many families across denominations find agreeable.
- The self-directed upper levels give moms of many children breathing
  room.

## Why families leave it

- Struggling readers: the phonics track is not a structured-literacy
  intervention; families with dyslexic kids move to Orton-Gillingham
  programs (All About Reading, Logic of English, Barton).
- Math confidence: some families judge the math too gentle or too new and
  pair TGTB LA with a different math (a very common hybrid); the hard
  ceiling at Math 8 forces a high school math decision anyway.
- Placement friction: levels run advanced, so kids placed by grade number
  can drown; the placement test is load-bearing and not everyone takes it.
- Worldview, in both directions: evangelical families who decide the LDS
  association is disqualifying leave loudly; secular-leaning families find
  the God-and-character saturation too much.
- Edition churn: frequent revisions and discontinued levels (Level 7 LA
  replaced Jul 2026, the pre-2025 history line discontinued) annoy
  families mid-sequence.
- No complete high school: everyone graduates out of TGTB eventually, so
  departure is built in; common destinations are Abeka, BJU, or online
  programs for high school, or eclectic per-subject stacks.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: course book, course set, Level K-8 vs grade level
  (advanced placement), Simply Good and Beautiful Math, Math Box, Simply
  Spelling, Student Explorer, Family Set, the Homeschool app, "open and
  go." When a mom says a level number, do not assume it equals grade.
- TGTB proved Homa's aesthetic thesis: in this market, visual softness and
  taste are product features with conversion power. Homa's materials
  studio should treat TGTB as the design bar for the gentle segment, using
  Homa's own design system.
- The free-PDF-plus-printer model normalizes printing curriculum at home,
  which is exactly Homa's materials delivery path. TGTB families already
  own the habit.
- Gap Homa fills: TGTB has no assessment story, no records story, and no
  personalization; lessons are fixed pages. Homa can generate the missing
  per-child reinforcement (especially math practice, the line's perceived
  weak spot) in a TGTB-compatible gentle register, and quietly keep the
  progress evidence the curriculum never captures.
- Handle worldview with care: TGTB families chose broadly-Christian on
  purpose. Homa should never re-litigate the LDS debate; know it exists,
  stay neutral, respect the family's settled choice.
- Edition churn is a support opportunity: Homa can know which edition a
  family holds and adapt, which the publisher's own FAQ pages struggle
  with.
