# Accelerated Christian Education (ACE)

## Snapshot

ACE (School of Tomorrow) is a 50-plus-year-old fundamentalist Christian
curriculum built on PACEs: thin self-instructional worktexts, twelve per
subject per year, that students complete alone and in silence at their own
pace, with diagnostic placement and score keys for self-checking. It runs
K-12, serves both private schools and homeschools (independently or
through Lighthouse Christian Academy, its accredited distance-learning
provider), and claims use in over 140 countries. It is also the market's
most criticized curriculum: peer-reviewed research (Scaramanga and Reiss)
found its rote fill-in-the-blank method and content leave students at an
educational disadvantage; its science is young-earth creationist (a 2012
UK controversy involved a PACE citing the Loch Ness Monster against
evolution, later removed); and former students, including some who remain
creationists, describe the isolated desk-and-booklet system as harmful.
Families comparing formats usually treat LIFEPAC or CLE as the more
academically respected versions of the same booklet idea.

## Reputation and rankings

- No mainstream reviewer ranks ACE highly; comparisons consistently place
  its rigor below [LIFEPAC](alpha-omega.md) and [CLE](christian-light-education.md).
- Documented criticism is unusually strong: Wikipedia catalogs academic
  findings that the curriculum does not meet science standards, relies on
  rote recall, and embeds conservative political framing; ex-student
  communities (r/HomeschoolRecovery, Leaving Fundamentalism) treat ACE as
  a case study in curriculum harm. A creationist alumnus still calls the
  system "isolating, oppressive" in a widely shared account.
- It persists because it is cheap, fully independent, and institutionally
  entrenched in some church-school networks; trajectory is fading in the
  US homeschool mainstream.
- Honest note for Homa: if a family says they use ACE, treat it as a
  functioning system they chose, know the criticisms, and never shame.
