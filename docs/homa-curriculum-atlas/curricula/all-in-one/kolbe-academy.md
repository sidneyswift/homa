# Kolbe Academy (home school)

## Snapshot

Kolbe Academy is the flexible classicist among the Catholic enrollment
schools: an accredited PK-12 program, founded in Napa, California in 1980
as a lay-run classical day school and offering home study since 1987,
that now serves over 2,000 homeschool students with a mix-and-match model
(parent-taught traditional courses, digital self-paced courses,
asynchronous teacher-accompanied courses, and live online classes can be
combined per child, per subject). Its pedagogy is Ignatian, its content
classical (Great Books pairings in high school), and its trust signals
institutional: accreditation by Cognia and NAPCIS, a Newman Guide
Recommended curriculum designation from the Cardinal Newman Society, and
explicit fidelity to the Magisterium. The day school (now Kolbe-Trinity
in the Diocese of Santa Rosa) and the homeschool program have operated
separately since a 2008 merger. Among Catholic families, Kolbe is the
enrollment school you pick when you want accreditation and honors-track
rigor without being locked into one publisher's texts.

## Reputation and rankings

- Reviewer synthesis rates Kolbe alongside Seton and MODG at the top of
  the Catholic market, with a distinct reputation: exceptionally
  rigorous ("often operating a grade level above public school
  standards" in literature and composition, per the aggregated reviews)
  and unusually flexible about substitutions while keeping enrollment.
- Community critiques mirror the classical pattern: volume of work, and
  live-class costs that stack up for large families. Long-form user
  reviews (Jennifer Fitz's multi-part series is representative) praise
  the course plans' day-by-day division of labor while noting the plans
  organize rather than teach, so parents supply instruction or buy it.
- Institutional proof points Kolbe itself advertises: 2019 graduating
  class with 65% four-year college acceptance and a 28 average ACT
  composite (self-reported), NCAA course approval noted by reviewers,
  and the Newman Guide listing.
- Trajectory: stable to rising; its online tiers grew through the
  pandemic years and its "school of record for eclectic Catholics" role
  (see below) keeps it relevant beyond its own curriculum.

## Pedagogy and method

Kolbe's method is Ignatian, drawn from the Ratio Studiorum tradition of
Jesuit schooling: formation over information, with mastery built through
prelection (guided preview), self-activity, repetition, and reflection.
The parent is explicitly the teacher; Kolbe frames itself with the
principle of subsidiarity (it is "not a correspondence program") and
supplies structure, review, and counsel rather than replacing the home
classroom. Content is classical in phases: elementary students meet
Egypt, Greece, and Rome as story; high schoolers revisit the ancients
with literature and history in lockstep (the Iliad read alongside
Herodotus, Roman history alongside the Aeneid). Assessment combines
parent grading with optional institutional verification: quarterly
exams ship with course plans, and parents submit recommended grades
plus work samples through the Kolbe Portal for validation onto official
records. High school offers honors-level work via course designations
(Kolbe Core and Honors in the program guide's system, with designation
requirements listed per course plan) and a tiered diploma ladder
(Standard, Magna Cum Laude, Summa Cum Laude), so ambitious students can
formalize rigor without leaving home study.

## Structure and scope

- Levels: PK-12. Curriculum is organized as per-course "course plans"
  rather than one grade box: complete PK-K programs, then a catalog of
  1-12 courses families assemble, following Kolbe's curriculum map or
  substituting freely (substitution while enrolled is a Kolbe
  distinctive).
- Course plan anatomy: syllabus (description, texts, learning outcomes,
  scope and sequence, and for high school the grade-reporting and
  designation requirements), then weekly plans with page numbers,
  exercises, and discussion questions, teaching notes, quarterly or
  semester exams with answer keys, and appendices (rubrics, grading
  support, enrichment). K-8 plans run quarterly in a daily, four-day
  format; high school plans run by semester in a weekly format; 36
  weeks total.
- Math is a choice of tracks rather than a house program: MCP for
  kindergarten plus Sadlier Math (grades 1-8), or Singapore Math (K-6),
  with Saxon long the upper-elementary default and still supported into
  high school alongside Foerster texts (see `../math/saxon-math.md` and
  `../math/dimensions-math.md` for the method families being chosen
  between; some Saxon course plans are now archived).
- Delivery formats per course: traditional homeschool (parent-led),
  digital homeschool (online version of the plan with recorded
  instruction and auto-graded checks, select courses grades 5-12),
  asynchronous online (teacher-accompanied), and live online classes.
- Enrollment tiers: Full-Time Online (5+ live classes), Full-Time
  Standard (traditional with bookstore credit, CLT testing, diploma
  program), Full-Time Flex (same academics, fewer extras), and
  Part-Time (1-2 courses). Advisors are assigned per family; the Classic
  Learning Test (CLT) is the built-in standardized test; summer courses
  and clubs run year-round.
- Diplomas: 8th grade and high school diplomas; students may count
  pre-approved outside courses (community college, co-ops, other
  providers) with at least 25% of credits from Kolbe courses.

## A lesson up close

The parent's week starts in the course plan: review the coming week's
table (pages to read, exercises, discussion questions, teaching notes),
then run it as a daily checklist. A grade 5 day might pair a Sadlier or
Saxon math lesson (the plan divides the work; instruction comes from the
text or the digital course's videos), a literature reading with the
plan's discussion questions at lunch, a history section with a map
exercise, religion from a catechism text with the plan's teaching note,
and Latin or science per the family's course mix. Quarter's end is
administrative: the parent administers the included exam, assigns a
recommended grade, photographs one work sample per subject (K-8), and
submits both in the Kolbe Portal; Kolbe's grade reporters verify and the
advisor reviews the quarter. A high schooler pursuing an Honors
designation follows the extra requirements listed in the course plan and
submits two samples per semester. What a completed term produces is a
verified grade on an accredited transcript plus a shelf of exams and
samples, with the family free to have swapped any text along the way.

## Materials and visual design

Kolbe's own artifacts are the course plans: clean, text-dense PDF or
print booklets with tables, teaching notes, and exams, professional but
unadorned. The bookshelf they orchestrate is classical-eclectic:
standard publisher math (Sadlier, Singapore, Saxon), Ignatius Press and
other Catholic religion texts, classic literature in trade editions.
The aesthetic signal is a college-prep registrar's office rather than a
designed box: families buying Kolbe are buying structure, verification,
and permission to customize, not a visual identity. The Kolbe Portal
and Schoology carry the digital courses.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026 (rates effective Feb 4, 2026; the tuition page is the
live truth):

- Full-Time Standard: $335-995 per student by grade, including course
  plans for up to 12 elementary or 8 high school courses, a $200
  bookstore credit, CLT testing, diploma program, records, and an
  advisor. Other books cost extra.
- Full-Time Flex: $195-535 per student, same academics and records
  without the bookstore credit and extras.
- Full-Time Online: $1,795-6,995 per student by grade, for students
  taking up to six live or asynchronous courses (grades 6-12), including
  grading by teachers.
- Part-Time: from $199 (one course, records for enrolled courses).
- Individual course add-ons: live and asynchronous courses carry
  per-course deposits ($150 and $85 respectively); family discounts for
  five or more students; second-semester discounts for online tiers open
  in November.
- Payment plans run through Blackbaud with per-tier deposits.

Multi-kid economics improve with the family discount and reusable texts;
the real cost driver is how many live classes the family adds. A
traditional-enrollment family can run Kolbe near CHC prices; a full live
online high schooler costs private-school money.

## Why families choose it

- Accreditation plus freedom: the only major Catholic program that lets
  families substitute texts freely while keeping enrolled status,
  verified grades, and an accredited transcript.
- Rigor with a ladder: honors designations, AP-weighted grading, CLT
  testing, and diploma tiers for college-bound students.
- The mix-and-match formats: a struggling subject can move to a live
  class, a strong one can run self-paced, per child, per year.
- Ignatian and Newman Guide trust signals for magisterium-minded
  parents.
- The umbrella role: eclectic Catholic families using other providers
  (Homeschool Connections explicitly recommends Kolbe for this) enroll
  with Kolbe to turn outside coursework into an accredited record.

## Why families leave it

- Workload: classical volume plus quarterly administration wears on
  families; gentler Catholic options (CHC) or lighter enrollment
  (dropping to Flex or leaving enrollment) are the common moves.
- Cost stacking: live online tiers for several children price like
  tuition; families retreat to traditional enrollment or per-course use.
- Wanting a consultant rather than an advisor: families needing deeper
  personal guidance gravitate to MODG's consultation model.
- Wanting house texts and grading done for them: Seton's all-in-one
  registrar model fits better than Kolbe's assemble-and-verify.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: course plans, curriculum map, designations (Core,
  Honors), diploma types (Standard, Magna, Summa), grade reporting,
  work samples, Kolbe Portal, family advisor, Full-Time Standard versus
  Flex versus Online, CLT. "We're with Kolbe" implies enrolled records
  plus freedom to substitute, so ask what they actually use per subject.
- Kolbe proves the record-verification wedge: parents will pay for an
  institution to validate their own grading with samples. Homa's
  evidence log is the same job done continuously; for Kolbe families it
  should make quarterly sample-and-grade submission near-zero effort,
  and for non-enrolled families it substitutes for it.
- The substitution culture makes Kolbe families ideal multi-vendor
  stacks: Homa's stack-aware assistance (knowing the Sadlier, Singapore,
  Saxon, and classical text vocabularies at once) matters more here
  than brand-deep knowledge of any single text.
- Materials fit: plain, serious, classical register; discussion
  questions and exam-prep scaffolds fit the Ignatian frame (prelection
  aids for the parent, reflection prompts), but never answer keys or
  essays for the student: the verified-grade model makes authenticity
  load-bearing, same landmine as the classical segment
  (`../../comparisons/classical-charlotte-mason.md`).
- Kolbe's course plan is a strong artifact pattern: syllabus, weekly
  table, teaching notes, rubrics, exams in one booklet per course. It is
  the per-course analog of Memoria's per-grade manual and a good model
  for Homa-generated course companions.
