# Classical Academic Press

## Snapshot

Classical Academic Press (CAP) is the subject-by-subject publisher of the
classical homeschool movement: not a boxed curriculum but a family of
award-winning lines in Latin, Greek, Spanish, French, English grammar,
writing, logic, rhetoric, history, Bible, and (since acquiring Novare in
2020) science. Founded in 2001 in Camp Hill, Pennsylvania by Dr.
Christopher Perrin and partners, its motto is "Classical Subjects
Creatively Taught," and the house style is exactly that: full-color pages,
songs and chants, video instruction, and gentle humor wrapped around
rigorous trivium content. Where [Memoria Press](memoria-press.md) sells a
finished all-subject plan and [Classical Conversations](classical-conversations.md)
sells a community, CAP trusts the parent to assemble a stack line by line,
and its Song School Latin and Latin for Children sequences are the
market's default "engaging Latin" answer. It swept the 2025 Practical
Homeschooling Reader Awards with eight first places and claims more than
50 awards since founding. The company also runs Scholé Academy (live
online classes), ClassicalU (teacher training), and the Scholé Communities
co-op network.

## Reputation and rankings

- Practical Homeschooling Reader Awards 2025: eight first-place awards
  (Latin for Children and Latin Alive for Latin, Song School Latin for
  Latin, Song School Greek for Greek, the Song School Greek/Latin/Spanish
  family for song-based curriculum, Well-Ordered Language for grammar,
  Art of Argument and Discovery of Deduction for logic, Argument Builder
  and Rhetoric Alive for public speaking, Novare Science for middle
  school science), plus seven second places (including Writing &
  Rhetoric, Spanish for Children, and French for Children) and two
  thirds ([publisher announcement](https://classicalacademicpress.com/blogs/cap-news/award-winning-excellence-classical-academic-press-recognized-for-outstanding-curriculum)).
  The same reader-award sweep pattern as Heart of Dakota's 2025 wins;
  see `market-overview.md` on how these awards skew to the
  traditional-Christian core and get advertised for years.
- Cathy Duffy carries favorable reviews across the catalog and CAP
  markets Top Picks placements for Well-Ordered Language, Writing &
  Rhetoric, and Latin for Children. Her Song School Latin review is
  representative: conversational approach, no lesson prep, "parents need
  no Latin background," videos highly recommended.
- Community sentiment: CAP vs Memoria is the standing "which classical
  Latin" decision, and the consensus split is consistent everywhere it
  appears: CAP for color, chants, and engagement; Memoria for the
  tighter K-12 sequence, plainer pages, and 30-40% lower per-level cost
  ([everyhomeschool comparison](https://everyhomeschool.com/comparisons/classical-academic-press-vs-memoria-press-latin)).
  Reviewers describe CAP as classical Christian in ownership but
  academically neutral on the page, which widens its secular reach.
- Standing critiques in forums: Spanish for Children is thin on practice
  and review compared to the Latin flagship; some families find Writing &
  Rhetoric's early books slow; the colorful pages read as busy to kids
  who prefer plain layouts.
- Trajectory: rising with the broader classical surge. The company moved
  to larger headquarters in April 2025, and the Novare acquisition plus
  The Curious Historian rollout mark deliberate expansion from languages
  toward full-stack coverage (still no math).

## Pedagogy and method

CAP implements the trivium with a softer touch than its classical peers,
under the banner of scholé, "restful learning," a concept Perrin
popularized through books and ClassicalU courses:

- Grammar-stage memory work is delivered through music. Song School
  courses teach vocabulary by song; Latin for Children chants declensions
  and conjugations to audio tracks in classical or ecclesiastical
  pronunciation. The bet is that paradigm memory is unavoidable, so make
  it singable.
- Grammar-translation Latin, not immersion: the sequence teaches forms
  and syntax systematically and translates, the same lineage as Memoria,
  differing in presentation not method.
- Video does the expert teaching. Every language level and the logic
  books have recorded teachers, so a parent with no Latin, Greek, or
  logic background supervises rather than lectures. Parent involvement is
  real but medium: run the video, drill the chant, check against the
  answer key.
- English derivatives are a signature thread: Song School's videos
  feature a "derivative river" segment, and derivative work continues
  through Latin Alive, selling Latin as English vocabulary payoff.
- Writing & Rhetoric follows the ancient progymnasmata (fable, narrative,
  chreia, refutation, and so on) but pairs it with Charlotte Mason-style
  narration and discussion; Cathy Duffy notes its lesson rhythm of Tell
  It Back, Talk About It, Go Deeper, Writing Time, and Speak It.
- Well-Ordered Language teaches grammar through a recurring oral analysis
  routine: students chant questions at the sentence, mark up its parts,
  then diagram, with songs and physical movement keeping it lively.
- Novare Science brings John Mays's mastery-integration-wonder model:
  fewer topics taught to durable mastery with cumulative review, science
  integrated with math, history, and theology.

## Structure and scope

The catalog is organized as ladders per subject; families climb one or
several:

- Latin: Song School Latin 1-2 (about K-3) into Latin for Children
  Primers A, B, C (about grades 3-6, one primer per year) into Latin
  Alive! Books 1-3 (middle and high school, ending in authentic authors
  and AP readiness).
- Greek: Song School Greek, then Greek for Children Primers A-B.
- Modern languages: Song School Spanish 1-2, Spanish for Children A-B,
  French for Children A.
- English grammar: Well-Ordered Language Levels 1A through 4B (two books
  per year, about grades 3-8), by Daniel Coupland (Hillsdale College) and
  Tammy Peters.
- Writing: Writing & Rhetoric Books 1-12 (Fable through Declamation), one
  book per semester, about grades 3-9 plus high school continuation via
  Rhetoric Alive!.
- Logic and rhetoric: The Art of Argument (informal fallacies, about
  grades 7-8), The Argument Builder, The Discovery of Deduction (formal
  logic, about grades 8-9), Everyday Debate, then Rhetoric Alive!.
- History: The Curious Historian, six semester texts (1A-3B) walking the
  ancient through medieval world for upper elementary and middle school.
- Science: Novare Science texts for grades 6-12 (Physical Science,
  Earth Science, General Biology, Chemistry, Physics), Christian
  editions plus Centripetal Press secular versions.
- Bible: God's Great Covenant (Old and New Testament, elementary).
- Early years: A Year of Playing Skillfully (preschool/K play-based
  guide).
- Services: Scholé Academy (live online sections capped around 15
  students, a la carte, unaccredited, founded 2014), ClassicalU
  (subscription teacher training, 100+ self-paced courses), Scholé
  Communities (co-op network), plus free placement and "recommendation"
  flows on the site.

A per-level language program bundle contains student edition, teacher's
edition, activity book (Latin), answer key, streaming or DVD video, chant
audio, and optional online practice, test packets, and Clash Cards games.
There is deliberately no CAP math; families pair with any math program,
and science only begins at middle school, so a "full CAP" family is
really an assembler with CAP as the biggest vendor.

## A lesson up close

Latin for Children, the flagship, runs on a weekly chapter rhythm of
about 20-30 minutes a day. The child watches the chapter video, where an
on-screen teacher introduces the chant (say, a noun declension's endings)
and the week's vocabulary; the family then chants along with the audio
daily, the single most-loved mechanic in the program. The student primer
page presents the paradigm in a bright grid with a short grammar lesson
written to the child; the activity book supplies puzzles, matching, and
derivative games; a worksheet-and-quiz cycle closes the week. Parents
report kids singing the chants in the car, which is the retention model
working as designed. Song School Latin, for younger siblings, is gentler:
a song from the audio, a ten-minute video with the magistra chatting
about words like casa and porta, an animated monkey story, the
"derivative river," then a few workbook pages of circling and drawing.
A Writing & Rhetoric day is discussion-heavy: the parent and child read a
fable or narrative aloud, the child tells it back (narration), they talk
through the craft questions, then the child writes a guided imitation or
amplification in the consumable workbook and reads it aloud with
expression ("Speak It"). Completed days produce visible artifacts:
chanted paradigms, filled workbook pages, a short composition.

## Materials and visual design

CAP's look is the counterpoint to Memoria's restraint: full-color student
books, cartoon mascots (Simeon the monkey in Song School Latin), maps,
photographs, and illustration-rich margins, on standard letter-size
consumable workbooks with sturdy perfect binding. Typography is friendly
and generously sized; paradigm charts are boxed and color-coded. The
aesthetic signals "rigor without tears": it tells the buyer a child can
like Latin. Upper-level materials (Latin Alive!, Novare texts, Rhetoric
Alive!) sober up considerably: more text, fewer cartoons, clean textbook
design, Novare hardcovers with fine-art and photographic covers. Videos
are studio-produced with real teachers rather than animations. The whole
system is print-first with streaming upgrades; nothing requires a screen
except the video instruction itself.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026, from the publisher's own collection pages (linked; prices
drift):

- Song School Latin Book 1 program: from $102.95 (Book 2 from $111.95).
- Latin for Children full programs: Primer A from $132.95, B from
  $147.95, C from $139.95.
- A la carte: student editions $29.95-31.95, activity books $21.95,
  answer keys $18.95, video/audio sets from $63.95-74.95, chant audio
  $11.95, online practice $23.95, test packets from $11.95.
- Well-Ordered Language and Writing & Rhetoric run cheaper per year
  (paired student/teacher books per semester rather than video-heavy
  bundles).
- Scholé Academy live classes are a separate budget line (hundreds of
  dollars per course per year, typical of live academies).

Multi-kid economics are decent: teacher's editions, videos, audio, and
cards are reusable; student and activity books are consumable re-buys.
Reviewers consistently note Memoria's equivalent Latin levels price
30-40% lower; CAP's premium buys color and production values. A family
running three CAP lines (Latin, grammar, writing) spends roughly $250-400
a year per student, still under one Memoria grade box.

## Why families choose it

- The chants: kids who would fight flashcards will sing declensions.
  This one mechanic drives most CAP-vs-Memoria wins.
- Video teachers remove the "I never took Latin" objection entirely.
- Color and humor: the parent wants classical rigor, the child gets
  pages that look friendly, and the family stops fighting about Latin.
- Ecumenical, lightly-worn faith: Christian enough for church families,
  neutral enough on the page for many secular classical assemblers.
- Award shelf and Cathy Duffy blessing make it a safe pick under
  social proof.
- A la carte freedom: no box commitment; try one line, add the next.

## Why families leave it

- Price sensitivity in Latin: Memoria's Forms cost less and feel more
  no-nonsense; families who decide the color is clutter switch there.
- Busy-page kids: the same full-color design that engages one child
  distracts another; those families move to Memoria or Getting Started
  with Latin (see `../electives/getting-started-with-languages.md`).
- Latin fatigue, the classical constant: when the family quits Latin,
  CAP usually loses its anchor line and the rest of the stack unwinds.
- Spanish and French lines underdeliver: families wanting real modern
  language proficiency outgrow Spanish for Children's practice volume
  and move to tutors, apps, or immersion programs.
- No math and late science: CAP can never be the whole plan, so a family
  consolidating vendors (into Memoria, BJU, or an online academy) drops
  CAP lines in passing.
- Writing pace: families finding Writing & Rhetoric's early books slow
  jump to IEW or Essentials in Writing.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: Song School (Latin, Spanish, Greek), the Primers
  (Latin for Children A/B/C), Latin Alive!, Well-Ordered Language (WOL,
  levels 1A-4B), Writing & Rhetoric book names (Fable, Narrative,
  Chreia & Proverb, Refutation & Confirmation, up through Declamation),
  Art of Argument, Discovery of Deduction, Novare, Scholé (Academy and
  Communities), ClassicalU. "We're in Primer B" means roughly grade 4-5
  Latin, chant-driven, video-taught.
- The chant insight transfers: CAP proves memory work sells best as
  audio. Homa materials for memorization-heavy families should think in
  recordable, singable, car-friendly formats, not only worksheets.
- The assembler posture is Homa-shaped: CAP families are by definition
  multi-vendor (no CAP math exists), so they already live the stack
  problem Homa solves. Knowing which CAP line maps to which subject slot
  lets Homa slot the rest of the stack around it without explanation.
- Materials fit: practice generated alongside CAP lines should be
  colorful, friendly, and lightly humorous, the opposite calibration
  from Memoria families. Derivative games and paradigm drills are the
  native supplement shapes.
- Same landmine as all classical homes: never do the child's
  translation, composition, or logic exercises. Drill scaffolds, chant
  tracking, review schedules, and parent answer-checking aids are the
  safe lane.
- Award-circuit lesson: CAP's eight 2025 firsts show how a mid-size
  publisher compounds reader awards into durable social proof (see
  `market-overview.md`); expect moms to cite the awards as shorthand
  for "safe choice."
