# Our Lady of Victory School (OLVS)

## Snapshot

Our Lady of Victory School is the traditionalist pole of the Catholic
homeschool market: a K-12 correspondence school, now in Post Falls,
Idaho, whose curriculum is deliberately "adapted from traditional
Catholic schooling from before 1960," meaning pre-conciliar religion
texts, Baltimore Catechism formation, and reprinted mid-century readers
and textbooks. It began as a parent-founded traditional school in
Mission Hills, California in 1973 (William and Ina Bowman among the
founders), became a homeschool program in 1977, and on that basis calls
itself the oldest Catholic homeschool provider in the United States,
predating Seton's 1980-82 organization (each claim is the school's own
framing). It moved to Idaho in 1991; its publishing arm Lepanto Press,
founded 1995, has reprinted hundreds of out-of-print Catholic texts that
also supply other programs. Full enrollment ($525-575 per student, books
extra) buys 36 weeks of lesson plans, quarter tests, an assigned tutor
who reviews submitted work, quarterly progress reports, a final report
card, and NAPCIS-accredited records; single-course and more independent
options exist, and the school now also brokers co-op, dual enrollment,
and private tutoring arrangements. OLVS serves the Latin Mass and
traditionalist families for whom even Seton reads as too modern, and its
enrollment mechanics mirror Seton's at a smaller, plainer scale.

## Reputation and rankings

- Coverage lives in traditionalist Catholic circles and directory
  listings rather than mainstream review sites; no Cathy Duffy review
  was found. Its reputation rests on longevity (three generations,
  second-generation students now enrolling, per the handbook), doctrinal
  reliability by traditionalist standards, and low cost relative to
  other enrollment schools.
- The trade-offs prospective families discuss are inherent to the
  positioning: dated texts and aesthetics (a feature for its audience,
  a defect outside it), a plain print-and-mail program in an online
  market, and a worldview stricter than mainstream diocesan Catholicism.
- Trajectory: stable niche, likely buoyed by the documented growth of
  traditionalist and Latin Mass communities; a fuller profile should
  gather enrollment figures, community sentiment from traditionalist
  forums, and how its texts overlap with MODG and Seton book lists via
  Lepanto Press reprints.
