# AmblesideOnline

## Snapshot

AmblesideOnline (AO) is a free, complete Charlotte Mason curriculum for
Years 0 through 12, built and maintained since 1999 by a volunteer Advisory
of homeschooling mothers. Its stated mission is to approximate, as closely
as modern book availability allows, the actual curriculum Mason ran in her
PNEU schools in England. AO is not a company and sells nothing: the website
provides booklists per year, 36-week reading schedules, subject rotations,
and a large support forum, and the family buys or borrows the books (many
are public domain). It began as Parents Union Online with grades 1-6 in
November 1999, had 400 families within two years, and was renamed for
Ambleside, the Lake District town where Mason taught. No usage statistics
exist (the project does not track users), but its footprint (a
decades-old forum, a published history book, and its status as the default
"real CM" answer in every community thread) gives it arguably the largest
mindshare per dollar in the homeschool world, since the dollar is zero.

## Reputation and rankings

- Standing in the CM community: AO is treated as the purist's benchmark, the
  closest thing to Mason's original programmes. Paid CM curricula (Simply
  Charlotte Mason, A Gentle Feast) are routinely explained to newcomers in
  terms of how they differ from AO.
- Community sentiment, positive: the book selections ("I absolutely LOVE the
  literature selections" is representative of forum threads), the price, the
  depth of the free support forum, and visible narration gains in children.
- Community sentiment, critical, and consistent across years of threads and
  blog posts: it is "100% overwhelming at first"; the books are hard (a
  common recommendation is to place a child a year below grade level); some
  selections are old, out of print, or archaic in language; the website is
  dense and dated; and running several children on separate year-tracks is a
  scheduling burden. A recurring failure mode parents self-report: cutting
  the "riches" (artist study, composer study, poetry) when overwhelmed, then
  regretting it.
- Awards: none in the usual commercial sense; AO does not market itself and
  is absent from paid-award circuits. Its longevity and the volume of
  third-party "how we AO" content are the evidence of trajectory: stable
  for two decades, with steady mindshare among CM families.

## Pedagogy and method

AO is the strictest widely-used implementation of Mason's method (for the
philosophy itself see
`docs/homa-planning/icp-research/10-homeschool-methods-glossary.md`):

- Living books over textbooks: every content subject is taught through
  whole, literary books, chosen for the highest standard the Advisory could
  find in print or online. Abridgments and "twaddle" are rejected on
  principle.
- Narration as the engine of assessment: every scheduled reading is
  narrated (told back) by the child, orally in the early years, in writing
  from roughly age 10. There are no comprehension worksheets, no quizzes in
  the early years; term exams are narration-style questions.
- Short lessons and wide curriculum: many subjects touched in a day, each
  brief (Year 1 copywork is capped at 5-10 minutes).
- The "riches" done together as a family and as a worldwide community: AO
  publishes shared term rotations for artist study, composer study, folk
  songs, hymns, Plutarch, Shakespeare, and nature study; all years, and in
  principle all AO families everywhere, study the same artist and composer
  each term.
- Habit formation, outdoor life, and the child as a person are the
  underlying Mason principles; the Advisory repeatedly insists the
  curriculum is a tool and that parents must read Mason's own volumes
  (hosted free on the site) to use it well.
- Deliberate caution about audiobooks: the site advises "extreme caution,"
  arguing Mason's language-arts results depend on visual exposure to text.

## Structure and scope

- Years 0 (gentle kindergarten, mostly outdoor life and read-alouds) through
  Year 12. Years only loosely correspond to grades; combining siblings into
  one year is officially encouraged. Years 7-11 offer "Basic" and
  "Detailed" booklist variants; a child finishing Year 9 is described as
  having read at senior-high level.
- The school year is 36 weeks in three 12-week terms (roughly Sep-Nov,
  Jan-Mar, Apr-Jun), matching PNEU practice.
- Per year, AO provides: a booklist page (with detailed footnotes on
  editions and substitutions), a weekly schedule spreading each book across
  the term, term exam questions, and links to free online texts where they
  exist.
- What AO deliberately does not provide: math, phonics/early reading
  instruction, and foreign language courses. Families choose their own
  programs for those; AO schedules only the content and culture subjects.
  History in Year 1 covers roughly 55 BC to 1066 chronologically alongside
  biography-style "people" stories; the sequence proceeds chronologically
  through the years with a Book of Centuries (timeline notebook) from the
  middle years.
- Support infrastructure: the AO Forum (free), the AO-HELP emergency
  minimalist plan, digitized Mason volumes and Parents' Review articles,
  and the Advisory/Auxiliary volunteer structure.

## A lesson up close

There is no teacher script and no student workbook: the parent's artifact
is the year's 36-week schedule (a table of readings per book per week,
printable from the site) plus the stack of books it references. A Year 1
morning in practice: the parent reads one scheduled history or tale
selection aloud (experienced users break long readings into 5-6 page
chunks), then the child narrates it back before anything else happens. One
reading, one narration, several times across the morning, interleaved with
the child's separate math and phonics lessons (whatever program the family
chose), 5-10 minutes of copywork in best handwriting, and one daily "rich"
(looking at the term's painting, listening to the term's composer, a folk
song, a poem). Afternoons are intended for outdoor time and nature study
with a nature notebook.

The parent's real workload is invisible on the page: pre-reading or
skimming selections, deciding day-by-day pacing (AO schedules by week, not
by day, on purpose), tracking which of several children has narrated which
book, and sourcing editions. What a completed day produces is almost
entirely oral and observational: narrations, a copywork line or two, a
nature-journal entry. There is no worksheet output at all in the early
years, which is the point of the method and the pain point for
compliance-state record keeping. By the upper years the output shifts to
written narrations, mapwork, dictation, and a Book of Centuries.

## Materials and visual design

AO has no house materials: the "materials" are trade books and public
domain texts, so the visual reality is whatever editions the family buys
or prints, plus the website itself. The site is text-dense, hand-built,
and utilitarian (long scrolling pages, footnotes, few images); veterans
defend it, newcomers bounce off it, and a cottage industry of third-party
planners, printable schedule cards, and "AO organized" Etsy products
exists precisely because AO ships no polished parent-facing artifact. The
aesthetic signal of an AO shelf is old books: fairy tale collections,
Victorian-era histories, hardback classics, a nature journal, watercolor
supplies. Families who want beautiful printed schedule books or
morning-time binders make or buy them from the community, not from AO.

## Pricing

- Curriculum, schedules, exams, forum, and digitized Mason volumes: free.
  No tuition, dues, or required donations (the About page is explicit).
- Real cost is books: many selections are free online (Project Gutenberg
  and AO's own hosted texts), and the rest are buyable used; forum users
  describe buying a modest set each year and borrowing what they can.
  Library use is partly frustrated by 12-week reading spans exceeding loan
  periods, pushing families toward owning. A realistic annual out-of-pocket
  is tens of dollars to low hundreds per child depending on how much is
  read on screens vs owned in print (no official figure exists; this is a
  community-reported range). As of Jul 2026 the model is unchanged: free.
- Multi-kid economics are the best in the category: books are reusable,
  nothing is consumable, and the riches are shared family-wide.

## Why families choose it

- Free, and not free-cheap but free-canonical: the most faithful Mason
  implementation available at any price.
- The book quality: a vetted, footnoted, two-decade-refined list of the
  best living books per subject per year.
- The community: the forum answers real scheduling and method questions,
  and worldwide shared rotations make a solo homeschool feel like a school.
- Trust in provenance: volunteers with no commercial motive, which this
  segment reads as integrity (the mirror image of CC's for-profit
  controversy).
- Depth without gimmicks for families philosophically committed to
  narration over worksheets.

## Why families leave it

- Overwhelm: the parent is the entire execution layer (pacing, pre-reading,
  narration, multi-child orchestration). Families wanting the same method
  with the planning done for them move to paid CM products: Simply
  Charlotte Mason, A Gentle Feast, The Peaceful Press, or CM-flavored
  open-and-go programs like The Good and the Beautiful.
- Book difficulty and archaism: children (and parents) who cannot connect
  with Victorian prose swap books or leave for gentler literature-based
  programs (BookShark, Sonlight, Build Your Library on the secular side).
- Multi-child load: separate year-tracks per child do not scale for some
  families; whole-family programs (Gather Round, My Father's World, A
  Gentle Feast's family style) are the common destination.
- Worldview mismatch: secular families use AO with substitutions but often
  migrate to secular CM lists (Build Your Library) rather than keep
  editing.
- Record-keeping states: no written output in early years means extra work
  inventing evidence; some families add a paper-producing program for that
  reason alone.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: Year (not grade, and possibly offset from grade),
  term (1-3, 12 weeks), narration (oral vs written), living books, twaddle,
  the riches, picture/artist study, composer study, Book of Centuries,
  copywork, dictation, Plutarch, free reads, Year 0, AO-HELP, Basic vs
  Detailed (Years 7-11). "We're in Y4 Term 2" pins the child's entire
  content scope; Homa should resolve it and never call it "4th grade."
- The gap Homa fills is exactly AO's missing execution layer: day-level
  pacing from a weekly schedule, multi-child orchestration, tracking which
  readings are narrated, generating compliance-friendly records from oral
  work (a narration log is evidence), and library-vs-buy logistics. None
  of that touches the method; all of it is the burden parents report.
- Materials rules for this family, per the segment card
  (`docs/homa-planning/icp-research/08-segments-curricula.md` §7 card 2):
  narration prompts, nature-study logs, Book of Centuries pages, and
  beautiful schedule artifacts are native; fill-in-the-blank drill sheets
  read as twaddle. Worksheets are tolerated for math, which AO does not
  supply anyway.
- Never generate "narrations" for the child or summarize the reading for
  them: narration IS the child's work, and automating it is the exact
  "AI does the child's thinking" landmine. Homa serves the parent's
  clipboard, not the child's mouth.
- What AO proves about the market: this segment pays with attention, not
  money, and rewards provenance and fidelity. A tool that quietly respects
  AO's structures (terms, years, rotations) without trying to replace or
  re-sell them earns trust that no amount of features buys.
