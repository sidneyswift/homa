# Moving Beyond the Page

## Snapshot

Moving Beyond the Page (MBTP) is a secular, literature-based,
interdisciplinary unit-study curriculum founded in 2005 by Kim and Keith
Howe, written for gifted and creative learners ages 4 to 14. It is the
integrated alternative in the secular box universe: instead of parallel
subjects, each 3-4 week unit organizes language arts, social studies, and
science (plus math at younger ages) around one concept, with real books,
projects, and science kits shipped in the package. Levels are age bands,
not grades, and the publisher openly pitches the content as running one
to two grade levels ahead. It sits price-wise with the boxes, not the
PDFs, and includes an online layer (Beyond the Page Online with adaptive
"Learning Gates" quizzes) bundled with full-year purchases.

## Reputation and rankings

Reviewer coverage is consistent: praised as convenient, scripted-but-
natural, hands-on, and genuinely challenging, with the same warnings
everywhere that the accelerated pace and low review-and-drill volume make
it a poor fit for kids who are behind, even one level down (Smarter
Learning Guide's long-form review is the fullest treatment). Community
sentiment in gifted and secular homeschool circles treats it as the
default "my kid is ahead and bored" answer, with recurring complaints
about cost, the volume of writing expected, and units that some kids find
over-structured. Its secularism is unambiguous (evolution and old-earth
science taught), so it passes SEA-style vetting without the debates that
follow BookShark. Trajectory: stable niche leader for gifted-leaning
secular families.

## Pedagogy and method

Concept-based interdisciplinary unit study. Each unit introduces a theme
and runs every subject through it, aiming at synthesis: kids explore,
discuss, build, and write rather than memorize. Teacher guides are
scripted enough to be open-and-go but deliberately stop short of
word-for-word dialogue. Critical-thinking questions, capstone projects,
and papers close out units. The math strand blends mastery and spiral,
leans conceptual, and is supplemented by literature connections; science
is standards-aligned and experiment-heavy, with kits supplying lab
materials. Parent involvement is high at ages 4-7 and tapers by design:
older students work increasingly from their own manuals, and the online
Learning Gates give auto-graded checks of new and past material at unit
ends. This is the rare curriculum in the category with built-in
assessment.

## Structure and scope

Nine age levels from 4-5 up to 12-14, each mapping to two possible grades
(the publisher's own chart reads "gifted" one grade and "average" the
next; age 7-9 is grade 2 gifted or grade 3 average). Subjects: language
arts, social studies, science, plus math and learning-to-read packages at
younger levels. A full-year package contains the publisher's own unit
workbooks and guides, the trade books each unit is built on,
manipulatives, and science supplies, plus 18 months of Beyond the Page
Online (Learning Gates, portfolio, purchased activity pages). Units can
be bought individually as supplements, which is how many eclectic
families actually consume MBTP: one science or social studies unit at a
time. Full-year buyers get 15% off retail. Additional students need a
consumables package rather than a full second kit.

## A lesson up close

A day inside a unit at the elementary levels: the parent opens the unit
guide to the day's lesson, which starts with a short scripted
introduction of the concept, moves into a reading from the unit's trade
book, then a discussion with provided questions, then an activity page or
hands-on project (poster, experiment, timeline card, model) from the
included materials. Older students read their own student manual and work
semi-independently, checking in for discussion. Lessons expect real
written output (short answers, paragraphs, and eventually papers), which
is the workload critics cite. At unit end the child takes a Learning Gate
online (adaptive questions over the unit plus earlier material) and often
completes a capstone project that pulls the subjects together. A
completed unit leaves a visible trail: a filled workbook, a project, and
a quiz record in the portfolio, the most conventional evidence artifact
in this category.

## Materials and visual design

Boxed kits combine the publisher's own color-printed unit workbooks and
guides (consumable, spiral or stapled, worksheet-and-activity hybrid
pages) with trade books, manipulatives, and science equipment (the kits
include the annoying-to-source lab items: thermometers, wire, beakers).
The look is bright, contemporary, and school-adjacent without being
textbook-dry; the signal is "enrichment program," closer to a gifted
classroom than to Charlotte Mason. Online components run in the
publisher's own web platform.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026, publisher store: age 5-7 full year with science, social
studies, LA, math, and reading $953.18 (or $808.68 without reading;
$552.74 for the three core subjects); age 12-14 three-subject full year
$1,100.50 (retail $1,294.70); additional-student consumables at that
level $576.87. Third-party reviews record full-year kits from about $499
to $1,153 and single subjects $156 to $415. Individual units run tens of
dollars, the common entry point. The consumable workbooks are the
per-child tax; books and manipulatives reuse. 18 months of the online
platform is bundled free with full-year packages.

## Why families choose it

- A genuinely challenging secular program: the standard pick when a
  gifted-leaning kid is bored by grade-level material.
- Integration: subjects reinforce one concept instead of running as
  parallel tracks (the thing BookShark families say they miss).
- Everything ships in the box, kits included, with online assessment
  built in: minimal assembly for a curriculum this project-heavy.
- Age-band placement flatters asynchronous kids and lets families place
  by readiness, not grade.

## Why families leave it

- Price: full-year packages cost box money, and the consumables repurchase
  stings for multi-kid families.
- Workload and writing volume: kids who resist output, or parents who
  wanted gentle, burn out; these families move to Blossom and Root or
  Build Your Library.
- The pace assumes ahead-of-grade: struggling or average learners fall
  off even a level down, by the publisher's own positioning.
- Structure fatigue: some families find the units prescriptive and drift
  to looser eclectic stacks, keeping only favorite single units.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: age levels (4-5 through 12-14), units, Learning
  Gates, Beyond the Page Online, consumables package, "concept." A family
  saying "we do MBTP 8-10" has an ahead-of-grade child, integrated
  content, and real assessment data already; Homa should meet them at
  that altitude, not below it.
- Materials lesson: MBTP proves the category's families will accept
  worksheets when they are activity-shaped and concept-tied, and that
  an end-of-unit adaptive check plus portfolio is a welcome evidence
  pattern. Homa's materials studio and progress evidence can borrow both
  shapes.
- Gap Homa fills: the single-units-as-supplements usage pattern means
  many families run MBTP units inside an eclectic stack; Homa can be the
  layer that schedules units alongside other vendors and carries progress
  across them. Also differentiation downward: MBTP has no answer for the
  family whose second child is not ahead; Homa can generate
  level-adjusted material inside the same themes.
- Direct overlap: Learning Gates plus portfolio is a light version of
  Homa's progress surface, but locked to one vendor's content.
