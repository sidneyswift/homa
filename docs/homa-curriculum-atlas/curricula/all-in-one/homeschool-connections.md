# Homeschool Connections

## Snapshot

Homeschool Connections is the course platform of the Catholic homeschool
world: an online provider founded in October 2008 by Walter Crawford (a
corporate learning-and-development veteran) and Maureen Wittmann (a
Catholic homeschooling author and speaker), offering 200+ live online
courses per year and a recorded library it markets at 500-700+ courses
for grades 3-12, all "loyal to the Magisterium of the Catholic Church."
It is deliberately not a school: no accreditation, no transcripts, no
diplomas, on the stated principle that parents are the educators and
accreditation hands their authority to outside agencies. The signature
product is Unlimited Access, a Netflix-style subscription ($39.97 a
month) that opens the whole recorded catalog to up to five students in
one family. It calls itself the largest Catholic online course provider,
a self-description no competitor obviously contradicts. Wittmann also
co-directs its sister event, The Catholic Homeschool Conference.

## Reputation and rankings

- Community reputation is strongly positive on value: the recorded
  library's family-wide pricing is repeatedly called the cheapest way to
  outsource taught instruction in the Catholic market (the platform's own
  comparison: typical online courses run $400-1,300 per year elsewhere,
  its live courses average $360 a year and recorded access far less).
- Teachers are a selling point: college professors and working
  professionals, many known names in Catholic education (its Theology of
  the Body courses are taught by a Theology of the Body Institute
  lecturer, for example).
- The critical themes are structural rather than quality complaints:
  recorded courses put grading back on the parent unless Instructor
  Access is bought per course; course quality and difficulty vary across
  a big catalog; and there is no school of record, so transcript-minded
  families must assemble records themselves (the platform recommends
  Kolbe Academy as an accrediting umbrella and partners with Fast
  Transcripts for professional transcript assembly; see
  `kolbe-academy.md`).
- Homa-relevant: Homeschool Connections is the Catholic publisher whose
  AI posture the repo has already documented; it publishes how-to guides
  on using ChatGPT for homeschool planning while warning against letting
  AI hollow out children's thinking
  (`../../../homa-planning/icp-research/08-segments-curricula.md` §7).
- Trajectory: rising; the recorded catalog compounds yearly and the
  subscription model fits the market's cost pressure.

## Pedagogy and method

The platform's method is taught courses, parent-administered. Live
courses (4 to 16 weeks, one or two meetings weekly) run synchronously
with instructor grading in most cases; recorded courses are self-paced
video lecture series with readings, assignments, auto-graded quizzes
where available, and answer keys or rubrics for the parent, who is the
grader of record. Instructor Access, sold per recorded course, buys
grading and feedback from the original instructor. There is no single
pedagogical school: the catalog spans classical trivium-styled offerings
(its classical page maps middle school to the logic stage and high
school to rhetoric), Great Books literature, apologetics and theology,
sciences, writing (the in-house Aquinas Writing Advantage lane), math,
and electives. The unifying commitments are doctrinal fidelity and
parental authority: the platform supplies instruction and structure,
and explicitly refuses the school-of-record role.

## Structure and scope

- Grades 3-12, with middle and high school the deep end of the catalog;
  the platform notes grammar-stage (elementary) course development is
  ongoing on its classical track.
- Live courses: 200+ per year across fall, spring, and summer terms,
  from one-week bootcamps to full-semester courses, priced individually.
- Recorded library: 500-700+ courses (the platform's own pages vary in
  the count as the catalog grows), all included in Unlimited Access;
  courses are retired with notice.
- One subscription covers up to five student profiles; a family can mix
  live and recorded simultaneously.
- Platform: Caravel, the in-house LMS, houses course content,
  assignments, and quizzes.
- Support layer: free parent webinars, a weekly podcast, curriculum
  consultants and accountability mentors, record-keeping templates, and
  reading lists. Materials for some courses (texts, novels) are
  purchased separately.
- Records: parents issue transcripts and diplomas; the platform provides
  templates, the Fast Transcripts partnership, and the Kolbe umbrella
  recommendation for families needing accredited records.

## A lesson up close

For a recorded high school course, the student's week in Caravel is a
video lecture (typically 45-60 minutes), an assigned reading, and an
assignment or quiz; auto-graded items return instantly, written work
goes to the parent with the course's rubric or answer key, unless the
family bought Instructor Access, in which case the instructor grades
and comments. A live course adds a scheduled class meeting (Zoom-style,
with chat participation), weekly deadlines, and instructor grading; a
$200-ish semester course meets about 12-15 times. The parent's role is
administrator: choosing courses, keeping the pace honest, recording
grades into their own transcript. What a completed course produces
depends on the tier: a graded record and instructor feedback (live or
Instructor Access) or a parent-graded portfolio (recorded), plus, in
either case, no institutional transcript unless the family builds one.

## Materials and visual design

The product is screens, not shelves: video lectures, PDF syllabi and
assignments, quizzes in Caravel. Production polish varies across a
catalog built over 15-plus years, from studio-quality recent courses to
older webinar-era recordings, a spread typical of large course
libraries. The brand look is functional Catholic-warm rather than
designed; the visual identity that matters to buyers is the instructor
roster and the imprimatur-adjacent trust language, not page design.
Required texts are mainstream Catholic publishers (Ignatius, Sophia,
TAN) and trade books, bought separately.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026 (the FAQ and live catalog are the live truth):

- Unlimited Access: $39.97 per month or $429.97 per year, covering all
  recorded courses for up to 5 students in one family; 7-day free trial.
- Single Access: $21.97 per month for one recorded course at a time.
- Live courses: $89-250 per semester course (the platform's stated
  range; "as a general rule, the cost for each course is $200"), with a
  $20 early enrollment discount per course and a $25 late surcharge.
- Instructor Access (grading for recorded courses): per-course stipend
  on top of the subscription, where offered.
- Course texts and materials: separate, varying by course.

The family-wide subscription is the pricing story: five students of
recorded courses for under $430 a year undercuts every enrollment school
in this category by an order of magnitude, at the cost of records and
grading landing back on the parent.

## Why families choose it

- Outsourced teaching at commodity price: real instructors for the
  subjects the parent cannot or will not teach, without tuition-scale
  cost.
- Doctrinal trust: the Magisterium pledge means no vetting per course.
- Flexibility: start any month, mix live and recorded, cancel the
  subscription; no contracts or enrollment windows.
- Big-family economics: one subscription, five students.
- The parent-authority stance: families who share the anti-accreditation
  philosophy get support without surrendering the records role.

## Why families leave it

- Records burden: transcript-minded high school families move to or add
  an enrollment school (Kolbe, Seton, MODG) when college prep begins.
- Grading burden on recorded courses: parents who wanted teaching
  outsourced discover assessment was not, and either pay per-course
  Instructor Access or leave for schools with grading built in.
- Self-direction limits: the recorded model needs a student who runs
  independently; younger or unmotivated students stall.
- Catalog unevenness: quality and difficulty vary; families curate or
  churn.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: Unlimited Access, Single Access, Instructor
  Access, Caravel (the LMS), live versus recorded, bootcamps, Aquinas
  Writing Advantage. "The kids do Homeschool Connections" usually means
  a multi-course recorded stack with the parent as registrar, and often
  another program underneath for elementary skills.
- The structural gap is Homa-shaped: the platform explicitly hands
  planning, pacing, grading, and records to the parent and sells
  templates for them. An assistant that tracks course progress across a
  five-student subscription, nudges pacing, and assembles a
  transcript-ready record does the exact clerical work the platform
  declines on principle, without touching its teaching.
- Its AI posture makes it a bellwether: this publisher already tells
  its families to use ChatGPT for planning while guarding the child's
  thinking. Homa's positioning (assist the parent's clipboard, never do
  the child's work) matches the line this market has already drawn.
- Subscription pricing is the reference point: Catholic families
  comparing Homa's price will have the $39.97 family-wide anchor in
  mind for "online help with school".
- Cross-links: recommended umbrella is Kolbe (`kolbe-academy.md`);
  the founder circle overlaps Sophia Institute Press publishing
  (Wittmann's The Catholic Homeschool Companion; see
  `sophia-institute-spirit-of-truth.md`).
