# My Father's World

## Snapshot

My Father's World (MFW) is a Christian all-in-one built for the multi-kid
family: one purchased core per year teaches Bible, history, geography,
science, literature, art, and music to all children in grades 2-8 at once,
with math and language arts added per child. Founded in 1998 by David and
Marie Hazell, who spent eight years in Bible-translation missionary work
in Russia before returning to run the company, it blends unit study,
Charlotte Mason, and classical methods with a strong missions thread
(students read missionary biographies and study world cultures with
"God's heart for the world" framing). Its signature mechanic is the
five-year Family Learning Cycle; its signature economics are one core
package per family instead of one per child. It is a Cathy Duffy Top Pick
and a long-standing mid-priced fixture of the Christian convention
circuit.

## Reputation and rankings

- Cathy Duffy reviews MFW as a complete PreK-12 program blending unit
  study, Charlotte Mason, and classical methods on a strong Protestant
  missionary base, and aggregators note her Top Pick designation.
- Community sentiment: praised for easy teacher's manuals, short days
  that can finish by midday, teaching multiple ages together, and
  affordability relative to per-child boxes. Recurring criticisms:
  science is widely called the weakest strand, the daily grid can feel
  repetitive by spring, and everything is paper (no digital option).
- Trajectory: stable legacy player. It holds the family-style niche
  against newer family-style entrants (Gather Round, TGTB history) that
  compete with more modern aesthetics.

## Pedagogy and method

- A hybrid by design: unit-study topical integration (subjects loosely
  organized around the year's theme), Charlotte Mason practices (living
  books, narration, short lessons, nature study), and a classical
  five-year history rotation.
- The teacher's manual is a week-per-spread daily grid telling the parent
  what to read and do per subject per day, with per-age depth guidance
  (a 7th grader completes assignments more thoroughly than a 3rd grader
  using many of the same resources).
- Bible is the declared organizing center, and missions saturate the
  reading list (missionary biographies like Hero Tales, prayer for
  world cultures via Window on the World).
- Parent role is high: the parent runs the family core sessions daily,
  then supervises per-child math and language arts.

## Structure and scope

- Three tiers: Discover (preschool-grade 3 programs like God's Creation
  from A to Z for kindergarten), Investigate (the grades 2-8 Family
  Learning Cycle), and Declare (grades 9-12 one-year packages combining
  history, English, and Bible with daily plans written to the student).
- The Family Learning Cycle rotates five cores: Exploring Countries and
  Cultures, Creation to the Greeks, Rome to the Reformation, Exploration
  to 1850, and 1850 to Modern Times. All 2nd-8th graders do the same core
  each year and rotate together; 7th-8th graders add a supplement.
- A core package includes the teacher's manual, student sheets, and the
  scheduled books and resources (atlases, encyclopedias, read-alouds,
  science resources); math (Singapore recommended in early grades, Saxon
  later) and language arts are separate per-child purchases.
- Pace: 34ish weeks, four or five days per week depending on the core.

## A lesson up close

- The parent opens the teacher's manual grid for the day: a Bible
  reading and memory item, a history or geography reading from a
  scheduled real book, a science reading or simple hands-on activity,
  and rotation extras (composer, artist, or handicraft touches).
- The family does these together at the table or couch, oldest to
  youngest; children narrate or complete a student-sheet page (maps,
  notebooking prompts, simple written responses) at their own depth.
- Kids then split for individual math and language arts from whichever
  separate programs the family bought.
- A completed day produces checked grid boxes, a student sheet or
  notebook entry per child, and read-aloud progress; days are designed
  to finish by early afternoon.

## Materials and visual design

- The teacher's manual is a utilitarian spiral or binder-style volume;
  student sheets are functional black-and-white printables; the visual
  richness comes from the packaged trade books (Usborne encyclopedias,
  atlases, picture-rich readers).
- Kits include tactile extras by core (the ECC package famously includes
  a toy passport for its around-the-world year).
- The aesthetic signals practical, missions-minded family school:
  warmer than Abeka, plainer than TGTB.

## Pricing

All prices as of Jul 2026.

- Exploring Countries and Cultures package: $538.05 sale, $597.83 list
  ([package page](https://www.mfwbooks.com/item/95101/Exploring-Countries-and-Cultures-Package/));
  other cores and Discover-level programs run lower, and the repo's
  Jul 2026 market table records MFW packages at $310-705
  ([segments research](../../../homa-planning/icp-research/08-segments-curricula.md) §4).
  Deluxe add-on bundles raise totals; the publisher's specials page lists
  current discounts ([pricing page](https://www.mfwbooks.com/about/specials/)).
- Math and language arts add roughly $100-300 per child per year
  (third-party estimate).
- Consumables economics are the pitch: one core per family per year, only
  student sheets and per-child skill subjects re-bought, and the packaged
  trade books resell or pass down. A three-kid family runs a year for
  well under one Sonlight ASP.
- High school Declare packages are per-student and cost more; an optional
  coaching service adds $399 (publisher pricing page).

## Why families choose it

- One purchase teaches the whole family: the cheapest credible way to
  school three or four kids together in the Christian market.
- Open-and-go manuals with short days, praised by moms of many littles.
- Missions and world-culture identity: for ministry-minded families it
  is the box that matches the household's values.
- The CM-classical blend gives structure without Abeka-style drill.

## Why families leave it

- Science thinness pushes families to add or switch (Apologia,
  Masterbooks science, secular programs).
- The grid gets monotonous: by February the same daily shape wears on
  some families (the generic burnout-and-drift lifecycle applies; see the
  [segments research](../../../homa-planning/icp-research/08-segments-curricula.md) §7 card 6).
- Families with one child lose the whole economic rationale.
- All-paper workflow frustrates families wanting digital planning or any
  video support.
- Wide age spreads strain the one-core model: a 2nd grader and an 8th
  grader share topics awkwardly.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: Family Learning Cycle, core (ECC, CTG, RTR, EX1850,
  1850MOD abbreviations circulate), Discover/Investigate/Declare tiers,
  student sheets, book basket (its optional extra-reading habit). Ask
  which core and which kids share it.
- MFW proves multi-kid economics drive purchases: Homa's per-child
  generation should also respect the teach-together pattern (one family
  activity, per-child depth), which maps directly to MFW's per-age
  assignment depths.
- Gap Homa fills: the per-child skill subjects MFW does not provide. An
  MFW family is already assembling math and LA separately, so Homa's
  planner reconciling family core plus per-child subjects is the exact
  shape of her week.
- Science supplementation is a socially accepted need in this community:
  generated hands-on science support offends no loyalty.
