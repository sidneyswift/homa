# Build Your Library

## Snapshot

Build Your Library (BYL) is the secular Charlotte Mason curriculum: full
K-12 literature-based lesson plans written by Emily Cook, a homeschool
mother of four and a co-founder of SEA Homeschoolers, which gives the
program near-official standing in the secular community. Each level is a
$30-60 PDF that schedules history, literature, poetry, science, and art
around living books, following a chronological history rotation across
the grades; families add their own math and skills-LA and source the
books from libraries or bookstores. It is the only program in the
secular PDF trio (BYL, Torchlight, Blossom and Root) that runs all the
way through grade 12. Homa's segment research records it as the
archetype of the $0-200 assembly universe
(`docs/homa-planning/icp-research/08-segments-curricula.md` §4).

## Reputation and rankings

Cathy Duffy carries a full review describing it as a secular option for
literature-based education along a historical timeline, with classic
Charlotte Mason mechanics (narration, copywork moving to dictation,
poetry memorization from first grade, nature journals) and modern
children's literature alongside older titles. Her review notes the
deliberate secular sourcing: even Bible stories appear only as mythology
among Egyptian, Chinese, Greek, and other myths. Community sentiment is
strongly positive in SEA and r/secularhomeschool circles: it is the
default recommendation for "secular Sonlight feel without the box price,"
praised for book selection quality, per-level completeness, and value.
Recurring criticisms: the book-sourcing legwork (dozens of titles per
level), the need to add math and skills LA, reading loads that strain
some families, and the single-author bus factor (updates and new levels
depend on one writer). Trajectory: stable and quietly institutional; the
newsletter, unit studies, and Read Aloud Book Club keep the audience
engaged between levels.

## Pedagogy and method

Charlotte Mason updated and secularized. Content subjects are taught
through living books read aloud or independently; the child narrates,
copies, and eventually takes dictation; poetry memorization, artist and
composer study, and nature journaling run through the levels. History is
the organizing spine, cycling chronologically from ancients to modern
across the program, with literature chosen to match periods. Science is
scheduled from secular trade books and, at some levels, integrates
outside programs. The parent teaches and reads; the lesson plans are
open-and-go in the sense that the week is fully scheduled, but the family
assembles the physical materials. No tests; assessment is narration and
the accumulating notebook work.

## Structure and scope

Thirteen year-long levels (0 through 12), each keyed to a grade with an
age band of about three years, so siblings close in age can share a
level. Kindergarten (Level 0) tours the continents; elementary and middle
levels run history cycles; upper levels include thematic deep dives (for
example, Level 12 is an American history year). Each level PDF includes
full daily and weekly schedules for 36 weeks, comprehensive book lists
(with spine books flagged for purchase versus library borrowing),
activities, supplemental links, and free lifetime updates within the
edition. Shorter multi-age unit studies ($8-13) cover popular series and
topics; a subscription Read Aloud Book Club adds curated family reading
with discussion booklets. Math, spelling, grammar, and phonics are
explicitly out of scope at every level; Cathy Duffy's review and the
publisher both direct families to pair their own programs.

## A lesson up close

The parent prints or reads the level PDF's weekly grid: each day lists
readings by book and pages (history spine, literature, poetry, science),
plus scheduled narration, copywork or dictation, mapwork, timeline
entries, and periodic art or nature study. A day at an elementary level:
the parent reads the history and literature selections aloud (or the
child reads assigned pages independently at higher levels), the child
narrates back, completes a short copywork passage, adds a timeline
figure, and marks a map; a couple of days a week bring a science reading
with an activity or a picture study. Output is a notebook-and-timeline
trail rather than worksheets. The plans are dense enough that most
families treat the grid as the spine of the whole homeschool day, fitting
their separate math and phonics around it.

## Materials and visual design

The purchased artifact is a functional PDF: schedule grids, book lists,
activity instructions, designed for home printing. The lived materials
are the books themselves: 30-50 real titles per level spanning modern
diverse fiction and classic nonfiction. The aesthetic signal is a home
library with a timeline on the wall, deliberately anti-textbook. The
family supplies binders, timeline figures, maps, and notebooks. Because
plans are digital with lifetime edition updates, there is no consumable
economy at all; the cost of a second child is printing and library holds.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026, the publisher's posted price structure: Level 0 $29.95,
Levels 1-4 $39.95, Levels 5-8 $49.95, Levels 9-12 $59.95, unit studies
$7.95-12.95 (https://buildyourlibrary.com/new-pricing-restructure/).
Books add $300-600 per level if purchased new (numaschool's estimate,
matching the repo's segment research), but heavy library use is normal
and assumed. Periodic sales run about 20% off. Total realistic annual
cost for a committed library user: under $100 plus a separate math
program, which is why BYL anchors price comparisons across the secular
assembly universe.

## Why families choose it

- SEA-grade secular trust: the author co-founded SEA Homeschoolers, so no
  hidden-content vetting is needed.
- Full K-12 runway: the only secular CM program a family can stay in
  through high school.
- Price: a complete year of content subjects for the cost of two
  hardcovers.
- Charlotte Mason mechanics without religious framing, with modern and
  diverse book lists.

## Why families leave it

- Book logistics: sourcing dozens of titles each year defeats families
  without strong library systems; some move to BookShark to buy the
  books in one box.
- Read-aloud fatigue and the parent-led load, same as every program in
  this family.
- The skills gap: math and phonics must come from elsewhere, and
  families wanting one-vendor simplicity drift to boxes or online
  programs.
- Kids who need more hands-on or visual variety than a book-heavy day
  provides; those families blend in unit studies or switch to Blossom
  and Root or Moving Beyond the Page.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: levels 0-12 (grade-keyed), spines, narration,
  copywork and dictation, timeline, unit studies, Read Aloud Book Club.
  "We do BYL 3 with Singapore and ETC" is a canonical Homa beachhead
  stack: BYL for content, separate math and phonics, library workflow.
- Materials lesson: the weekly grid plus book list with spine flags is
  the assembly universe's standard planning artifact. Homa's planner
  should be able to represent "readings from N books plus narration plus
  timeline" days natively, not just worksheet-style tasks.
- Gap Homa fills: the library-sourcing labor (holds, substitutions when
  a title is unavailable), the skills subjects BYL omits, progress
  evidence from narration-based learning (Homa's observational model
  fits perfectly), and continuity when families bounce between BYL,
  Torchlight, and Blossom and Root levels.
- Respect the copyright posture: schedules are the paid product across
  this whole category; Homa must never reproduce plan content, only
  organize a family's own use of it.
