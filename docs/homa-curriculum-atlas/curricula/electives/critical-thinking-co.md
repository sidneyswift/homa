# The Critical Thinking Co.

## Snapshot

The Critical Thinking Co. is the workbook empire of the thinking-skills
aisle: a family-owned Oregon publisher (founded 1958 by John Baker as
Midwest Publications, renamed in stages to the current name in 2003, now
run by his son Michael Baker) with 200+ titles that wrap logic,
reasoning, and puzzle mechanics around reading, writing, math, science,
and history. Its anchor series are fixtures of homeschool life: Building
Thinking Skills (the publisher calls it the world's bestselling thinking
skills program), Mind Benders deductive grid puzzles, Editor in Chief
editing workbooks, the Detective series (Reading, Math, Science, U.S.
History), Word Roots, and the Mathematical Reasoning full math line. The
company is also the market's most decorated award collector: product
pages stack Cathy Duffy Top Picks, Mensa recommendations, Old
Schoolhouse and Practical Homeschooling awards, and parenting-media
seals going back decades, all under a money-back guarantee of "better
grades and higher test scores."

## Reputation and rankings

- The award stack on a single product (Building Thinking Skills Level
  2) is a case study: Cathy Duffy 100 and 101 Top Picks, Mensa
  Recommended Materials for the Gifted, Old Schoolhouse 1st Place 2012,
  Practical Homeschooling Reader Awards placements across years
  (including 3rd place in Logic, 2025), Learning Magazine Teachers'
  Choice, Dr. Toy 100 Best, Creative Child, National Parenting Center,
  PTPA. The company markets "200+ award-winning" titles and has an
  entire awards page; it is the elective aisle's clearest example of
  award-circuit economics (see `market-overview.md`).
- Cathy Duffy calls Building Thinking Skills "probably the most
  comprehensive resource for thinking skills for younger students,"
  with 10-20 minute low-prep lessons.
- Community sentiment (Well-Trained Mind forums, review crews): the
  gold standard for cognitive skill-building, credited with test-score
  and math-reading transfer; the standing critique is volume and
  dryness, thick books that turn into busy work, with veterans
  advising families to skip sections once a concept is mastered.
- The Well-Trained Mind lists it as a recommended logic resource,
  which cements its default status in classical homes' logic-stage
  planning alongside [The Fallacy Detective](fallacy-detective.md).
- Trajectory: stable institution. Convention-hall mainstay for
  decades, used by Sylvan and Club Z tutoring and gifted programs (per
  the publisher), with digital and eBook formats layered onto the
  print catalog.

## Pedagogy and method

The house method is puzzle-forward explicit reasoning: instead of drill
and memorization (the company's stated enemy), every workbook teaches a
thinking operation (classifying, sequencing, analogy, deduction,
inference, evidence citation) and exercises it against academic
content. Building Thinking Skills sequences each skill from
figural-spatial form (shapes, patterns) to verbal form (words,
statements), the developmental ramp that reviewers credit for its
test-prep transfer. Mind Benders are pure deductive logic grid puzzles
graded PreK to adult. The Detective series requires evidence citation:
students answer comprehension questions and must cite the sentence
numbers that support the answer, a mechanic teachers praise
specifically. Editor in Chief inverts grammar instruction: students
find and fix embedded errors in passages rather than completing rules
exercises. Most books are designed for independent work after a short
parent start; assessment is the answer key in the back. There is no
scripted teaching, no video, no religion, and no fuss: the workbook is
the whole product.

## Structure and scope

- Building Thinking Skills: Beginning (toddler-PreK), Primary (K-1,
  with optional manipulatives: interlocking cubes, attribute blocks),
  Level 1 (grades 2-3, 300+ pages), Level 2 (grades 4-6, 412 pages),
  Level 3 Figural and Level 3 Verbal (grades 7-12).
- Mind Benders family: Mind Benders (PreK-12+), Math, Science, and
  Crypto Mind Benders, Balance Benders (algebraic balance puzzles).
- Language arts: Editor in Chief (2-12+), Language Smarts, Language
  Mechanic, Word Roots, Punctuation Puzzler, Inference Jones, Reading
  Detective.
- Math: Mathematical Reasoning (a full PreK-9 math curriculum, not
  just a supplement), Math Detective, Math Analogies, CrossNumber
  puzzles, Understanding Algebra and Fractions lines.
- Content subjects: Science Detective, Science Mind Benders, Critical
  Thinking in United States History, U.S. and World History Detective,
  Developing Critical Thinking through Science.
- Test prep: OLSAT, CogAT-style Thinking Skills for Tests lines,
  Cornell Critical Thinking Tests.
- Formats: paperback workbooks and printable eBooks; many titles have
  historically carried family-reproducible licensing (a beloved
  feature), though some current products and bundles are marked
  non-reproducible, so families check per title.
- Usage pattern: almost nobody runs "a Critical Thinking Co.
  curriculum"; families buy one to three workbooks a year as the
  logic slot, the car-ride book, or the morning-basket warmup.

## A lesson up close

A Building Thinking Skills session is a two-page spread and a pencil:
the child reads a one-line instruction (complete the figural analogy,
sort the words into classes), works 8-15 items, and is done in ten to
twenty minutes. Early pages are
figural (match, rotate, complete the pattern); later pages run the
same operation verbally (word analogies, Venn diagrams, if-then
statements), and Level 2 asks for written explanations. A Mind Benders
session is one grid puzzle: read the clues, mark the grid, deduce who
owns which dog. An Editor in Chief page is a short passage with a set
number of hidden errors to find and correct. The parent's role is to
start young kids and check answers against the key; olders self-run.
What a completed day produces is a filled spread, a satisfying solved
puzzle, and, in portfolio states, exactly the kind of dated workbook
page evaluators like. The pages themselves are dense: item after item,
minimal decoration, full-color in newer editions but never cartoonish.

## Materials and visual design

Thick perfect-bound workbooks (224-416 pages is normal), letter size,
newer editions in full color with clean geometric art, older lines
still black and white. Layout is high-density and no-nonsense: numbered
items, answer blanks, the occasional illustration in service of a
problem. Covers are bright and badge-covered (award seals are a design
element, literally). The aesthetic signals "serious brain food from a
company older than the movement," and the heft-per-dollar is part of
the value perception: a $35-43 book lasts a year of ten-minute days.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026 (publisher product pages linked; street prices at
Rainbow Resource and Christianbook often a dollar or two lower):

- Building Thinking Skills: Beginning about $26.99, Level 1 about
  $34.99, Level 2 $42.99 (paperback or eBook); Primary sold alone
  ($29.99) or as a set with teacher manual and manipulatives.
- Mind Benders and similar puzzle books: roughly $11-15 each.
- Editor in Chief, Detective series, Word Roots: roughly $12-25 per
  book.
- eBook (printable PDF) versions typically price at parity with print.
- Family economics: single-purchase workbooks with answer keys, no
  subscriptions, no teacher editions required; reproducible-licensed
  titles stretch across siblings. This is one of the cheapest
  subject slots in a homeschool budget.

## Why families choose it

- The logic slot filled for under $45 a year with zero planning.
- Independence: a child with a pencil self-runs it, which makes it
  the default keep-one-child-busy book while the parent teaches a
  sibling.
- Gifted-program and Mensa aura plus test-score claims: parents
  buying cognitive advantage trust the badges.
- Test prep without test-prep dread: the puzzle framing does OLSAT
  and CogAT-adjacent work painlessly.
- Portfolio-friendly output in low-paper homeschool styles.

## Why families leave it

- Busy-work fatigue: the books are long, and completionist families
  burn out; veterans skip mastered sections or quit mid-book without
  guilt.
- Dryness for puzzle-averse kids: children who do not find grid
  puzzles fun experience it as extra school; those families drop the
  logic slot or move to game-based logic (logic games, chess, apps).
- Classical families at the formal-logic stage outgrow it upward:
  Building Thinking Skills is pre-formal, so trivium homes graduate
  to The Fallacy Detective, The Art of Argument (see
  `../all-in-one/classical-academic-press.md`), or formal logic
  texts.
- It is a supplement by nature: when budgets or hours tighten,
  supplements go first, the elective-triage pattern (see
  `../../comparisons/languages-electives.md`).

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: Building Thinking Skills (BTS) and its level
  bands, Mind Benders, Editor in Chief, the Detective books, Word
  Roots, Mathematical Reasoning (their full math line, distinct from
  the supplements). "We do Mind Benders on Fridays" is a common,
  complete description of a family's logic program.
- The evidence-citation mechanic (Detective series: cite the sentence
  that proves your answer) is a materials pattern Homa should adopt
  for generated comprehension work; it is cheap to generate, easy to
  check, and teachers already trust it.
- The figural-to-verbal ramp in BTS is a proven difficulty grammar
  for young kids: same skill, concrete form first, abstract form
  second. Homa's generated practice can use the identical ramp.
- Gap Homa fills: right-sizing. The chronic complaint is
  thick-book-as-busy-work; an assistant that assigns five items when
  five suffice, skips mastered sections, and logs the skill (not the
  page count) fixes the exact failure mode veterans hand-manage.
- Award-circuit lesson, shared with CAP: decades of small awards
  compound into default-choice status for a low-consideration
  purchase. Elective buyers lean on badges precisely because they
  will not research a $15 workbook; Homa's curriculum knowledge can
  be the better-than-badges answer.
