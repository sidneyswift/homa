# The Fallacy Detective

## Snapshot

The Fallacy Detective is the informal-logic gateway book of Christian
homeschooling: 38 short lessons on recognizing bad reasoning (23
fallacies plus propaganda techniques, red herring to post hoc), written
by homeschool-graduate brothers Nathaniel and Hans Bluedorn of the
Trivium Pursuit family and self-published since the early 2000s. The
publisher calls it the best-selling text for teaching logical fallacies
for over 15 years. Each 15-minute lesson pairs a conversational
explanation with comic strips (Calvin and Hobbes, Dilbert, Peanuts, and
the brothers' own Nuna and Toodles) and 10-20 exercises with an answer
key; the current workbook edition adds write-in pages and includes the
Fallacy Detective Game. It is the standard logic-stage on-ramp in
classical Christian homes (often before or instead of CAP's The Art of
Argument, see `../all-in-one/classical-academic-press.md`), a Sonlight
package inclusion, and a co-op staple, typically started around ages
12-13 and often done aloud as family discussion.

## Reputation and rankings

- Cathy Duffy reviews it warmly (humor, reader-friendly style, low
  parent load) and, notably, files it as "secular but Christian
  friendly" despite the authors' own conservative-Christian framing.
- Community sentiment: beloved as the logic book kids actually enjoy;
  the recurring critique on Well-Trained Mind and Reddit is that some
  examples carry the authors' worldview and feel dated or politically
  slanted to some families.
- Reviewers position it as a living-book introduction, lighter than a
  semester course: families wanting formal logic graduate to The
  Discovery of Deduction (CAP) or Memoria's Traditional Logic; its own
  sequel, The Thinking Toolbox, teaches building arguments rather than
  spotting bad ones.
- Trajectory: stable classic with periodic edition refreshes (third
  edition current).
