# Duolingo (in homeschool practice)

## Snapshot

Duolingo is the dominant consumer language app (40+ languages, publicly
traded, launched 2012) and, in homeschool practice, the single most
common answer to "what do you do for foreign language."
This profile covers how homeschoolers actually use it, not the company:
as a free, gamified daily habit (streaks, XP, leaderboards, the Birdbrain
adaptive algorithm) that delivers vocabulary and listening exposure with
essentially zero parent effort. The community verdict is remarkably
uniform: universally loved as a supplement, universally judged
insufficient as a curriculum, because systematic grammar instruction,
writing, conversation, and cultural context are thin. Two extensions
matter for families: Duolingo ABC, a genuinely free, ad-free early
literacy app for ages 3-8 (700+ phonics-forward lessons), and the paid
Max tier's AI conversation features. The free Duolingo for Schools
dashboard, which some homeschool parents used for oversight, is being
sunset on July 31, 2027.

## Reputation and rankings

- The homeschool review ecosystem speaks with one voice: "universally
  loved as a fun supplement... universally considered insufficient as a
  standalone high school foreign-language credit"
  ([homeschoolfox](https://homeschoolfox.com/curriculum/duolingo)).
  Cathy Duffy and Well-Trained Mind forum sentiment are cited to the
  same effect ([Homeschool Tools](https://homeschooltools.net/offering/duolingo)).
- The clearest statement of the limit comes from language-teaching
  homeschoolers: it "can help your child practice a language, but it
  won't teach your child a new language" in the systematic way a
  curriculum would ([Language Learning At Home](https://languagelearningathome.com/blog/why-duolingo-not-homeschool-foreign-language-curriculum)).
- Standing complaints: streak pressure that becomes burnout; silly
  machine-generated sentences; kids quietly disengaging with no parent
  dashboard to reveal it; gamification optimizing for streaks over
  retention; occasional content sentences conservative families object
  to.
- Duolingo ABC has a separately strong reputation: free, no ads, no
  in-app purchases, built on National Reading Panel-style phonics
  pillars, and commonly recommended as a car-seat literacy supplement.
- Trajectory: massive and stable as a consumer product, but its
  education-facing scaffolding is retreating (Schools dashboard
  sunsetting 2027) while AI conversation features (Max) expand.

## Pedagogy and method

Duolingo teaches through translation-based micro-exercises: match,
tap-to-order, type, listen, and speak items in 3-10 minute sessions,
sequenced by an adaptive model (Birdbrain) with spaced review woven in,
a spiral by construction. Grammar is taught implicitly by pattern
exposure; explicit explanations exist in small "tips" surfaces but the
app never requires understanding a paradigm, which is precisely the
homeschool critique. Motivation design is the actual product: streaks,
XP, leagues, hearts (mistake limits on free tier), and a pushy owl
mascot. Speaking practice is minimal (short repeat-after prompts;
GPT-4-powered Video Call conversations exist only in the $30/mo Max
tier). There is no writing instruction, no authentic reading, and no
assessment a parent can act on. For a homeschool parent the method
question is inverted from a normal curriculum: the app supplies practice
and consistency; the family must supply instruction, conversation, and
accountability from elsewhere.

## Structure and scope

- Language courses in 40+ languages for English speakers, with Spanish
  and French deepest (aligned loosely to CEFR bands; Duolingo for
  Schools exposed ACTFL/CEFR mappings). Latin exists but is short,
  a frequent disappointment for classical families.
- Course structure: a single path of units and levels per language, with
  placement via a short diagnostic. No scope-and-sequence document a
  parent can plan against, one of the most-cited homeschool frustrations
  ([Homeschool Languages](https://www.homeschoollanguages.com/blogs/blog/language-learning-apps-how-s-duolingo-for-homeschoolers)).
- Adjacent tracks: math and music courses inside the main app, and
  Duolingo ABC as a separate free app (ages 3-8, 700+ lessons, phonics,
  sight words, tracing, interactive read-along stories).
- Oversight: Duolingo for Schools (free classroom dashboard, assignment
  and progress visibility) sunsets July 31, 2027, after which parents
  are back to looking over shoulders or sharing accounts.
- In-practice usage pattern per reviewers: 10-20 minutes daily as a
  warm-up or habit layer, paired with a textbook program, tutor, co-op
  class, or graded readers when the family is serious.

## A lesson up close

A homeschool Duolingo session is a phone or tablet ritual, often
attached to breakfast or a car ride: open the app, defend the streak,
clear one or two lessons of tap-and-match exercises with instant
feedback and sound effects, maybe a league check. Five to fifteen
minutes, no paper, no parent. What a day produces is app state: XP, a
longer streak, a progress bar. There is nothing for a portfolio, and
nothing that tells the parent what was actually learned beyond "she did
her Duolingo." In Duolingo ABC the session is a young child tracing
letters, sounding out words, and tapping through a read-along story,
with the same reward-loop energy tuned gentler. The contrast with every
print program in this atlas is the point: zero prep and zero friction,
but also zero artifacts and zero parent visibility, which is why
reviewers describe families discovering weeks of motion without real
progress only when the streak dies.

## Materials and visual design

Pure app: bright flat-color cartoon world, the green owl (Duo),
character voices, celebration animations, haptics. It reads as a game
because it is designed as one, and that is both the hook (kids ask to do
it) and the tell (it does not read as school). No print component
exists; nothing is designed for paper. Duolingo ABC shares the visual
family with softer pacing and picture-book-style story screens. The
design signal to a homeschool buyer is "this will require no pushing,"
the exact inverse of the workbook programs it supplements.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026 ([homeschoolfox](https://homeschoolfox.com/curriculum/duolingo)):

- Free tier: full course access with ads and hearts (mistake limits).
- Super Duolingo: $14.99/mo or $83.88/yr (no ads, unlimited hearts).
- Family Plan: $119.99/yr for up to 6 members, the strong homeschool
  economics (siblings plus parents on one plan).
- Duolingo Max: $30/mo or $167.88/yr, adds GPT-4-powered Video Call
  conversation practice and Explain My Answer.
- Duolingo ABC: entirely free, no ads, no in-app purchases.
- Credit math, for context: one high school foreign language credit is
  typically documented as 120-150 hours; families using Duolingo toward
  credit log hours and pair it with graded work
  ([homeschoolpicks guide](https://homeschoolpicks.com/curriculum-reviews/best-homeschool-foreign-language-curriculum-complete-guide/)).

## Why families choose it

- Free, instant, and zero-prep: the lowest possible activation energy
  for "we should really do a language."
- The child asks to do it: streak mechanics outsource the nagging.
- Multi-language sampling: a kid can try Japanese, drop it, try Spanish,
  at no cost, which fits interest-led homes.
- Family Plan covers everyone, and mom often runs her own streak
  alongside, making it a shared family habit.
- Duolingo ABC gives the preschooler a trusted free literacy app while
  older kids do languages.

## Why families leave it

- The proficiency reckoning: after months of streaks the child still
  cannot hold a conversation, and the family realizes exposure is not
  instruction. Destinations: textbook programs (Breaking the Barrier,
  Spanish for Children), tutors and co-op classes, or immersion-style
  products.
- The high school credit decision: when a transcript needs "Spanish I"
  with hours, grading, and writing, Duolingo alone cannot justify it,
  so it gets demoted to daily supplement next to a credit-bearing
  course (this exact fork is the languages story in
  `../../comparisons/languages-electives.md`).
- Streak burnout and gamification fatigue: the motivation loop stops
  working or starts feeling coercive, and parents kill the app rather
  than fight the owl.
- Silent stalling: no parent dashboard (worse after the 2027 Schools
  sunset), so disengagement is discovered late.
- Content objections: sporadic example-sentence themes push some
  conservative families to curated alternatives.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: streak, XP, hearts, leagues, Super vs Max, Family
  Plan, Duolingo ABC, the Latin course's shortness. "We do Duolingo"
  most often means a daily 10-minute Spanish or French habit with no
  other language instruction; the right follow-up is whether anyone
  needs credit and whether proficiency matters yet.
- Duolingo owns the habit layer, not the curriculum layer. Homa should
  never compete with the streak; it should complement it the way the
  community already does: treat Duolingo as logged practice minutes and
  supply the missing structure (grammar spine, conversation plan,
  records).
- The records gap is concrete and Homa-shaped: families using Duolingo
  toward credit need hour logs, course descriptions, and evidence
  artifacts the app will never produce, and the one oversight surface
  (Schools dashboard) is being retired in 2027. Turning "247-day
  streak" into a defensible transcript line is exactly Homa's clerical
  lane.
- The engagement lesson cuts both ways: Duolingo proves kids will
  self-run a daily loop with the right reward design, and also proves
  (per the churn stories) that engagement mechanics without visible
  learning breed parent distrust. Homa's kid-facing surfaces should
  borrow the consistency loop, not the streak coercion.
- Framing competitor, like Khan: Duolingo anchors "language learning is
  free," so Homa should position language help as orchestration and
  evidence, never as content the app already gives away (same posture
  as [khan-academy](../online-digital/khan-academy.md)).
