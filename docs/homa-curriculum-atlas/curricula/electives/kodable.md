# Kodable

## Snapshot

Kodable is the pre-reader end of coding: a K-5 game suite whose core app
(Kodable Basics) has children guide fuzzy ball creatures through mazes by
arranging arrow and logic tiles, teaching sequencing, conditionals, and
loops before a child can read. Jon Mattingly and Grechen Huebner founded
it in 2012 as a University of Louisville dorm-room startup (legal name
SurfScore, later a Y Combinator/Imagine K12 company), and it grew into an
elementary-school staple; the company's self-reported claim, echoed on
its App Store listing, is use in over half of US elementary schools. The
home product has broadened into a "digital literacy suite" for ages 4-10:
Basics (K-2 coding), Creator (grades 3-5, game design moving from blocks
toward JavaScript, with an AI tutor layer), Bug World (debugging), and a
Kodable Typing app, all under one Pro subscription covering up to four
child profiles. For homeschoolers the fine print matters: the school
plans that make Kodable free for classmates only work weekdays 7am-4pm,
so home use effectively requires the paid Pro plan.

## Reputation and rankings

- Teacher-side reputation is the moat: K-2 teachers treat it as the
  default first coding unit, and its standards alignment (Common Core,
  CSTA, ISTE) plus zero-prep dashboards drove the school footprint the
  marketing leans on. The 50-percent-of-schools figure is self-reported
  and worth labeling as such.
- Parent reviews are warm about engagement (the fuzz characters land
  with 4-to-7-year-olds) and about kids progressing without help; the
  recurring critiques are the subscription price for what feels like a
  single long game, content that thins for kids past about age 8, and
  upsell screens when school-linked accounts hit the after-hours gate.
- In homeschool coding roundups it plays the "before Scratch" slot:
  recommended for pre-readers, with the expectation that families
  graduate to Scratch or a block-to-text platform by grade 2-3.
- Trajectory: stable, with the product visibly repositioning from
  "coding app" to broader digital-literacy suite (typing, STEM,
  creativity) to widen the home subscription's value case.
