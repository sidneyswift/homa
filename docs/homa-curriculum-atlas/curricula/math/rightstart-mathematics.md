# RightStart Mathematics

## Snapshot

RightStart Mathematics (second edition, RS2) is Dr. Joan Cotter's
visualization-first program from Activities for Learning, in levels A
through H that the publisher pairs with kindergarten through grade 7. Its
centerpiece is the two-sided Cotter Abacus (AL Abacus), grouped in fives
and tens so children see quantities instead of counting them, and its
practice engine is math card games instead of worksheets. It is the most
teacher-intensive major math program and the one with the deepest
number-sense reputation: users evangelize it, and quitters usually quit
the daily teaching load, not the math.

## Reputation and rankings

A Cathy Duffy 103 Top Pick, praised for the abacus technique and its
de-emphasis of counting in favor of subitizing. Community sentiment is
consistently "best number sense we ever built" set against "I could not
sustain an hour a day per kid"; reviewers put first-year cost near $300
and daily parent-led time at 30 to 60 minutes. Trajectory: stable niche
anchor of the hands-on conceptual corner, with newer scripted programs
(notably Math with Confidence) now competing for its parents with
lighter logistics.

## Pedagogy and method

Cotter's research-based method teaches quantity recognition without
counting: fives-and-tens grouping on the abacus, early place value with
"math way" number naming (forty-two as four-ten two), and mental
strategies before written algorithms. Lessons are scripted dialogues;
six special card-game decks replace flash cards and most drill. Levels
interleave strands (arithmetic, geometry, measurement, money) with
continuous game-based review, fitting neither strict mastery nor strict
spiral. Levels G and H pivot to a drawing-tools geometry approach that
is unusual and polarizing.

## Structure and scope

Eight levels, A through H, publisher-paired to K through 7. Each level
needs its Book Bundle (scripted lessons book plus consumable worksheets)
and the shared RS2 Math Set: AL Abacus, place value cards, game decks,
fraction charts, geoboards, math balance, drawing tools, and more, all
non-consumable. A free placement test places by level, not age. The
repo's math crosswalk maps levels A through H to grade anchors and bank
items and flags G and H as geometry-led years where families often
insert a separate pre-algebra year: see
`docs/homa-benchmarks/13-curriculum-crosswalk-math.md` section 5.

## A lesson up close

The parent teaches from a script: a short warm-up review, a hands-on
segment where the child builds or visualizes on the abacus while the
parent follows the printed dialogue, usually a card game as the practice
block, and often a single thin worksheet page or none. The worksheets
are deliberately secondary; the game is the practice. A lesson runs 30
to 45 minutes of genuine side-by-side teaching, longer in upper levels.
A completed day produces little paper, which parents love pedagogically
and record-keepers find awkward.

## Materials and visual design

Spiral-bound lessons books in utilitarian black and white, thin
worksheet books, and a manipulative box that is the real product: a
sturdy abacus and classroom-quality decks and tools built to serve
levels A through F and multiple children. The aesthetic is functional
workshop, not decorated workbook.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026: Book Bundles about $97.50 per level (about $97 to $99
at Christianbook and Rainbow Resource), RS2 Math Set about $218.50, so
year one lands near $316 and later years near $100. Levels G and H add
roughly $50 to $75 of tools. Manipulatives resell well. Price pages:
https://rightstartmath.com (store).

## Why families choose it

They want deep number sense and a child who thinks in quantities rather
than counted fingers, they have a hands-on or wiggly learner, and they
enjoy teaching. Game-based practice builds fact fluency without tears,
and the abacus gives visual learners a model that sticks.

## Why families leave it

Parent time: one scripted hour per child per day breaks down with
multiple kids, typically around levels C to E. Destinations are Math
with Confidence (scripted but lighter), Math Mammoth or Teaching
Textbooks (independence), or Beast Academy pairings for strong kids.
The G and H geometry years are a second exit toward conventional
pre-algebra sequences.

## Competitive notes for Homa

Assume high parent investment and strategy-first vocabulary (abacus,
math way counting, games by deck name). Little worksheet output does not
mean little math, and level G's geometry year makes flat arithmetic
movement the curriculum's shape, not a stall (the crosswalk carries
this). Homa's openings: game-adjacent printable practice honoring the
no-drill philosophy, record-keeping help for a low-paper program, and
bridge guidance after level F or H. The scripted-dialogue-plus-game loop
is a strong pattern reference for Homa's parent-led materials.
