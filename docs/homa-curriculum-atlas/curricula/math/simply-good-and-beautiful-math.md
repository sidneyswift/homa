# Simply Good and Beautiful Math

## Snapshot

Simply Good and Beautiful Math is The Good and the Beautiful's K through
8 math line, and the market's boldest pricing play: every course book is
a free PDF download, with low-priced printed sets and wooden Math Boxes
sold alongside. The 2021-era redesign made it a self-contained,
beautifully produced spiral program: parent-led for K to 3, student-led
with short videos from Math 4 up, capped deliberately at Math 8
(Pre-Algebra) with a published exit ramp to other high school programs.
Its distribution rides the enormous TGTB ecosystem; this profile covers
the math line, and the company-level story lives at
`../all-in-one/the-good-and-the-beautiful.md`.

## Reputation and rankings

A Cathy Duffy 103 Top Pick; Duffy calls the program "unusually creative
and interesting" and a bargain even in print. Community sentiment tracks
the wider TGTB split: fans praise the beauty, the open-and-go lessons,
and the price; critics note the spiral jumps topics quickly, upper
levels run gentle for STEM-track students, and secular families avoid
the publisher on principle even though the math is nearly religion-free.
The publisher states the program "aligns closely with standard grade
levels and is not considered an advanced curriculum," the only publisher
in the crosswalk set to disclaim advanced positioning. Trajectory:
strong and stable, powered by the free PDF funnel and the ecosystem.

## Pedagogy and method

Primarily spiral: concepts are taught, revisited, and expanded in small
steps with a labeled review section closing most lessons. The style is
eclectic and sensory: manipulatives and games in the early years, visual
variety everywhere ("no two pages look alike" is Duffy's summary),
connections to nature and art, and stories carrying character virtues.
K through 3 are scripted for the parent; Math 4 through 8 hand the lesson
to the student through 12 to 15 minute videos plus written mini-lessons,
an unusually early independence handoff. Assessment is end-of-unit tests
inside the course books.

## Structure and scope

Nine levels, Math K through Math 7 plus Pre-Algebra (Math 8), each with
120 lessons in three or four units. Components: a single consumable
course book per level (Math 6 has two, Math 7 four), free video lessons
for Math 4 up, answer keys (free PDFs; printed for purchase from Math 4),
and wooden Math Boxes for K through 3 (purchasable, or DIY instructions
provided). Placement is a single free PDF test covering K through 8. The
publisher states no higher levels will be made and provides a high school
tracks chart for what comes next. Grade alignment and level details are
mapped in the repo's crosswalk: see
`docs/homa-benchmarks/13-curriculum-crosswalk-math.md` section 8.

## A lesson up close

In the early levels the parent opens the course book (teacher and
student material in one volume) to a lesson run as a guided
conversation: a short warm-up, a mini-lesson with Math Box
manipulatives, a game printed inline with its game mat on the page, a
modest set of practice problems, and a labeled closing review. From
Math 4 the child watches the day's short video, works the written
mini-lesson, and completes practice and review independently while the
parent checks the answer key. Pages are full color with varied layouts
and moderate problem counts. A lesson runs 20 to 40 minutes and
produces two to four colorful worked pages in the consumable book.

## Materials and visual design

The visual identity is the company's signature: soft palettes, classic
art, generous layouts, nothing that looks like a standard workbook.
Course books are consumable softcovers; the K to 3 Math Boxes hold
wooden manipulatives in a slide-lid box, durable and giftable. The
aesthetic is the purchase argument for many families and defines what
"beautiful curriculum" means to a large share of the market; the atlas
README's shorthand, "TGTB's fine-art softness," applies fully here.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026: every course book PDF is free (Math Boxes are physical
only, with DIY instructions free). Printed sets: Math K course set
$49.98; Math 1 to 3 course sets $58.98 (book $29.99, box $28.99); Math 4
or 5 book sets $58.97; Math 6 $69.97; Math 7 $79.95; videos free online.
Printing a 120-lesson color course at home can cost more than the printed
set, which is part of the pricing psychology. Price pages:
https://www.goodandbeautiful.com/pages/free-homeschool-math-curriculum
and per-course store pages.

## Why families choose it

Free, beautiful, and open-and-go, in that order. Families already inside
the TGTB ecosystem default into it; budget families adopt it as the only
zero-cost complete program with mainstream polish; parents burned by
teacher-heavy programs like the video handoff at Math 4. The single
course book per level keeps logistics trivial.

## Why families leave it

Depth and structure: mastery-minded families find the spiral scattered,
and STEM-ambitious families supplement or leave around Math 5 to 7 for
Saxon, Math Mammoth, Dimensions Math, or Teaching Textbooks. The hard cap
at Math 8 forces an exit for high school regardless. Secular families
leave (or never arrive) over the publisher, not the math. Some children
find the decorative pages distracting rather than delightful.

## Competitive notes for Homa

The free-PDF model is the sharpest customer-acquisition weapon in the
category and the reason TGTB families are hard to displace: assume
price-zero anchoring in this segment. The design bar matters too:
materials Homa generates for these families will be judged against
fine-art softness, not workbook utility. Practically: grade labels map
cleanly (publisher-stated standard alignment, crosswalk section 8), the
Math 8 cap creates a guaranteed transition moment Homa can own with
placement help, and the religious nuance (LDS founder, minimal in-math
religious content) decides real purchases in both directions, so Homa
should represent it accurately and neutrally.
