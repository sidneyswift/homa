# Math Mammoth

## Snapshot

Math Mammoth's Light Blue series is Maria Miller's complete grades 1 to 8
curriculum sold primarily as inexpensive PDF downloads: $39.50 buys a full
grade of self-teaching worktexts where the explanation is written directly
to the student on the same page as the exercises. It is mastery-organized,
conceptually rigorous, heavy on mental math and multi-step word problems,
and famous as the best value in the category. The companion Blue series
repackages the same content as single-topic books for gap filling. Its
audience is budget-conscious families, secular homeschoolers, remediators,
and parents who want rigor without teaching every lesson themselves.

## Reputation and rankings

Included in Cathy Duffy's Top Picks (the publisher cites the 102 Top
Picks edition), with Duffy calling the download pricing fantastic and
the material amazingly complete for the cost; Kate Snow and others
recommend it as a rigorous independent option. The complaint that
appears everywhere, including in glowing reviews, is visual density:
many problems per page and a text-heavy feel that overwhelms some
children. Trajectory: stable, holding its niche for nearly two decades
on price and quality without marketing flash.

## Pedagogy and method

Mastery worktext design: each chapter teaches one topic to depth with
instruction boxes, visual models (number lines, bar-model-adjacent
diagrams, place value imagery), then exercises that ramp from mechanical
to genuinely hard multi-step word problems. The author states the
curriculum uses multi-step word problems extensively from grade 2 onward,
and her placement page documents that transferring students commonly test
below their nominal grade for exactly that reason. Mental math strategies
are developed deliberately before and alongside algorithms. The program is
"nearly self-teaching": the parent's role is to check work, discuss stuck
points, and decide pacing. Free instructional videos matched to the
curriculum exist for grades 1 to 7.

## Structure and scope

Grades 1 to 8, each grade in two worktexts (A and B) plus answer keys,
chapter tests, cumulative reviews, an end-of-year test, and a worksheet
generator. Grade 7 is explicitly pre-algebra and grade 8 is an
algebra-leaning eighth grade course, so the program hands students
directly into Algebra 1. Placement instruments are end-of-year exit tests
with a published scoring rubric (90 percent and above means test the next
level up; below 50 percent means test a level down), grouped by topic so
gaps are visible per section, with Blue series books prescribed per gap.
The repo's math crosswalk records the full placement mechanics and grade
mapping: see `docs/homa-benchmarks/13-curriculum-crosswalk-math.md`
section 4.

## A lesson up close

The child opens the worktext (printed or on a tablet) to a lesson that
begins with a teaching box: a short written explanation with a worked
visual model, addressed to the student. Exercises follow on the same
page, typically a dozen to two dozen items ramping from routine practice
to a word-problem finish. The child reads, works, and self-corrects
against the answer key with the parent nearby; no script, no required
video. Pages are dense full-color columns of problems, printable in
grayscale. A day runs 20 to 45 minutes and produces two to four finished
worktext pages.

## Materials and visual design

A digital-first product: PDFs formatted for home printing, with printed
softcover worktexts through Lulu, Rainbow Resource, Amazon, and others.
Design is functional and unpretentious: colored instruction boxes,
modest illustration, tight spacing. The density that makes it economical
to print is the density children complain about. No manipulatives ship
with it.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026: $39.50 per grade as a download; discounted digital
bundles cover grades 1 to 4, 5 to 8, and all of 1 to 8 (the all-grades
bundle has sold around $184 to $225 depending on channel); printed
worktexts cost roughly $15 to $25 per volume through print partners. The
license allows printing for all children in one family, which makes the
per-child cost of a large family approach zero. Price page:
https://mathmammoth.com/complete-curriculum (per-grade product pages
carry the add-to-cart pricing).

## Why families choose it

Price first, then rigor: it is the cheapest complete, respected program,
and its word-problem depth compares with far more expensive conceptual
programs. Self-teaching design frees the parent. The topical Blue series
makes it the market's default remediation tool, and the exit-test
placement system is unusually honest about gaps.

## Why families leave it

Page fatigue: children who find the layout overwhelming or who need a
live teacher's voice move to video-taught programs (Teaching Textbooks,
CTCMath) or scripted parent-led ones (Math with Confidence, RightStart).
Some strong students find it dry and move to Beast Academy for the same
rigor with more delight; community threads show Math Mammoth used as the
easy fluency partner alongside Beast. Families who want hands-on
manipulatives go to Math-U-See or RightStart.

## Competitive notes for Homa

Math Mammoth's placement tests are the cleanest exit-test model in the
market, with a published rubric and per-topic routing to specific
remediation books; that diagnostic-to-prescription loop is a pattern for
Homa's map interview and materials generation. Families using it need
what the program lacks: warmth, pacing encouragement, and page
de-densification (Homa can regenerate a crowded page as a lighter set).
Grade labels are trustworthy (the crosswalk marks it grade-matched with
strong word-problem demands), and a below-grade placement usually
reflects its word-problem bar, not a deficit; reassurance copy should
carry that.
