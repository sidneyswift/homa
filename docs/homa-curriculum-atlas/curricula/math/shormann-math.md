# Shormann Math

## Snapshot

Shormann Math is the self-appointed and largely community-ratified successor
to Saxon Math for homeschool high school: a self-paced online curriculum
from Pre-Algebra through Calculus 2, written by Dr. David Shormann of DIVE
Interactive Education, the Texas company that spent two decades selling
video lectures that taught Saxon's textbooks lesson by lesson. When the
College Board redesigned the PSAT and SAT in 2015 and Houghton Mifflin
Harcourt's fourth editions stripped John Saxon's methods, Shormann wrote
his own program: Saxon's incremental lessons and continual review, geometry
integrated into algebra rather than a separate year, new-SAT content, and
an explicit biblical foundation, delivered in an eLearning platform with
automated grading and video solutions for every problem. Its core buyer is
the Christian family that trusted Saxon and hit either the algebra wall or
the edition problem. Mindshare is significant and concentrated: it is the
default "what comes after Saxon" answer in Christian and classical circles.

## Reputation and rankings

Cathy Duffy reviews the full series favorably, crediting the courses with
"a keen understanding of independent study" and noting they are challenging
but structured so students succeed without parental help. Smarter Learning
Guide rates it a rigorous, good-value program, praising the video quality,
TruePractice retry mechanics, and sibling pricing, with mild dings for the
plain platform aesthetics and less granular progress reporting than some
competitors. Community sentiment in homeschool forums and blogs tracks the
same lines: parents switching from Saxon report relief at 20-problem sets
(versus Saxon's 30) and built-in grading, and math-anxious families report
the help links and video solutions keep students unstuck. The critical
threads are consistent too: the Christian content is thick and inseparable,
which reads as a feature to its market and a dealbreaker outside it;
lessons run an hour or more daily; and spiral-averse kids dislike it for
the same reasons they dislike Saxon. Trajectory: rising within its niche,
riding both Saxon's slow institutional decay and the general migration of
high school math to self-grading online formats.

## Pedagogy and method

The lineage is John Saxon, stated on every page: incremental bite-sized
lessons, continual mixed review, cumulative assessment, and fluency (speed
plus accuracy) as the goal. Shormann's claim is that he kept the method and
modernized the content: integrated geometry (which Saxon pioneered in the
US), computer math, technology applications, non-standard solutions, and
over 100 PSAT/SAT/ACT questions per algebra course. The orientation is
procedural and rigorous, with the "why" carried by math history rather
than conceptual modeling: lessons trace ideas through Euclid, Euler, and
Newton and present modern math as a Christian intellectual heritage.
The worldview is not a veneer; lessons connect math to scripture and to
"understanding God and His creation," and reviews agree it is one of the
most deeply faith-integrated math programs sold. The distinctive assessment
philosophy is TruePractice: homework answers get instant right/wrong
feedback, a wrong answer costs a small penalty, and the student re-learns
via links to a similar example and the relevant lecture segment, then
re-answers. Shormann explicitly coaches a "timed method" pace: 4 to 5 days
a week, 60 to 90 minutes, stop when time is up regardless of lesson
completion, on the argument that fluency, not schedule, sets speed.

## Structure and scope

Six courses: Pre-Algebra (12-month subscription), then Algebra 1 with
Integrated Geometry, Algebra 2 with Integrated Geometry, Precalculus with
Trigonometry, Calculus 1, and Calculus 2 (24-month subscriptions). Core
courses have 100 daily lessons, roughly 26 to 28 weekly quizzes, and 4
quarterly exams, set on a 30-week schedule that the publisher expects
average students to stretch to 36 or more weeks. The credit accounting is
the signature structural fact: because geometry is integrated, completing
Shormann Algebra 1 and 2 earns three full credits (Algebra 1, Algebra 2,
Geometry), and the publisher documents transition credit rules for
students arriving from Saxon editions (for example, Saxon Algebra 2
completers earn the remaining half geometry credit inside Shormann
Precalculus). Algebra 2 includes a CLEP Professor prep course for CLEP
College Algebra, and Precalculus and Calculus include CLEP/AP prep, with
marketing claiming up to 14 college credits via CLEP and AP exams. The
first 25 lessons of each course deliberately review fundamentals to absorb
students from any prior program. There is no in-house placement test;
the company points families to Saxon placement tests plus published
transition checklists and cutoff guidance (roughly 75 percent in the
prior course). The older DIVE product line (video lectures keyed to Saxon
textbook editions) remains on sale alongside, and is profiled in this
atlas only as the add-on economy noted in the Saxon profile.

## A lesson up close

A daily lesson has a fixed rhythm. The student first reads the lesson's
rules and definitions in the eText and runs a short Quizlet-style flashcard
review of facts and vocabulary, a few minutes at most. Then the video
lecture: Dr. Shormann teaching over a dark digital chalkboard with a pen,
average around 20 minutes (first-quarter review lessons run longer, some
later ones under 10). The student is expected to take notes in a binder,
and the lecture directs them to pause after each worked example and try it
themselves before watching the solution. Then homework: 20 problems, worked
on paper, answers entered into the platform. Each question carries a help
link to a similar solved example and a link back to the exact lecture
segment; wrong answers cost a small penalty and can be retried. After
submission the student can open a PDF solution sheet or a video solution
for every single problem. Weekly, a timed open-book quiz (about 20 minutes)
is recorded to the gradebook; quarterly, a closed-book exam arrives with
two unrecorded practice exams beforehand. The parent's seat is genuinely
minimal: the platform grades and records everything, parents can reset
assignments or adjust grades, and the publisher provides guidance for
grading lecture notes as a participation component. A completed day
produces a page of worked problems on paper, a notes page, and a recorded
score. Expect 60 to 90 minutes, noticeably more than a middle schooler's
prior math block, and the reason reviews call it a step up in rigor.

## Materials and visual design

There is nothing physical: no textbook, no kit, no shipped anything. The
eLearning platform is clean, white-space-heavy, and text-forward, with a
sidebar table of contents and a central lesson pane; reviewers describe it
as easy to navigate and visually plain. Lectures are slide-and-digital-pen
recordings in the Khan Academy visual genre rather than filmed teaching.
The printable eTextbook, eSolutions manual, and eTeacher's guide exist as
PDFs but printing is optional and most families skip it. The aesthetic
signal is deliberate: this looks like a college online course, not a game,
and the marketing leans into that ("similar to college math classes").
For a mom whose reference points are Saxon's dense gray textbook pages,
the platform reads as the same seriousness with the grading done for her.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026, official store sale prices: Pre-Algebra $119.99 (list
$149.99) for a 12-month single-student subscription; Algebra 1, Algebra 2,
Precalculus, Calculus 1, and Calculus 2 each $154.99 (list $199.99) for a
24-month subscription. After one full-price purchase, sibling subscriptions
for the same course cost $45.99 (list $54.99) each, with no stated time
limit; Pre-Algebra has no sibling discount, which is why it is priced
lower. Everything is included (grading, video solutions, Q&A support with
Dr. Shormann, CLEP prep in the relevant courses); there are no consumables
and nothing to resell. Because access windows are generous (24 months) and
courses earn multiple credits, the per-credit economics undercut most
outsourced alternatives: three high school credits from Algebra 1 plus
Algebra 2 cost about $310 for a first student and about $92 for a sibling.
Price pages: https://diveintomath.com/shormann/ and the individual course
pages linked below. Third-party reviews quote a spread of older prices
($104 to $169); the figures above are the official store's.

## Why families choose it

The Saxon exit ramp is the big one: families who trust Saxon's method but
want the redesigned-SAT content, fewer problems per lesson, automated
grading, or an escape from the HMH fourth-edition confusion. Second, the
algebra wall: it is a full teacher-in-a-box for upper math at a fraction
of live-class outsourcing prices. Third, the worldview: for families who
want faith genuinely inside the curriculum rather than a verse on the
cover, this is the deepest option in math. Fourth, the college-credit
story: integrated geometry compressing the sequence, CLEP prep included,
and the pitch that a student can finish calculus and bank college credits
before graduation. Fifth, large-family economics via sibling pricing.

## Why families leave it

Secular and neutral families rarely arrive at all, and some Christian
families find the scripture density more than they wanted in math. The
rigor and length grind down students who were surviving rather than
thriving in Saxon; the common soft landing is Teaching Textbooks (gentler,
also self-grading), profiled at `teaching-textbooks.md`. Students who need
a live human and deadlines move to Mr. D Math
(`../online-digital/mr-d-math.md`) or co-op classes. Spiral-averse and
conceptual-leaning families were never the market and exit to mastery
programs. Screen fatigue is a steady low-grade complaint, as with all
courseware. And a few families still on legacy Saxon books simply buy the
older DIVE lectures instead of switching curricula.

## Competitive notes for Homa

Shormann is the cleanest case study of a succession brand: it inherited
Saxon's trust by teaching Saxon for 20 years, then productized the exit.
When a family says "we use Shormann," Homa should hear "Saxon values,
digital delivery, Christian worldview, transcript-aware." Three practical
implications. First, credit vocabulary: integrated geometry means this
family's transcript shows Geometry credit earned inside Algebra 1 and 2,
and the transition rules from Saxon editions are genuinely confusing;
Homa's transcript and credit-planning surfaces (see
`../../comparisons/high-school.md`) should encode these rules rather than
assume the layer-cake sequence. Second, the grading contract: parents
chose this product so they never grade math; Homa should ingest its
gradebook reality (scores, quizzes, exams) as evidence rather than
generate parallel math assessment. Third, the pace philosophy: the timed
method (fixed daily minutes, variable completion) is a scheduling pattern
Homa's planner should support natively, because Shormann families measure
progress in fluency and minutes, not lessons per week. Vocabulary to know:
DIVE (the Saxon lecture product line), TruePractice, integrated geometry,
timed method, CLEP Professor, quarterly exams, sibling subscription.
