# Dimensions Math

## Snapshot

Dimensions Math is Singapore Math Inc.'s flagship PK-8 curriculum: the
modern, US-adapted expression of the Singapore math method (concrete to
pictorial to abstract, number bonds, bar models, mental math). The
elementary series (PK-5) was written in-house by the publisher's team and
launched around 2018; the middle school series (6-8) came from a
collaboration with Star Publishing of Singapore. It is widely treated as
the gold standard for conceptual elementary math in the homeschool world,
and it is also sold into schools, which shapes its two-guide structure
(Teacher's Guides for classrooms, Home Instructor's Guides for
homeschools). Among the atlas's math profiles it is the reference point
that other conceptual programs get compared to, which is why this profile
is the flagship: it goes one level deeper than the others, down to lesson
and page anatomy.

Lineage matters here because "Singapore math" is a brand cloud, not one
product. Primary Mathematics (the original US import, from 1998, in US,
Standards, and Common Core editions) built the reputation. Dimensions Math
is the publisher's own fuller-featured successor. Primary Mathematics 2022
Edition is a separate, entirely new K-5 program more aligned to US
standards. The Common Core editions of the old Primary Mathematics were
sunset from the publisher's store on 2025-10-31, but the US and Standards
editions remain on sale. A family saying "we do Singapore" could mean any
of these, and the differences are real (see the sibling programs section
below).

## Reputation and rankings

- Cathy Duffy reviews both series favorably. On PK-5 she highlights that
  it keeps the advanced scope of Primary Mathematics with fuller lesson
  presentations: "Many parents will appreciate having more direction for
  teaching lessons and more optional activities"
  ([Cathy Duffy PK-5 review](https://cathyduffyreviews.com/homeschool-reviews-core-curricula/math/math-prek-k/dimensions-math-grades-pk-5)).
  On 6-8 she notes the content runs ahead of US grade labels and that
  students completing 7 and 8 "should be given credit for algebra 1"
  ([Cathy Duffy 6-8 review](https://cathyduffyreviews.com/homeschool-reviews-core-curricula/math/math-grades-7-8/dimensions-math-levels-6-8)).
- Kate Snow (Math with Confidence author, an influential homeschool math
  voice) reviewed it as the more accessible packaging of Singapore math
  for American parents, while telling families already happy on Primary
  Mathematics not to feel pressure to switch
  ([Kate Snow review](https://kateshomeschoolmath.com/dimensions-math-review/)).
- Community sentiment (Well-Trained Mind forums, Reddit homeschool threads,
  aggregated by review sites): "gold standard" for conceptual math,
  significantly more rigorous than typical US programs, with recurring
  complaints that it demands real daily parent involvement and that
  juggling textbook, workbook, and guide per lesson is cumbersome
  ([review aggregation](https://homeschooltools.net/offering/singapore-math-dimensions-math)).
- Trajectory: stable to rising. It is not an awards-circuit brand; its
  mindshare comes from reviewer consensus, school adoption, and the
  academic-leaning eclectic segment. The main brand risk is self-inflicted
  confusion with Primary Mathematics 2022.

## Pedagogy and method

The Singapore method, as the publisher itself frames it: students are
pushed to think through concepts and apply them in new ways rather than
replicating a worked example with different numbers, and connectivity
across topics is woven in deliberately
([homeschool FAQ](https://www.singaporemath.com/pages/faq)).

Core mechanics:

- CPA progression (Concrete, Pictorial, Abstract): physical objects first,
  then pictures of them, then symbols. Not every lesson walks all three
  stages; the principle is woven through the sequence.
- Number bonds: part-part-whole diagrams that make composition and
  decomposition of numbers the foundational mental model.
- Bar modeling: rectangular-bar diagrams for word problems, the signature
  Singapore technique that scales from grade 2 arithmetic to complex
  multi-step ratio problems.
- Mental math: explicit strategies (make ten, compensation, left-to-right
  arithmetic) practiced continuously.
- Mastery sequencing: topics are taught to depth in blocks rather than
  spiraled; review exists but the program trusts depth over repetition.

Standards posture: PK-5 is deliberately not organized around the Common
Core but covers its standards within its own progression (a few gaps
covered in the Teacher's Guides); 6-8 is described as meeting and
exceeding Common Core ([FAQ](https://www.singaporemath.com/pages/faq)).

Grade-label honesty: the publisher says its grades align with US grades,
and the transfer reality is that students moving in from other programs
commonly place a level below their US grade. The repo's math crosswalk
covers this precisely, including placement-test philosophy: Dimensions
Math K-5 tests are mastery tests of the level named on the test, not
entry-readiness tests. See
`docs/homa-benchmarks/13-curriculum-crosswalk-math.md` §2.

## Structure and scope

Each PK-5 grade is split into A and B semester halves, and every component
follows the A/B split with color-coded covers per level.

PK-5 components per grade:

- Textbook A and B (concept introduction; the teaching happens here)
- Workbook A and B (independent practice; consumable; perforated in PK-2)
- One of: Home Instructor's Guide A and B (one-on-one home teaching:
  background notes, lesson suggestions, activities, answer keys) or
  Teacher's Guide A and B (classroom-oriented, full color, reduced-size
  copies of textbook and workbook pages, differentiation ideas, math
  literature suggestions)
- Tests A and B (optional; two test versions per unit, one focused on key
  concepts, one on analytical application; single Tests book for K, none
  for PK)
- Home Instructor's Printouts A and B (game boards, cards, and activity
  masters the guide's activities call for)

Textbook, workbook, and one guide are required components per the
publisher; the program is explicitly incomplete without all three
([school FAQ](https://www.singaporemath.com/pages/school-faq)).

Middle school:

- Grade 6: Textbooks, Workbooks, Teacher's Guides, Workbook Solutions
  (A and B each)
- Grades 7-8: Textbooks, Workbooks, Teaching Notes and Solutions, Workbook
  Solutions. No tests exist for 6-8; the publisher advises assessing from
  the workbook Practice sections
  ([6-8 resources](https://www.singaporemath.com/pages/dimensions-math-6-8-resources)).

Scope shape: PK-5 covers the arithmetic ladder with the standard Singapore
ahead-lean (times tables close by grade 3, fractions and decimals
operations by grade 5, heavy multi-step bar-model word problems from grade
3 on). Dimensions Math 6 is a grade 6 text with some pre-algebra; 7 and 8
are designed as a set that together covers pre-algebra and Algebra 1 with
some geometry, so a student finishing 8 exits a year ahead and moves to
geometry or Algebra 2 elsewhere. The program ends at grade 8 by design.
Level-by-level strand detail and grade anchoring is already tabulated in
`docs/homa-benchmarks/13-curriculum-crosswalk-math.md` §2; the publisher's
scope and sequence PDFs are linked in the samples section below.

Supporting infrastructure: free placement tests (PK-5 entry points), free
Blackline Masters, pacing guides for 6-8, suggested manipulative lists,
and an optional Dimensions Math Video Lessons subscription covering grades
1-6 (recorded lessons by a Singapore math teacher; explicitly a supplement
that does not replace the guides, and some problems are deliberately left
unworked for the student to finish).

## A lesson up close

The publisher describes the grade 1-5 lesson arc directly: a lesson opens
with a problem for discussion, moves to an explanation of the concept with
vocabulary and several solution approaches, continues through learning
tasks that extend the concept, and ends with problems that assess
understanding ([FAQ](https://www.singaporemath.com/pages/faq)). In the
books this arc is visible as named lesson phases (a Think prompt, a Learn
segment, a Do segment), and home reviewers describe the rhythm as the same
five beats every day: think, learn, do, activities, practice
([WOLFe Pack review](https://thewolfepackden.com/homeschool-curriculum-review-singapore-dimensions-math/)).

From the parent's seat, a day looks like:

1. Open the Home Instructor's Guide to the day's lesson. The guide gives
   the objective, background notes on the math itself (written to teach
   the parent the why, not just the steps), materials needed, and a
   suggested activity, often a game using the Printouts book or common
   objects.
2. Pose the textbook's opening Think problem, usually a picture-based
   scenario with the series' recurring cast of illustrated kid characters.
   The parent lets the child wrestle with it before teaching.
3. Walk the Learn segment in the textbook: the same problem resolved
   pictorially (place-value discs, number bonds, ten frames, bar models),
   then abstractly. Multiple named characters often show different valid
   strategies side by side, which is how the program teaches strategy
   flexibility.
4. Work the Do tasks together orally or on a whiteboard.
5. Send the child to the corresponding workbook exercise for independent
   practice.

Workbook page anatomy (publisher-documented): every exercise is
differentiated into three ordered bands, Basics (the core concept,
restated so a parent can see how it was taught), then Practice, then
optional Challenge problems, and the publisher explicitly says not every
student needs to complete every problem and that any correct method should
be accepted
([Workbook 2A product page](https://www.singaporemath.com/products/dimensions-math-workbook-2a)).
That three-band ramp on every single exercise is one of the most
copy-worthy patterns in this entire atlas: difficulty differentiation is
built into the page, not left to the parent.

Session length: roughly 15 to 30 minutes of taught lesson plus workbook
time at elementary grades, per community reports (unverified, varies by
child; the publisher does not prescribe times).

At 6-8 the lesson shape matures: each section opens with a class activity
for discovery, worked examples follow with parallel try-it questions, and
exercises are tiered as Basic Practice, Further Practice, Challenging
Practice, and Maths@Work (real-world application), with Brainworks
questions for open-ended higher-order thinking. Chapters in 7-8 end with a
review exercise and an Extend Your Learning Curve reflection activity that
asks for sentence or paragraph responses about the learning experience
([FAQ](https://www.singaporemath.com/pages/faq)). A middle schooler can
run it semi-independently; the publisher still positions the Teaching
Notes as necessary.

## Materials and visual design

- Elementary textbooks are full-color softcovers with a consistent cast of
  illustrated children and animals guiding lessons. The aesthetic is
  clean, flat-color, whitespace-generous instructional design: friendly
  but never busy, visual models (discs, bars, bonds) drawn large and
  uncluttered. It reads as "serious math that respects young kids,"
  clearly distinct from both the photograph-heavy US textbook look and
  the cartoon-saturated worksheet look.
- Workbooks are simpler and consumable, perforated through grade 2, with
  generous room to work. Answer keys deliberately live in the guides, not
  the student books.
- The A/B color coding per grade level is a repeatedly praised usability
  touch: every component of a semester shares cover color, so a shelf of
  Singapore math stays organized
  ([WOLFe Pack review](https://thewolfepackden.com/homeschool-curriculum-review-singapore-dimensions-math/)).
- Teacher's Guides are full color with reduced-size student pages inline;
  Home Instructor's Guides are the homeschool-shaped equivalent with
  extensive background notes.
- Manipulatives are suggested, not bundled: linking cubes, place-value
  discs, number cards; suggested lists live on the resources pages, and
  the Printouts books plus free Blackline Masters cover the paper
  manipulatives.
- Middle school books drop the character cast for a cleaner, more
  textbook-like design while keeping worked visual models.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026, publisher direct
([Grade 2 set page](https://www.singaporemath.com/products/dimensions-math-grade-2-set-with-home-instructors-guides-1),
[component pages](https://www.singaporemath.com/products/dimensions-math-workbook-2a)):

- PK-5 components run $14.50 per textbook or workbook half, $22.50 per
  Home Instructor's Guide half, $32.50 per Teacher's Guide half, $14.50
  per Tests half, $18.50 per Printouts half.
- Core year (textbooks, workbooks, HIGs, A and B): about $103.
- Full grade set with HIGs, tests, and printouts: $169 (grade 2 example;
  sets range roughly $120 to $170 across grades).
- Grades 6-8: textbooks $28.50 per half, workbooks $14.50 per half,
  Teacher's Guides $34 per half, Workbook Solutions $12; a full year runs
  roughly $150 to $170.
- Dimensions Math Video Lessons: $85 per grade for a 12-month
  subscription, grades 1-6 only; third-party videos (Singapore Math Live)
  cover 7-8.
- Consumables economics: workbooks and tests are consumable (about $29 to
  $58 per additional child per year); textbooks and guides are reusable,
  so a second child costs a fraction of the first. Resale on guides and
  textbooks is active in the used market.
- No sales on core material as policy; the publisher sells blemished
  copies at a discount instead ([FAQ](https://www.singaporemath.com/pages/faq)).

## Why families choose it

- The conceptual reputation: it is the name brand for "my kid will
  actually understand math," backed by Singapore's decades at the top of
  TIMSS and PISA rankings, which the publisher leans on.
- Bar models and number bonds visibly work, and word-problem power
  compounds through the grades.
- The Home Instructor's Guides teach the parent the math. For moms
  insecure about teaching math conceptually, the guide is the product.
- The video subscription can carry the actual teaching for $85 a year.
- Worldview-neutral content: acceptable in every segment, purchasable
  with charter and ESA funds, no vetting anxiety for secular buyers.
- Moderate price: the $103 core year undercuts most boxed math and all
  courseware.

## Why families leave it

- Parent load: it wants a taught lesson most days. Parents juggling
  multiple kids or seeking independence migrate to Teaching Textbooks,
  CTCMath, or CLE.
- The multi-book shuffle: textbook plus workbook plus guide plus printouts
  per lesson is real overhead, a recurring community complaint.
- Runs hot: the ahead-lean scope overwhelms struggling learners; common
  destinations are Math-U-See (pace and manipulatives) or Math with
  Confidence (gentler scripting).
- Mastery structure under-serves kids who need continuous review; families
  add Sprints or Extra Practice books, or move to spiral programs (Saxon,
  Horizons, CLE).
- Unfamiliar methods: parents who were taught procedurally sometimes fight
  the mental-math strategies instead of learning them; without the guide,
  the program degrades badly.
- It ends at grade 8, forcing a high school transition decision anyway.
- Brand confusion: choosing among Dimensions Math, Primary Mathematics US,
  Standards, and 2022 editions is a genuinely confusing purchase, and some
  families bounce off at the door.

## Sibling programs from the same publisher

Facts a competent assistant must keep straight
([FAQ](https://www.singaporemath.com/pages/faq)):

- Primary Mathematics US and Standards editions: the originals, still
  sold, sparser teaching support, beloved by veterans.
- Primary Mathematics Common Core edition: sunset 2025-10-31.
- Primary Mathematics 2022: an entirely new K-5 program, not an update;
  components are Student Book, Additional Practice, Mastery and Beyond,
  plus HIG or TG; more aligned to US standards, more differentiation, and
  its placement tests are entry-readiness tests (opposite philosophy from
  Dimensions Math K-5 mastery tests).
- Primary Mathematics Singapore International Edition: semester bundles of
  the current actual Singapore-school curriculum, digital teacher guide
  included, bundle-only purchase.
- eBooks exist only for classroom bulk purchase; homeschoolers buy print.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary Homa must speak natively when a family uses this program:
  levels as "2A/2B" halves, HIG (Home Instructor's Guide), Think/Learn/Do,
  number bonds, bar models, place-value discs, mastery placement tests,
  "DM 7 and 8 count as pre-algebra plus Algebra 1."
- The single most instructive materials pattern in this program is the
  three-band workbook ramp (Basics, Practice, Challenge on every
  exercise) plus the multiple-strategies-side-by-side textbook pattern.
  Homa's materials studio should treat per-page difficulty ramps and
  strategy plurality as house patterns, expressed in Homa's own design
  language, never cloned from these pages.
- The gaps Homa can fill for a Dimensions Math family, which are exactly
  its churn drivers: generate mixed spaced review (the mastery structure's
  known weakness), generate extra practice in the same visual grammar
  (bar models, number bonds) at a named level's topic, explain the why of
  a method to the parent on demand (the HIG's job, conversationally), and
  ease the placement decision when a family transfers in or out.
- Progress mapping: "finished Dimensions Math 2A" is a precise signal.
  The crosswalk in `docs/homa-benchmarks/13-curriculum-crosswalk-math.md`
  anchors each level to grade months and topics; wiring that into
  `packages/taxonomy/` skill states is the wave 3 goal.
- Positioning: Homa should never present as a replacement curriculum to
  this family. The Dimensions Math buyer chose a high-conviction product;
  Homa wins as the assistant that makes her chosen program sustainable
  (planning, review generation, parent explanations, record-keeping).
- Signal value: a family on Dimensions Math is usually academically
  ambitious, comfortable teaching, conceptual-friendly, and often
  secular-or-neutral in curriculum taste. Onboarding can infer a lot from
  this one answer.
