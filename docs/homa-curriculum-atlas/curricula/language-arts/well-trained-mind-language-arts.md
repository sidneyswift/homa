# Well-Trained Mind Press language arts

## Snapshot

Well-Trained Mind Press (Susan Wise Bauer and Jessie Wise, the authors of The
Well-Trained Mind) publishes the classical homeschool world's standard
elementary grammar and writing pair: First Language Lessons (FLL), a fully
scripted four-level oral grammar course, and Writing With Ease (WWE), a
four-level composition-foundations course built on narration, copywork, and
dictation, continued by Writing With Skill (WWS) for the middle grades. They
are deliberately narrow: no phonics, no spelling, no literature program, just
grammar and writing taught the trivium way. Families assemble them into
stacks (classically: FLL plus WWE plus a phonics program plus a spelling
program). Mindshare is anchored by The Well-Trained Mind book itself, which
has funneled new classical homeschoolers to these titles for two decades.

## Reputation and rankings

- Cathy Duffy reviews both series favorably; WWE and FLL are fixtures on
  classical curriculum lists and in The Well-Trained Mind's own
  recommendations, which function as the community's buying guide.
- Community sentiment: parents praise the scripting (open the book, read the
  dialogue) and the gentle, low-output early years; the recurring complaints
  are that FLL's repetition gets tedious (especially Level 2), that WWE's
  passages-and-questions rhythm can bore kids by Level 3, and that the pair
  overlaps (both assign copywork and narration; the publisher itself says to
  pick one source for those exercises).
- WWS is respected but known as a difficulty jump; Cathy Duffy notes it
  targets grades 5 to 8 across three published levels (a planned fourth never
  shipped).
- Trajectory: stable classic. New revised editions of WWE keep it current.

## Pedagogy and method

Trivium staging applied to language arts. Grammar stage: FLL teaches grammar
orally through scripted dialogues, definition memorization (chanted until
automatic), poem memorization, narration, copywork, and picture study;
Levels 1 and 2 are entirely oral with no student book. Writing is built as
skills-before-composition: WWE's four levels drill narration (summarizing
heard text), copywork, then dictation, on the theory that children should
master holding and transcribing language before inventing it. Original
composition is deliberately deferred; WWS then teaches outlining, summaries
across the curriculum, and documented essays, written directly to the
student. Both elementary series are scripted word for word, teacher-led, and
short (10 to 20 minutes daily). Review is constant and cumulative, a spiral
in practice. Levels are explicitly not grade labels; WWE ships placement
diagnostics and FLL's publisher targeting spans two grades per level.

## Structure and scope

FLL: four levels, one year each; publisher targeting Level 1 to grades 1 or
2 through Level 4 at grades 4 or 5. Levels 1 and 2: instructor book only.
Levels 3 and 4: instructor guide plus consumable student workbook, and
sentence diagramming begins at Level 3 (which is a valid fresh entry point).
WWE: four levels; revised editions pack instructor section and student pages
into one complete workbook per level (Levels 1 to 3 revised as of 2025-2026),
with the Strong Fundamentals core text as the optional theory spine. WWS:
three levels for roughly grades 5 to 9, instructor text plus student
workbook, all source material included. Per-level grammar scope, the
level-to-grade table, and bank-skill mapping are in
[14-curriculum-crosswalk-ela.md](../../../homa-benchmarks/14-curriculum-crosswalk-ela.md)
sections 8 and 10; the FLL-WWE overlap warning there comes from the
publisher's own support page.

## A lesson up close

FLL Level 1, from the parent's seat: open the instructor book to the next
numbered lesson (two to four pages, large type). The script is literal:
bolded text for the parent to read aloud, expected child answers in gray.
A lesson might chant the noun definition, name common nouns around the room,
read a short poem toward memorization, and end with one or two sentences of
copywork; ten minutes, no prep, no student book to manage. WWE Level 2:
four short days per week; days alternate copywork of a sentence or two from
a real children's book excerpt (the workbook reprints the passage with
comprehension questions and a scripted narration dialogue) and
dictation of previously copied material; the student pages give handwriting
lines under the printed passage. Output per day is deliberately tiny: one
good copied sentence beats a page of weak ones. A completed week produces a
few copywork lines, one narration the parent scribed, and one dictation.
WWS shifts the seat: the student reads their own lesson, outlines a provided
source, and drafts; the instructor text tells the parent exactly what to
check.

## Materials and visual design

Plain classical utility: black-and-white pages, restrained serif typography,
occasional line drawings (Jeff West's illustrations in newer WWE editions),
softcover 8.5 by 11 workbooks with perforated student pages. FLL's
instructor books look like scripts because they are. The aesthetic signals
seriousness and tradition; nothing here is designed for Instagram, and the
audience takes that as a feature. Consumables are the student workbooks
(FLL 3-4, WWE, WWS); instructor books are reusable.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026, list prices from the publisher and major retailers: FLL
Level 1 about $18.95 (often discounted near $12 at Rainbow Resource); FLL
Levels 3 and 4 are two-book purchases (instructor guide plus workbook,
roughly $45 to 60 together at list). WWE revised complete levels run about
$24 to 39 depending on retailer; the older format splits a core text (about
$21 to 33) from per-level workbooks. Extra children need only student pages
or workbooks. A typical FLL-plus-WWE year lands between $40 and 70, among
the cheapest name-brand LA stacks in the market. WWS levels are pricier
(instructor text plus workbook, roughly $60 to 80 together at list; verify
per page). Price pages:
[FLL series](https://welltrainedmind.com/c/series/first-language-lessons/),
[WWE series](https://welltrainedmind.com/c/series/writing-with-ease/).

## Why families choose it

- The Well-Trained Mind book told them to; the funnel is that direct.
- Total scripting: a nervous parent can teach grammar on day one with zero
  knowledge.
- Gentle early output matches young kids: oral work and tiny copywork, no
  tears over pages of writing.
- Cheap, durable, resale-friendly, and reusable across siblings except thin
  workbooks.
- It composes cleanly with any phonics and spelling choices; classical
  families like picking each piece.

## Why families leave it

- Repetition fatigue: the chanting and review that make FLL work read as
  drudgery to some kids and parents (common exit: Fix It! Grammar for
  story-based editing, or dropping formal grammar until later).
- WWE's rhythm (passage, questions, copywork, again) bores story-hungry kids
  by Level 3; Brave Writer is the philosophical escape hatch.
- The WWE-to-WWS jump is steep; some families detour into IEW for the middle
  grades instead.
- Parents wanting one integrated purchase go to TGTB or Logic of English
  rather than assembling grammar, writing, spelling, and phonics separately.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: FLL, WWE, WWS, narration, copywork, dictation,
  diagramming, "levels are not grades" (publisher-stated), the FLL-WWE
  overlap rule (choose one source for copywork and narration). A family
  saying "FLL 3 and WWE 2" is running a two-book classical LA stack and
  almost certainly has separate phonics and spelling somewhere.
- Materials lesson: the fully scripted dialogue page (parent line, expected
  child answer) is the gold standard for making an insecure parent feel
  competent; Homa-generated lesson helps should meet that bar of
  say-this-then-this concreteness.
- Gap Homa can fill: the deferred-composition philosophy leaves parents
  unsure whether copying sentences is really enough. Homa can translate
  narration and dictation work into visible skill progress, and generate
  fresh narration passages matched to the child's interests, which WWE's
  fixed excerpts cannot.
- Switching note: families leaving WWE for IEW or Brave Writer are not
  leaving classical education, just its writing pace; recommendations should
  respect the trivium frame.
