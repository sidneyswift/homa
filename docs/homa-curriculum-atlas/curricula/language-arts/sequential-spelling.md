# Sequential Spelling

## Snapshot

Sequential Spelling, from Don McCabe's AVKO Educational Research
Foundation, is the anti-list spelling program: no weekly word lists to
memorize, no pretests, no rules taught. Instead the parent dictates words
built from word families (a base like "in" growing into begin, beginning,
and kin across days), the student attempts each word, and self-corrects
immediately, letter by letter, which the method treats as the actual
teaching moment. Seven levels, about 180 daily lessons each, 10 to 20
minutes a day. Originally designed for dyslexic learners (AVKO stands for
Audio, Visual, Kinesthetic, Oral), it is now a general-market budget
option. Everyone starts at Level 1 regardless of age once reading is
roughly second-grade solid. Mindshare is niche but durable: it is the
standing alternative recommendation when All About Spelling's price or
teacher load is the objection.

## Reputation and rankings

- No major award record; its reputation lives in dyslexia-adjacent forums
  and budget-spelling threads, where it has been a fixture for decades.
- Sentiment: fans call it the first program where spelling finally stuck,
  praising the immediate self-correction and pattern discovery; critics
  note it requires daily parent dictation (not independent), teaches no
  explicit rules (a mismatch for kids who need Orton-Gillingham
  explicitness), and feels monotonous. Reviewers split on the dyslexia
  claim: designed for it, but modern OG-first guidance often routes
  severe cases to rules-explicit programs instead.
- Trajectory: stable niche legacy; the AVKO catalog around it (Patterns of
  English Spelling) is respected reference material.
