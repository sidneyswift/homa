# Logic of English

## Snapshot

Logic of English (LOE), created by Denise Eide out of her book Uncovering the
Logic of English, is the integrated alternative in the Orton-Gillingham
family: instead of separate reading and spelling purchases, one program
teaches phonics, reading, spelling, handwriting, vocabulary, and early grammar
through 75 basic phonograms and 31 spelling rules that the publisher says
explain 98 percent of English words. Foundations (levels A to D) is the ages
4 to 7 complete language arts track; Essentials (30 units, each teachable at
three difficulty levels) serves ages 8 to adult, including remediation. Its
pitch is that English is not crazy, just untaught. Mindshare sits a tier below
All About Reading but with a fiercely loyal base, especially among parents who
themselves want to finally understand spelling.

## Reputation and rankings

- Cathy Duffy reviews both tracks favorably; review aggregators call LOE a
  "gold standard" for rules-based literacy (Homeschool Tools summary of
  reviewer consensus).
- Community sentiment: praised as thorough, logical, and effective for
  dyslexia and for gifted why-asking kids; the two standing complaints are
  cost and component overwhelm (teacher manual, workbooks, three card decks,
  game cards, tiles, whiteboards), plus longer lessons than AAR (30 to 45
  minutes, often split).
- Trajectory: stable, with science-of-reading tailwinds; the publisher added
  online lesson supplements and keeps both tracks current.

## Pedagogy and method

Orton-Gillingham lineage, delivered as linguistics-first direct instruction:
every phonogram sound is taught explicitly (all sounds of each phonogram at
once, a difference from AAR), spelling rules are stated and applied rather
than absorbed, and reading and spelling are taught as one reversible system.
Multisensory work runs through phonogram cards, letter tiles, whiteboard
work, and a heavy rotation of games. Mastery-based with skill-based entry:
Foundations has exactly two entry points (A or B, decided by a free
assessment), and Essentials has its own placement test with three internal
difficulty levels (A, B, C) so one purchase can be retaught at rising depth.
Handwriting (manuscript or cursive, family's choice) is built in from the
first lessons. Sessions are scripted but denser than AAR's; the parent
teaches actively throughout.

## Structure and scope

Foundations: four levels, A to D, 40 lessons plus 8 assessments each,
commonly two levels per year (A and B in K, C and D in grade 1). A starts at
letter sounds; by end of C all 75 basic phonograms and most spelling rules are
taught; D adds advanced phonograms and the rest of the rules, with readers
and composition alongside. Essentials: 30 units in five parts each
(phonograms and rules, spelling analysis, grammar, vocabulary, review and
dictation), volumes sold in bundles, with the optional Essentials Reader for
older struggling readers. Components per level: teacher's manual, consumable
student workbook, plus the reusable core kit (phonogram cards, spelling rule
cards, game cards, tiles). Full level-by-level mapping, entry-point rules,
and the crosswalk to bank skills live in
[14-curriculum-crosswalk-ela.md](../../../homa-benchmarks/14-curriculum-crosswalk-ela.md)
section 2; this profile does not repeat it.

## A lesson up close

A Foundations lesson runs three to five segments across an open two-page
teacher spread: quick phonogram review with cards, a new phonogram or rule
taught with scripted dialogue and a multisensory action (writing it large,
saying all its sounds), a game (bingo, matching, movement games; games are
core mechanics here, not rewards), then workbook pages and, on a rhythm,
handwriting practice and a controlled reader passage. The student workbook
page is colorful and varied: circle-the-phonogram items, cut-and-glue
sorts, handwriting lines, and short dictation boxes; item counts per page are
modest and the visual style is bright primary-color graphic design rather
than illustration. Essentials days are more sober: phonogram dictation,
spelling analysis of the week's list (marking each word's phonograms and
rules), then grammar and vocabulary exercises in the workbook. A completed
day produces marked-up spelling words, a workbook page or two, and a played
game. The publisher posts full sample lessons for every level (linked below).

## Materials and visual design

Bright, modern, and systematic: teal-and-lime brand palette, clean sans-serif
headings, tabbed hardcover or coil teacher manuals, and workbooks printed in
full color with graphic-design energy (arrows, sorting boxes, big phonogram
tiles) rather than storybook art. The phonogram and game card decks are the
program's physical heart and shelf bulk. Rhythm of Handwriting components use
large motor-pattern arrows and gray guide lines. The aesthetic signals
serious, science-adjacent literacy instruction with a friendly face: closer
to a well-designed classroom product than to AAR's storybook warmth.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026: Foundations A bundle from about $88; a first-time buyer
pairing any Foundations level with the reusable Core Materials set lands
near $227 (homeschoolfox pricing summary; the publisher sells many bundle
permutations). Essentials 1-7 with Core Materials is $222.17 on the publisher
store; Essentials Core Materials alone $138.51. Consumable student workbooks
are the per-child rebuy (roughly $15 to 25 each); cards, tiles, manuals, and
readers are reusable across children and levels. Reviewer estimates for the
complete Foundations-through-Essentials journey run around $700 total, which
undercuts buying AAR plus AAS separately for families who want everything
integrated. Price pages: [Foundations](https://logicofenglish.com/pages/foundations),
[Essentials bundles](https://logicofenglish.com/products/essentials-1-7-core-materials-bundle).

## Why families choose it

- One integrated purchase replaces reading, spelling, handwriting, and early
  grammar programs; no four-way stack to assemble.
- The rules actually explain English; parents report learning to spell
  alongside the child, and why-minded kids stop fighting.
- OG-grade explicitness for dyslexia with a games-heavy, kid-friendly surface.
- Essentials cleanly serves older struggling spellers and readers without
  babyish materials.

## Why families leave it

- Lesson length and density: 30 to 45 teacher-led minutes beats some
  families' budget; AAR's 20-minute scripted days are the common landing.
- Component and prep overwhelm; minimalists go to workbook-only options
  (Explode the Code, TGTB).
- Price shock at entry, especially against TGTB's free PDFs.
- Kids who read early find Foundations redundant and skip to Essentials or
  out of the ecosystem entirely.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: phonograms (all 75), spelling rules by number,
  Foundations A-D, Essentials units and A/B/C difficulty levels, spelling
  analysis, Rhythm of Handwriting. "She is in Foundations C" is a skill
  position (multi-letter phonograms, schwa) per the crosswalk, not a grade.
- Materials pattern worth studying: spelling analysis notation (marking
  phonograms and rule numbers inside words) is a compact, teachable encoding
  of why a word is spelled as it is; Homa explanations of spelling can
  borrow the idea of always answering "which rule."
- Gap Homa can fill: LOE families juggle many components per lesson; a
  daily just-these-pieces brief (which cards, which game, which page) is
  low-hanging assistance.
- When a family reports LOE plus nothing else for LA, that is normal and
  complete for the early years; do not suggest adding spelling or handwriting
  programs on top.
