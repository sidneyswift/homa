# Lindamood Phoneme Sequencing (LiPS)

## Snapshot

LiPS (the Lindamood Phoneme Sequencing Program for Reading, Spelling, and
Speech, formerly Auditory Discrimination in Depth) is the clinic-grade
program beneath the programs: where OG systems assume a child can hear and
compare speech sounds, LiPS builds that ability from the mouth up.
Students learn the physical, articulatory identity of each phoneme (the
program's famous "lip poppers" and "tip tappers" mouth-picture labels,
its "brothers and cousins" sound groupings, and a vowel circle), then
track sounds in syllables with colored blocks and mouth pictures before
letters enter at all. Written by Patricia and Phyllis Lindamood and now in
its fourth edition from Gander Publishing ($515.95 for the kit), it is
the engine of Lindamood-Bell's intensive clinics and a speech-language
pathology staple. Homes buy it in two situations: a child fails the
auditory part of Barton's student screening (Barton's own site routes
those families to LiPS or Foundation in Sounds first), or a family wants
clinic-grade phonemic work without clinic prices, since Lindamood-Bell
intensives reportedly run $90 to $167 per hour, often 20-plus hours a
week for weeks, five figures total.

## Reputation and rankings

- Deeply respected in the clinical and research literacy world;
  Lindamood-Bell markets LiPS as evidence-based and it appears throughout
  the intervention research literature. Homeschool visibility is low and
  mediated through the dyslexia community rather than curriculum
  reviewers.
- The standing caution comes from inside that community: Susan Barton's
  site states plainly that LiPS "is not a parent-friendly program" and
  recommends hiring a professional with LiPS training and experience.
  Parents who self-deliver describe a steep learning curve (the manual is
  written for professionals; Lindamood-Bell's recommended workshop is 20
  hours) but a program that can move children nothing else touched.
- Trajectory: stable institutional fixture; home use remains niche and
  purpose-driven (pre-Barton remediation being the classic case).
