# The Good and the Beautiful Language Arts

## Snapshot

The Good and the Beautiful (TGTB) Language Arts is the flagship subject line
of Jenny Phillips's fast-growing publisher and the single most disruptive
pricing move in homeschool language arts: complete, full-year courses for
Levels K through 8 as free PDF downloads, with beautiful printed sets sold
cheap. Each level bundles what other families buy as four or five products
(phonics or reading, writing, spelling, grammar, literature, plus geography
and art appreciation) into one course book. Courses are parent-led through
Level 3 and student-directed from Level 4, which makes upper levels nearly
open-and-go for the parent. It is everywhere on Instagram and in Facebook
groups, and it is the default budget answer for eclectic Christian families.
This profile covers the language arts line only; the publisher-level profile
(company, worldview positioning, the whole catalog) is at
[../all-in-one/the-good-and-the-beautiful.md](../all-in-one/the-good-and-the-beautiful.md).

## Reputation and rankings

- Enormous grassroots mindshare driven by price, aesthetics, and the founder's
  audience; frequently the most-mentioned LA program in beginner Facebook
  threads. Award-list presence is thinner than its market presence.
- Praise centers on the free PDFs being genuinely complete courses (not
  samples), the fine-art look, and the all-in-one convenience.
- Criticism clusters: workbook-style density behind the pretty covers, gentle
  or shallow writing instruction at upper levels by rigorous-classical
  standards, frequent new editions (Levels 4 to 5 revised Mar 2026, 6 to 7 in
  development for summer 2026) that orphan used copies, and the recurring
  worldview debate: an LDS founder writing broadly Christian content draws
  objections from parts of the evangelical market and from secular families
  alike, though many of both use it anyway.
- Trajectory: rising, still, on distribution economics alone.

## Pedagogy and method

Integrated, gently traditional, and aesthetic-first: short varied segments
inside each lesson (a grammar point, copywork or dictation, spelling list
work, literature reading, picture study) rather than one skill per book. The
publisher positions its levels as "slightly advanced" relative to public
school grades and tells advanced readers not to skip levels because concepts
are woven through. Phonics in the early levels is systematic with its own
booster-card apparatus; spelling and grammar spiral within and across levels;
writing builds to full-length essays by the end of Level 4's new edition.
Parent-led K to 3 (scripted enough to be open-and-go), student-directed from
Level 4 with an answer key, which is the line's biggest practical selling
point for multi-kid families. Faith and character content is woven through
passages and art selection rather than taught as doctrine.

## Structure and scope

Course ladder: Preschool, Kindergarten Prep, Level K, Levels 1 to 7, then
High School 1 to 3 (Level 8 currently exists as Book Studies, with a full
course planned). Most levels are 120 lessons at 4 lessons per week; Level 7
is 140. Components per level: one or two course books, an answer key from
Level 3 up, level-specific readers or read-together books, and the free
Simply Spelling program for levels 4 to 8; Level K uses Reading Booster cards
and books. Placement: start at grade level unless reading needs help; the
free placement test assesses reading only (timed oral passages), because
spelling and grammar are reviewed inside each level. Full course-by-course
detail, lesson counts, the "slightly advanced" positioning quote, and the
crosswalk to bank skills are already captured in
[14-curriculum-crosswalk-ela.md](../../../homa-benchmarks/14-curriculum-crosswalk-ela.md)
section 6; link, do not re-derive.

## A lesson up close

A Level 2 day from the parent's seat: open the course book to the next
numbered lesson (everything is in the one book, in order, no planning page
needed) and work down the page: a short scripted grammar or phonics teaching
moment with a colorful diagram, a few practice items written directly in the
book, a spelling exercise from the level's list system, then a literature
passage read aloud or from the level reader, sometimes closing with picture
study of a classic painting or a geography tie-in. Thirty to forty-five
minutes total. A Level 5 day looks the same but the student runs it alone and
the parent checks against the answer key. The page anatomy is TGTB's
signature: soft cream background, fine-art reproductions and watercolor
spot illustrations, serif typography, moderate item counts with the teaching
text and the practice items interleaved on the same page (course book as
combined textbook and workbook). A completed day produces filled course-book
pages and read pages, with no separate products to reconcile.

## Materials and visual design

The category leader in beauty per dollar: matte covers, cream paper,
classical paintings, botanical illustration, and a consistent soft palette
that photographs like a lifestyle brand. Course books are consumable
(written-in), typically 8.5 by 11 softcovers, several hundred pages across
one or two parts per level. The aesthetic is the moat: it signals calm,
wholesome, curated education, and it is the thing families cite first. The
free PDFs are the identical content without the print quality; home printing
several hundred color pages often costs more than buying the printed set,
which is part of the pricing genius.

## Pricing

As of Jul 2026: Levels K through 8 are free, complete PDF downloads from the
publisher's
[free language arts page](https://www.goodandbeautiful.com/pages/free-homeschool-language-arts).
Printed course sets run about $48.97 (Level K) to $89.92 (upper elementary),
with most levels near $70 to 80; High School 1 to 3 sets are $117.93 to
$125.92 and are not free. Handwriting workbooks (a separate small line) are
about $19.99 in PDF. Course books are consumable, so multi-kid families
rebuy or reprint per child; readers are reusable. Editions turn over faster
than most publishers, which weakens resale. Price page:
[choose your course](https://www.goodandbeautiful.com/pages/choose-language-arts).

## Why families choose it

- Free, actually: a complete K-8 language arts education for printing costs.
- One course covers the whole ELA block plus art and geography; nothing else
  to assemble or schedule.
- Self-directed from Level 4 frees the parent for younger siblings.
- The look: families want school to feel like this.
- Gentle Christian character content without denominational doctrine (a
  feature for its core market, a bug for others; see religious_notes).

## Why families leave it

- Struggling readers need more explicit help than the integrated phonics
  provides; these families move to AAR or Logic of English for reading and
  often keep TGTB for everything else.
- Writing depth: families wanting rigorous composition add or switch to IEW
  or WWS in the middle grades.
- Worldview friction in both directions (too LDS-adjacent for some
  evangelicals, too God-present for secular families), sending them to
  Abeka/BJU or to secular stacks respectively.
- Edition churn frustrates used-market buyers and multi-year planners.
- Some kids find the all-in-one pages busy and prefer one-skill-at-a-time
  workbooks.

## Competitive notes for Homa

- Vocabulary to know: levels not grades ("slightly advanced" claim), course
  book, Reading Boosters, Simply Spelling, Book Studies, the placement test
  that only tests reading. "We do TGTB 3" implies the whole ELA block is one
  product and the parent still teaches; from Level 4 the child self-runs.
- The crosswalk porting note applies verbatim: TGTB carries a built-in ahead
  offset, so a child switching away mid-year is not behind when their skills
  sit at bank anchors; treat the curriculum's pacing as the family's chosen
  frame.
- Materials lesson: interleaved teach-then-practice on one page, plus art
  that earns shelf pride, is the bar Homa-generated materials get judged
  against in this market. Free-plus-beautiful is the distribution playbook
  Homa's own free surfaces compete with.
- Gap Homa can fill: TGTB's integration means a family has no per-skill
  visibility (spelling vs grammar vs writing progress are one course). Homa
  can give the skill-level map without asking the family to change products.
- When TGTB LA appears in a stack, phonics-for-a-struggler is the most likely
  add-on need; the complements pattern is TGTB plus AAR, not TGTB vs AAR.
