translation for these programs is sourced in

positioning, switching flows, and what the category means for Homa.

## Switching paths

Curriculum switching is normal in this market, and the flows are
directional, not random.

Out of Saxon, two lanes. Families whose children cried through the
30-problem mixed sets go to Teaching Textbooks for independence with
mercy (the single best-documented flow in forum archives). Families who
respected the rigor but wanted meaning go to Singapore-family programs,
Math Mammoth, or Math-U-See. Traffic also runs back: families who left
Saxon for gentler options and watched test scores sag return for the
retention.

Out of Math-U-See, the exits split by child. Strong students leave for
challenge (Beast Academy, Dimensions Math, Saxon upper levels), usually
around Epsilon or the pre-algebra transition. Test-anxious families leave
over strand timing (no decimals until Zeta) toward grade-labeled
programs. Inbound, MUS is the classic landing spot for children who
failed with abstract programs, arriving from Saxon, Horizons, and
public-school Common Core alike.

Out of RightStart, the story is parent burnout around levels C to E:
destinations are Math with Confidence (same conceptual heart, lighter
script), Math Mammoth (independence), or a Beast-plus-easy-practice
pairing for strong kids. RightStart G and H's geometry-led years push
conventional families to standard pre-algebra sequences early.

Out of Teaching Textbooks, the trigger is rigor doubt approaching high
school: families step up to Saxon, Dimensions Math, or AoPS lines, or
simply run TT one level ahead and stay. Inbound flow into TT is the
largest in the category, arriving from every teacher-heavy program.

Around Beast Academy, flows are asymmetric: families rarely leave it for
another core (they either pair it with an easy fluency program like Math
Mammoth or drop to one), and its completion dumps into AoPS Prealgebra,
which loses the comic scaffolding and sheds some families to
conventional middle school programs.

The free-gravity flow: Simply Good and Beautiful Math pulls trial
adoption from everywhere because trying it costs nothing, and it sheds
STEM-ambitious families to Saxon, Math Mammoth, Dimensions, or TT around
Math 5 to 7, plus everyone at its Math 8 cap. Math with Confidence is
the new first-choice gravity for teaching-minded parents of young kids,
absorbing families who would have started RightStart or Singapore, and
it exits everyone at grade 6 into the pre-algebra decision.

## What wins in this category

Four currencies buy market share, and no program holds more than two.
Independence (Teaching Textbooks, CTCMath, Math Mammoth) wins the
multi-kid and tired-parent households that make up much of the market.
Reassurance (Math-U-See, Math with Confidence, SGBM) wins parents whose
real fear is their own competence or their child's tears. Rigor identity
(Beast Academy, Dimensions Math, Saxon) wins families who see math
outcomes as the point of homeschooling. And price (SGBM free, Math
Mammoth $39.50, CTC family cap, TT family cap) decides ties and owns the
large-family segment. Design is the quiet fifth: SGBM and Beast prove
that page beauty and character art create loyalty that survives
pedagogical doubts, while Saxon proves an installed base can survive
without any. Placement infrastructure is the professional tell: the
strongest programs publish real placement instruments, and their
semantics differ enough (exit tests, readiness tests, skill interviews)
that the crosswalk's translation layer is genuinely scarce knowledge.

## Gaps Homa can fill

1. Translation across level languages. The category speaks Greek letters,
   dual-grade fractions, letter levels, and understated comic levels; the
   crosswalk already maps them to one ladder, and no competitor owns this
   parent-facing. "She finished Epsilon, is that grade 5?" is a question
   only Homa can answer reassuringly and correctly.
2. The practice layer each core lacks. Saxon families need less review,
   Beast families need more fluency, MUS families need cross-strand
   variety, TT and CTC families need paper and word problems, RightStart
   families need independent practice that honors the no-drill ethos.
   Generated materials tuned to the family's declared curriculum
   vocabulary hit each of these without asking anyone to switch.
3. Transition moments. Every program has a structural exit (SGBM after
   Math 8, MWC after grade 6, Horizons after Algebra I, Beast after level
   5, TT before Calculus, RightStart after F or H). These are scheduled,
   predictable anxiety spikes where Homa can own the placement decision.
4. Parent-confidence scripting. Math with Confidence demonstrates the
   demand for exact words plus the why; Homa's lesson briefs can deliver
   that pattern across any curriculum a family already uses.
5. Progress visibility without a platform. TT and CTC set the bar for
   effortless gradebooks; paper-program families (Saxon, MM, Horizons,
   SGBM) have nothing comparable, and Homa's progress surfaces can serve
   exactly that majority.
