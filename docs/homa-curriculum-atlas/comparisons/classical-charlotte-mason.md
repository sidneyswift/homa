# Classical and Charlotte Mason systems: comparison and switching paths

Companion to the ten profiles in `../curricula/all-in-one/`. Covers the
classical and Charlotte Mason full-curriculum systems profiled 2026-07-13:
Classical Conversations, Memoria Press, AmblesideOnline, Simply Charlotte
Mason, Veritas Press, Tapestry of Grace, The Well-Trained Mind ecosystem,
Gather Round, A Gentle Feast, and The Peaceful Press. Segment behavior and
AI-framing evidence live in
`docs/homa-planning/icp-research/08-segments-curricula.md` §7 (cards 1 and
2); the philosophies themselves are covered in
`docs/homa-planning/icp-research/10-homeschool-methods-glossary.md`.

## Community-based vs curriculum-based classical: CC vs Memoria

The deepest split in classical homeschooling is not pedagogy, it is what
the family is actually buying. Classical Conversations sells belonging
with materials attached: tuition buys a weekly room of like-minded
families, tutors modeling the method, public-speaking reps, and social
accountability, while the actual curriculum (one parent guide, audio,
cards) is thin by design and the family still supplies math and language
arts. Memoria Press sells the opposite: a complete finished plan (every
book, every day, every checkbox) with no community at all, at roughly half
CC's all-in cost. The two fail differently: CC breaks when the local
community is weak, the volunteer expectations grate, or the business-model
controversies (director licensing economics, church tax exposure) turn
conscience into churn; Memoria breaks by sheer load, a classroom-derived
schedule wearing down one parent alone at a kitchen table. The exchange
between them is the category's busiest switching corridor: ex-CC families
wanting classical-without-the-co-op land on Memoria's manuals, and
isolated Memoria families wanting other humans land in CC, hybrid
academies, or Scholé-style co-ops. Both prove the same product law from
opposite ends: in classical education, structure is worth more than
content, whether that structure is social (CC) or clerical (Memoria).

## The free commons vs paid CM products

Charlotte Mason is the only segment in this atlas whose reference
implementation is free. AmblesideOnline's volunteer Advisory built the
purist benchmark, gave it away, and by doing so set the terms for every
paid CM product: SCM, A Gentle Feast, and The Peaceful Press all get
explained to newcomers as "AO, but..." (easier, prettier, more planned,
gentler book levels). What the paid products actually sell is not
content, since the method is public domain and the books are mostly trade
or public-domain titles; they sell execution: daily itineraries, leveled
copywork sheets, morning time packets, training videos, phone support.
The commons also disciplines pricing (AGF reads as expensive at $197 per
cycle only because free exists) and enforces provenance values: this
segment noticed that AO's volunteers have no commercial motive, and it
extends trust accordingly. The lesson for any product entering CM space,
Homa included: you cannot out-content the commons, you can only
out-execute it, and you must be transparently respectful of it (link AO,
never re-sell what it gives away). The same dynamic barely exists on the
classical side, where the anchor artifacts (CC's guide, Memoria's
manuals, Veritas's cards) are all commercial and copyright-guarded.

## Memory work and narration as product mechanics

The two traditions run on two different core mechanics, and every product
in this category is an implementation of one of them.

Memory work (classical) is a content mechanic: a fixed corpus (CC's
weekly grid across seven subjects, Veritas's 32 cards per year, Memoria's
recitation schedules) drilled to automaticity, with cyclical review,
audio as the real delivery medium (songs in CC and Veritas), mastery
rituals as motivation (Memory Master's four-proof gauntlet), and a
laminated-card economy as the physical expression. It produces measurable,
performable outcomes (a child who can recite a 161-event timeline), which
is precisely its parent appeal and its critic target (facts without
context is the standing churn complaint at both CC and Veritas).

Narration (CM) is a process mechanic: the child tells back every reading,
orally then in writing, and that act is simultaneously the comprehension
work, the composition curriculum, and the assessment. It needs no
worksheets, produces no paper by default (the compliance-state pain), and
is a skill the parent must be coached into (the "narration didn't come
easily and I doubted the books" arc appears in nearly every AO Year 1
retrospective). The paid CM products' student sheets (Peaceful Press's
leveled copywork, AGF's packets, Gather Round's response ladder from word
bank to essay) are all attempts to give narration-shaped work a paper
body.

For Homa's materials studio the mechanics dictate opposite outputs: for
memory-work families, drill scaffolds, review games, and recitation
tracking (never the memory work text itself, which is copyrighted); for
narration families, prompts, narration logs, and notebook pages (never a
summary of the reading, which would do the child's work).

## The most prep-burdened and the most AI-skeptical, simultaneously

These two segments carry the heaviest parent execution load in
homeschooling: TOG's 40-50 page week-plans need a weekend of
plate-picking; AO parents pre-read Victorian prose, pace by the page, and
orchestrate per-child book tracks; WTM parents assemble and schedule an
entire curriculum from an 800-page manual; even open-and-go Memoria runs
3-5 teaching hours a day. And yet, per the segment cards (08 §7), they are
also the families most articulate against AI in education: the classical
objection is philosophical (CiRCE: "judgment cannot be downloaded"), the
CM objection is aesthetic-moral (machine-generated content is presumptive
twaddle, dumbed-down words displacing living ideas).

The resolution is that the skepticism has a precise shape. Both
philosophies locate education in the child's own act (memorizing,
narrating, wrestling with a hard book), so anything that performs the
child's act is corrosive, while the parent's clerical layer (scheduling,
pacing, rebalancing after a sick week, multi-child orchestration, record
generation, materials logistics) is not sacred at all; it is the part
they resent. The evidence in the profiles supports this: SCM's CM
Organizer (a scheduling tool) is beloved; Veritas's self-paced screens
are accepted because the worldview is trusted and the parent stays in
charge; CC families happily buy an app for memory-work audio. The
skepticism is a boundary, not a wall, and the boundary is the child's
mind.

## Switching paths

The corridors that appear repeatedly in exit narratives and forum threads:

- CC to Memoria Press: keep classical, drop the co-op, halve the cost.
  The single most common classical switch.
- CC or Memoria to CM (SCM, AGF, AO): rigor fatigue; families keep the
  book culture and drop the drill. The reverse flow (CM to Memoria) is
  rarer and usually about wanting more structure for logic-stage kids.
- AO to SCM or AGF: same method, purchased execution. The reverse (SCM
  to AO) is the purist's graduation, usually after reading Mason's
  volumes.
- TOG to Sonlight, MFW, or Gather Round: buffet overwhelm to scheduled
  days. Gather Round is increasingly the modern landing spot because it
  keeps the whole-family mechanic.
- Gather Round to per-subject stacks: sufficiency anxiety in the middle
  years; families keep units for history/science and add formal LA and
  math, which is drift toward eclectic, the market's default direction.
- WTM is less a stop than a substrate: families "doing WTM" flow into
  and out of every other program while keeping SOTW and the forums.
- High school consolidates: whatever the family ran K-8, grades 9-12
  push toward credit-bearing structure (Veritas Omnibus or VSA, CC
  Challenge, Memoria packages or Academy, WTM Academy), because
  transcripts start mattering.

## Gaps Homa can fill without stepping on the landmine

The landmine, restated from the segment cards: never appear to do the
child's thinking (classical) and never produce twaddle or displace the
child's narration (CM). The safe, valuable ground the profiles expose:

1. Execution relief for the parent's clerical layer. The Fog (TOG), AO's
   weekly-to-daily pacing gap, WTM's assembly burden, Memoria's
   fall-behind rebalancing: all are scheduling and orchestration
   problems, not pedagogy problems. This is Homa's planner surface, and
   SCM's rolling "do what's next" model is the segment-native pattern to
   match.
2. Records from oral work. Narration-heavy and memory-heavy homes
   produce little paper; compliance states demand it. Logging narrations,
   recitations, and read-alouds into a portfolio-ready record turns the
   method's biggest bureaucratic weakness into a Homa feature (the same
   compliance-automation wedge the unschooling card documents).
3. Multi-child orchestration on one topic. TOG and Gather Round prove
   one-topic-many-levels is the shape these families want; Gather
   Round's response ladder (trace, word bank, sentence, paragraph,
   essay) is the cleanest difficulty grammar in the market and the right
   model for Homa's multi-kid materials.
4. Coverage mapping and gap-filling. Gather Round's "is it enough"
   anxiety, Foundations' known incompleteness (no math or LA), and WTM's
   science softness at high school are all askable questions Homa can
   answer neutrally from its own skill graph, then fill with
   method-respecting practice.
5. Stack-aware assistance. Every one of these programs assumes external
   math (AO, SCM, AGF, Gather Round, TOG, CC) or external subjects;
   these families are already multi-vendor. Knowing the named program's
   vocabulary (cycle and week, Year and term, Form, learning level,
   strand) so the mom never has to explain her own curriculum is the
   trust-building table stakes this atlas exists to provide.

What stays out of scope on this ground: generating memory-work
recitations or answers, summarizing the child's readings, writing
narrations, producing "worksheets" for CM content subjects uninvited, and
reproducing any program's copyrighted text. The products themselves prove
the demand boundary: tools that serve the parent's clipboard thrive here;
tools that touch the child's work do not survive the philosophy.
